In brief · Agents and memory

Harness-Zero reports distilling agent-harness behavior into a model for use under a minimal deployment scaffold

Harness-Zero uses an optimized source harness and a harnessing agent to rewrite a student's proposals into trajectories compatible with a fixed, minimal target harness; supervised fine-tuning then aims to retain useful behaviors in the model. Across SpreadsheetBench Verified, AppWorld and USPTO Retrosynthesis, the authors report that Qwen3.5-9B under the minimal harness rises from 23.3% to 44.3% macro-average task success after distillation, above 41.7% for the base model with the optimized harness still attached. The experiments also report an 81.1% versus 78.1% average for agent-as-harness versus code-as-harness in six frontier-model benchmark settings, and 82.3% recovery of 28 mined harness-induced behaviors. These are author-reported results from a single SFT seed; independent reproduction, broader transfer and full cost accounting remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Harness-Zero uses an optimized source harness, a reference harness and a harnessing agent to convert student proposals into supervision compatible with a fixed minimal target harness, then uses supervised fine-tuning to distill resulting behavior into the model.
  • Claimed — Across SpreadsheetBench Verified, AppWorld and USPTO Retrosynthesis, the authors report Qwen3.5-9B macro-average task success of 23.3% for the base model under a minimal harness, 44.3% after Harness-Zero distillation under that harness, and 41.7% for the base model with the optimized harness attached. They report one SFT seed (42).
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that harness behaviors can be broadly removed across models and domains, that the distilled approach reduces total compute or cost, or that results are stable across training seeds.

Strongest baseline

Base Qwen3.5-9B under the same minimal target harness at 23.3% macro-average task success; the base model with optimized harness attached reports 41.7%, versus 44.3% after distillation under the minimal harness.

Limitations

  • All performance and behavior-recovery results are author-reported; the research desk did not reproduce them.
  • The distillation experiment trains Qwen3.5-9B separately for three domains with LoRA SFT using seed 42; variance across seeds and student models is not reported in the inspected setup.
  • The tasks and harnesses are bounded to SpreadsheetBench Verified, AppWorld and USPTO Retrosynthesis with a fixed minimal mini-SWE-agent target harness.
  • The paper does not establish broad transfer, stable gains under other deployment harnesses, or lower total training and inference cost.

What we still don't know

  • The reported task-success improvements and behavior-recovery rates have not been independently reproduced.
  • Reported SFT uses one seed and one Qwen3.5-9B student; uncertainty across seeds, models, domains and target harnesses is unknown.
  • The paper does not provide a matched accounting of harness evolution, review-agent inference, trajectory generation, fine-tuning and deployment costs.

Why it matters

Agent capabilities often depend on tools, middleware, memory and workflow scaffolds that are expensive to build and maintain. If useful scaffold behavior can be learned into model weights, deployment might use a simpler fixed harness. This paper reports an initial cross-domain experiment, but does not establish broad transfer, lower total cost or reliable scaffold removal across models.
