What changed

An October 27 arXiv preprint introduces leverage, the Fisher quadratic form of a weight update divided by the same form for a random direction of equal size at the same checkpoint. Across released Olmo-3, Tülu-3, Zephyr and OLMo-2 checkpoints, the author reports leverage near 1 for two pretraining windows on a 51,167-token mixed corpus, about 5 for SFT, hundreds to low thousands for RL-Zero/RLVR, and values from hundreds to tens of thousands for DPO. Controlled training and one released OLMo-2 DPO recipe rerun support some of the reported contrasts. The paper also reports that a sign-structure term predicts damage from random delta sparsification in tested cases. These are author-reported research results. The metric measures local next-token distribution change, not task capability; results are bounded by the corpora and training regimes studied, and no independent reproduction was performed here.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The author defines leverage as an update direction’s Fisher quadratic form divided by the mean form for random directions of the same size at the same checkpoint, using an exact forward-mode Jacobian-vector product rather than a finite-difference approximation. It measures local next-token-distribution change per weight displacement, not task capability.
  • Claimed — Across released checkpoints, the paper reports leverage near 1 for two Olmo-3 pretraining windows on a 51,167-token four-domain corpus, about 5 for SFT, roughly 388–1,508 for RL-Zero/RLVR, and roughly 378–22,570 for DPO. These are author-computed values on selected models, deltas and corpora.
  • Claimed — In controlled experiments, the author reports that update leverage falls as SFT or DPO trajectories move at 0.5B and 7B, and that two reruns of one released OLMo-2-1B DPO recipe match its released displacement and leverage within 1% and 4%, respectively. Seed counts are two or three in many comparisons; the 7B controlled arms have one seed.
  • Claimed — For tested released deltas where predicted damage was below about 0.5, the paper reports that its sign-flip null predicted random drop-and-rescale sparsification damage within a factor of 2.1. The relation breaks down when predicted damage is large; the study tested six deltas and three keep rates.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish that leverage measures task capability, that higher leverage causes better downstream performance, that local rankings persist on longer or task-specific data, or that its conclusions generalize across training systems and model families. The stated extrapolation tests are inconclusive.

Strongest baseline

At the author’s 51,167-token mixed corpus, two Olmo-3 pretraining windows had reported leverage 0.78 and 0.95 against an isotropic random-direction null of 1; selected post-training deltas had much higher reported values. The paper’s evaluation is local output-distribution curvature, not task performance.

Limitations

  • All quantitative findings are author-reported and were not independently reproduced here.
  • The headline corpus has 51,167 tokens across four domains; transfer to longer or task-specific corpora is not established.
  • Many controlled comparisons use two or three seeds; 7B controlled arms use one seed, and the OLMo-2 reproduction covers one released DPO stage.
  • Leverage is local Fisher curvature of next-token distributions and does not directly measure task capability or usefulness.
  • The paper reports inconclusive extrapolation tests.
  • bf16 storage can zero coordinates and distort some reported alignment measures.

What we still don't know

  • The reported exact Fisher measurements, null draws and checkpoint comparisons have not been independently reproduced in this review; the paper says it will release code and corpora.
  • The headline measure is averaged over a 51,167-token four-domain corpus and a small number of released deltas; it is not established on longer or task-specific evaluation data.
  • The paper’s preregistered extrapolation predictions perform inconclusively on small numbers of scorable GSM8K and MATH-500 deltas.

Why it matters

If reproduced and linked to task-level outcomes, a matched-random Fisher measure could help compare how training stages alter model outputs and estimate when weight-delta sparsification will cause damage. The paper does not establish that higher leverage means better capability or that its conclusions generalize to other models, tasks or training pipelines.
