Zyphra reports hierarchical sparse attention results through 256K context

A preprint introduces Zyphra Hierarchical Attention (ZHA), which routes queries through a tree of compressed key summaries to select sparse attention keys. What changed

A preprint introduces Zyphra Hierarchical Attention (ZHA), which routes queries through a tree of compressed key summaries to select sparse attention keys. In author-run tests on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, six-level routing reportedly scored 93.3% on RULER-13 versus 96.0% for dense attention while attending to 1%–6% of the original KV cache; the authors also report favorable comparisons with Minimax Sparse Attention on long-context retrieval. They did not release optimized kernels. Latency advantages beyond measured evaluation lengths, including at millions or billions of tokens, are model-based projections, and performance at those lengths remains unvalidated.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv:2610.42112v1 on 2026-10-30 describing Zyphra Hierarchical Attention, a tree-routed sparse-attention method.
  • Claimed — On Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, the authors report reasoning and agentic-coding scores near the dense-attention baseline and a 93.3% versus 96.0% score on RULER-13 for a six-level router while attending to 1%–6% of the original KV cache. In a LongBench v2 comparison, they report a 14-point advantage over Minimax Sparse Attention at a similar 1.1% decode-key share, despite using one-tenth of its training-token budget.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, optimized GPU-kernel latency or measured performance beyond 256K tokens; multi-million- and billion-token speed claims are roofline-model extrapolations, and the authors state evaluation quality at those lengths has not been validated.

Strongest baseline

Dense attention and Minimax Sparse Attention on the same Qwen3.6 backbone are reported; the MSA indexer uses 40B training tokens. Other model-family and production-serving comparisons are not established.

Limitations

  • All evaluation results are author-reported; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The principal RULER sweep is measured at 64K, 128K and 256K; performance quality beyond 256K is not validated in the paper.
  • The paper does not release optimized GPU kernels. Million- and billion-token latency comparisons use a roofline model rather than measured end-to-end service.
  • Results include Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and a separate Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B conversion; broad model-family transfer is not established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the retrieval and reasoning results was checked.
  • End-to-end latency with optimized kernels and empirical accuracy beyond 256K are not established; longer-context speed results are modeled.

Why it matters

If the reported retrieval quality and sparse selection hold across models and real serving, hierarchical routing could reduce the cost of attention at long contexts. Evidence is currently author-reported on a small set of backbones and measured tasks through 256K tokens; projected latency at much longer contexts is not an empirical result.
