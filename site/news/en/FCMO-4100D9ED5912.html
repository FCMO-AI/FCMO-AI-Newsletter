TauIndianBankBench reports that outcome-only scoring misses procedural failures in banking agents

A paper introduces a 1,000-task synthetic Indian retail-banking benchmark for tool-using conversational agents. It scores both final database state and whether required calls—including read-only policy checks—occurred, while rejecting unrequested writes. In author-run evaluations of six models, final-state-only scoring raised pass rates by 6.5–12.0 percentage points over the paired action-plus-state criterion. The results are reported by the authors and have not been independently reproduced; simulator error, real customer behavior, non-English performance and deployment reliability remain unestablished.

What changed

A paper introduces a 1,000-task synthetic Indian retail-banking benchmark for tool-using conversational agents. It scores both final database state and whether required calls—including read-only policy checks—occurred, while rejecting unrequested writes. In author-run evaluations of six models, final-state-only scoring raised pass rates by 6.5–12.0 percentage points over the paired action-plus-state criterion. The results are reported by the authors and have not been independently reproduced; simulator error, real customer behavior, non-English performance and deployment reliability remain unestablished.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The released benchmark contains 1,000 synthetic Indian retail-banking tasks and evaluates both final records and required tool calls, including policy-mandated reads; unrequested writes fail the action check.
  • Claimed — In author-run evaluations of six models, scoring final records alone increased pass rates by 6.5–12.0 percentage points relative to the combined action-plus-final-state criterion.
  • Claimed — The authors report that a separate scorer reimplementation matched the action-plus-state verdict on all 600 sessions and all 705 paired calls; this audit is described within the same paper and is not an external reproduction.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish real-bank customer failure rates, suitability for deployment, performance in Indian languages, or generality to other regulated service domains.

Strongest baseline

The benchmark’s own paired final-state-only check on the same recorded model sessions; this isolates scoring-rule differences in the reported results but does not provide an external benchmark or real-world baseline.

Limitations

  • All benchmark scores are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • The benchmark uses a simulated bank and synthetic customer records; task frequencies do not estimate real customer traffic.
  • The customer simulator affects results and its error rate was not measured.
  • Version 1.0.0 is English-only; no non-English or real-bank deployment results are reported.
  • A pass is benchmark-specific and does not establish deployment suitability.

What we still don't know

  • The 6.5–12.0-point gap and model scores are author-reported; this activation did not independently run the code or re-score the released corpus.
  • The authors state that error in the LLM customer simulator was not measured; all customer data are synthetic and the benchmark is English-only.
  • Benchmark pass rates do not establish real-world banking performance, deployment reliability or suitability.

Why it matters

The benchmark demonstrates a plausible measurement failure in outcome-only agent evaluation: an assistant can leave the right records while skipping policy-required checks or making an unrequested change. If the reported gap persists under independent audit, regulated-domain evaluations may need to score process and outcome together. This is one synthetic, English-language retail-banking environment, not evidence of real-bank failure rates.
