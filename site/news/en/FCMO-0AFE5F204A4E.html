SWE-Proof reports that machine-checkable specifications improve issue resolution, while specification faithfulness limits autonomous gains

The v2 SWE-Proof paper builds a 500-issue benchmark from SWE-bench Verified and a Python subset of SWE-bench Pro, pairing each issue with a formal specification, a verifying implementation, and a ground-truth patch. On SWE-bench Verified, the authors report that a correctly supplied specification raised resolution from an 85% unaided baseline to about 95%, while adding verification to an end-to-end agent that writes its own specification did not improve its roughly 85% baseline. In a separate specification-synthesis test, only 56% of model-written specifications passed the paper’s five-property audit; incomplete behavioral coverage (faithfulness) was the main failure. The results are author-reported. Their guarantee depends on audited axioms and faithful refinement from verified code to the executed patch, and the audit panel itself uses language models.

What actually changed

The v2 SWE-Proof paper builds a 500-issue benchmark from SWE-bench Verified and a Python subset of SWE-bench Pro, pairing each issue with a formal specification, a verifying implementation, and a ground-truth patch. On SWE-bench Verified, the authors report that a correctly supplied specification raised resolution from an 85% unaided baseline to about 95%, while adding verification to an end-to-end agent that writes its own specification did not improve its roughly 85% baseline. In a separate specification-synthesis test, only 56% of model-written specifications passed the paper’s five-property audit; incomplete behavioral coverage (faithfulness) was the main failure. The results are author-reported. Their guarantee depends on audited axioms and faithful refinement from verified code to the executed patch, and the audit panel itself uses language models.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The paper reports a 500-issue benchmark covering SWE-bench Verified and a Python fragment of SWE-bench Pro, with formal specifications, verifying implementations, ground-truth patches and recorded gate outcomes.
  • CLAIMED — On SWE-bench Verified, the authors report that a correct supplied specification increases issue resolution from about 85% unaided to about 95%; verification catches defects that the held-out tests miss.
  • CLAIMED — When the agent must synthesize its own specification, end-to-end resolution shows no gain over the unaided baseline; only 56% of synthesized specifications pass the paper’s five-property audit, with faithfulness the main failure.
  • CLAIMED — The paper states that its verify-implies-resolve guarantee depends on audited axiom soundness and fidelity of refinement from the verified implementation to the executed patch; specification audits are evidence from a language-model panel, not a mathematical proof of the full pipeline.

Strongest baseline

Within-paper SWE-bench Verified comparisons include an unaided baseline, an end-to-end setting where the agent writes a specification and patch, and a verified-from-spec setting where the specification is supplied. Appendix reports repeated runs for major settings.

The caveat that matters

  • The guarantee is conditional on the soundness of axioms and fidelity of the refinement from verified implementation to executed patch.
  • The specification audit is a calibrated language-model panel; its verdicts are evidence, not proof.
  • Model-generated specifications and artifacts remain difficult to synthesize faithfully: only 56% of specifications in the cited synthesis setting passed the five-property audit, with faithfulness the dominant failure.
  • A few instances use manual modeling or relax a secondary no-leakage property, limiting their evidential strength.
  • All outcome, benchmark and audit results are author-reported; the research desk has not independently rerun the pipeline or inspected bundle artifacts.
  • The central comparison is conditional on supplied formal specifications and specific SWE-bench tasks; it does not establish autonomous formal verification across arbitrary repositories or software properties.

What remains unknown

  • No independent recomputation of the benchmark outcomes, repeated-run variation or verification-vs-test comparison has been performed.
  • The authors’ axiom-soundness and refinement-fidelity evidence, including language-model audit panels, has not been independently red-teamed.
  • Transfer to other programming languages, repositories, issue distributions and verification backends is not established by the headline result.

FCMO Lens

The paper separates proof checking from the harder task of expressing the intended behavior faithfully. Its reported gap suggests formal verification can catch defects missed by tests when the specification is sound, while an agent that writes its own incomplete specification can gain little. This is consequential for software-agent evaluation and tool design, but does not show that general coding agents can autonomously produce correct proofs or specifications in ordinary repositories.
