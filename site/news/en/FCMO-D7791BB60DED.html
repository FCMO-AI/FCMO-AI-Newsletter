A preprint reports a ten-day autonomous agent campaign that preserved task state across 47 context resets

Nijkamp, Koul, Pakhomov and Pang propose a harness architecture for agents operating beyond individual contexts and processes. It uses time-scale levels with bounded state, clocked action ticks, named state protocols and review-and-escalate model tiers. The authors report a ten-day campaign reproducing a published asynchronous reinforcement-learning result, with continuity across session/context resets; Appendix A lists 211 driver ticks, 47 context resets and 2,215 GPU-hours against a 3,000-hour cap. These are author-reported results from one deployment, not an independent evaluation or general benchmark.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Nijkamp, Koul, Pakhomov and Pang published arXiv:2609.19519v1 on 2026-09-17, proposing an architecture based on time-scale levels, clocked ticks, protocols and cascaded intelligence.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report a ten-day agent campaign that reproduced a published asynchronous reinforcement-learning result, maintained the task thread across context/session resets, and used 211 driver ticks and 47 context resets.
  • CLAIMED — Appendix A reports 2,215 GPU-hours against a 3,000-hour cap and 25 escalations to the principal during the campaign. These campaign totals and the reproduction outcome were not independently verified.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the campaign against its own previously published RL reproduction target, not a matched episodic-agent control for long-horizon reliability.

The caveat that matters

  • The evidence is one ten-day campaign on a single asynchronous reinforcement-learning reproduction task; it is an existence report, not a benchmark of long-horizon agents.
  • The primary source reports campaign metrics and outcome, but this activation did not inspect implementation code, raw logs or an independent reproduction.
  • The paper distinguishes persistent operating knowledge from parametric continual learning; its campaign does not establish general autonomous capability, generalized learning or reliable performance across deployments.

What remains unknown

  • The reported ten-day campaign, reset recovery, reproduction outcome, resource totals and operating-knowledge effects are author-reported for one deployment; no independent reproduction or transfer test was inspected.

FCMO Lens

Long-running agent work depends on what survives between sessions, how actions are checked and when stronger models are used. The paper offers a concrete harness design and a reported ten-day campaign, but its evidence remains a single author-run task; comparative reliability, transfer and human-attention costs remain open.
