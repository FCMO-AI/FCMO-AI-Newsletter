A paper reports explanation-free shortcuts in automated interpretability evaluations

An arXiv paper reports that construction cues in several automated-interpretability evaluation pipelines can predict stored scores without reading feature explanations. On rebuilt marks for 500 GPT-2 small features, the authors report a centre-mark rule at 0.979 balanced accuracy versus 0.838 for stored scorers, and a mark-count rule at 0.969. Their separate preregistered ranking test did not show that the tested simulation cue reordered features; an explanation-sensitive scorer exceeded explanation-free baselines on random-offset natural windows. These are bounded author-reported results, not evidence that automated interpretability as a field is invalid.

What changed

An arXiv paper reports that construction cues in several automated-interpretability evaluation pipelines can predict stored scores without reading feature explanations. On rebuilt marks for 500 GPT-2 small features, the authors report a centre-mark rule at 0.979 balanced accuracy versus 0.838 for stored scorers, and a mark-count rule at 0.969. Their separate preregistered ranking test did not show that the tested simulation cue reordered features; an explanation-sensitive scorer exceeded explanation-free baselines on random-offset natural windows. These are bounded author-reported results, not evidence that automated interpretability as a field is invalid.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — arXiv lists version 1 of the paper as submitted on October 10, 2026.
  • Claimed — On rebuilt marks for Neuronpedia stored fuzzing runs across 500 random GPT-2 small features, the author reports that 91.3% of activating examples place the peak on the centre token; a centre-mark rule reaches balanced accuracy 0.979 versus 0.838 for stored scorers, and a mark-count rule reaches 0.969. The marks were rebuilt from a scorer fork whose version is inferred.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that its tested simulation cue did not demonstrate feature reordering in a preregistered comparison; its 4-level rank-correlation result favors GPT-4, while the 10-level comparison does not separate the scorers. On random-offset natural-text windows over 49 features, the author reports that Jev with the actual explanation reaches pooled correlation 0.331 versus 0.013 with shuffled explanations and 0.015 for the best shared fixed-offset prior.
  • Not established — The reviewed paper does not establish independent reproduction, that the reported cues cause broad feature-ranking changes, or that the findings transfer beyond the sampled models, scorers, pipeline versions and protocols.

Strongest baseline

For rebuilt Neuronpedia marks, a centre-mark rule is reported at balanced accuracy 0.979 versus 0.838 for stored scorers; the mark-count rule reaches 0.969. For natural windows, the reported shared fixed-offset prior is 0.015, versus 0.331 for Jev with the real explanation and 0.013 with a shuffled explanation.

Limitations

  • Headline fuzzing baselines are calculated on marks rebuilt from a fork with inferred version; the author notes that displayed scores differ for 112 of 1,001 runs.
  • The paper reports no demonstrated feature-ranking change from the tested simulation cue. Its 4-level preregistered rank-correlation result favors GPT-4, while the 10-level comparison does not separate the scorers.
  • The natural-window control covers 49 features and changes the item construction; it does not by itself establish transfer to other models, scorers or evaluation pipelines.
  • No independent reproduction, code/data replay or preregistration artifact inspection was performed in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No code, archived raw examples, or data snapshot was inspected; some stored token marks are rebuilt from a fork whose exact version is inferred.
  • No independent group reproduced the baseline scores or assessed whether matched-item controls change the paper’s interpretation.
  • Transfer across other model families, scorer configurations and pipeline versions is unknown; the paper reports the centre cue was absent in a sampled set of 12 Gemma features.

Why it matters

If these pipeline-specific shortcuts replicate, evaluation scores may partly reflect how examples and marks are constructed rather than the feature explanation itself. This affects how researchers compare automated-interpretability scorers and motivates same-item explanation-free controls. The paper’s mixed and adverse ranking results constrain the conclusion; it does not establish broad ranking harm or invalidate the field.
