Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen with broad availability and agentic-workflow claims

Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November 2026 and says it is rolling out across Gemini API, AI Studio, business products, the Gemini app and Search. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November 2026 and says it is rolling out across consumer, developer and enterprise surfaces.
  • Demonstrated — Google lists a 1-million-token context window and API prices of $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens.
  • Claimed — Google reports Hydrogen scores 51 at high and 54 at xhigh on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, scores 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 versus Gemini 3.8 Flash at 18.2%, and in Antigravity Orchestrator achieves near-Carbon Terminal-Bench 4.0 success while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster.
  • Claimed — Google’s model card says Hydrogen did not show a material general-capability increase over Gemini 4 Argon under its assessment, while targeted cyber and biological capability tests reached alert thresholds but not the corresponding critical capability levels; the full Safety & Security Report was still forthcoming.
  • Not established — Independent reproduction of the reported performance, safety and efficiency results, and realized cost savings in external workloads, were not established in this review.

Strongest baseline

Google compares Hydrogen with Gemini 3.8 Flash on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and Gemini 4 Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index; Orchestrator results are compared with Carbon. Comparisons use different benchmark versions and do not establish general superiority.

Limitations

  • All reported benchmark scores and workflow efficiency figures in the launch are provider claims; no independent matched reproduction was inspected.
  • Artificial Analysis Index, Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 4.1 scores are different measurements and cannot be directly combined.
  • The API prices are posted rates, not measured cost per successful task; output length, thinking level and workflow routing can change realized cost.
  • The model card reports a March 2026 knowledge cutoff and a 5.57 percentage-point increase in unjustified refusals versus Gemini 3.8 Flash; the full Safety & Security Report was forthcoming at review time.

What we still don't know

  • No independent matched evaluation of Hydrogen’s reported benchmark performance, tool-use reliability or relative latency was inspected.
  • The claimed 24% quota reduction and 19% faster Orchestrator result have no inspected task-level methodology or external replication.
  • The model card says the full Gemini 4 Hydrogen Safety & Security Report is forthcoming; it is needed to inspect detailed methods behind alert-threshold and safety claims.

Why it matters

If its reported results generalize, a broadly available model designed for faster execution and delegation could affect agent-stack design, model routing and cost/performance choices. The launch establishes availability and price claims; it does not establish independent task performance or realized savings.
