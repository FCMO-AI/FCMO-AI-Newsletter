Critical-State RL selects trainable calls before optimizing multi-turn tool use

The preprint proposes diagnosing which occurrence in a multi-turn interaction is trainable before applying a local reinforcement-learning update. Nested sampling holds each candidate action fixed while resampling reward-only continuations to separate action-dependent signal from downstream noise. In a four-cell BFCL v4 multi_turn study using Gemma-4-26B-A4B, authors report that training the diagnostically selected recovery call raised missing-function accuracy from 0.14 to 0.283 ± 0.015 across four seeds on 200 paired examples; training the alternative decision call reduced it to 0.095. In the missing-argument category, the selected decision call rose from 0.435 to 0.473 ± 0.010, while the alternative recovery call reached 0.445. These are paper-reported results, not an independent reproduction.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Critical-State RL uses nested sampling to separate action-dependent reward variation from continuation noise, then applies an update to a diagnostically selected model call.
  • Claimed — In the paper's BFCL v4 multi_turn four-cell Gemma-4-26B-A4B study, selected-call training improved the reported scores in the two tested categories while the alternative call was flat or worse.
  • Not established — The reported results do not establish independent reproducibility or general improvement across deployed multi-turn agents, models, tasks or reward designs.

Strongest baseline

Within-category alternative occurrence training under the same occurrence-local RL interface: for missing-function, decision-turn training moved accuracy from 0.14 to 0.095 versus selected recovery at 0.283 ± 0.015. For missing-argument, recovery moved 0.435 to 0.445 versus selected decision at 0.473 ± 0.010.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are reported by the paper authors; the research desk did not run code or reproduce them.
  • The four-cell selection experiment is limited to two BFCL v4 multi_turn categories and one no-think Gemma-4-26B-A4B setup.
  • The selected cells summarize four seeds, but alternative-turn comparisons are point estimates; the paired evaluation uses 200 examples.
  • Task-specific local reward construction is part of the method and may affect transfer to other settings.
  • Additional cross-setting examples do not establish general deployment gains.

What we still don't know

  • The diagnostic and BFCL training results have not been independently reproduced.
  • Generalization beyond the two BFCL categories and the tested Gemma setup is unknown.

Why it matters

Trajectory-level rewards can mix the effect of one model call with randomness from later turns. A diagnostic that estimates action-dependent reward variation offers a concrete way to select where to train in a multi-turn agent, while the reported study also shows that the useful training location can vary by task.
