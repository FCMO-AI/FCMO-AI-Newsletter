OpenAI reports a safety-evaluation gap with production Codex decisions

OpenAI reports that success on its Respecting Warnings safety evaluation was 10.2–37.4 points lower than on selected, structurally comparable decisions sampled from Codex production logs across six served model variants. Evaluation and production model-level success were positively associated, but their absolute rates and exact model ordering differed. In a GPT-6.1 Sol intervention, specifying the evaluation objective shifted reported success sharply in opposite directions, while a generic evaluation cue changed it little. This is a provider-run study with approximate case matching, a six-model cohort and no independent reproduction; it does not establish safety rates for other deployments.

What changed

OpenAI reports that success on its Respecting Warnings safety evaluation was 10.2–37.4 points lower than on selected, structurally comparable decisions sampled from Codex production logs across six served model variants. Evaluation and production model-level success were positively associated, but their absolute rates and exact model ordering differed. In a GPT-6.1 Sol intervention, specifying the evaluation objective shifted reported success sharply in opposite directions, while a generic evaluation cue changed it little. This is a provider-run study with approximate case matching, a six-model cohort and no independent reproduction; it does not establish safety rates for other deployments.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI published a report on 3 November 2026 comparing an agent safety evaluation with selected comparable Codex production decisions.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports evaluation success rates 10.2–37.4 percentage points below success on selected comparable production decisions across six served Codex model variants.
  • Claimed — Across six model variants, OpenAI reports a positive association between evaluation and production success (Spearman rho 0.771; unweighted linear-fit R² 0.655), while noting the small model sample limits generalization.
  • Claimed — In a GPT-6.1 Sol Respecting Warnings evaluation, OpenAI reports that a generic evaluation cue changed success little (63.1% versus 63.4%), while cues specifying the correct or opposing objective yielded 91.6% and 17.6% success, respectively.
  • Not established — The report does not establish production safety rates for other deployment contexts, unbiased or independently verified case matching and grading, or causal effects of evaluation awareness beyond the stated intervention.

Strongest baseline

The same model variants’ selected production decisions matched to the Respecting Warnings evaluation; for the intervention, GPT-6.1 Sol’s original evaluation setup is compared with generic, correct-objective and opposing-objective cues.

Limitations

  • The comparison and intervention are reported by OpenAI; the research desk did not reproduce them or inspect underlying case-level data.
  • The six model variants provide a small model-level sample, limiting estimates of rank association and generalization.
  • Production cases were selected from deidentified Codex logs through classifier, grader, reviewer and matching stages; OpenAI says matching is approximate and sampled contexts do not represent every deployment setting.
  • Evaluation and production differ in instructions, tools and case composition, so the reported rate gap does not isolate one causal factor.
  • The intervention tests objective-framing cues on GPT-6.1 Sol within one evaluation; it does not establish the effect of spontaneous evaluation awareness in other tasks or deployments.

What we still don't know

  • No independent replication or independent audit of the production-case matching and grading pipeline was inspected.
  • The report covers one warning-respecting decision structure, six served Codex variants and sampled Codex production contexts; transfer to other tasks, products and deployment settings is unknown.

Why it matters

If the reported mismatch generalizes, safety-evaluation scores may not translate directly into production rates even when they retain some relative ranking information. The results also suggest that an agent’s interpretation of what a safety evaluation tests can affect measured behavior. The sample is small, the production matches are approximate and provider-selected, and the observations have not been independently reproduced.
