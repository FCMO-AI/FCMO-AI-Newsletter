What changed

In an Oct 19 arXiv preprint, authors report that how tool results are delivered changed answer accuracy in PHREEQC agent tasks. Across 158 execution-verified multiple-choice items, table-of-contents retrieval rather than inline report delivery was associated with an 11.3-point drop for GPT-5.1; GPT-5.2’s reported 7.9-point drop was not significant after Holm correction. Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4 showed no detectable net effect. The authors traced some errors to agents answering from higher-precision output files instead of the simulator report, and report that naming the authoritative report improved results in intervention runs. These are author-reported results in one deterministic scientific simulator, not an independent reproduction or evidence of a general retrieval penalty.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated by a public artifact — arXiv records v1 of this paper as submitted 19 October 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report that table-of-contents retrieval of PHREEQC reports, instead of inline output, lowered accuracy by 11.3 percentage points for GPT-5.1 (95% item-bootstrap interval −18.0 to −4.9; Holm-corrected p=0.005) and by 7.9 points for GPT-5.2 (interval −14.9 to −0.9; Holm-corrected p=0.128, not significant after correction). They report no detectable net effect for Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6 or GPT-5.4.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the models produced the keyed simulator values at similar rates, but under retrieval sometimes answered from their own higher-precision output files instead of the simulator report and selected a neighboring multiple-choice option. They call this “evidence substitution.”
  • Claimed — In the paper’s intervention runs, an instruction to answer from the simulator report reduced report-skipping to under 2% and raised closure to 99%; inline delivery of outputs that fit had a similar effect, while a rounding instruction alone did not.
  • Documented limitation of the source — The paper studies one deterministic geochemistry simulator (PHREEQC) and 158 retained items from 19 setups. It reports execution-based verification of the keys and offline replay of logged simulator calls, but the paper does not establish transfer to other tools, domains, harnesses or model settings.

Strongest baseline

Within each model, table-of-contents retrieval is compared with inline simulator output; paired replay for GPT-5.2 holds the simulation result fixed and changes only delivery for the analyzed checkpoints.

Limitations

  • The reported study is a preprint and its experimental results have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The task regime is 158 execution-verified PHREEQC multiple-choice questions from one scientific simulator. The authors excluded defective items from a 200-item pool and report that conclusions are similar on a stricter 134-item subset.
  • The reported accuracy change differs by model. GPT-5.1’s result is significant after the paper’s Holm correction; GPT-5.2’s result does not meet that corrected threshold. Claude Opus 4.6, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4 show no detectable net change.
  • The paper uses closed models and API defaults for GPT; conditions differ in tool-description prompts, repeat counts are incomplete, and full-agent runs occurred at different times. The authors do not determine which model property drives the differences.
  • The findings do not establish that file retrieval generally reduces agent accuracy or that the observed mechanism transfers to other domains, tools or production harnesses.

What we still don't know

  • No independent group has reproduced the reported accuracy effects, evidence-substitution mechanism or interventions.
  • The evaluation uses one simulator, a narrow task format, closed API models and differing run/prompt conditions; transfer to other long-output tools and natural agent tasks is unknown.
  • For GPT-5.2, the reported retrieval effect is not significant after Holm correction across five models; some conditions have limited repeats, and models were served at different times.

Why it matters

If the reported mechanism generalizes, an agent can compute the right result yet answer from a less authoritative copy when long tool output is stored for later retrieval. That could affect choices about how harnesses expose logs, search results and simulator reports. The paper tests one simulator and shows different effects across models, so it does not justify a general rule against retrieval.
