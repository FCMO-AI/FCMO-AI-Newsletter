Paper reports attacker-controlled binary narratives shifting LLM malware-triage verdicts

The arXiv v1 paper studies whether static LLM malware triage can be misled by attacker-controlled narrative content in a binary’s extracted evidence. On a fixed PE set, Gemini 2.5 Pro labeled 35/50 samples malicious at baseline; the paper reports that 30/35 became benign under its primary ALIBI condition (85.7%). On 40 ELF samples, Gemini’s reported Strict ASR was 40.0%. GPT-5.5 Pro and Claude Opus 4.7 had zero reported benign flips on the locked PE subset but still showed non-benign severity downgrades. A separate prompt-hardening test reduced Gemini Strict ASR from 82.9% to 42.9%, leaving 15/35 benign outcomes; its undefended arm differs by one sample from the primary result. The authors argue for separating parser-verified facts from binary-supplied claims. These are fixed-sample, single-query paper results; no independent reproduction or complete provenance-defense evaluation was performed.

What actually changed

The arXiv v1 paper studies whether static LLM malware triage can be misled by attacker-controlled narrative content in a binary’s extracted evidence. On a fixed PE set, Gemini 2.5 Pro labeled 35/50 samples malicious at baseline; the paper reports that 30/35 became benign under its primary ALIBI condition (85.7%). On 40 ELF samples, Gemini’s reported Strict ASR was 40.0%. GPT-5.5 Pro and Claude Opus 4.7 had zero reported benign flips on the locked PE subset but still showed non-benign severity downgrades. A separate prompt-hardening test reduced Gemini Strict ASR from 82.9% to 42.9%, leaving 15/35 benign outcomes; its undefended arm differs by one sample from the primary result. The authors argue for separating parser-verified facts from binary-supplied claims. These are fixed-sample, single-query paper results; no independent reproduction or complete provenance-defense evaluation was performed.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors present ALIBI as a semantic reframing attack in static malware triage: a post-compilation, non-executed binary narrative is interpreted alongside suspicious static evidence, while the paper reports that executable behavior and imports were left unchanged. The review did not independently validate those preservation claims.
  • CLAIMED — On a frozen 50-sample PE set, 35 samples were malicious under Gemini 2.5 Pro at baseline. The paper reports that ALIBI changed 30/35 (85.7%) of those Gemini verdicts to benign in its primary comparison. On a 40-sample ELF set, it reports a 40.0% Strict ASR for Gemini. These are author-run, single-query static evaluations on fixed samples.
  • CLAIMED — On the same locked PE subset, the paper reports zero benign flips for GPT-5.5 Pro and Claude Opus 4.7, but non-benign downgrades (Soft ASR) of 71.4% and 42.9%, respectively. It also reports confidence reduction in some unchanged-verdict groups; confidence is model self-report, not an internal uncertainty measure.
  • CLAIMED — In a separate paired prompt-hardening evaluation on 35 malicious-baseline PE samples, the authors report Strict ASR falling from 82.9% (29/35) to 42.9% (15/35), while benign outcomes remain for 15/35 samples. The undefended result differs by one sample from the paper’s primary comparison (85.7%), as the authors note. The prompt reduces but does not eliminate the measured attack.
  • CLAIMED — The paper recommends that malware evidence builders distinguish parser-verified attributes from attacker-controlled strings and metadata; it proposes provenance checks as a stronger defense than prompt hardening alone. A complete provenance-checking pipeline was not evaluated.

Strongest baseline

Within the study, original unmodified binaries provide the baseline and a direct token-level override variant is compared on Gemini. The prompt-hardening study is paired on the same locked 35-sample PE subset, but its undefended Gemini arm differs by one result from the primary comparison.

The caveat that matters

  • Single arXiv v1 study with author-run, fixed-sample tests; no independent reproduction or sample-level audit was performed.
  • The main PE evaluation uses 50 MalwareBazaar samples, with 35 Gemini baseline-malicious samples as the denominator; the ELF set contains 40 samples. These do not establish prevalence across malware families or deployed systems.
  • Each sample received one model call; no multi-sampling, majority voting or adaptive query budget was tested.
  • The most resistant models had zero reported Strict ASR but still showed nonzero Soft ASR and, for some unchanged-verdict groups, self-reported confidence reductions. Self-reported confidence is not internal uncertainty.
  • The prompt-hardening evaluation reports a different undefended Gemini result (29/35; 82.9%) than the primary comparison (30/35; 85.7%); this one-sample discrepancy is noted but unexplained.
  • The verification-guided prompt reduced but did not remove benign flips, and the proposed provenance-aware evidence-builder defense was not implemented or tested as a complete system.
  • The study concerns static PE/ELF triage and does not evaluate dynamic execution, sandboxing, signature validation, analyst behavior or production outcomes.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction, sample-level output audit or artifact/code audit was conducted.
  • The evaluation uses fixed samples, one query per sample and model snapshots that may not represent current analyzer deployments; prevalence across malware families and production pipelines is unknown.
  • The primary Gemini result (30/35 flips; 85.7%) differs by one sample from the separate prompt-hardening evaluation’s undefended arm (29/35; 82.9%); the paper reports the discrepancy but does not resolve its cause. The proposed provenance-check defense was not implemented and evaluated end to end.

FCMO Lens

AI-assisted malware triage may influence which alerts analysts investigate first. If the reported behavior generalizes, strings and metadata supplied by a file under analysis can shift model judgments even when the suspicious code remains unchanged. The study points toward evidence pipelines that keep verified attributes separate from claims embedded in artifacts; the fixed sample and model conditions leave real-world prevalence unknown.
