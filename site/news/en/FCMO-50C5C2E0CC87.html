Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper describes D-Quant, which entropy-codes quantized KV values and uses a drift assignment to satisfy a fixed per-token storage budget while preserving regular layouts for parallel decoding.
  • Claimed — On Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at 2.26 bits per value, the authors report RULER average scores of 86.50 and 88.94, compared with BF16 scores of 87.31 and 89.69; at 128K context, D-Quant reports 70.92 and 74.65 versus strongest listed low-bit baselines of 57.14 and 66.15.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper evaluates two 8B models on RULER and English LongBench-E under its stated GPU/configuration setup; it does not establish production serving savings or results on other architectures.

Strongest baseline

At 128K on RULER, OScaR is the strongest listed quantized baseline: 57.14 for Qwen3-8B and 66.15 for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, compared with D-Quant 70.92 and 74.65. The full-precision BF16 reference scores 72.13 and 76.44.

Limitations

  • All benchmark and implementation results are author-reported; this activation did not inspect code or independently reproduce them.
  • Evaluation covers Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, NVIDIA H20 GPUs, RULER and English LongBench-E; transfer to other model architectures and serving stacks is unknown.
  • Qwen3-8B results at 64K and 128K use static YaRN beyond its native 40K context, which degrades absolute performance even though baselines share the setting.
  • LongBench-E differences among compressed methods are smaller than the RULER differences.
  • The paper’s storage result does not alone establish production throughput, concurrency, energy or monetary savings.

What we still don't know

  • The code, fused kernels, benchmark scripts and storage-accounting implementation were not inspected.
  • No independent reproduction of accuracy, memory use or throughput was performed.
  • The paper does not establish serving impact under production batch sizes, concurrency or different hardware.

Why it matters

KV-cache memory can limit the context length and concurrency available for serving large models. D-Quant reports a way to use entropy coding with regular fixed-stride access patterns, and near-BF16 RULER scores at about 2.26 bits per value on two 8B models. If reproduced in real serving stacks, this could improve the memory side of long-context inference, but the paper does not by itself establish production throughput, cost or transfer to other architectures.
