RFD4-Proteina couples biomolecular generation with latent reasoning and reports selected wet-lab results

RFD4-Proteina is an open biomolecular design model from the University of Washington Institute for Protein Design and NVIDIA collaborators. Its authors describe a shared physics-informed latent space with recurrent refinement and optional reward-guided search for generating and ranking molecular designs. The authors report selected wet-lab results, including 12 of 18 calcium-dependent calmodulin designs below 1 µM, 89 of 288 screened phosphotriesterase designs active, and four of 67 lenacapavir-step designs forming amide above background; the best reported design converted 79% of the (S)-amine and 8.8% of the (R)-amine. These are author-reported results from selected assays, not independent reproduction or evidence of therapeutic or manufacturing impact. NVIDIA separately reports faster generation and lower peak memory than RFD3 in a 200-residue benchmark. The full manuscript and supplement were not inspected in this review.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors describe RFD4-Proteina as a biomolecular design model using a shared, physics-informed latent space, recurrent refinement and optional reward-guided search to generate and rank structures across protein and other molecular design tasks.
  • Claimed — The authors report that 12 of 18 tested calcium-dependent calmodulin binder designs had apparent KD below 1 µM and showed no detectable binding without calcium. The project page notes that its 69 pM fit reached the off-rate fitting floor, so that value and associated ranking remain unresolved.
  • Claimed — For an eight-residue phosphotriesterase active site, the authors report 89 of 288 screened RFD4-Proteina designs active across 25 of 47 scaffold families. Of six characterized designs, the best reported kcat/KM was 3,600 ± 500 M⁻¹s⁻¹; these are source-reported assay results, not an independent reproduction.
  • Claimed — For an enzyme design targeting an amide-forming step in lenacapavir synthesis, the authors report that 4 of 67 designs formed product above background. Their best design converted 79.0% of the (S)-amine and 8.8% of the (R)-amine in the reported assay; no manufacturing-scale or cost impact is established.
  • Claimed — NVIDIA reports that, at 200 residues, RFD4-Proteina generation took 1.35 seconds and 1.36 GB peak reserved memory versus 5.12 seconds and 8.50 GB for RFD3 in its reported benchmark. This is a vendor comparison and does not establish independently reproduced end-to-end design quality or total campaign cost.
  • Not established — The accessible sources do not establish independent reproduction, generalization across laboratories and targets, therapeutic activity, clinical utility, or commercial manufacturing impact. The full manuscript and supplementary methods were not inspected in this review.

Strongest baseline

For throughput, NVIDIA compares RFD4-Proteina with RFD3 at 200 residues: 1.35 versus 5.12 seconds and 1.36 versus 8.50 GB peak reserved memory. This vendor-reported comparison does not independently establish design-quality or total campaign-cost advantage. For the lenacapavir reaction, the authors report four of 67 designs above background versus zero of 72 in an earlier RFD3 campaign; assay comparability and broader manufacturing implications remain to be reviewed.

Limitations

  • The experimental outcomes are reported by the model authors and collaborators; no independent assay audit or reproduction was performed.
  • The accessible project page presents selected results and some displayed assay data, but the full manuscript and supplement were not inspected.
  • The reported calmodulin value of 69 pM is at an off-rate fitting floor; the authors state its precise affinity and ranking remain unresolved.
  • The phosphotriesterase and lenacapavir results cover specific designs and assay conditions. They do not demonstrate therapeutic effect, clinical utility or manufacturing-scale economics.
  • NVIDIA's 200-residue speed and memory figures are vendor-reported benchmark results; they do not independently establish end-to-end campaign efficiency or design quality.

Why it matters

If independently reproduced and extended, a model that jointly generates biomolecular structures and uses search to refine designs could improve the range and throughput of computational protein and enzyme design. The accessible sources report promising experimental results, but the full protocol remains unreviewed and the results do not establish therapeutic efficacy, generality or manufacturing-scale economics.
