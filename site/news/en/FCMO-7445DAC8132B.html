PACMAN paper reports a real-time AI control framework deployed in DIII-D experiments

A November 2025 author preprint, later identified in a July 2026 journal record, describes PACMAN, a real-time software framework integrating plasma diagnostics, model/controller components and actuator commands in the DIII-D tokamak control system. What actually changed

A November 2025 author preprint, later identified in a July 2026 journal record, describes PACMAN, a real-time software framework integrating plasma diagnostics, model/controller components and actuator commands in the DIII-D tokamak control system. Its authors report five experimental applications, including reinforcement-learning control, event prediction and profile control. The manuscript gives device-specific architecture and timing details, but the deployment is CPU-based and limited to DIII-D; the paper frames extension to other devices as prospective. These are author-reported demonstrations, with no independent reproduction or matched comparative performance analysis in this review.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that PACMAN was deployed in the DIII-D real-time plasma-control system as an end-to-end framework linking diagnostic inputs, model and controller blocks, and actuator commands. The manuscript specifies the CPU-based deployment and describes five experimental applications in §§III.1–III.5; these are author-reported experiments and have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • CLAIMED — The manuscript reports multiple controller and prediction applications on DIII-D, including reinforcement-learning control, an ELM predictor, an Alfvén-eigenmode controller, model-predictive profile control and a tearing-mode predictor-controller. The evidence is specific to the described DIII-D shots and settings; the paper frames use on other devices as a possible extension, not demonstrated transfer.

Strongest baseline

The paper describes prior standalone ML controller demonstrations and positions PACMAN as shared integration infrastructure; this review did not compare outcomes against conventional controllers under matched settings.

The caveat that matters

  • The bounded technical evidence comes from the authors' manuscript; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The demonstrations concern DIII-D and CPU-based implementation. The manuscript describes extension to other hardware/devices as prospective.
  • The review did not quantify comparative performance, reliability against matched conventional baselines, or effects on fusion-energy output.
  • PPPL's direct announcement page was inaccessible during this review; the manuscript was inspected at arXiv and the July 2026 journal publication metadata was corroborated through an institutional release mirror and article record.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of a PACMAN experiment, matched quantitative comparison against a predeclared baseline, or implementation on another tokamak was assessed. The manuscript reports experiments on DIII-D and states that its CPU-based design principles may be extended to other hardware and devices.

FCMO Lens

An integrated real-time pipeline spanning diagnostics to actuator commands could lower the engineering burden of deploying different machine-learning and control approaches in fusion experiments. The evidence currently supports an author-reported CPU-based deployment and application set on one tokamak; it does not show better plasma performance than matched alternatives or transfer to a power-producing reactor.
