Samsone reports open 99M–356M audio models with Android CPU inference results

A paper accepted at Interspeech 2026 describes three open small audio language models with 99M, 134M and 356M parameters, and an Android application for on-device inference. On the MMAU benchmark, the authors report that the 99M and 134M variants exceed the 167M Mellow model on the average scores shown; they also report 39–125 generated tokens per second on a Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra CPU using 15 audio-query pairs. Training code, checkpoints, mobile weights and Android application code are listed in the project repository. These are author-reported results; this review did not run the app or independently reproduce the benchmark or timing.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv paper and Samsung Research repository are public; the repository README says the work was accepted at Interspeech 2026 and lists model checkpoints, mobile weights, training/evaluation/export code and an Android application. This review confirmed the listings, not execution of those artifacts.
  • Claimed — The paper reports Samsone-99M, Samsone-134M and Samsone-356M with 99M, 134M and 356M parameters. On MMAU, Table 2 reports average scores of 57.20/58.13 for 99M and 63.00/61.33 for 134M (test-mini/test), compared with 52.30/53.34 for the 167M Mellow model. These are author-reported benchmark results, not independently reproduced here.
  • Claimed — The authors report on-device CPU timings on a Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra using 15 audio-query pairs: generation rates are 125, 87 and 39 tokens per second for the 99M, 134M and 356M variants, respectively. The timing sample is small and no independent handset measurement was performed.
  • Claimed — The paper reports the models as trained on publicly available datasets and releases project artifacts, including Android mobile weights and an app; repository README inspection confirms these artifacts are listed. This does not establish reproducibility or successful operation on other phones.

Strongest baseline

For MMAU, the paper compares against Mellow (167M) and larger audio-language models; the 99M/134M variants exceed Mellow on the reported MMAU average scores. The repository does not provide an independently matched mobile timing baseline in the inspected README.

Limitations

  • MMAU and MMAU-Pro results and the handset timing are author-reported; no independent reproduction or benchmark audit was located.
  • The on-device timing table uses 15 audio-query pairs on one Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra CPU; the paper does not provide a broad handset distribution or independently measured energy use.
  • The paper notes that extensive audio question-answering fine-tuning reduces general-purpose language capabilities and that mobile GPU/NPU-specific optimization has not been implemented.
  • Model size alone is not a complete efficiency measure; the paper does not establish comparative wall energy, memory pressure or sustained thermals against matched mobile baselines.

What we still don't know

  • Reported MMAU/MMAU-Pro performance has not been independently reproduced, and benchmark comparisons depend on the authors’ scoring and evaluation configuration.
  • The on-device latency report covers 15 audio-query pairs on one Galaxy S25 Ultra CPU and does not establish energy use, sustained thermal behavior, or performance across phones.
  • The official repository lists checkpoints and an Android application, but this review did not download, build or execute them.

Why it matters

The paper combines open audio models below 400M parameters with a reported Android CPU demonstration, making on-device audio question answering a concrete option for privacy-sensitive or connectivity-limited settings. The performance comparison is benchmark-specific, and the latency evidence is a 15-pair author-run measurement on one handset; broader device performance and quality trade-offs remain unknown.
