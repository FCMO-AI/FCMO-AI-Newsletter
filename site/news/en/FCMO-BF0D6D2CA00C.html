Apple describes a five-model foundation-model family with prompt-routed NAND-resident weights on device

In a June 8, 2026 research announcement, Apple described its third-generation Foundation Models as a five-model family spanning on-device and Private Cloud Compute deployment. Apple says AFM 3 Core Advanced is a 20-billion-parameter sparse model that activates 1 to 4 billion parameters at a time; its full model weights reside in NAND, with a dense block selecting experts per prompt and periodically during generation. Apple attributes the design to instruction-following pruning research. The company also says AFM 3 Cloud Pro extends Private Cloud Compute to NVIDIA GPUs in Google Cloud with Google and NVIDIA. These are first-party architecture and deployment statements, not independent performance measurements or a public reproduction.

What actually changed

In a June 8, 2026 research announcement, Apple described its third-generation Foundation Models as a five-model family spanning on-device and Private Cloud Compute deployment. Apple says AFM 3 Core Advanced is a 20-billion-parameter sparse model that activates 1 to 4 billion parameters at a time; its full model weights reside in NAND, with a dense block selecting experts per prompt and periodically during generation. Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Apple published a June 8, 2026 overview describing a five-model third-generation Apple Foundation Model family spanning on-device and Private Cloud Compute deployment.
  • CLAIMED — Apple says AFM 3 Core Advanced is a natively multimodal 20B-parameter sparse model that activates 1 to 4 billion parameters depending on the request, stores full weights in NAND and selects experts at prompt level.
  • CLAIMED — Apple says AFM 3 Cloud Pro extends Private Cloud Compute to NVIDIA GPUs in Google Cloud through work with Google and NVIDIA, while maintaining Apple’s stated privacy guarantees. This review did not independently audit those guarantees.

Strongest baseline

Apple contrasts its design with conventional dense or sparsely activated models that require all weights in active DRAM and describes standard token-by-token MoE swapping as limited by NAND-to-DRAM bandwidth. The announcement does not provide matched baseline measurements.
