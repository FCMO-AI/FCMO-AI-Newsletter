Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv:2611.12675v1, submitted November 9, 2026, describing experiments on cross-model transfer of interpretability tools.
  • Claimed — Across 11 instruction-tuned open-weight models from five families and 110 ordered model pairs, the authors report transferring refusal steering, correctness scoring and a sentiment probe through maps fit on unlabeled prompt representations. For refusal steering, a map fit on ordinary questions recovered 54% of each target's native effect on average; adding 16 harmful and 16 harmless prompts per target yielded 102% average recovery, with random/control directions recovering at most 1%.
  • Claimed — The authors report that transfer depends on whether the map's training prompts cover the region read by the tool: task-matched unlabeled prompts improve transfer, while a map fit only on GSM8K transfers little to other tested tools and tasks. Their linear-map coverage account is derived under stated correspondence and noise assumptions.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that interpretability tools generally transfer across model families, internal mechanisms, closed frontier models, tasks or deployment settings, and the reported results have not been independently reproduced in this review.

Strongest baseline

Each transferred tool is compared with the target model's own tool and with random, orthogonal and mapped-random controls; for correctness, the paper also compares against zero-shot answer scoring.

Limitations

  • This is one author-reported preprint; independent reproduction was not located.
  • The experiments cover three interpretability tools and 11 instruction-tuned open-weight models from five families, not closed frontier models or deployed systems.
  • Transfer changes with prompt coverage; the ordinary-question map is weak for some correctness and sentiment settings, and narrow GSM8K mapping transfers little.
  • The 110 model-pair results are not 110 independent model replications; models recur across pairs, and the authors use model-level bootstrap intervals for this reason.
  • Main experiments use five seeds, but ablation and some mapping conditions use one seed; evaluation prompts remain fixed.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or public implementation repository was located in the inspected paper and arXiv record.
  • Only three tools and 11 selected open-weight models are tested; transfer to other interpretability mechanisms, closed frontier systems, additional model families and operational uses is unknown.
  • Some ablation and mapping conditions use one seed, and the main evaluation uses fixed evaluation sets despite redraws of defining prompts, map prompts and controls.

Why it matters

If the result holds beyond the tested tools and models, task-covered maps could make some interpretability tools reusable across models without target labels, reducing duplicated probe-building work. The paper also identifies a concrete failure mode: general-purpose map data can omit the prompt region a tool uses. The evidence is author-reported and bounded to three tools and 11 open-weight models; broader transfer is unestablished.
