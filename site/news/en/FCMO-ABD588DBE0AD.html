ReAgent combines static inspection of paper claims against repository implementations with dynamic execution evidence. On 96 auditable repositories from a screened set of 188 AutoSOTA agent-generated paper–repository pairs, the authors report 70.83% overall audit accuracy versus 35.42% for Codex using GPT-5.4 and manually established ground truth. Ninety-two repositories were excluded, mostly because datasets or checkpoints were unavailable. The result is author-reported and limited to one platform.

Evidence

  • Claimed — ReAgent combines static claim-to-code review with dynamic experiment execution and evidence collection to assess consistency between research documents and repositories.
  • Claimed — On 96 auditable AutoSOTA repositories, the paper reports 70.83% overall audit accuracy for ReAgent versus 35.42% for Codex against manually established ground truth.
  • Not established — Repository consistency does not establish scientific validity or novelty, and the benchmark does not establish generalization beyond AutoSOTA.

Strongest baseline

Codex using the same GPT-5.4 model and task inputs; the paper reports 35.42% overall audit accuracy versus ReAgent’s 70.83%.

Limitations

  • All results are reported by the authors; the research desk did not independently reproduce the benchmark.
  • The 96 audited repositories come from one platform; 92 of 188 screened pairs were excluded, mostly because assets were unavailable.
  • The audit evaluates paper–repository consistency, not the scientific validity or novelty of methods or conclusions.
  • Generalization to other agent platforms and human-authored research artifacts is unknown.

What we still don't know

  • The reported benchmark comparison has not been independently reproduced.
  • Generalization beyond the AutoSOTA repository collection is unknown.

Why it matters

As research agents generate papers, code and experiments together, an audit that checks both implementation fidelity and execution evidence could catch unsupported claims that either static review or successful reproduction alone misses. This benchmark concerns artifact consistency; it does not certify whether a method or conclusion is scientifically valid.
