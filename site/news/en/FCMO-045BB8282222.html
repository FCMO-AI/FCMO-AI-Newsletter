What actually changed

Argentina's Resolution 264/2026 subjects new or expanded electricity demands at or above 0.5% of reference wholesale-market demand to XDEM rules; the government equates the threshold to about 80 MW. Data-processing loads, including AI training and inference, must contract eligible new energy for at least 80% of monthly consumption and firm power for 115% of peak demand. They must also meet strict grid-interconnection ramp, variability and disturbance-response requirements. Spot shortfalls face surcharges and possible operating restrictions; realized project costs and capacity effects remain unknown.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The XDEM threshold covers new demand or expansions at or above 0.5% of average MEM demand; the government describes this as about 80 MW. The rule applies regardless of transmission or distribution connection.
  • DEMONSTRATED — XDEM-DATOS includes loads used wholly or partly for intensive data processing, including AI training and inference. Projects must file a five-year rolling supply plan and receive prior approval; the regime applies for 15 years after commercial authorization.
  • DEMONSTRATED — XDEM loads must contract eligible new supply for at least 80% of monthly energy use; XDEM-DATOS must contract firm power equal to at least 115% of peak demand.
  • DEMONSTRATED — For uncovered data-load energy and firm-power deficits, the resolution sets surcharges of 100% and 400% respectively above the applicable reference charges; firm-power shortfalls can trigger operating restrictions before affecting other MEM demand.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The technical annex sets interconnection conditions for XDEM loads: active-power ramps in either direction must stay within the lower of 30 MW/min or 10%/min; load variability must be controlled down to sub-second intervals; and installations generally must remain connected through voltage disturbances and recover 90% of active power within 1 second once voltage exceeds 0.9 pu, subject to study-based adjustment.

Strongest baseline

The rule expands the 2025 large-demand supply requirements to distribution-connected projects and demand expansions and specifies XDEM treatment.

The caveat that matters

  • The approximately 80 MW threshold is the government summary’s current equivalence; the legal threshold is calculated against average MEM demand using the preceding 12 complete calendar months.
  • The resolution creates obligations and penalties, not evidence yet of resulting AI capacity, project cost, reliability or consumer-price outcomes.
  • Firm-power coverage can be reduced exceptionally for new loads in structurally constrained exporting areas after a reasoned OED report.
  • Qualifying supply is generally incremental; existing gas generation can qualify only with additional firm gas-transport capacity that effectively increases verifiable MEM supply.

What remains unknown

  • Implementation outcomes remain unmeasured: approved XDEM projects, contracted eligible supply, connection delays, realized project costs and any operating restrictions.

FCMO Lens

Large AI campuses may need to finance or contract incremental supply and firm capacity before market entry, and engineer controllable grid interfaces for ramping and disturbances; high penalties make spot-supply reliance more expensive. Compliance costs and effects on investment, grid reliability, or prices remain unmeasured.
