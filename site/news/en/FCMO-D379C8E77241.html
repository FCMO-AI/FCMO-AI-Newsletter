R3Con reports large gains from task-focused context representation on two long-context benchmarks

A September 2026 preprint introduces R3Con, a harness that repeatedly extracts task-relevant context from large document collections, builds a flexible structure such as a table or graph, then uses a coding agent to reason over it. On CorpusQA and Loong, the authors report overall accuracy gains of 19.88 and 8.42 percentage points over the strongest evaluated baselines. They also report that 4B and 9B R3Con configurations exceeded all evaluated 35B baselines, and that a Qwen3.5-35B-A3B R3Con run exceeded a Claude Code/Sonnet 5 comparison at about 3.7 times lower reported cost. These are single-paper results on two benchmarks; both use LLM judging, and the cross-system cost comparison does not isolate model and harness effects.

What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces R3Con, a harness that repeatedly extracts task-relevant context from large document collections, builds a flexible structure such as a table or graph, then uses a coding agent to reason over it. On CorpusQA and Loong, the authors report overall accuracy gains of 19.88 and 8.42 percentage points over the strongest evaluated baselines. They also report that 4B and 9B R3Con configurations exceeded all evaluated 35B baselines, and that a Qwen3.5-35B-A3B R3Con run exceeded a Claude Code/Sonnet 5 comparison at about 3.7 times lower reported cost. These are single-paper results on two benchmarks; both use LLM judging, and the cross-system cost comparison does not isolate model and harness effects.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On CorpusQA and Loong, the authors report that R3Con improved overall accuracy by 19.88 and 8.42 percentage points, respectively, over the strongest evaluated baseline in their experiments.
  • Claimed — The authors report that R3Con with Qwen3.5-4B on Loong and Qwen3.5-9B on CorpusQA outperformed all evaluated 35B baselines; their Qwen3.5-35B-A3B configuration also outperformed a Claude Code comparison using Claude-Sonnet-5 at about 3.7 times lower reported cost.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The results are author-reported on two benchmarks with LLM-as-judge evaluation; they do not establish general superiority across corpora, tasks or deployment regimes, and the Claude comparison does not isolate model or harness effects.

Strongest baseline

The best reported baseline is StructRAG on CorpusQA (50.76% overall) and CodeAgent on Loong (29.01% overall); R3Con reports 70.64% and 37.43%, respectively. The paper also compares against Claude Code using Claude-Sonnet-5, but this is a cross-system comparison rather than a model-matched harness ablation.

Limitations

  • The evaluation covers two benchmarks and their selected tasks; transfer to other corpora, domains or operational workloads is unknown.
  • Both benchmarks use an LLM-as-judge metric; independent human or deterministic adjudication was not established in this review.
  • The Claude Code comparison changes the model and system together and therefore does not isolate the effect of R3Con or establish a general cost-performance advantage.
  • Reported cost uses listed API prices rather than independently measured end-to-end compute, latency, engineering effort or infrastructure cost.
  • No independent reproduction or full code/data audit was performed in this activation.

What we still don't know

  • The reported benchmark gains and smaller-model comparisons have not been independently reproduced.
  • The model-size and Claude Code comparisons do not isolate representation-harness effects under a common model, runtime and cost accounting.

Why it matters

If replicated on broader corpora, a task-specific representation-building stage could improve multi-hop evidence integration while lowering the model scale needed for some long-context tasks. The paper gives a concrete design and benchmark comparison for that possibility. Its results remain bounded to two evaluations, author-selected baselines, and LLM-as-judge scores; the cost comparison is not a matched harness ablation.
