PreDE reports a policy-specific gate for screening robot-policy quantization before closed-loop tests

Xu et al. introduce PreDE, which calibrates policy-specific thresholds from a small set of quantized configurations with known closed-loop outcomes, then uses action deviation on a fixed observation log to accept, reject or defer new candidates. In the paper, 21 of 28 held-out configurations across Cosmos Policy and UVA received decisions, all matching their observed labels; seven were deferred, including both acceptable and degraded outcomes. A separate 450-trial Franka Research 3 study reports significant degradation for configurations assigned to high-deviation groups before trials, while low-deviation results were not statistically significant. W4A4 reduced median action-query latency 1.37× and peak memory about 44% on the tested setup. These are author-reported results, conditional on each policy and evaluation setting.

What actually changed

Xu et al. introduce PreDE, which calibrates policy-specific thresholds from a small set of quantized configurations with known closed-loop outcomes, then uses action deviation on a fixed observation log to accept, reject or defer new candidates. In the paper, 21 of 28 held-out configurations across Cosmos Policy and UVA received decisions, all matching their observed labels; seven were deferred, including both acceptable and degraded outcomes. A separate 450-trial Franka Research 3 study reports significant degradation for configurations assigned to high-deviation groups before trials, while low-deviation results were not statistically significant. W4A4 reduced median action-query latency 1.37× and peak memory about 44% on the tested setup. These are author-reported results, conditional on each policy and evaluation setting.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that PreDE issued decisions for 21 of 28 held-out configurations across two policies (75% coverage), and all issued decisions matched the observed acceptable or degraded labels; seven candidates were deferred.
  • CLAIMED — In a separate 450-trial Franka Research 3 study, the authors report significant degradation for every tested high-deviation configuration selected before trials; low-deviation comparisons did not reach significance, which does not establish equivalence.
  • CLAIMED — On the reported RTX 5080 setup, W4A4 reduced median action-query latency by 1.37× and peak allocated memory by about 44% relative to bf16.
  • CLAIMED — PreDE’s thresholds are policy- and setting-specific, and its accept/reject interpretation is conditional on a within-setting ordering hypothesis; uncertain cases require closed-loop evidence.

Strongest baseline

For prediction, the paper compares PreDE with post hoc fixed deviation and bit-width rules at 100% coverage; those rules falsely rejected six and five acceptable held-out candidates, respectively. Quantization outcomes are compared with matched bf16 references and task-specific closed-loop success.

The caveat that matters

  • The empirical results are author-reported; no independent execution or reproduction was performed in this review.
  • The held-out prediction set covers only two calibrated policies and 28 configurations; all seven deferred cases needed closed-loop evidence to settle their outcomes.
  • The decision rule relies on a within-setting label-ordering hypothesis; policy, task, control protocol, observation log or labeling changes may require recalibration.
  • The real-robot test uses two fine-tuned Cosmos policies and small task-specific trial counts; its deviation groups do not establish population-wide safety or performance preservation.
  • The absence of statistical significance in low-deviation comparisons does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority.
  • The public repository was inspected but not run; independent validation of artifacts and reported measurements remains open.

What remains unknown

  • PreDE prediction results and repository code have not been independently executed or reproduced.
  • The real-robot low-deviation comparisons are too small to establish non-inferiority; PreDE’s threshold results are limited to two simulation-calibrated policies.

FCMO Lens

Robot-policy quantization can save memory and latency, but nominal bit width does not predict task loss consistently. A calibrated offline screen could help prioritize expensive closed-loop evaluations, provided its abstentions, policy-specific scope and finite-trial uncertainty remain visible.
