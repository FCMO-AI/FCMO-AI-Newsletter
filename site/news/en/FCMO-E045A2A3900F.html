What changed

The update also adds email forwarding and says Auto Review asks less often for requested actions; requested email and Slack messages send after Auto Review allows them, while routine and other-turn sends remain drafts. Evidence

  • Claimed — xAI’s October 23 Grok Bot v0.75.5 changelog says Bots can save logins, payment cards and contact information in a Vault and fill those details at sign-in or checkout; it also adds forwarding messages from a Bot’s own inbox.
  • Claimed — The same changelog says Auto Review asks less often for actions requested in the user’s own words. It says user-requested email and Slack messages send after Auto Review allows them, while sends on routine and other turns remain drafts; a denied approval is not retried until another user message.
  • Claimed — Current xAI product documentation says Ask-first rules take precedence over automatic allowances, describes Auto Review as model-based, and says payment confirmations require user handoff.
  • Not established — Independent testing of the release behavior, rollout by plan, adoption, unintended-send rate and security effectiveness was not established.

Limitations

  • All feature descriptions are vendor-reported; no independent implementation test or usage measurement was performed.
  • Account-plan rollout and broad availability are not established by the changelog.
  • xAI documentation describes Auto Review as model-based and recommends least privilege and explicit approval rules; this does not establish that those controls prevent all unintended side effects.

What we still don't know

  • No independent test measured Vault field access, Auto Review approval decisions, email/Slack sending behavior or checkout/payment confirmation boundaries.
  • The changelog and docs do not establish which plans received every v0.75.5 feature or how widely users adopted them.

Why it matters

Credential storage and outbound messaging expand the actions a persistent assistant can take, while the update also changes how often a person sees an approval prompt. This changes the operational boundary worth evaluating; the current public account is based on vendor release notes and documentation, not an independent behavior or safety test.
