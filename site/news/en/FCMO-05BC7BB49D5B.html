What changed

MiniMax announced M3.1 Flash on October 19 with vendor-reported agent, coding, computer-use and cybersecurity results. Its API is available through MiniMax API, M Plan and MiniMax Code; weights are forthcoming. MiniMax claims nearly 3x faster online inference and 70% lower agent-task usage cost than its predecessor, without a matched serving protocol. The current official pricing table lists M3.1 Flash at $0.15/$0.60 per million input/output tokens versus $0.30/$1.20 for M3 at contexts up to 512K, a nominal posted-rate comparison that does not establish realized task cost or historical price timing.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — MiniMax announced and made M3.1 Flash available through its API, M Plan and MiniMax Code on 2026-10-19; the company says model weights will be released later.
  • Claimed — MiniMax reports M3.1 Flash scores of 76.2 on BankerToolBench, 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, 61.7 on OSWorld V2, 29.6 on Agents’ Last Exam and 82% on CyberGym, and describes it as competitive with leading models.
  • Claimed — MiniMax says M3.1 Flash is nearly three times faster online and reduces agent-task usage costs by 70% versus its predecessor; it reports output speeds up to 200+ tokens per second and median speeds above 170 tokens per second.
  • Not established — The release does not independently establish comparative benchmark performance, the stated speed or cost improvements, or general performance across workloads.
  • Demonstrated — Artificial Analysis lists MiniMax-M3.1-Flash at 42 on its Intelligence Index, rank 4 of 173 models, and $0.22 per index task; it reports no speed measurement. Its model-specific profile could not be directly fetched in this review, so task-level scores, tested version and uncertainty remain uninspected.
  • Demonstrated — MiniMax’s current API pricing table lists M3 at $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens for contexts up to 512K, versus M3.1 Flash at $0.15 input and $0.60 output. This is a posted nominal token-price comparison; it does not establish historical timing or end-to-end task cost.

Strongest baseline

Artificial Analysis provides an independently generated composite score and rank against 172 other models; its model profile could not be directly opened for task-level review. MiniMax’s own comparisons against named models and its predecessor remain vendor-reported, without sufficient matched protocol, model-version, hardware, tool, prompt or cost-accounting detail to validate them. The current official pricing table gives a nominal M3 list-price comparator for contexts up to 512K, but the time at which those M3 prices took effect and end-to-end task economics are unknown.

Limitations

  • All benchmark values, rankings, latency figures and predecessor-relative cost claims are from MiniMax; no independent evaluation was inspected.
  • The launch post does not provide benchmark protocols, matched prompts/model versions, tool settings, sample counts or uncertainty for the reported comparisons.
  • The stated lower token price does not establish end-to-end task cost; task success, retries, context, cached-token mix and tool costs affect total cost.
  • Model weights and detailed technical documentation were not available in the inspected launch post.
  • Artificial Analysis independently lists a composite Intelligence Index result, but the model-specific profile was inaccessible beyond its search-indexed summary; task-level methods/results and uncertainty for this model remain uninspected.
  • The current platform table lists M3 at twice M3.1 Flash’s nominal input/output token rates for contexts up to 512K; this is not evidence that the M3 price change is historical, that cached rates match, or that M3.1 Flash halves successful task cost.

What we still don't know

  • MiniMax does not provide enough serving protocol detail in the launch post to reproduce the nearly 3x speed and 70% task-cost comparisons.

  • The independent Artificial Analysis profile was available only through search indexing because direct retrieval exceeded the page-size limit; task-level scores, tested model/API version and uncertainty were not inspected.

Why it matters

The release adds a hosted model positioned for tool-using work, with explicit prices and claims spanning coding, computer use and cybersecurity. An independent composite index now supplies an initial cross-model comparison, but its model-specific page was not fully accessible and it does not establish MiniMax’s named benchmark or latency claims. If the reported task performance and serving efficiency hold under matched tests, the price and latency profile could change the economics of multi-step agent workloads.
