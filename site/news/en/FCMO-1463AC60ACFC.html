Limitations

  • The full-kernel prototype targeted selected audit regions, which OpenAI says covered about 7% of the kernel codebase.
  • The report says human researchers reviewed and reported outputs; it does not establish that every generated patch was accepted or merged.
  • A single provider-reported run does not estimate general vulnerability-discovery rates or attack prevalence.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the counts, exploitability assessments, patch correctness or reported cost was reviewed.
  • The account reports 1,169 working LPE exploits and 948 distinct patches but does not provide a per-finding public mapping in the inspected page.
  • Results and cost are from one provider-reported workflow on selected targets and do not establish performance or economics across kernels, models or threat models.

Why it matters

If independently reproduced, this pipeline could expand the volume of kernel security findings defenders can validate and remediate. The report also shows that candidate triage, dynamic validation, exploitation assessment and patch review remain separate costly stages. Its measured scale and cost are specific to one provider-reported run, so it does not establish a general discovery rate or cost advantage.
