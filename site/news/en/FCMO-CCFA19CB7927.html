What changed

The arXiv preprint introduces RECAP, which gives GRPO training step-level credit using LLM-annotated semantic dependencies plus a gold-answer likelihood signal. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the authors report 2.0–3.7 percentage-point pass@1 gains and 8–31% fewer reasoning tokens than GRPO across GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 2024 and AIME 2025. Results on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B are mixed, so the paper does not establish a uniform cross-model improvement. These are author-reported experiments, not independently reproduced; the tested regime is limited to two 7B math models and four benchmarks.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors introduce RECAP, a GRPO-based reasoning-training method that combines backward-propagated responsibility on LLM-annotated semantic dependency graphs with step efficacy measured by changes in gold-answer log-likelihood. The method does not require a separately trained process reward model or preconstructed concise traces.
  • Claimed — On Qwen2.5-Math-7B across GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 2024 and AIME 2025, the authors report RECAP improves pass@1 by 2.0–3.7 percentage points and reduces reasoning tokens by 8–31% relative to GRPO; the paper reports eight-run variability for its main table.
  • Claimed — The paper also evaluates DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. RECAP uses fewer tokens than GRPO across the four reported benchmarks, but accuracy is mixed and stronger baselines outperform it on several cells; this does not establish a uniform gain across models.
  • Claimed — The authors’ trace analysis attributes token savings primarily to fewer reasoning operations and less dead-end reasoning rather than shorter expression; their dependency-edge labels and analysis use model-assisted annotation.

Strongest baseline

GRPO is the direct baseline used for the reported 2.0–3.7 point accuracy and 8–31% token comparison on Qwen2.5-Math-7B. Other methods sometimes score higher, and results on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B are mixed.

Limitations

  • The source is an author-reported arXiv preprint; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The clearest accuracy-and-token result is on Qwen2.5-Math-7B and four math benchmarks; DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B results are mixed.
  • The method depends on LLM-annotated semantic dependencies and answer likelihood, adding annotation and model-scoring cost not summarized here as end-to-end deployment economics.
  • The reported reduction is in reasoning tokens; it does not by itself establish lower wall-clock latency, energy use or cost per solved task.
  • No code artifact or independent baseline audit was verified in this activation.
  • On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, RECAP is not uniformly more accurate than GRPO or the strongest compared methods; the paper’s claimed gains are model- and benchmark-dependent.

What we still don't know

  • No publicly linked executable implementation or data artifact was verified during this review, and the research desk did not reproduce the training or evaluation results.
  • Evaluation is confined to two 7B math-oriented models and four mathematics benchmarks; effectiveness on general reasoning, coding, multilingual or frontier-scale models is unknown.
  • Semantic dependency labels rely on LLM-assisted annotation; annotation error and sensitivity to annotator model may affect the credit signal.

Why it matters

Reasoning tokens can dominate inference budgets for long mathematical traces. If RECAP’s reported trade-off reproduces and transfers, it could change post-training choices by rewarding steps according to both their downstream use and their progress toward a correct answer. The current paper supports a bounded research result, not a general claim that dependency-aware credit improves reasoning across models or tasks.
