A cryptographic construction illustrates a limit of elicitation-based model capability audits

The paper constructs encrypted computations that can be embedded in model weights and conditionally hidden from polynomial-time elicitation under stated cryptographic assumptions. Its PyTorch proof of concept tests a small chemistry classifier: authors report resistance to supervised fine-tuning and latent adversarial training, while 8-bit gradient-based trigger search exhaustively enumerated 256 candidates and matched random search; the evaluated 16-bit query budget remained near chance. Correct-trigger chemistry accuracy was 73.5%. The authors report individual compiled GLU units in Gemma 3 270M and a separate small experiment using Gemma 4 E4B architecture, a 64-bit trigger and 12-round Keccak-p[1600]; they do not report a full evaluation in an actual pretrained checkpoint. The v2 arXiv record states that the paper was accepted at NeurIPS 2026. They also report raw compiled weights are anomalous, while obfuscation leaves possible within-layer correlation signals. This is a conditional construction and limited experiment, not evidence of hidden capabilities in deployed frontier models.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report a cryptographically locked chemistry classifier resisted supervised fine-tuning, latent adversarial training and tested trigger search under the reported settings; their 8-bit trigger search exhaustively enumerated all 256 candidates and matched random search.
  • Not established — The paper reports that individual compiled GLU units execute within Gemma 3 270M and adds a small experiment using a model with Gemma 4 E4B architecture, 64-bit triggers and a 12-round Keccak-p[1600] locker, reporting unelicitability results on small experiments. The full construction has not been evaluated in an actual pretrained checkpoint; independent reproduction and deployment impact are not established.

Strongest baseline

A separately trained password-locked classifier performs the same chemistry task but has a different correct-trigger accuracy (92.7% vs 73.5%). Random-network locker control shows no observed SFT/LAT benefit. The 8-bit trigger search enumerates all candidates, so it is not evidence that gradient search beats random search.

Limitations

  • The guarantee is conditional on stated cryptographic assumptions, compiler correctness and the specified threat model; the paper does not claim ordinary training creates these compiled structures.
  • The chemistry demonstration is a small classifier, not a frontier-scale language model or deployed system. The authors report individual compiled GLU units in Gemma 3 270M and a small experiment using Gemma 4 E4B architecture with a 64-bit trigger and 12-round Keccak-p[1600]. The paper does not report a full evaluation integrated into an actual pretrained checkpoint.
  • For chemistry, the encrypted classifier reached 73.5% with the correct trigger; 8-bit GCG search enumerated all 256 candidates and matched query-matched random search, while the evaluated 16-bit query budget remained near chance. This is not evidence against unconstrained attacks.
  • The paper reports raw compiled weights are statistically anomalous; its weight obfuscation only approaches selected Gaussian-initialization statistics and unusual within-layer correlations remain a possible detection signal.
  • the research desk has not audited the proof or independently reproduced the experiments.

What we still don't know

  • The cryptographic proof and experiments have not been independently audited or reproduced. The v2 paper reports small experiments using a Gemma 4 E4B architecture, but no full construction evaluation in an actual pretrained checkpoint.

Why it matters

The construction identifies a conditional limit for audits that rely on eliciting unwanted behavior, including white-box access, when an adversary can deliberately engineer model weights. It is a security result under cryptographic assumptions and a small proof-of-concept task, not evidence that deployed frontier models currently contain hidden dangerous capabilities.
