MirrorMoE reports improved pretraining for shared-expert MoEs by adapting the routing space

A paper introduces MirrorMoE, a pretraining router for mixture-of-experts models that combines sign-mirrored logits for expert selection with the standard routing probabilities for expert aggregation, then anneals the mirrored component to zero. The authors report that shared experts suppress routing-induced feature variance, while fine-grained experts increase the number of routes that must be explored; in four shared-expert models from 100M to 2.4B total parameters, MirrorMoE lowers test perplexity and improves the average on six commonsense tasks. At 2.4B, the reported changes are 0.67 lower perplexity and +1.64 percentage points in the six-task average. The work uses DCLM data and H100 GPUs; the released recipe identifies one DCLM shard and 90/5/5 train/validation/test split. These are author-reported results at modest scale, not independently reproduced or evidence of frontier-scale gains.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper reports that shared experts reduce routing-induced feature variance by about an order of magnitude in its analyzed model, while fine-grained expert segmentation increases the route combinations that pretraining must explore.
  • Claimed — Across four shared-expert MoEs with 100M–2.4B total parameters, the authors report lower test perplexity with MirrorMoE; for the 2.4B model, test perplexity falls from 21.17 to 20.50 and the average over six commonsense tasks rises from 45.23 to 46.87 (+1.64 points).
  • Demonstrated — The released author repository includes MirrorMoE and standard-routing baseline scripts for four model sizes, and documents an environment based on Megatron-LM, PyTorch 2.6.0 and CUDA 12.4; code availability is not a reproduced result.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares against standard shared-expert MoE routing and fixed global linear/cosine schedules for active experts. It reports comparable total training tokens; some no-shared-expert configurations use different GPU counts due to expert-parallelism constraints.

Limitations

  • All performance numbers are paper-reported and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The models range from 100M to 2.4B total parameters; the work does not establish performance at frontier scale.
  • The author repository documents one DCLM shard and a 90/5/5 data split. Results may depend on this data slice and training recipe.
  • Downstream evaluation averages six commonsense tasks; broader capability and instruction-following effects are unknown.
  • The paper does not report an explicit main-comparison seed count or uncertainty intervals for the downstream averages in the inspected version.
  • Iteration time is similar to or slightly lower in the reported runs, but no full training-cost, energy, inference-latency or serving analysis is given.
  • The repository provides scripts and code, but no code was executed and no released checkpoint was independently inspected.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or multi-seed uncertainty analysis was conducted; the inspected paper does not report a seed count for the main comparisons.
  • Experiments cover 100M–2.4B total parameters, H100 training and one DCLM shard; performance and stability at frontier-scale models, other data mixtures, hardware and longer budgets remain unknown.
  • Downstream evaluation is an average over six commonsense tasks; broader capabilities, instruction tuning, inference efficiency, routing communication costs and production serving were not established.

Why it matters

If the reported effects persist at larger scales and across training setups, routing that adapts how many selected experts materially contribute could help shared-expert MoEs realize the extra capacity implied by fine-grained routing without raising the nominal active-expert count. This could affect MoE architecture and pretraining choices. Current evidence is limited to one paper’s 100M–2.4B models, one released data recipe and a six-task downstream average.
