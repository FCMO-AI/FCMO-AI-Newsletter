BEAKER introduces an expert-curated benchmark for embodied multimodal models in chemical laboratories

A 2026-11-04 arXiv paper introduces BEAKER, an offline benchmark of 1,000 expert-curated question-answer samples across 11 image and video task types for multimodal models considered as embodied systems in self-driving chemical laboratories. The authors report evaluation of 28 models and find persistent weaknesses in fine-grained action grounding, laboratory apparatus and state recognition, safety norms, and ordering steps in mid-length procedures. These results are author-reported; the benchmark does not test closed-loop device interaction.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv paper published on 2026-11-04 introduces BEAKER, an offline benchmark with 1,000 QA samples across 11 image/video task types for embodied multimodal systems in chemical-laboratory scenarios.
  • Claimed — The authors report evaluation of 28 multimodal models and persistent difficulty with precise action grounding, apparatus/state recognition, safety norms, and mid-length procedure ordering. The results are reported by the authors and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish closed-loop laboratory operation, real-device execution success, or a correlation between BEAKER scores and robot execution outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares 28 models, with task-format results showing coordinate QA lower than MCQ for five representative models (reported coordinate/MCQ ratios at or below 0.7). These comparisons are within the paper’s benchmark and do not establish real-device transfer.

Limitations

  • The findings are reported by the paper authors and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The benchmark is offline and single-turn; the paper explicitly reports no relation between its scores and real-robot execution success.
  • The 1,000 questions cannot cover all chemical procedures, and source viewpoints may not match deployed robots.
  • Potential pretraining exposure to source images/videos cannot be ruled out; the paper says benchmark questions and labels are newly authored.
  • External image/video source terms vary; this development makes no claim that all assets are freely redistributable.

What we still don't know

  • The reported evaluation results and benchmark annotations have not been independently reproduced or audited here.
  • The offline, single-turn benchmark has no reported correlation with closed-loop real-device execution success.
  • The paper draws from public media sources with differing usage terms, including academic-only and JoVE terms; benchmark redistribution/access terms were not established in this review.

Why it matters

The benchmark targets a practical gap between describing laboratory scenes and making precise, safety-constrained action judgments. It can help structure evaluation of multimodal systems for automated experimentation, while its offline single-turn scores should not be treated as evidence of real laboratory execution capability.
