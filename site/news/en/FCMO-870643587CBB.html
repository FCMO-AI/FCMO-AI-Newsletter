Japan releases a terms-gated 5,025-hour HSR mobile-manipulation dataset

What changed

Japan’s GENIAC program and the AI Robot Association released the AIRoA MoMa 5k real-robot mobile-manipulation dataset with a continued-pretraining model and training code. The dataset card reports 1,184,259 successful episodes and 5,025.1 hours from 44 HSR robots at five sites, but access is terms-gated, limited to research and development, train-only and success-only. The program page separately reports a 3-point task-success gain without a task denominator or matched evaluation details.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The AIRoA MoMa 5k dataset card describes a public, terms-gated artifact containing 1,184,259 successful primitive-action episodes, 5,025.1 recorded hours and 180,905,084 frames from 44 physical HSR robots across five sites. It is train-only and success-only; the card reports approximately 4.0 TiB and limits use to research and development, with no redistribution.
  • Claimed — The METI/NEDO program-results page attributes to AIRoA a larger campaign with about 80,000 hours collected and about 18,000 hours cleaned, and reports a π0.5-based continued-pretraining model with a 3-point task-success advantage over an existing open-weight fine-tune. The page does not specify the task denominator, uncertainty interval, matched baseline protocol or evaluation cost.
  • Not established — The release does not establish general robot policy performance: it contains only successful episodes, has no evaluation split or standardized benchmark, covers one robot embodiment and has substantial site/task imbalance.

Strongest baseline

The METI/NEDO page says the π0.5-based continued-pretraining model exceeded an existing open-weight fine-tuned model by 3 task-success points. It does not identify the evaluation task count, uncertainty or full matched protocol in the inspected summary.

Limitations

  • The government and dataset-card measurements are first-party records; the research desk did not download or independently audit the full dataset.
  • The public artifact has 1,184,259 successful episodes and 5,025.1 recorded hours, but includes no failed/suboptimal examples and only a training split.
  • The dataset covers 44 Toyota HSR units across five sites; the card reports pronounced site imbalance and warns that counts do not measure task difficulty or policy performance.
  • The terms limit use to research and development and prohibit third-party redistribution; public accessibility does not imply unrestricted or commercial availability.
  • The program page reports a 3-point task-success gain but lacks a task denominator, confidence interval, matched evaluation protocol, full cost and independent reproduction.
  • No generalization beyond the HSR embodiment, industrial adoption, deployment readiness or broad VLA capability gain is established.

What we still don't know

  • The dataset is success-only and train-only; it provides no standardized held-out split or benchmark, limiting measurement of generalization and failure recovery.
  • The reported 3-point model-success advantage lacks a specified denominator, confidence interval, matched baseline protocol and independent reproduction in the inspected government summary.
  • The terms restrict dataset/model use to research-and-development purposes and prohibit redistribution; industrial downstream access and adoption are not established.

Why it matters

A terms-gated release of more than 5,000 hours of real mobile-manipulation data may lower data-collection barriers for research on HSR-scale vision-language-action models. Its success-only training data, single embodiment, site imbalance and research-only terms constrain evaluation and reuse; the separate model-performance claim is not independently established.
