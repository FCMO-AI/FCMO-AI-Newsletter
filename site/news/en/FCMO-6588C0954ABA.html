What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces JustMem, a long-term conversational-memory system that adapts retrieval breadth and source-reading fidelity to each query. In author-run tests using GPT-4.1-mini across LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S, it reports the best mean accuracy among the compared memory systems while using fewer generative-model tokens. Results are paper-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Not established — The authors published the JustMem preprint on arXiv on 2026-09-17.
  • Claimed — On LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S, the authors report that JustMem had the highest mean accuracy and retrieval recall among the compared memory systems, with 79.61% accuracy on LoCoMo and 83.40% on LongMemEval-S.
  • Claimed — Against LightMem, the strongest comparison by mean accuracy on LoCoMo, the authors report 50.7% lower memory-construction tokens and 67.2% lower query-time tokens on LoCoMo, and 64.9% and 55.7% reductions respectively on LongMemEval-S.
  • Claimed — On the 401-question cognitive split of LoCoMo-Plus, the authors report 62.59% accuracy for JustMem versus 31.67% for MemOS, the strongest baseline in that comparison.
  • Demonstrated — The paper states that evaluation is limited to established long-term conversational memory benchmarks and a specific memory/retrieval configuration; its efficiency analysis primarily counts generative-model tokens and omits other system-level computation.

Strongest baseline

LightMem is the strongest compared memory system by mean accuracy on LoCoMo; JustMem's paired comparison there has p=0.532. For LongMemEval-S, the paper reports 76.44% LightMem accuracy versus 83.40% JustMem. On LoCoMo-Plus, MemOS is the strongest baseline at 31.67% versus 62.59% for JustMem.

Limitations

  • All quantitative results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The main comparisons use selected long-term conversational-memory benchmarks and a specific configuration; generality to other memory tasks and model stacks is unknown.
  • On LoCoMo, the reported paired comparison against LightMem is not statistically significant (p=0.532).
  • Token accounting excludes embeddings, reranking and other system-level computation, so lower generative-token use does not establish lower total latency, energy or cost.
  • The authors identify privacy, retention, access-control and sensitive-information leakage risks from stored memories and provenance-based source recovery.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the benchmark results or reported token costs was identified.
  • No public implementation was located during this review, preventing direct inspection of retrieval, source-recovery and planner behavior.
  • The principal efficiency comparison reports generative tokens but not total system-level compute, latency, energy or dollar cost.

Why it matters

If the reported results generalize, separating broad evidence discovery from selective source replay could improve conversational recall while reducing generative-model token use. The result is meaningful for agent-memory design, but evidence comes from a bounded benchmark setup and does not measure total system cost.
