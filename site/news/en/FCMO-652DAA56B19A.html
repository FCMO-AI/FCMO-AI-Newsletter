A paper argues that latent monitors must separate prompt-injection exposure from unsafe-action commitment

A paper reports that latent monitors trained on a mixed unsafe-versus-rest contrast can flag prompt-injection exposure without reliably predicting which exposed agent will take an unsafe tool action. In a generated-action AgentDojo grid, Qwen's naive contrast gives pre-action AUROC 0.556 for NLA text and 0.672 for an activation probe; training on exposed cases only raises the activation probe to 0.853, close to a matched visible-text TF-IDF baseline at 0.855. The paper reports related commitment-contrast results on Mistral-7B and OLMo-2-7B, but all three model families use AgentDojo and the generated-action study is not a live rollout.

What changed

A paper reports that latent monitors trained on a mixed unsafe-versus-rest contrast can flag prompt-injection exposure without reliably predicting which exposed agent will take an unsafe tool action. In a generated-action AgentDojo grid, Qwen's naive contrast gives pre-action AUROC 0.556 for NLA text and 0.672 for an activation probe; training on exposed cases only raises the activation probe to 0.853, close to a matched visible-text TF-IDF baseline at 0.855. The paper reports related commitment-contrast results on Mistral-7B and OLMo-2-7B, but all three model families use AgentDojo and the generated-action study is not a live rollout.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — arXiv records version 1 of the paper as submitted on 12 October 2026 and identifies it as accepted as a poster at the 2026 Agents in the Wild workshop.
  • Claimed — On a 280-case AgentDojo workspace grid generated by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the paper reports that naive unsafe-versus-rest training predicts pre-action unsafe-tool commitment weakly (activation-probe AUROC 0.672; NLA-text AUROC 0.556), while a commitment-specific contrast raises activation-probe AUROC to 0.853. The trained visible-text TF-IDF baseline reaches 0.855 on the same in-distribution task split.
  • Claimed — The author reports commitment-contrast activation-probe AUROC of 0.896 for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and 0.912 for OLMo-2-7B-Instruct on AgentDojo-derived grids, with text baselines at 0.945 and 0.921 respectively. These remain single-paper results on the same benchmark family.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish live-rollout prevention, robustness to adaptive attackers, transferable production thresholds, acceptable clean-traffic false-alarm costs, or independent reproduction of the reported monitor results.

Strongest baseline

On Qwen's in-distribution commitment task, a matched visible-text TF-IDF logistic baseline reaches AUROC 0.855 versus 0.853 for the logistic activation probe. Under user-task-grouped folds, the corresponding means are 0.780 and 0.795. Held-out transfer results are based on only 33 and 15 positive cases and do not establish stable cross-population ranking.

Limitations

  • The generated-action experiment is explicitly not a live rollout; labels are based on the model's first generated tool call and inferred unsafe-tool sets.
  • All three model families use AgentDojo-derived tasks, so cross-model results do not establish benchmark-independent transfer.
  • Held-out template and suite transfer include only 33 and 15 unsafe positives, respectively; the paper calls these results suggestive.
  • The strongest in-distribution visible-text TF-IDF baseline matches the logistic activation probe, and the paper reports weak transfer for some probes.
  • The paper reports that monitors trained on the mixed contrast flag all attacked-but-safe cases and, at the selected threshold, 18 of 40 clean cases for the Qwen activation probe.
  • Adaptive attackers, deployment thresholds, production API models, and clean-traffic false-alarm costs are not evaluated.
  • No independent reproduction, artifact audit, or code execution was performed in this activation.
  • A cited prior paper, arXiv:2606.30449v1, reports scoped negative results across Qwen, Llama and Gemma probe families. Its tasks, models and target labels differ from this paper’s AgentDojo exposure-conditioned contrast; the comparison does not validate or refute the proposed mechanism directly.

What we still don't know

  • The paper's reported contrast effect and AUROCs have not been independently reproduced; public artifacts are linked but not audited here.
  • No live environment rollouts, adaptive attacks, frontier API models, calibrated deployment thresholds, or clean-traffic false-alarm costs were measured.
  • All three model families are evaluated on AgentDojo-derived grids; external benchmark transfer is unknown.

Why it matters

If the contrast effect replicates, a detector validated only on attacked-versus-clean examples may learn to recognize exposure rather than predict unsafe actions. Double-stratifying evaluation and training populations can distinguish those signals and limit misleading monitor claims; the paper does not establish an effective production defense.
