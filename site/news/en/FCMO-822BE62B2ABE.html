A preprint reports reduced image-swap sensitivity in report-conditioned medical vision-language models

The preprint introduces ModaLens, a paired image-swap audit that fixes the question and radiology report while replacing the image. On 3,199 MIMIC-CXR cases from 293 patients, the authors report a 16.7-point higher answer-flip rate without the report than with it (20.94% versus 4.26%) under an explicit answer instruction, across 44,786 trials. Similar direction is reported in three additional model lineages. This measures sensitivity to an image substitution, not whether the answer is visually correct: labels are derived from reports, effects vary across findings, and two findings reverse the pooled direction.

What actually changed

The preprint introduces ModaLens, a paired image-swap audit that fixes the question and radiology report while replacing the image. On 3,199 MIMIC-CXR cases from 293 patients, the authors report a 16.7-point higher answer-flip rate without the report than with it (20.94% versus 4.26%) under an explicit answer instruction, across 44,786 trials. Similar direction is reported in three additional model lineages. This measures sensitivity to an image substitution, not whether the answer is visually correct: labels are derived from reports, effects vary across findings, and two findings reverse the pooled direction.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — On 3,199 paired MIMIC-CXR cases from 293 patients, the authors report that the generated answer changed on 20.94% of image substitutions without the report and 4.26% with it under an explicit answer instruction, a paired difference of 16.7 points [15.6, 17.7] across 44,786 trials.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report the same direction in Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.5-27B and LLaVA-NeXT on Mistral-7B under their evaluated protocol; the additional model lineages do not establish clinical generality.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper states that the audit measures sensitivity to an image substitution rather than grounding of the queried finding, and that its labels are derived from radiology reports rather than visual ground truth.

Strongest baseline

Within-case paired conditions with the same question and image substitution, comparing report present versus absent. The estimate is protocol-specific and does not use visual ground-truth labels.

The caveat that matters

  • Author-reported preprint results; no independent reproduction was conducted in this review.
  • The primary cohort is retrospective MIMIC-CXR, using report text and report-derived CheXpert labels; these are not independent visual ground truth.
  • The image intervention substitutes a whole image; it measures sensitivity to the change, not grounding of the queried finding. Image view and acquisition time are not matched.
  • The pooled effect is not uniform: the paper reports lower image-swap flip rates without the report for atelectasis and support devices, and the result depends on readout and prompt protocol.
  • The study does not establish diagnostic accuracy, patient outcomes, clinical safety or behavior in deployed clinical workflows.
  • Two of 14 findings, atelectasis and support devices, show lower image-swap flip rates without the report than with it; the pooled increase is not uniform.
  • The study reports substantial modality and prompt dependence, including near-constant image-only answers for some findings and readout-dependent absolute rates.
  • Exploratory layerwise projection analyses fail calibration below layer 46 and the paper does not treat them as mechanistic evidence; the attention intervention is limited to a 400-case subset.

What remains unknown

  • The linked code and run records were not inspected or executed in this review; no independent replication was performed.
  • The primary label source is radiology reports, so the audit does not determine whether outputs are visually grounded or correct.

FCMO Lens

The study offers a paired audit for whether adding report text changes how much a medical VLM's answer responds to image substitution. If independently confirmed with visual ground truth and varied clinical tasks, this can improve evaluation of modality use. The result does not establish diagnostic accuracy, clinical safety or that a model ignores images in deployment.
