What actually changed

A September 15 preprint models how multi-agent decomposition affects the findings that survive to a root agent. It derives a yield relationship under a stated retention model and reports measurements from 600 production deep-research traces and 16,082 tool-result handoffs. The empirical estimates are author-reported and tied to one research scaffold.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors derive that, under their retention model r(b)=Cb^-δ, a depth-k tree over N findings yields C^k N^(1-δ), and report that flat organization is optimal for yield under the model.
  • CLAIMED — Across 600 production deep-research traces, the authors report δ=0.34 (95% CI 0.30–0.38); across 16,082 tool-result handoffs, they report C=0.571 (95% CI 0.527–0.615). They report that each additional tier costs roughly 45% of yield in that measured setting.

Strongest baseline

Flat processing is the comparison for yield under the stated retention model; empirical C is measured at tool-result handoffs with explicit item boundaries.

The caveat that matters

  • All empirical findings are reported by the paper authors and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The main trace corpus uses one research-agent scaffold; transfer to other systems or tasks is not established.
  • The analytical conclusion depends on the paper’s retention model and assumptions; it does not establish that flat systems are generally superior on total task quality, cost or latency.

What remains unknown

  • No independent execution of the released scripts/corpora or replication of the reported constants was performed.
  • The empirical constants come from one research-agent scaffold; stability across other agents, task classes and tool boundaries is unmeasured.

FCMO Lens

If the measured loss generalizes, adding agent tiers can reduce the information that reaches a final decision maker, even when parallel agents surface more sources. The paper identifies a tradeoff between smaller working contexts and end-to-end retention that system designers can measure.
