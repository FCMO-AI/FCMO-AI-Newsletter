A paper models how distractor growth can weaken evidence retrieval in long-context models

The authors formalize “context poisoning” as competition between a decisive evidence score and the maximum score among distractors. Under a softmax retrieval abstraction, they derive a finite-sample bound in which the evidence margin needed to hold accuracy above base rate grows as Ω(√log N), where N is the effective distractor count. Controlled experiments report decreasing retrieval accuracy as context grows with hard negatives; same-format distractors produce the largest drop among the tested constructions, while retrieval gating helps only when evidence recall is preserved.

  • Not established — The paper’s author-reported results do not establish independent reproduction or broader deployment impact.

Strongest baseline

Controlled comparisons across distractor constructions at fixed context length and with/without retrieval gating; the abstract gives no model-independent deployment baseline.

Limitations

  • The paper’s asymptotic bound is derived under a softmax retrieval abstraction, not shown as a general bound for all transformer architectures.
  • Reported experiments are author-run; the research desk did not execute or reproduce them.
  • Effective distractor count is not necessarily raw context length, and the abstract does not establish transfer to deployed agents.

What we still don't know

  • The bound’s assumptions and reported retrieval experiments have not been independently checked against implementations or other model families.

Why it matters

The analysis offers a mechanism for why simply increasing context capacity may not ensure reliable evidence use. Its practical relevance depends on whether effective distractor counts and score assumptions match deployed retrieval and agent systems.
