Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Dias and Lima posted version 1 of their note on arXiv on 2026-10-31, analyzing the constants and scope of OpenAI’s claimed ETH-refuting exact-counting algorithm.
  • Claimed — The authors report that their extraction from the Oct 25 tensor-contraction proof leaves the certified running time above 2^n for bounded-density inputs unless log2(n+m) exceeds about 1.03×10^9, and for random 3-CNF at the satisfiability threshold unless log2 n exceeds about 5.3×10^11.
  • Claimed — The authors say their structural limits apply to the clause-product interpolation route under their stated cost model; they explicitly say that the parity form and tensor-contraction proof escape those limits.
  • Not established — This review did not independently reproduce the authors’ constants or proof analysis. The note itself preserves the asymptotic subexponential claim and does not establish that OpenAI’s theorem is false.

Strongest baseline

The note compares its extracted bounds with brute-force 2^n counting and analyzes the Oct 25 OpenAI preprint’s stated theorem and proof routes.

Limitations

  • The constants are the authors’ extraction from a proof whose theorem remains asymptotic; the note says it certifies no feasible bounded-density speedup under the analyzed bound, not that no faster algorithm exists.
  • The clause-product structural limits apply under the paper’s specified cost model and do not cover the preprint’s parity or tensor-contraction routes.
  • The authors report using Claude Opus 5.5 for reading, derivation, code, verification and drafting, and a separate instance for draft review; their code and proof were not independently audited in this review.

What we still don't know

  • The note’s constant extraction from the Oct 25 tensor-contraction proof has not been independently reproduced.
  • The note explicitly leaves the sharper parity and tensor-contraction routes outside its structural lower bounds.

Why it matters

If the authors’ extraction is sound, it qualifies the practical interpretation of a major asymptotic complexity result: the claimed subexponential rate may have no certified advantage at feasible input sizes. The note does not refute the asymptotic claim, and its narrower structural results do not cover the sharper proof routes.
