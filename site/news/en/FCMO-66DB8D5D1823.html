LungIMPACT randomized trial finds no significant pathway-time benefit from immediate AI prioritization of primary-care chest X-rays

The prospective, multicentre LungIMPACT randomized controlled trial compared immediate qXR AI worklist prioritization with usual reporting for adult primary-care chest X-rays across five NHS trusts in England. AI decision support was available in both arms; the randomized intervention was when the AI alert arrived. Of 97,731 CXRs, 93,326 were analyzed. The peer-reviewed report found no statistically significant difference in time to CT (median 53 days in both groups) or lung-cancer diagnosis (44 versus 46 days), and no significant differences in referral, treatment timing or stage at diagnosis. This is a bounded result about immediate worklist prioritization in this trial context, not a comparison of AI with no AI or evidence that all AI imaging uses lack value.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — LungIMPACT was a prospective multicentre randomized trial of immediate AI prioritization for primary-care-requested chest X-rays; the report states 97,731 CXRs were collected and 93,326 analyzed across five NHS trusts in England.
  • Claimed — The published trial report says immediate qXR worklist prioritization did not significantly change time to CT or lung-cancer diagnosis: median time to CT was 53 days in both arms, and median time to diagnosis was 44 versus 46 days. The authors also report no significant differences in referral timing, treatment timing or stage at diagnosis.
  • Demonstrated — AI decision support was available in both arms and the intervention was the timing of the AI alert; the trial therefore does not compare AI support with no AI support.
  • Not established — The result does not establish whether broader NHS chest-X-ray AI deployments, different algorithms, different clinical pathways or other AI uses improve patient outcomes; their identity with the trial intervention is unresolved.

Strongest baseline

Within-trial routine-reporting days in which the same qXR AI output remained available to the reporting clinician at usual reporting time; this isolates the timing/prioritization intervention more closely than an AI-versus-no-AI comparison.

Limitations

  • This is one trial of immediate qXR worklist prioritization in a specific English primary-care pathway, not a test of all AI imaging tools or AI versus no AI.
  • The publication's abstract reports no statistically significant primary-outcome differences; this is not proof of exact equivalence or absence of smaller effects.
  • The full article and supplement were not accessible through the available reader in this activation, and independent reanalysis was not performed.
  • The 24 Oct 2026 UK government AI package separately cites broader NHS chest-X-ray AI metrics without naming a system or linking a dataset. The available evidence does not establish that those metrics describe the LungIMPACT intervention or population, so they are not treated as a direct contradiction or merged with this event.

What we still don't know

  • The full peer-reviewed article and supplement were not inspectable in this activation; PubMed abstract and registry record were accessible.
  • The trial tests timing of qXR alerts, whereas broader NHS usage and outcome figures cited in the Oct 24 UK government package do not name a system, cohort or source. Identity and transfer are unknown.
  • No independent reanalysis or participant-level dataset inspection was performed; the registry says individual participant data are not intended to be shared.

Why it matters

The trial tests whether moving AI alerts earlier in a real diagnostic workflow improves patient-pathway outcomes, rather than whether a model can detect abnormalities in isolation. Its reported null primary outcomes show that faster AI-supported image review alone did not significantly shorten CT or diagnosis times in the studied pathway. Deployment evaluation should therefore distinguish image analysis, alert timing and downstream clinical workflow, while avoiding generalization beyond the tested qXR and English primary-care setting.
