What changed

A UK government-commissioned business survey reports that AI use among businesses rose from 16% in 2025 to 32% in 2026. Its 2026 fieldwork interviewed 3,503 private-sector businesses with at least five employees; 50 follow-up interviews and seven adopter site visits added context. Half of AI adopters reported an overall productivity increase, generally modest, while cost effects were mixed. These are survey-reported findings, not causal estimates; the technical annex reports a 5% response rate among businesses called.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The UK government’s 2026 business survey reports that 32% of surveyed UK businesses used at least one AI technology, compared with 16% in the 2025 wave; use was higher among larger firms and knowledge-intensive sectors.
  • Demonstrated — The published report documents a telephone survey fielded from 7 January to 21 April 2026, with 3,503 interviews, 50 follow-up interviews and seven adopter site visits. Its target population was UK private-sector businesses with at least five employees; the data were weighted by business size and sector.
  • Claimed — Among AI adopters, the report says 50% reported an overall productivity increase, usually modest; reported cost effects were mixed, with 24% reporting reductions, 64% no immediate financial effect and 9% increases.
  • Not established — The survey does not establish that AI caused productivity or cost changes, and its findings do not represent businesses with fewer than five employees or the excluded public-sector and other activities.

Strongest baseline

The report compares the 2026 survey wave with its 2025 wave; this record does not independently reconcile all question and sampling comparability.

Limitations

  • The 32% and 16% values are survey estimates based on reported use, not direct observation of deployments.
  • The survey covers UK private-sector businesses with at least five employees and excludes several public-service and other sectors.
  • The report records 3,503 completions from 69,370 businesses called at least once (5% response rate), leaving potential nonresponse uncertainty despite weighting.
  • Reported productivity and cost outcomes are not causal estimates; the 2025-to-2026 comparison requires attention to question comparability.

What we still don't know

  • The technical annex reports 3,503 completions among 69,370 businesses called at least once (5%). The report weights by size and sector, but nonresponse may still limit how well self-reports represent the target population.
  • Productivity and cost effects are respondents’ reported experiences, not measured causal effects attributable to AI.

Why it matters

This nationally scoped survey offers a timely measure of reported AI diffusion and the gap between adoption and realized business effects. It suggests use is spreading faster than transformative outcomes, but self-reporting, nonresponse and limited population scope constrain how far the results generalize.
