What actually changed

Prior Labs’ v2 TabPFN-3.5 report describes a tabular foundation model family and reports first-place results on seven benchmark leaderboards, spanning standard, grouped/temporal, string, multimodal, relational and distribution-scoring tasks. The report recommends use up to 1M rows and 6K features. These results are author-reported; several comparisons use published benchmark results without rerunning later-released baselines, and tuned MLPs remain stronger on some BeyondArena grouped, temporal and large-data slices.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — In its v2 technical report, the authors rank a TabPFN-3.5 family member first on seven reported benchmarks: TabArena, BeyondArena, STRABLE, MulTaBench, RelArena-α, TALENT and ScoringBench. The best TabPFN variant differs by benchmark.
  • CLAIMED — The report gives recommended capacity limits of up to 1 million training rows and 6,000 features for TabPFN-3.5; these are recommended limits for strong performance, not computational maxima. It reports TabPFN-3.5-Fast can be up to 3x faster than TabPFN-3 while retaining most accuracy gains.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The arXiv v2 technical report, model documentation and released checkpoints are publicly referenced; this activation inspected the report but did not run the code or reproduce benchmark scores.

Strongest baseline

Benchmark-dependent strongest competitors include TabFM, RealMLP, TabICLv2, XGBoost, TabM, RT-PluRel, KurveRSC, CatBoost and EXAONE; some comparisons reuse published baseline results rather than newly rerunning them.

The caveat that matters

  • The evidence is a Prior Labs technical report; this activation did not independently run checkpoints or benchmark harnesses.
  • For STRABLE and MulTaBench, the report compares to results published with the benchmark, so models released after those benchmark papers are absent from those comparisons.
  • On BeyondArena, tuned/ensembled MLPs retain the highest performance on grouped, temporal and large datasets; exact ordering in smaller slices is uncertain because confidence intervals are wide.
  • Reported speed depends on hardware and inference mode; TabPFN-3.5 can be up to 2x slower than TabPFN-3 on large training sets, while Fast/Plus variants trade latency and accuracy differently.
  • The 1M-row and 6K-feature values are recommended limits for strong performance, not measured computational limits or evidence of robust production performance.

What remains unknown

  • No independent benchmark reproduction or artifact audit was performed in this activation.
  • Some benchmark comparisons reuse published results and exclude baselines released after the benchmark papers.
  • Performance on real operational datasets, leakage risks, local calibration and distribution shift were not validated in this activation.

FCMO Lens

Tabular prediction remains common in scientific and business workflows, but many tabular models lose ground when data are temporal, grouped, wide or mixed with text and images. A single model family that performs across more of those regimes could simplify model selection, if its gains survive matched comparisons and real-world distribution shifts.
