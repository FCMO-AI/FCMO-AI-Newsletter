UK Data Centre Covenant links strategic support to energy, community, environmental and security tests

HM Treasury published a Data Centre Covenant on 28 October 2026. All data centres are encouraged to meet its tests; projects designated strategic must meet them when seeking enhanced government support. The tests cover energy-system costs and flexibility, local-benefit plans, efficient energy and water use, annual reporting for strategic sites, UK jobs and supply chains, and secure compute. New facilities are expected to achieve PUE of 1.3 or better. In return, government describes faster or reallocated grid connections and a tightly controlled route to temporary on-site generation for exceptionally strategic projects when other routes cannot provide capacity in time. The government estimates the UK will need 6 GW of AI data-centre capacity by 2030. These are policy terms and a government estimate, not evidence of implementation, capacity delivered or measured outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The policy document provides no baseline for data-centre energy/water use, grid connection waiting times, household cost allocation, local benefits, or domestic AI capacity against which effects can be evaluated.

Limitations

  • The Covenant and Budget establish policy text and government plans, not project-level designation, approvals, grid connections, temporary-power grants, or facility compliance.
  • The definition of strategic data centres and additional criteria remain to be published; the meaning and enforcement of several expected commitments are not yet clear.
  • The 6 GW figure is a government estimate, not an independently assessed forecast or evidence that capacity has been contracted or built.
  • No facility-level energy, water, emissions, heat reuse, flexibility, household cost, security or local-benefit outcomes are reported.

What we still don't know

  • The definition of a strategic data centre and additional project criteria are pending, and the practical verification/enforcement route for several Covenant expectations is not stated.
  • No project-level baseline or measurements establish actual connection acceleration, energy/water/emissions performance, household cost allocation, local-benefit delivery or effects on secure UK AI capacity.

Why it matters

The Covenant connects the expansion of AI infrastructure with energy-system costs, local benefits, resource reporting, security and access to government support and power connections. If applied as described, it could affect data-centre project economics and the allocation and timing of UK grid capacity. The definition of strategic sites and additional criteria remain pending, and the document reports no project-level compliance or outcome evidence.
