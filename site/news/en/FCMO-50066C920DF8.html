What changed

A preprint and its authors’ benchmark site describe a closed-course test in which four general-purpose vision-language models controlled steering and speed in a 2022 Toyota Corolla through a 127 m cone course, using vendor-native agent harnesses and up to three attempts in one conversation. The authors report GPT-6 Astra was the only model to finish, on attempt 2; Claude Fable 5.1 reached 45% on attempt 3, while no other attempt passed 50%. The test used a human operator ready to brake, a single course and one evaluation session per model. The arXiv paper reports Astra’s finish at 4:44 after its first command, while the live site and attempt trace list 5:22; the timing difference is unresolved. This is evidence of a narrow author-reported physical-control result, not general road-driving ability or vehicle safety.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that, among GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-5.6 Sol and Grok 4.6, only Astra completed their 127 m low-speed cone course, doing so on its second attempt; Fable reached 45% on its third attempt and no other attempt passed 50%.
  • Claimed — The benchmark gave each model up to three attempts in one continuous conversation and used a human operator in the vehicle, ready to brake; the tested result does not establish open-road performance or safety.
  • Contradictory — The paper states Astra reached the finish 4 minutes 44 seconds after its first command, while the current site leaderboard and trace list the attempt duration as 5 minutes 22 seconds; the difference in timing boundary is not resolved in the inspected sources.

Strongest baseline

The within-study comparisons use four named models in vendor-native harnesses on the same single course, with up to three attempts and retained conversation context. Harness differences and one session per model limit causal model ranking.

Limitations

  • The study evaluates four model/harness combinations on one course, with only one evaluation session per model; three attempts in the same conversation are not independent trials.
  • A human operator sat in the vehicle with a foot over the brake and could intervene; the setup was low-speed and on a closed lot.
  • The paper and current leaderboard/trace report different Astra finish durations (4:44 versus 5:22); the timing boundary was not resolved.
  • The course/map and cone placement were not fully surveyed; the authors note camera blind spots, actuator constraints and launch overshoot.
  • No independent reproduction, road test, crash-risk estimate, broad driving benchmark or safety validation was inspected.
  • The arXiv paper says Astra reached the finish 4 minutes 44 seconds after its first command, while the live benchmark site and the attempt-2 trace list 5 minutes 22 seconds. The exact timing definition differs across the accessible pages and was not resolved in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent researcher has repeated the physical benchmark or audited the published raw traces and outcome calculation in this review.
  • The result covers one closed course and one session per model; reliability across maps, weather, lighting, vehicle models and repeated trials is unknown.
  • The paper’s 4:44 Astra completion time differs from the live site/trace duration of 5:22; the timing boundary remains unclear.

Why it matters

The evaluation moves a general-purpose multimodal model from image interpretation to closed-loop action on a real vehicle, where latency and stale observations matter because the car continues moving while the model reasons. The result supports a constrained capability claim and makes harness, task framing and in-context reflection part of the measurement. A single low-speed course run with a human safety operator does not establish reliable driving or safe autonomy.
