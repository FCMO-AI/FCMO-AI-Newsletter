ACCT reports cross-model context-state transfer across three model families

A preprint introduces ACCT, which converts KV states already computed by one model into sparse KV states that a different-size model in the same family can use without re-encoding the full context. Across six directed pairs in Qwen3, Llama and Qwen2.5, authors report 4x KV-slot compression and mixed task results. The 6.5–21.2x speed headline measures conversion against target prefill and excludes source-model prefill. Appendix K includes source prefill: at the longest tested context, only three smaller-to-larger directions are faster than target-only prefill, while reverse directions are slower. These are author-reported experiments, not independent production evidence.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv:2610.42116v1 on 2026-10-30 describing ACCT, a method for converting source-model KV states into sparse target-model states within the same model family.
  • Claimed — The authors evaluate six directed model pairs across Qwen3, Llama and Qwen2.5 and report context transfer with a 4x KV-slot budget. At about 19K tokens, transfer conversion alone is 6.5–21.2x faster than target context prefill; task performance varies by direction and benchmark, and often falls below the target-full baseline.
  • Not established — The conversion-only speed comparison excludes the time needed to prefill the context with the source model. Appendix K reports that when source-state prefill is included at the longest context, only three smaller-to-larger directions are faster than target-only prefill; reverse directions are slower. The paper does not establish production-scale throughput, quality parity generally or independent replication.

Strongest baseline

Target Full is the task-performance reference and target-only context prefill is the timing reference. Comparisons are author-run and do not establish production throughput or costs.

Limitations

  • All reported outcomes are from the authors; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The 6.5–21.2x headline excludes source-context prefill; complete costs depend on whether those source states already exist.
  • With source prefill included, only three smaller-to-larger directions beat target-only prefill at the longest tested context; reverse directions are slower.
  • Task results are mixed, with several settings materially below the target-full baseline; evaluation is limited to six directed within-family pairs and contexts around 19K tokens.

What we still don't know

  • No accessible implementation or independent reproduction was established during targeted review.
  • Production concurrency, longer contexts, model-family transfer and task-quality preservation beyond the reported settings are unestablished.

Why it matters

If cross-scale context reuse can preserve task quality while lowering total compute in repeated-context workloads, it could change how heterogeneous model systems route long conversations and documents. The paper provides a bounded feasibility result across three model families, but conversion-only latency overstates the advantage when source context states must first be constructed, and reported task performance is mixed.
