Anthropic Fellows report coordination failures in multi-user agent teams sharing resources

An Anthropic Fellows paper studies agents acting for different users who share budgets, calendars, orders or software workflows. Across four simulated environments and 77 scenarios, the authors report that a single coordinator achieved better group outcomes than separate user agents in every environment. They evaluated up to five models, with model coverage varying by environment; the paper describes 20 episodes per scenario by default, with exceptions. The study identifies stalling as teams grow, overriding other users’ actions and unsupported claims, and reports setting-specific mitigations. Results are author-reported simulations, not independent replication or real-world deployment evidence.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper reports evaluation in four simulated shared-resource environments and 77 scenarios, testing up to five frontier models with model coverage varying by environment. The default was 20 episodes per scenario and formation, with exceptions listed in the appendix.
  • Claimed — The authors report that a single coordinator achieved better group outcomes than separate user agents in every environment; in the personal-assistant environment, it fulfilled targeted user requests about twice as often as teams.
  • Claimed — The authors report behavior analyses including stalling as teams grow, overrides of other users’ actions and unsupported claims about others. Some behaviors are judged by another model, and the paper explicitly says its analyses do not establish that overrides or fabrications cause the performance gap. Mitigations vary by environment.
  • Demonstrated — Anthropic published a dated research article on Nov 11, 2026; the linked arXiv paper is titled Worse Together and was submitted on Sep 30, 2026.
  • Not established — The inspected sources do not establish that the reported outcome gap or failure rates generalize to real deployments, user populations or environments outside the simulated scenarios; the benchmark code was not available in the linked repository during this review.

Strongest baseline

Within-paper comparisons of coordinator and user-specific agent formations in matched scenarios. Outcome metrics differ by environment; reported 95% intervals condition on the evaluated scenario set. This is an author-reported comparison, not an independent audit.

Limitations

  • Reported outcomes come from simulated users and tasks; the paper does not establish prevalence or causal effects in real deployments.
  • Results compare specific coordinator and team formations under the paper's scenarios; they do not show that a centralized coordinator is universally better.
  • The paper and article are primary sources for the reported methods and results; the research desk did not independently reproduce the experiments or audit scenario construction and scoring.
  • The paper says code will be released after review; the linked GitHub URL was unavailable to the reader during this review.
  • Model coverage and reasoning settings vary by environment; 77 scenarios do not imply every model was tested on every scenario. The default of 20 episodes has documented exceptions, and reported intervals condition on the evaluated scenario set.
  • The authors state that observed overrides and fabrications are not shown to cause the group-performance gap; some behavior labels rely on model judges.

What we still don't know

  • The reported comparisons have not been independently reproduced, and the linked code was unavailable during this review.
  • It is unknown how the reported coordination failures transfer to real users, deployed platforms, longer-running tasks or different resource and governance arrangements.

Why it matters

Agent systems increasingly share resources while representing different users. The study suggests that coordination overhead and conflicts among user-specific goals can reduce group outcomes and harm individual users, and that evaluation should measure both group results and actions affecting others. Its bounded simulations provide a testable research agenda, not evidence that centralized coordination is always preferable in deployed systems.
