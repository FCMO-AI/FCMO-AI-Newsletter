CausalWAM adds sparse action-relevance routing to a world-action model

The CausalWAM preprint presents an identifiability result for action-relevant latent factors under support-sparsity assumptions and reports adding a sparse router to a world-action model. In author-run evaluations, the paper reports gains over its baseline on 13 of 17 RoboCasa365 tasks (macro success 0.24 to 0.43), higher results across 40 LIBERO tasks, and a majority of successes over the baseline in 50 Franka cube-stack trials per policy. These are single-preprint results, not independent replications. The implementation retains a dense visual-to-action path; the paper's routed-only intervention covers four selected high-success tasks and does not establish that the learned theoretical support alone drives actions generally.

What changed

The CausalWAM preprint presents an identifiability result for action-relevant latent factors under support-sparsity assumptions and reports adding a sparse router to a world-action model. In author-run evaluations, the paper reports gains over its baseline on 13 of 17 RoboCasa365 tasks (macro success 0.24 to 0.43), higher results across 40 LIBERO tasks, and a majority of successes over the baseline in 50 Franka cube-stack trials per policy. These are single-preprint results, not independent replications. The implementation retains a dense visual-to-action path; the paper's routed-only intervention covers four selected high-success tasks and does not establish that the learned theoretical support alone drives actions generally.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Under the paper's support-sparsity and regularity assumptions, its theorem states that the action-relevant latent block can be identified up to permutation and invertible transformation, separating it from nuisance factors.
  • Claimed — In the authors' RoboCasa365 evaluation, CausalWAM reports macro success increasing from 0.24 to 0.43 over the baseline, with improvement on 13 of 17 tasks and 100 episodes per task.
  • Claimed — The authors report higher results across 40 LIBERO tasks and majority success over the baseline in 50 Franka real-robot cube-stack trials per policy; the inspected paper does not provide independent replication of these outcomes.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish that the mathematical support identified by its theorem is the sole or necessary information path driving deployed actions: its implementation retains a dense visual-to-action pathway, and its routed-only C-only intervention is reported only on four selected high-success tasks.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares its model with its stated baseline under the same reported evaluation suites and reports per-task RoboCasa365 outcomes; the present record treats those comparisons as author claims, not independent confirmation.

Limitations

  • Results are reported in one preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The theoretical identifiability result depends on support-sparsity and regularity assumptions; the paper notes that individual latents are not component-wise identifiable.
  • The deployed architecture includes a dense visual-to-action path alongside sparse routing, so the implementation does not by itself demonstrate that actions depend only on the theorem's selected support.
  • The routed-only C-only ablation covers four selected top-success tasks; it does not establish sufficiency across the reported task suites.
  • Generality beyond the tested model, tasks, data and hardware remains unknown.

What we still don't know

  • The simulation and real-robot results have not been independently reproduced.
  • Transfer beyond the reported Cosmos 3 Nano, RoboCasa365, LIBERO and cube-stack conditions is not established.
  • The contribution of sparse routed factors versus the retained dense visual-to-action pathway is unresolved outside four selected C-only intervention tasks.

Why it matters

If independently reproduced and transferable, identifying and routing action-relevant visual factors could improve data efficiency and robustness in learned robot policies. The present evidence is promising but bounded to author-run experiments and one model family; the gap between the support-sparsity theorem and the deployed architecture is material.
