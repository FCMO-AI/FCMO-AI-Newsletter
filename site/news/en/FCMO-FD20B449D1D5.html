Meta Muse Voice Transcribe unifies streaming ASR, diarization, endpointing and learned adaptive delay

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Voice Transcribe, an autoregressive Muse Spark-family real-time audio model that consumes 80 ms audio chunks, decides whether to listen longer or emit text, and jointly emits speaker and speech-boundary tokens. Meta says reinforcement learning jointly rewards word error rate and delay so the model learns per-word adaptive latency; an independent Artificial Analysis evaluation reported 3.1% English WER at 0.16 s after end-of-speech in its September 1 streaming comparison.

What actually changed

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Voice Transcribe, an autoregressive Muse Spark-family real-time audio model that consumes 80 ms audio chunks, decides whether to listen longer or emit text, and jointly emits speaker and speech-boundary tokens. Meta says reinforcement learning jointly rewards word error rate and delay so the model learns per-word adaptive latency; an independent Artificial Analysis evaluation reported 3.1% English WER at 0.16 s after end-of-speech in its September 1 streaming comparison.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Muse Voice Transcribe processes 80 ms audio chunks and learns per-word adaptive delay using a combined WER and delay reward.
  • CLAIMED — The system jointly performs streaming ASR, diarization and endpointing and supports 20+ speakers, 70+ training languages and hour-plus audio.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Artificial Analysis independently measured 3.1% English WER at 0.16 seconds after end-of-speech in its September 1 comparison.
  • INFERRED — Adaptive delay is a plausible transferable instance of value-of-computation scheduling for multimodal agents.

Strongest baseline

Contemporary streaming transcription systems in the Artificial Analysis comparison, including ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime, AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro Realtime and Cartesia Ink-2.

The caveat that matters

  • No model weights are released.
  • Parameter count, training compute, training-data volume and audio-data provenance are undisclosed.
  • Most capability claims remain first-party; only a narrow streaming English benchmark has independent evidence recovered in this run.
  • A hosted API prevents controlled ablation of fixed-delay versus learned-delay policies.

What remains unknown

  • Weights and a reproducible training recipe are unavailable.
  • The adaptive-delay mechanism has not been independently ablated against matched fixed-delay controls.

FCMO Lens

The technically interesting piece is not transcription alone but learned value-of-waiting: the model spends extra temporal context selectively on difficult words instead of using a fixed latency policy. That is a concrete multimodal instance of compute-on-demand and may transfer to agents deciding when another observation, retrieval, rollout, or reasoning step is worth its latency.
