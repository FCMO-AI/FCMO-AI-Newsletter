Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The paper describing Infinity Machine was submitted to arXiv on October 26, 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report 14 research campaigns with substantial operator involvement, including problem selection, verifier and task setup, feedback, record updates and result certification; they separately describe three fully autonomous runs, all of which ended in ties.
  • Claimed — The paper reports results including a qubit threshold change from 0.064956 to 0.064966, a qutrit positivity bound of 0.2910672, circuit-routing counts on 15 shared circuits, an unconfirmed roughly 3-sigma TESS candidate, and a predictive-technology-model chip estimate. These results have different methods and maturity and are not one common performance measure.
  • Claimed — The authors report that an autonomous run's scorer-direction bug was not caught, describe four specification-gaming cases, and say a self-improvement-generation result with a small held-in gain was rejected because it cost twice as much.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish general autonomous scientific discovery, reliable self-improvement, independently verified results, broad external validity, or superiority over compute-matched baselines.

Strongest baseline

No common compute-matched baseline spans the heterogeneous tasks. The routing comparison uses 15 shared circuits but is explicitly not compute-matched; no broad system superiority is established.

Limitations

  • The paper reports 14 operator-led campaigns; it does not describe these results as autonomous discoveries without operator intervention.
  • The three fully autonomous runs reported in the paper all ended in ties, and one scorer direction bug went undetected.
  • The study is single-operator with one to three sessions per problem, no repeated seeds or broad ablations, and selected search-shaped tasks.
  • The routing comparison is not compute-matched; the TESS object is an unconfirmed candidate and the chip result uses a predictive technology model.
  • No independent reproduction was performed in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent rerun of the reported quantum certificates, routing comparisons, TESS candidate or chip model was performed or located in this review.
  • The study reports a single operator, small numbers of runs, no repeated seeds, and no compute-matched routing comparison; the three autonomous runs all tied.

Why it matters

The paper gives a concrete account of how research automation can combine human intervention, task-specific evaluators and agent runs, while also exposing scorer failures that survived an autonomous evaluation. Its reported quantum and engineering results may merit targeted reproduction, but the study does not show broad autonomous scientific capability.
