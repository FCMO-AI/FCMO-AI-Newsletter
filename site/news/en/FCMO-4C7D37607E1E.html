What changed

An October 2026 preprint evaluates six released vision-language-action and world-action robot policies across clean LIBERO tasks, visual/embodiment perturbations, instruction paraphrases and observed runtime costs. Across the six systems, the paper reports only a 1.58-point spread on clean LIBERO but larger spreads on visual robustness and paraphrase success. Three systems are marked protocol-faithful; near-reproduction and pending-verification rows remain diagnostic and are excluded from strict comparisons. Results are limited to selected checkpoints in LIBERO-family simulation, not real-robot performance or causal architectural comparisons.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Across six selected released policies, the authors report a 1.58-point range in clean LIBERO averages versus 14.62 points in non-language robustness and 31.08 points in LIBERO-Para paraphrase success. Restricting the comparison to the three protocol-faithful systems preserves the separation.
  • Claimed — The paper distinguishes protocol-faithful, near-reproduction and pending-verification results; only protocol-faithful rows are used for strict comparisons. It does not infer causal effects of VLA versus WAM architecture from the checkpoint set.
  • Claimed — The paper reports observed latency, peak VRAM, runtime mode and setup burden for the evaluated checkpoints, while cautioning that those observations are not controlled intrinsic efficiency rankings.

Strongest baseline

Three protocol-faithful released checkpoints compared under fixed checkpoint/inference configuration and three run-level seeds on LIBERO-family protocols.

Limitations

  • The paper reports simulation-only results on a purposive set of six released checkpoints; it does not claim coverage of all open robot policies.
  • Three entries are protocol-faithful; one is near-reproduction and two are pending verification. Strict claims should use only the faithful subset.
  • Latency and memory are observed runtime profiles, not controlled cross-model efficiency measurements.
  • Results do not establish causal advantages of VLA or WAM architectures, real-world robustness or safety.
  • No independent reproduction was performed in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction was performed. Three of six reported systems are not protocol-faithful, and the paper’s strict cross-model comparison covers only the other three.
  • The evaluation uses LIBERO-family simulation and six purposively selected checkpoints; no real-robot task, broader benchmark census or architecture-causal comparison is established.

Why it matters

A clean benchmark score may hide important differences in robustness, instruction handling and deployability for executable robot policies. This evaluation makes those dimensions and evidence tiers visible together, while explicitly limiting strict comparisons to runs that meet its protocol-fidelity criteria.
