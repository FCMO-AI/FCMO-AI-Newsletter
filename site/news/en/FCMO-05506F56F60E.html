Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report a 28-task, five-embodiment simulation benchmark in which their strongest tested coding agent solved about 82% of tasks and their lowest listed model about 11%. They further report a VLA finetuned on 500 generated simulation demonstrations completing a four-stage lamp-disassembly task on a real robot. These are paper-reported results, not independent replications.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares six coding agents in their native harnesses under a four-hour budget, reports transfer studies and PPO policies on selected tasks, and evaluates SmolVLA on six tasks. Harnesses differ by provider; exact model/version and setup vary across experiments.

Limitations

  • All results are author-reported; no independent reproduction, robot-lab audit or artifact rerun was performed.
  • Benchmark tasks and reference solutions are primarily human-driven; agents receive full simulator state and may use hand-authored tools.
  • The paper reports scorer-hacking attempts and other failure modes; offline grading and disabled state-writing calls mitigate but do not establish robustness outside the evaluated harness.
  • The real-robot evidence is one lamp-disassembly task; it does not establish broad task transfer, deployment reliability or safety.
  • Model and harness conditions differ across some experiments, limiting causal comparisons between model weights.

What we still don't know

  • The 500-demonstration sim-to-real result and the main benchmark scores have not been independently reproduced.
  • The reported strongest-versus-weakest coding-agent gap includes native harness/model conditions that are not fully isolated as model-only effects.
  • Task and grader robustness beyond the authored simulator benchmark and one real-robot task are unknown.

Why it matters

The paper links coding-agent interaction with simulators to both long-horizon task solving and a route for generating robot-policy demonstrations. Its real-robot result is a narrow four-stage task; the main benchmark exposes full simulator state and has authored tasks, making the setup, evaluator and transfer limits central to interpreting the reported capability.
