MindEval2 reports guidance-calibration failures across mental-health support models

An October 15 preprint introduces MindEval2, pairing psychologist-developed operational rubrics with MindSim, a longitudinal synthetic-patient simulator with cross-session memory. The authors evaluate 18 language models and report that guidance calibration—over-exploring, under-guiding or giving poorly matched advice—accounts for 60–88% of each model’s rubric findings; under-guiding rises from 12% of early replies to about 70% from turn 9 onward. The evaluation uses 50 synthetic patient profiles and a single GPT-6.1 Sol judge. It is a benchmark result, not evidence of clinical effectiveness or safety in patient care.

What changed

An October 15 preprint introduces MindEval2, pairing psychologist-developed operational rubrics with MindSim, a longitudinal synthetic-patient simulator with cross-session memory. The authors evaluate 18 language models and report that guidance calibration—over-exploring, under-guiding or giving poorly matched advice—accounts for 60–88% of each model’s rubric findings; under-guiding rises from 12% of early replies to about 70% from turn 9 onward. The evaluation uses 50 synthetic patient profiles and a single GPT-6.1 Sol judge. It is a benchmark result, not evidence of clinical effectiveness or safety in patient care.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce Mental Health Quality Rubrics, developed with PhD-level psychologists, and MindSim, a longitudinal synthetic-patient harness with memory updated between interactions. They evaluate 18 proprietary and open-weight counsellor models using 50 synthetic patient profiles.
  • Claimed — The authors report that guidance-calibration issues—over-exploring, under-guiding and giving bad advice—account for 60–88% of each counsellor model’s rubric score. Under-guiding is flagged in 12% of replies in the first two turns and about 70% from turn 9 onward. Scores are produced by a single GPT-6.1 Sol judge.
  • Claimed — Patient-simulator realism was assessed using 180 excerpts comprising 666 turns; annotator agreement was Krippendorff’s alpha 0.58. The paper also compares surface features with 1,584 sessions from 886 people, which are not released and are reported only in aggregate.
  • Not established — The study does not establish clinical effectiveness, patient safety, real-world prevalence of harmful responses, or that its synthetic-patient scores predict outcomes in care settings.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares 18 models under a shared simulation and rubric protocol; no external clinical benchmark or patient-outcome baseline is used. GPT-family counsellor results may benefit from judge-family self-preference because the judge is GPT-6.1 Sol, as the authors note.

Limitations

  • All model findings are benchmark results reported by the paper authors; no independent replication was performed.
  • The main evaluation uses synthetic patient profiles generated with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and a single GPT-6.1 Sol judge; GPT-family results may be affected by same-family judge preference.
  • Human realism ratings cover 180 excerpts and 666 turns, with Krippendorff alpha 0.58; this is moderate agreement, not proof that simulated patients represent patients in care.
  • The real-user reference consists of 1,584 anonymized sessions from 886 people, used in aggregate and not released. It does not provide outcome labels for counsellor effectiveness or harm.
  • The benchmark does not establish clinical effectiveness, patient safety or real-world prevalence of harmful advice.

What we still don't know

  • No independent replication or alternative-judge evaluation of the 18-model benchmark findings was performed.
  • The simulator is synthetic and realism ratings show moderate inter-annotator agreement; alignment with diverse patient populations and longitudinal care remains uncertain.
  • The benchmark does not measure clinical effectiveness, patient outcomes or real-world rates of harmful guidance.

Why it matters

If the rubric and simulator are useful beyond the tested setup, they shift mental-health chatbot evaluation toward whether guidance is calibrated to conversational context over time, rather than only surface quality and boundary compliance. The result also identifies a failure mode across tested systems. Synthetic patients, one LLM judge and the absence of clinical outcomes limit what the scores establish.
