What changed

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report a collection comprising 1,788 fine-tuning runs and 405 serving runs across NVIDIA L40S, A100, H100 and H200 GPUs, with nominal 100 ms device power telemetry and run configuration/event metadata.
  • Claimed — For the measured configurations, the authors report fitted power estimates with median mean-node-power error of about 2.7% and held-out errors generally between 1.3% and 6.1%. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced here.
  • Claimed — The authors describe a public explorer containing selected reference traces, while stating that the complete dataset will be released upon acceptance. The full raw collection was not inspected in this review.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, generalization beyond its measured GPUs and workloads, facility-level electricity use, grid impact, or a validated method for translating device traces into site energy.

Strongest baseline

The paper reports its fitted estimates against measured power on its held-out data. No independent baseline model or external facility-meter comparison was inspected in this review.

Limitations

  • This is an author-reported arXiv preprint and has not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The paper states the full dataset will be released upon acceptance; the full raw collection was unavailable for inspection here.
  • Reported fit errors apply to evaluated configurations; the paper describes larger errors for ramp transients and an out-of-family model.
  • GPU-device traces do not establish server/facility electricity use, cooling overhead, or grid-level effects.

What we still don't know

  • The complete raw trace dataset is stated to be forthcoming upon acceptance; only selected public reference traces are described in the accessible explorer. The anonymous code endpoint returned HTTP 403 during this review.
  • No independent reproduction of the reported model fits or data-collection results was found or performed.
  • The reported traces are GPU-device measurements in selected training and serving configurations; server power, cooling, facility overhead and grid response are not measured.

Why it matters

Time-resolved power traces could improve estimates of accelerator energy use and help evaluate workload scheduling if the reported coverage and fit quality hold. The evidence concerns selected GPU devices and software/workload regimes, and does not establish server/facility energy or grid-level consequences.
