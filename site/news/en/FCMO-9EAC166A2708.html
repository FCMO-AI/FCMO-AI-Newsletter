An open FlashAttention mapping on AMD XDNA 2 moves long-sequence attention into the compute-bound regime

A September 18 preprint compares four FlashAttention mappings on AMD XDNA 1 and XDNA 2 accelerators. On a Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 with XDNA 2, its fused MLIR-AIR mapping keeps attention-score intermediates in compute-tile memory and uses the cascade interconnect for reductions; for GPT-2 Small dimensions it reports 3.62 effective GEMM TFLOP/s at 32K sequence length, about 2.0× the IRON mapping. The authors also report 6.5× energy efficiency over an optimized Triton/ROCm integrated-GPU implementation on the same system at 32K. This is a paper-reported operator result, not end-to-end LLM serving or independently reproduced deployment performance.

What changed

A September 18 preprint compares four FlashAttention mappings on AMD XDNA 1 and XDNA 2 accelerators. On a Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 with XDNA 2, its fused MLIR-AIR mapping keeps attention-score intermediates in compute-tile memory and uses the cascade interconnect for reductions; for GPT-2 Small dimensions it reports 3.62 effective GEMM TFLOP/s at 32K sequence length, about 2.0× the IRON mapping. The authors also report 6.5× energy efficiency over an optimized Triton/ROCm integrated-GPU implementation on the same system at 32K. This is a paper-reported operator result, not end-to-end LLM serving or independently reproduced deployment performance.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On XDNA 2 (Ryzen AI 9 HX 370), a fused MLIR-AIR FlashAttention mapping reports 3.62 effective GEMM TFLOP/s at 32K sequence length for GPT-2 Small attention dimensions, about twice the IRON mapping.
  • Claimed — At 32K sequence length, the paper reports 6.5× energy efficiency versus an optimized Triton/ROCm integrated-GPU implementation on the same Ryzen AI 9 HX 370, using the same bfloat16 causal-attention configurations and effective-matmul workload.
  • Claimed — Roofline analysis and measurements indicate that on XDNA 2, the fused mapping removes intermediate MemTile traffic and moves the evaluated FlashAttention workload into a compute-bound regime; on XDNA 1, streamed mappings already reach that regime and full fusion adds little throughput.
  • Not established — The paper does not demonstrate end-to-end LLM generation speed, application-level energy savings, or independent reproduction of the reported results.

Strongest baseline

IRON attention mapping on the same XDNA 2 device for the operator throughput ablation; an optimized fused Triton implementation compiled through AMD ROCm 7.2 on the same Ryzen AI SoC for the energy comparison.

Limitations

  • The performance and energy numbers are author-reported in one preprint; independent reproduction was not inspected.
  • The headline comparison measures attention kernels rather than end-to-end model inference.
  • The iGPU baseline is a same-device, optimized Triton/ROCm attention implementation, but other model stacks and workloads may change relative performance.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the XDNA 2 fused-kernel throughput or energy measurements was inspected.
  • The paper evaluates FlashAttention as an operator and does not measure end-to-end LLM decode/prefill throughput, whole-system energy or application quality.

Why it matters

The paper gives a concrete memory-hierarchy rule for deciding when fusing attention kernels helps on spatial NPUs: XDNA 2 benefits when fusion removes MemTile traffic, while XDNA 1 is already compute-bound after streaming. If the reported operator results transfer to deployed workloads, this could improve long-context edge inference efficiency; whole-model latency, power and application-level benefit remain unmeasured.
