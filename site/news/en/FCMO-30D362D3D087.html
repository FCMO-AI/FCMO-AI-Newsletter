A preprint finds a faithful internal map can coexist with unreliable navigation in TaxiGPT

An arXiv v1 mechanistic study reports that GPT2-XL trained on random walks through Manhattan represents intersections and streets in an internal map and uses these representations, position tracking and a goal compass to navigate. The authors attribute failures in out-of-distribution stress and detour tests to interference among superposed position features that disrupts localization, rather than the absence of a map; interventions that strengthen the position signal improve reported behavior. This is a single synthetic navigation case study, with broader transfer and independent replication unestablished.

An arXiv v1 mechanistic study reports that GPT2-XL trained on random walks through Manhattan represents intersections and streets in an internal map and uses these representations, position tracking and a goal compass to navigate. The authors attribute failures in out-of-distribution stress and detour tests to interference among superposed position features that disrupts localization, rather than the absence of a map; interventions that strengthen the position signal improve reported behavior. Evidence

  • Claimed — In a mechanistic study of GPT2-XL trained on random walks through Manhattan, the authors report that intersection and street representations form a faithful internal map; targeted interventions indicate that these representations causally influence navigation, alongside a goal compass.
  • Claimed — The paper attributes failures under out-of-distribution stress and detour tests to localization errors caused by interference among superposed intersection features, while the representations remain available; strengthening the position signal or reducing interference improves reported behavioral measures.
  • Not established — The study does not establish that this mechanism or its proposed indicators generalize beyond the examined TaxiGPT models, training setup and Manhattan navigation task; no independent replication was reviewed.

Strongest baseline

The paper reanalyzes behavioral failures previously interpreted as evidence of an incoherent map, using internal representation probes and causal interventions rather than a comparative external model baseline.

Limitations

  • Single author-team arXiv v1 preprint; no independent replication reviewed.
  • Case study of GPT2-XL trained on random walks through Manhattan; results do not establish transfer to other models or tasks.
  • Stress and detour tests are out-of-distribution regimes; findings are specific to the tested setup.

What we still don't know

  • The reported mechanistic findings have not been independently reproduced in this review, and the study concerns a single synthetic navigation setup.
  • Transfer of the proposed distinction and mechanistic indicators to other model architectures, training regimes and non-navigation tasks is unknown.

Why it matters

The study separates what a model represents from whether it can reliably locate and use those representations. If replicated in other environments, this distinction could improve mechanistic evaluation of apparent model failures. The evidence comes from a single GPT2-XL TaxiGPT setup trained on Manhattan random walks and does not establish generalization to other tasks or deployed models.
