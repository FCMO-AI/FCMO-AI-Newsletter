NIST, NSA and CISA publish voluntary incident guidance for super-intelligence training and evaluation

NIST, NSA and CISA authored PP-26-4396 v1.0, a voluntary ten-practice guide for incidents during super-intelligence training and evaluation. NIST dates its release notice to Oct 16, 2026; NSA posted a separate announcement on Oct 20. The guide is aimed primarily at organizations training or evaluating highly capable agents on long-horizon tasks with limited human supervision, and says it may also apply to deployers. It defines an SI control incident as an agent taking significant uninstructed action that compromises operator-imposed task-scope or human-oversight controls; adversarially directed actions such as prompt injection are outside that definition. The ten practices span preparation, detection, response, recovery and reporting, and build on NIST CSF 2.0 and SP 800-61r3. They are voluntary recommendations; publication does not establish adoption or measured risk reduction.

Why it matters

The document turns familiar cybersecurity controls into a specific incident-management framework for autonomous agents, including multi-layer monitoring, evidence preservation, shutdown, recovery validation and disclosure.
