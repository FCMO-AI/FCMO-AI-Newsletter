LLM-guided search reports lower Toffoli and qubit costs across reversible quantum-circuit tasks

A preprint introduces an evolutionary search in which LLMs propose reversible circuits and an evaluator scores them. Across seven of eight specified tasks, the authors report lower Toffoli and peak live-qubit counts than the listed baselines; the eighth presents a prime-field modular-inversion design using divsteps without a single prior reversible baseline. The linked author repository documents exact resource counting and randomized correctness tests, and two relied-on results have Lean proofs. Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper reports that its discovered circuits reduce both Toffoli count and peak qubit count versus listed baselines in seven of eight specified tasks; reported TQ ratios range from 4.1 to 39 for the comparable rows, with the Hermitian decoder comparing against a naïve reversible implementation and no ratio claimed.
  • Claimed — For prime-field modular inversion, the authors report a reversible divsteps architecture using 4.0 million Toffolis and 935 qubits within a complete 256-bit elliptic-curve point-addition benchmark with fresh per-shot addends; the paper does not claim a single comparable prior baseline for that table row.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper defines task-specific circuit contracts and correctness/error budgets. Its pinned author README says Lean proofs cover the divsteps step bound and branch-recovery rule used by the modular-inversion task, and a decoder-error bound used by DQI tasks. The evaluator exactly counts resource operations and tests random inputs derived from the emitted circuit hash; the Hermitian DQI task reports 30 wrong signs in 100,000 samples against a 100-failure budget. These formal sub-results and author-artifact checks are not an independent reproduction of the benchmark circuits.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper states that practical relative value depends on depth, routing, measurements, hardware constraints, decoder performance and error-correction architecture; its TQ objective and fixed computational-basis interfaces do not establish hardware speedup or end-to-end quantum advantage.

Strongest baseline

Task-specific baselines are reconstructed prior circuits listed by the authors. The Hermitian DQI baseline is explicitly a naïve reversible compilation because no prior reversible compilation existed; its 30 wrong signs in 100,000 tests are within the task allowance of 100. Modular inversion has no single table baseline: the complete point-addition benchmark includes divsteps variants with trade-offs and a Luo et al. EEA reference at 69,529,757 Toffolis/838 qubits, versus 4,006,673/935 for the divsteps circuit. Baseline fidelity and full task-contract matching remain to be reviewed across the suite.

Limitations

  • All quantitative gains are author-reported circuit resource results under the specified task contracts; no independent reproduction was performed in this activation.
  • The evaluator uses exact resource accounting but finite random correctness sampling for several tasks; random test acceptance does not prove all-input correctness. Some rows are reported as exact over the entire finite domain.
  • The Hermitian DQI result is compared with a naïve reversible implementation rather than a prior reversible compilation; prime-field modular inversion has no single table baseline.
  • TQ multiplies Toffoli count by peak live qubits and does not include depth, routing, measurement/feed-forward, decoder, magic-state or architecture-specific error-correction costs.
  • No physical quantum hardware execution, end-to-end quantum-advantage result or broad transfer outside the fixed computational-basis task contracts is demonstrated.

What we still don't know

  • No independent evaluator replay or expert review of the task-specific circuit contracts, grader correctness or baseline fidelity has been recorded.
  • The TQ objective omits circuit depth, routing, measurement/feed-forward cost, magic-state demand and hardware/error-correction implementation costs.
  • Table comparisons are task-specific; the Hermitian decoder uses a naïve reversible baseline and modular inversion has no single comparable table baseline. Transfer outside the fixed computational-basis contracts is not established.
  • The reviewed methods specify the search loop and model but do not report per-task LLM-call/token totals, wall-clock time or search-compute cost, so the cost and repeatability of obtaining the circuits cannot be compared with expert optimization.

Why it matters

If the reported constructions survive independent task and baseline review, jointly redesigning algorithms, representations and uncomputation could change resource estimates for several quantum subroutines and expand evaluator-guided algorithm search. Practical value remains dependent on each application and hardware objective; Toffoli-count × peak-qubit count does not account for depth, routing, measurements or error-correction architecture.
