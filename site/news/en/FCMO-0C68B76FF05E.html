TierKV combines predictive KV-cache compression and flash tiers for mobile LLM inference

Shu et al. describe TierKV, an accepted EuroSys 2027 system that predicts cache demand from prefill states and jointly places tokens in exact, low-rank and flash-offloaded tiers. In the authors’ eight-model, three-mobile-SoC evaluation, it reduced RAM-resident KV cache by 12.5–34% and improved prefill throughput by up to 17.6× versus MNN-LLM; the largest speedup is baseline-specific. Average end-to-end speedup was 1.10× versus llama.cpp, while decoding throughput fell in some cases from SVD reconstruction. These are paper-reported results; no independent reproduction was performed.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v2 paper identifies TierKV as an accepted EuroSys 2027 paper and describes a predictive three-tier KV-cache system for mobile LLM inference.
  • Claimed — Across eight text, vision and audio models on three mobile SoCs, the authors report reducing RAM-resident KV-cache footprint by 12.5–34%; maximum supported context length increases by up to 2.6× in the tested memory regime.
  • Claimed — The paper reports prefill throughput improvements up to 17.6× over MNN-LLM for selected text models and an average end-to-end speedup of 1.10× over llama.cpp across evaluated workloads; decoding throughput decreases in some cases due to SVD reconstruction.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish these gains on other device families, models, workloads or independent implementations; its maximum prefill gain is not a general end-to-end speedup.

Strongest baseline

Three mobile inference frameworks are compared, but availability differs by model; MLC-LLM supports only a subset. The reported 17.6× maximum is against MNN-LLM, while the paper reports 1.10× average end-to-end speedup versus llama.cpp.

Limitations

  • Author-reported system measurements; no independent implementation audit or reproduction was performed.
  • The maximum 17.6× prefill throughput comparison is against MNN-LLM on selected workloads; average end-to-end improvement over llama.cpp is 1.10×.
  • Comparisons are incomplete where baseline frameworks do not support the same model or long-context regime.
  • SVD reconstruction lowers decode throughput in some cases; prefill gains do not imply faster decoding in every workload.
  • Device results are limited to three evaluated mobile SoCs; thermal throttling and external validity remain concerns.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the system, speed, memory or accuracy measurements was conducted.
  • Cross-device generality and behavior under realistic prompt-length distributions, thermal throttling and competing storage/memory load remain uncertain.

Why it matters

On-device inference is constrained by shared memory and storage bandwidth. Jointly planning compression, cache residency and offload before decoding is a concrete systems approach to longer contexts under fixed device memory, but measured gains depend on the workload and comparator.
