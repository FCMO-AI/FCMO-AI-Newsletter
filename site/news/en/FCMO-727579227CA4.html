Agentic high-level synthesis with RTL refinement reports faster FPGA kernels than direct RTL on a bounded benchmark

The authors compare agents designing FPGA kernels directly in RTL with workflows that use high-level synthesis (HLS) and post-HLS RTL refinement. On an 11-task benchmark, they report a 2.62× geometric-mean speedup in simulated execution time for agent-based HLS with RTL refinement over direct RTL across 13 valid task/model pairs; agent-based HLS alone reports 2.31× across 15 valid pairs. The experiments use Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-5.6 Sol in the same pi harness, AMD Alveo U55C and Vitis 2025.2, with each experiment run once. The metric combines simulated cycle count with the greater of post-route clock period or target period. These are paper-reported FPGA results, not an independent reproduction, production ASIC benchmark or measured cost reduction.

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv preprint 2609.21157v1 on 2026-09-17.
  • Claimed — On an 11-task FPGA benchmark, the authors report that agent-based HLS with post-HLS RTL refinement achieves a 2.62× geometric-mean speedup in simulated execution time over direct RTL across 13 valid task/model pairs; HLS alone reports 2.31× across 15 pairs.
  • Claimed — Each experiment was run once. The evaluation is limited to AMD Alveo U55C with Vitis 2025.2 and two named models; the geometric mean includes only valid direct-RTL baselines, and two GEM1 three-AXI cases were excluded from AHRR.

Strongest baseline

Direct RTL Design by the same model on the same task; geometric means include only valid direct-RTL pairs.

Limitations

  • Single-run results do not quantify run-to-run variation or sensitivity to prompts and agent sampling.
  • The result is specific to an AMD Alveo U55C/Vitis 2025.2 FPGA flow, two model configurations and the selected 11-task suite; some workloads are scaled down.
  • The aggregate metric is simulated execution time based on cycle count and post-route/target clock, not measured throughput, energy, chip area-cost trade-offs or deployed workload performance.
  • AHRR excludes two GEM1 three-AXI cases; geometric means use only valid direct-RTL baseline pairs. Some small kernels perform better under direct RTL.
  • No independent artifact reproduction was performed.

What we still don't know

  • The author repository and evaluation artifacts are publicly listed, but this review did not pin a revision, inspect source/result-generation files or run the artifacts; single-run reported results remain independently unverified.
  • Transfer beyond the chosen U55C/Vitis toolchain, two model configurations, task suite and simulated execution metric is unknown.

Why it matters

AI accelerator design agents can work at several abstraction levels. This paper reports that HLS can supply compiler-encoded structure that agent-written RTL may miss, while later RTL refinement can recover additional optimization opportunities. Its bounded FPGA results may inform agentic hardware-design workflows, but do not establish improved production-chip throughput or lower chip cost.
