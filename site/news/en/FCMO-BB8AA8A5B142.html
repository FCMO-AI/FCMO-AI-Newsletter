What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces MolDesignBench, a benchmark of 2,000 small-molecule generation and optimization tasks. Tasks combine implicit narrative requirements with explicit chemical constraints, include infeasible specifications, and expose 17 chemistry tools. Across six general and chemistry-focused agent configurations, the authors report best success rates of 38% for generation and 43% for optimization. The benchmark tests constraint handling and tool use; it does not test experimental property measurements or whether generated molecules lead to discoveries.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The preprint defines a benchmark with 2,000 small-molecule generation and optimization instances, a 17-tool interface, explicit and implicit constraints, and infeasible cases.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the best evaluated agent achieved 0.38 success on generation and 0.43 on optimization in their benchmark; tool access generally improved performance.
  • Claimed — In the reported tests, agents often missed implicit requirements and struggled to identify infeasible specifications; the paper reports large implicit-constraint error shares for some tested models.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The study evaluates computationally predicted molecular properties in synthetic small-molecule tasks; it does not establish experimental properties, successful synthesis, or novel molecular discovery.

Limitations

  • All benchmark outcomes are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • The task set focuses on small molecules, not polymers, crystals or other chemical domains.
  • Molecular properties rely on computational predictions rather than experimental measurement; the benchmark does not establish synthesis or novel discovery.
  • Results are bounded to six tested agent configurations, this tool interface and the stated prompt/turn protocol; broader transfer is unknown.
  • The authors report the best success rate separately for generation and optimization, not a single aggregate score.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or audit of task construction, tool correctness, or the reported agent scores has been completed.
  • The relationship between benchmark scores and experimental molecular-design outcomes is unmeasured.

Why it matters

The benchmark makes implicit constraints, conflicting requirements and infeasible requests part of a structured evaluation, areas that simpler chemistry tasks may not test. Its reported results suggest current agents can perform poorly even with specialized tools. This is useful evidence for evaluation design, while its synthetic small-molecule setting cannot establish real-world discovery performance.
