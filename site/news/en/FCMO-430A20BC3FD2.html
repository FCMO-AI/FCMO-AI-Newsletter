OpenAI offers GPT-6 Astra’s weights as GPT-Rosalind-6 for screened life-sciences research

OpenAI’s Oct 30 system-card addendum says GPT-Rosalind-6 uses GPT-6 Astra’s weights without additional training or post-training. OpenAI’s release and setup notes say GPT-6 Rosalind Research is rolling out to limited approved Research organizations in Work and Codex, and through separately approved API access; it is not available in Chat. Enterprise workspaces begin with a two-week Admin Preview with access off by default. At the highest reasoning budget tested, OpenAI reports higher GPT-Rosalind-6 scores than GPT-Rosalind-5.5 on three shared benchmark sets: MedChemBench v7 (49.6% vs 28.9%, 99 problems), GeneBench Pro v13 single-attempt success (37.7% vs 16.7%, 129 problems), and LifeSciBench Gold (61.3% vs 44.0%, 94 of 100 problems after excluding six flagged tasks). OpenAI classifies biological capability as High but below Critical on its selected Preparedness evaluations. These are first-party results; GeneBench used a configuration unlike production, the Critical thresholds are preliminary, and no independent reproduction or real-world productivity/safety outcome is established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI’s Oct 30 system-card addendum states that GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra and adds no training data or post-training for this release.
  • Claimed — At the highest tested reasoning budget, OpenAI reports GPT-Rosalind-6 scores above GPT-Rosalind-5.5 on three shared benchmark sets: MedChemBench v7, 49.6% vs 28.9% on 99 problems; GeneBench Pro v13 single-attempt success, 37.7% vs 16.7% on 129 problems; and LifeSciBench Gold, 61.3% vs 44.0% on the 94 of 100 tasks retained after six were excluded for possible task or environment issues. The comparisons are first-party; GeneBench used a configuration different from production.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that, after refusal adjustment, GPT-Rosalind-6 exceeds indicative High-capability thresholds on three of four biological troubleshooting evaluations; none of four Critical-capability evaluations meets its indicative threshold.
  • Demonstrated — OpenAI’s system card describes trusted access with review of mission, use case, governance and access control. Its Oct 30 release/setup notes say GPT-6 Rosalind Research is rolling out to limited approved Research organizations in Work and Codex, and through separately approved API access, not Chat; Enterprise access starts in a two-week Admin Preview and is off by default. Approved enterprise users must apply least-privilege controls and qualified human review before outputs inform experiments, publications or consequential decisions.
  • Not established — The card does not establish independent reproduction, prospective real-world laboratory productivity, comprehensive biological risk, or safety across all deployment contexts.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI compares both models on the same benchmark versions and problem sets for three beneficial evaluations. Comparisons are first-party and at the highest tested reasoning budget; GeneBench used a benchmark-specific configuration unlike production, and LifeSciBench excluded six potentially problematic tasks.

Limitations

  • All biological capability and safeguard descriptions are OpenAI-authored; the card is primary evidence for what OpenAI assessed and deployed, not independent confirmation of scores or safeguard effectiveness.
  • The four Critical evaluations are described as preliminary proxies informed by expert judgment, and their thresholds may change as evaluations are refined.
  • The card states none of four Critical evaluations met its indicative threshold; this does not establish absence of biological risk.
  • This activation did not run benchmarks, inspect confidential evaluation artifacts, or measure laboratory productivity or real-world misuse outcomes.
  • The three beneficial benchmark comparisons are OpenAI-reported at the highest tested reasoning budget, not independent or prospective workflow results. The GeneBench configuration differs from production; six LifeSciBench Gold tasks were excluded over possible task/environment issues.

What we still don't know

  • The biological capability scores and thresholds are first-party evaluation results; this activation did not independently reproduce the benchmarks or audit the full scoring protocol.
  • Published benchmark classifications do not measure prospective scientific productivity or deployment safety under real user workflows.

Why it matters

The card describes a screened deployment of a general frontier model for more dual-use life-sciences research and supplies matched, author-reported comparisons against GPT-Rosalind-5.5. The reported score differences are consequential for choosing research systems, but they come from selected benchmarks at the highest tested reasoning budget, not independent or prospective lab outcomes. The same-weight disclosure also means this release is a deployment and evaluation change, not a new training run.
