A preprint reports black-box audits for undisclosed output-token inflation in LLM services

Chen et al. define provider-side token inflation attacks as undisclosed provider-controlled changes that increase returned, billable output while largely preserving task utility. They instantiate five attack paths and report output lengths above 10.2 times a clean baseline. Their saturation-based probe achieves 85.1% macro q-AUC with false-positive rates below 2% across four open-weight models. In a black-box audit of 15 API services, they flag seven as showing behavior consistent with the attack. These are author-reported preprint results: a flag does not prove manipulation or dishonest intent, and broader prevalence is unknown.

What actually changed

Chen et al. define provider-side token inflation attacks as undisclosed provider-controlled changes that increase returned, billable output while largely preserving task utility. They instantiate five attack paths and report output lengths above 10.2 times a clean baseline. Their saturation-based probe achieves 85.1% macro q-AUC with false-positive rates below 2% across four open-weight models. In a black-box audit of 15 API services, they flag seven as showing behavior consistent with the attack. These are author-reported preprint results: a flag does not prove manipulation or dishonest intent, and broader prevalence is unknown.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors instantiate five provider-side token inflation attacks and report that each increases mean output length to more than 10.2x the clean baseline while largely preserving task accuracy, with an average decrease of 1.88 percentage points.
  • CLAIMED — Their saturation-based single-probe audit reports 85.1% macro q-AUC and false-positive rates below 2% across four open-weight models under three benign system-prompt settings.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that their audit flagged 7 of 15 real API services as PTIA-consistent; the black-box result does not identify provider intent or prove manipulation.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The inspected v1 preprint defines the threat model and reports the above experiment; this activation did not run code or reproduce results.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares its audit to RUT and historical-KS controls, including a trusted-local-model baseline; the reported service audit does not establish hidden provider behavior as ground truth.

The caveat that matters

  • All measured results are reported in one v1 preprint and were not independently reproduced here.
  • The four open-weight model results use a selected evaluation setup; transfer across model families, languages, workload types and service updates is unknown.
  • The 15-service audit is a small sample and not an estimate of service-wide prevalence.
  • A service flagged as PTIA-consistent may have alternative explanations; black-box behavior does not establish provider intent, deception or policy violation.
  • The paper assumes billed output tokens are returned and correctly counted; the audit does not test billing-meter integrity or hidden reasoning-token charges.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of attack construction or detection performance was inspected.
  • The 15-service sample does not establish prevalence or persistence across providers, models, regions or time.
  • Black-box flags cannot distinguish deliberate inflation from benign model, routing or serving changes without additional evidence.

FCMO Lens

If independently reproduced, a black-box audit that detects output-length changes beyond ordinary service variation could help users assess opaque token-billed APIs. The reported 7/15 flags make follow-up consequential, but do not establish provider wrongdoing; the work is a single preprint and its service sample is not a prevalence estimate.
