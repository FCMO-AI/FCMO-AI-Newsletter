What changed

FDE-Gym introduces 95 end-to-end AI delivery tasks adapted from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors. Across 13 model configurations, the authors report Claude Opus 5.5’s best mean L3 score as 0.56 (95% task-bootstrap interval 0.50–0.62) and 14 resolved tasks out of 95, using customer-metric uplift adjusted for operational incidents and stakeholder contact costs. These are paper-reported results from simulated stakeholders and production-like workspaces; a human baseline is still being collected, and the scores have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce FDE-Gym as 95 end-to-end delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors. The tasks use scripted stakeholders, production-like workspaces and held-out data, and score delivered systems against each customer's metric.
  • Claimed — Across 13 model configurations and three runs per task-level combination, the authors report that Claude Opus 5.5 achieves the best mean score at L3: 0.56 (95% confidence interval 0.50–0.62, bootstrapped over tasks), with 14 of 95 tasks resolved (score at least 0.8 with no incident). They also report that obtaining every key stakeholder fact did not ensure delivery success.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish how these results transfer to real customer engagements or compare with human FDE performance; its human baseline is still being collected.

Strongest baseline

Within the benchmark, unchanged systems define zero and expert-authored reference solutions define one. There is not yet a human FDE baseline; the authors say it is being collected.

Limitations

  • The 0.56 mean and task-resolution counts are author-reported; an independent reproduction has not been performed.
  • Stakeholder responses are scripted, and the benchmark's contact costs, tasks, customer metrics and reference solutions are designed by its authors.
  • A human FDE baseline is not yet reported.
  • Only three examples are openly downloadable; the remaining original cases are request-gated, while variants and reference solutions are held out.
  • The study uses one minimal agent framework and three runs per task-level combination; contamination and transfer to actual customer environments remain unresolved.

What we still don't know

  • No independent run of the paper's model suite or task-level review of grader correctness was performed.
  • The paper reports that a human baseline is being collected but does not yet provide it.
  • Transfer from scripted stakeholders and production-like benchmark systems to actual customer deployments is unknown.

Why it matters

Most agent benchmarks start from a well-specified ticket. FDE-Gym tests a broader delivery loop in which an agent must decide whether the sponsor identified the right problem, discover constraints, work in a customer-like system, and improve a held-out business metric. If the task design and reported results generalize, this could broaden evaluation beyond code completion and task success. The benchmark does not yet show real-world transfer or performance relative to human FDEs.
