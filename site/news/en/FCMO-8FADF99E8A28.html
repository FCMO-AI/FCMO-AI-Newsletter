Alignment Inertia measures when system prompts and LoRA fail to override prior behavior

The Alignment Inertia preprint measures whether system prompts and LoRA fine-tuning override each model’s observed policy behavior on cases where that behavior conflicts with a target policy. It evaluates Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 on medical-misinformation and hate-speech datasets. Zero-shot prompting leaves some conflicting cases unresolved, with results varying by model, domain and policy direction; LoRA sometimes improves and sometimes worsens override success. These are author-reported behavioral results conditional on the study’s constructed divergence zones and adaptation data, not a universal measure of prompt or fine-tuning reliability.

It evaluates Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 on medical-misinformation and hate-speech datasets. These are author-reported behavioral results conditional on the study’s constructed divergence zones and adaptation data, not a universal measure of prompt or fine-tuning reliability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The study evaluates two open-weight instruction-tuned models across medical misinformation and hate-speech tasks, using N=500 evaluation examples per domain and condition; override success is measured only on cases where the model’s instruction-free baseline conflicts with the target policy.
  • Claimed — Zero-shot system prompting leaves 20.0%–49.1% of conflicting cases unresolved under the more permissive target policy across the reported model/domain conditions. Results differ by model and policy direction; LoRA improves some conditions and worsens others.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper describes behavioral results, not a mechanism proving why prior behavior persists; the results cover two open-weight models and two domains, and some medical LoRA training pools exhausted before the planned 1,500 examples.

Strongest baseline

Instruction-free empirical baseline and the same model under zero-shot system prompting, compared with LoRA adaptation on target-policy examples. Measures are conditional on observed policy conflicts rather than a model-wide safety score.

Limitations

  • Author-reported results; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • Only two open-weight models and two policy domains were evaluated; results do not establish behavior for closed models or deployment settings.
  • Override success is conditional on empirically constructed policy-divergence cases and the chosen policy prompts and label mappings.
  • Some medical LoRA conditions exhausted the training pool well below the planned 1,500 examples, which may bias results.
  • The paper’s behavioral audit does not establish a mechanism or universal resistance to system prompts or fine-tuning.

What we still don't know

  • Override-success and LoRA findings have not been independently reproduced with repeated seeds and additional models.
  • The divergence zones and policy mappings are constructed from observed baselines and selected label mappings; LoRA training pools were exhausted in some medical conditions.

Why it matters

An operator may need to make a model more permissive or restrictive than its observed training prior. If the reported, direction-dependent resistance replicates, testing controllability on actual policy conflicts could expose cases that a generic instruction-following score misses. The study’s small model/domain set does not establish behavior across providers or deployments.
