Preprint reports conditional decay of prompt influence in bounded transformer regimes

A 2026-10-16 arXiv preprint proves a conditional O(P(log t)^(L-1)/t) upper bound on prompt-induced hidden-state offsets for finite-depth pre-LN transformers with full causal softmax attention, bounded logits and non-degenerate pre-norm activations. Experiments on Pythia and Qwen2.5 report faster teacher-forced prompt-offset decay in trained than untrained models and a relay-path mechanism that training suppresses early. On Pythia-160m, a uniform logit boost at s=0.5 lowers measured decay exponents by 0.17–0.25 for +2.6% perplexity; on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, fully French outputs rise from 31% to 94% after 2048 tokens. Small multi-turn tests suggest improved adherence, but samples are limited and boosts can cause instruction echoing. These are author-reported results, not evidence of widespread deployed-model failure or a validated production remedy.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors prove a conditional bound O(P(log t)^(L-1)/t) on the prompt-induced hidden-state offset for finite-depth pre-LN transformers with full causal softmax attention, bounded attention logits and non-degenerate pre-norm activations; conditional on a shared generated history, prompt influence on the next-token distribution vanishes at the same asymptotic rate.
  • Claimed — Across Pythia and Qwen2.5 experiments, the authors report teacher-forced prompt-offset decay exponents around 0.55–0.7 in trained models beyond early layers, versus about 0.2–0.5 in untrained models. Their path decomposition attributes much of the untrained late offset to a slowly decaying relay path; training suppresses relay within the first billion tokens.
  • Claimed — On Pythia-160m real-corpus prompt pairs, the authors report that a uniform prompt-logit boost with strength s=0.5 lowers measured decay exponents by 0.17–0.25 at a +2.6% WikiText perplexity cost. On Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, the fraction of outputs fully in French after 2048 generated tokens rises from 31% to 94% at s=0.5.
  • Claimed — In an eight-turn Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct setup, prompt boosts improved reported French adherence trends across 32 conversations per condition, while excess instruction echoing increased in single-turn forced-length tests. The authors say these adherence differences are trends rather than statistically significant effects and found no clear advantage of log-shaped boost over a constant boost with the same mean.
  • Not established — The study does not establish broad system-prompt failure in deployed frontier models or robust production benefit from logit compensation: results are author-reported, tested on small model families, free-generation samples are limited, and boosts can raise perplexity or cause instruction echoing. Independent replication remains open.

Strongest baseline

Matched prompt pairs under shared teacher-forced continuations, trained versus untrained checkpoints, and matched prompt-logit versus placebo/constant boosts.

Limitations

  • One author-reported preprint; no independent proof review or empirical reproduction was conducted.
  • The theorem assumes finite depth, pre-layer normalization, full causal softmax attention, bounded attention logits and pre-norm activations bounded away from zero.
  • Most offset measurements use teacher-forced shared continuations, which isolate hidden-state prompt influence but do not alone capture free-running interaction. Generated text can itself preserve prompt information.
  • The empirical model set is limited to small Pythia and Qwen2.5 sizes; broad frontier-model or production generalization is not established.
  • The reported Qwen instruction-adherence and multi-turn tests are limited in scale; the authors characterize small multi-turn differences as trends and find no clear log-shaped-boost advantage over matched constant bias.
  • Prompt-logit compensation can raise perplexity and induce instruction echoing; stronger per-head compensation produced very large perplexity costs.
  • The promised toolkit was not located in the targeted search.

What we still don't know

  • The theorem and empirical experiments have not been independently checked or reproduced. The paper says it will release a Hugging Face toolkit, but no official artifact repository was located.
  • Prompt-bias adherence results come from small model families and limited samples; the multi-turn study uses 32 conversations per condition, while boosts also carry perplexity and echoing costs.

Why it matters

If independently sustained, the result provides a formal lens for when prompt effects attenuate inside transformer hidden states and how long-context evaluations should distinguish hidden-state decay from information retained in generated text. Its present scope is conditional and does not show general instruction loss in deployed models.
