Bonsai 2 27B whitepaper reports roughly three-quarter retention on two agentic coding benchmarks

PrismML’s September 17, 2026 whitepaper reports Ternary Bonsai 2 27B at 52.8 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 60.8% resolved on SWE-bench Verified, compared with 69.7 and 80.6 for Qwen3.8-27B FP16. What changed

PrismML’s September 17, 2026 whitepaper reports Ternary Bonsai 2 27B at 52.8 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 60.8% resolved on SWE-bench Verified, compared with 69.7 and 80.6 for Qwen3.8-27B FP16. The paper says the Terminal-Bench run used Terminus-2 on 89 tasks with one attempt each, while SWE-bench Verified used mini-swe-agent on all 500 instances. These two agentic results are separate from the paper’s 20-benchmark aggregate, where Bonsai 2 scores 83.9 versus 85.4 (98.2% retention). This is a bounded vendor-reported quality tradeoff, not independent validation; checkpoint hashes, run traces and independent reproduction remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — PrismML’s September 17 Bonsai 2 27B whitepaper reports scores of 52.8 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 60.8% resolved on SWE-bench Verified, versus 69.7 and 80.6 for its Qwen3.8-27B FP16 comparator. The paper describes these as separate agentic benchmarks, outside the 20-benchmark suite behind its 83.9 versus 85.4 aggregate (98.2% retention) headline.
  • Claimed — The paper says Terminal-Bench 2.1 used Terminus-2 with one attempt on all 89 tasks, eight concurrent trials, and an 80,000-token output budget per turn; SWE-bench Verified used mini-swe-agent on all 500 instances and reports resolved rate. The two evaluations ran on an identical Harbor-driven agent framework; detailed per-instance results or independent reproduction were not inspected.

Strongest baseline

The paper directly compares Bonsai 2 27B to Qwen3.8-27B FP16 on both agentic benchmarks; no other low-bit baseline is reported for these two task-specific scores.

Limitations

  • The results and protocol are reported by PrismML in its own whitepaper; no independent reproduction was inspected.
  • The paper’s main 20-benchmark aggregate reports 83.9 versus 85.4, but Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-bench Verified are separately reported and excluded from that 20-benchmark suite.
  • This review did not inspect checkpoint hashes, per-instance result artifacts, or uncertainty intervals.
  • Terminal-Bench reports one attempt per task on 89 tasks; SWE-bench Verified reports resolved rate across 500 instances.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or instance-level audit of the reported Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-bench Verified scores was performed. The whitepaper gives benchmark denominators and agent protocols but no audited run traces in this review.
  • This review did not verify the exact checkpoint hashes or independently confirm that the compared Bonsai and FP16 artifacts share the stated model lineage.

Why it matters

A small 27B-class model can preserve most of a broad 20-benchmark average while showing a materially larger reported drop on long-horizon coding agents. Separating the agentic tasks from the aggregate helps deployment decisions account for workload-specific quality. The exact scores remain vendor-reported pending reproduction.
