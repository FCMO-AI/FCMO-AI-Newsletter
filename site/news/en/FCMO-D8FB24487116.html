What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces ProCredit, a reinforcement-learning method that reruns task acceptance checks after each interaction turn and credits progress according to changes in the environment state. On AppWorld, authors report the highest task-goal completion among tested methods at all three Qwen3.5 model sizes on both the normal and challenge test sets, including tasks using applications unseen during training. The largest margin over the strongest outcome-only baseline is 4.1 percentage points at 4B; gains narrow at 9B and 35B. A same-progress ablation attributes the reported benefit to assigning credit at the turn where progress occurs. Results average three author-run training runs and have not been independently reproduced here.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The preprint describes rerunning task acceptance checks after each turn, measuring the change in passed checks, and using that change for trajectory-level and turn-level reinforcement-learning credit; progress is read during training, not predicted by the policy.
  • Claimed — In AppWorld, authors report that ProCredit had the highest task-goal completion at 4B, 9B and 35B on both Test-Normal (168 tasks) and Test-Challenge (417 tasks, including applications unseen in training). It exceeded the strongest outcome-only baseline by 4.1 points at 4B Test-Normal and 3.9 points at 4B Test-Challenge; the margin narrowed to 1–3 points at larger model sizes.
  • Claimed — Against GRPO-Phi, which uses the same trajectory score but no turn-level progress credit, ProCredit had higher task-goal completion at all three model sizes on both AppWorld test sets. The paper reports improvement in ToolSandbox at all three sizes over GRPO.
  • Demonstrated limitation — ProCredit requires progress that can be computed from accessible intermediate environment state and rerunnable acceptance checks; its signal is limited by the number and granularity of checks. The paper reports narrower gains at larger model sizes.

Strongest baseline

Outcome-only methods include GRPO, RLOO, DAPO and SALT. GRPO-Phi uses the same trajectory score but only trajectory-level advantages; it isolates the additional turn-level credit. GiGPO uses similar progress rewards with a different default discount factor, which the paper analyzes separately.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported; this activation did not independently reproduce the training runs or benchmark tables.
  • Intermediate progress requires environment state access and acceptance checks that can be rerun after each turn; the method is not directly applicable when progress is not verifiable.
  • The current progress measure equally weights the available checks. Its resolution depends on the number and timing of checks; with few checks or progress only at the final step, turn-level differences can disappear.
  • The paper reports that gains narrow at larger model sizes and are related to how often outcome-only training encounters failed attempts with distinguishable progress.
  • Evidence covers three Qwen3.5 sizes in AppWorld and a selected ToolSandbox evaluation subset; broader model families, task types, costs and production behavior remain untested.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of ProCredit, GRPO-Phi or outcome-only comparisons across the AppWorld seeds has been completed.
  • Transfer to environments with different acceptance-check granularity, models beyond Qwen3.5, and matched end-to-end training costs remains unmeasured.

Why it matters

Long-horizon tool agents often receive a single reward after a task ends, leaving training unable to distinguish a failed attempt that made progress from one that did not. ProCredit uses existing executable checks on intermediate environment states to measure progress and assign additional credit to the turn that produced it. The paper reports improvements on AppWorld and a second tool environment, with three model scales and unseen applications in the challenge set. The method depends on intermediate state access and meaningful rerunnable checks, and its gains are author-reported.
