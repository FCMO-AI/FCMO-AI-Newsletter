Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The European Commission published proposal COM(2026) 502 for a Cloud and AI Development Act on 2026-06-03. The proposal combines data-centre deployment measures, an EU-wide cloud and AI sovereignty assessment framework and public-sector adoption/procurement mechanisms. The European Parliament's procedure file 2026/0138(COD) listed the proposal as awaiting committee decision when checked on 2026-11-02; the public Council register lists member-state comments dated 2026-10-23, but their contents were not accessible.
  • Claimed — The proposal aims to at least triple EU data-centre capacity over five to seven years. The Commission's 2026 impact assessment estimates that EU27 installed capacity would reach 12.4 GW in 2025 but remain almost 3 GW below estimated current demand; it attributes 65% of EU27 capacity to Germany, France, the Netherlands and Ireland. The Commission says these market estimates draw on a 2025 Technopolis Group study whose methodology excludes private enterprise sites, including most dedicated HPC facilities.
  • Not established — The documents do not establish the proposal's enactment, subsequent project investment, permitted or operational capacity, actual access to power, service-provider market shares, procurement outcomes, cost savings or environmental benefits. The impact assessment's market snapshot is not a complete census of private and dedicated compute facilities.

Strongest baseline

The impact assessment compares six policy options and selects a package combining PO1-B, PO2-C and selected PO1-C measures. It is an ex ante Commission assessment, not an observed comparison of post-implementation outcomes.

Limitations

  • CADA is presented by the Commission as a proposal, not an enacted or implemented regulation.
  • The Commission's impact assessment presents targets, projections and modeled expected effects; it does not demonstrate actual cost savings, investment, capacity, energy efficiency or sovereignty outcomes.
  • The commissioned capacity study's inventory focuses on data-centre operators, cloud providers and telecom sites. It excludes private enterprise facilities because consistent data were unavailable and its Data Centre Map dataset does not include HPC centres; its surveyed 100 companies covered 280 data centres but do not make the inventory exhaustive.
  • The French-language official page was surfaced but could not be opened because the server returned HTTP 429; the English official page and Commission impact assessment were directly inspected.

What we still don't know

  • The European Parliament procedure file currently lists COM(2026) 502 as awaiting committee decision; the adoption path and implementation timeline remain unresolved. The Council register lists member-state comments dated 2026-10-23, but their contents were not accessible in this review.
  • The Commission's 2025 infrastructure estimates rely on a commissioned study that uses a dataset of operator, cloud-provider and telecom sites and excludes private enterprise facilities; its Data Centre Map dataset also excludes HPC centres. It supplements the inventory with a survey of 100 companies covering 280 data centres, but is not a complete census of EU compute resources.

Why it matters

If enacted and implemented as proposed, CADA could affect data-centre investment, permitting, access to power, public procurement and how European cloud sovereignty is assessed. The Commission's supporting assessment also describes capacity concentration and rising colocation prices, but those estimates depend on a bounded external dataset and are not a complete census of EU compute capacity.
