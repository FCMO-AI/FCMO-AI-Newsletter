Google Research links agentic lab control with closed-loop molecular discovery

A Google Research overview dated October 16 links three papers on AI-assisted wet-lab work. VERID uses a persistent semantic digital twin, simulation gate and synchronized execution traces; its demonstrations use dye and water to expose physical lab errors and recover from faults. A separate aptamer study reports three design-test rounds on myoglobin and alpha-thrombin: new-design hit rates reached 33.5% and 42.1%, respectively, after intermediate specificity failures; strictly de novo thrombin binders remained low-micromolar. A FAP binder-design study combines target-region excision with agent-guided parameter and compute allocation. For one 719-residue target under a shared GPU budget, the human-agent arm reported a 9.33% computational hit rate using 77.18 GPU-hours versus 5.06% using 132.87 GPU-hours for the human baseline; the authors report four representative enriched yeast-display clones with apparent 3.0–9.1 nM affinities. All results are first-party paper reports without independent reproduction. They concern specific targets and workflows, not general autonomous science or demonstrated therapeutic efficacy.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Google published an overview on October 16, 2026 describing VERID, aptamer design-test cycles, and an agent-guided FAP binder-design workflow; the overview links three technical papers.
  • Claimed — The VERID paper reports a persistent semantic digital twin that tracks labware and reagent state, gates command blocks through simulation, and synchronizes state, video, telemetry and execution logs. Its three case studies used dye and deionized water.
  • Claimed — In three iterative design-test rounds, the aptamer paper reports new-design bead-corrected hit rates of 33.5% on myoglobin and 42.1% on alpha-thrombin in round three, after earlier failures including a myoglobin specificity collapse. Strictly de novo alpha-thrombin scaffolds showed low-micromolar binding; reported nanomolar values involved literature-derived motifs.
  • Claimed — In an equal-budget comparison on the FAP target, the paper reports 9.33% computational hit rate for a human-agent arm using 77.18 GPU-hours, compared with 5.06% for a human arm using 132.87 GPU-hours. The fully autonomous arm had lower hit rate than the hybrid arm but more hit-producing epitopes.
  • Claimed — In a pooled yeast-display validation campaign spanning four study arms, the authors report four representative enriched FAP binder clones with apparent dissociation constants of 3.0–9.1 nM. The paper does not attribute those four measurements specifically to the human-agent arm; soluble-expression characterization, domain specificity and inhibition assays remain future work.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The described evidence is from Google-authored papers; no independent reproduction was identified. VERID demonstrations are non-biological dye/water experiments; aptamer outcomes cover two targets and three rounds; the FAP autonomy comparison covers one large target, one binder class and one LLM controller.

Strongest baseline

The FAP study includes human-only, human-agent hybrid, fully autonomous agent and an ablated hybrid scoring variant under the same budget cap and hit definition. This is a bounded one-target comparison, not an independent or multi-target baseline.

Limitations

  • First-party Google reports and papers; no independent reproduction or independent assay audit was found in this review.
  • VERID’s proof-of-principle physical experiments use dye and deionized water, not biological discovery. Safety claims describe implemented gates and human supervision, not autonomous operation without oversight.
  • The aptamer paper covers myoglobin and alpha-thrombin in three cycles. It reports intermediate specificity failures and subsequent recovery; strictly de novo thrombin scaffolds remained low-micromolar, while nanomolar figures involved known literature-derived motifs.
  • The FAP computational autonomy comparison is limited to one large enzyme target, three-helix binder scaffolds and one LLM controller. The fully autonomous arm used about 94% of the budget and had lower hit rate than the hybrid arm, though it covered more hit-producing epitopes.
  • The yeast-display library contained 5,000 members (1,250 initial designs per each of four study arms) plus local sequence mutagenesis; 60 exact computational designs appeared among enriched sequences, and four representative clones were measured for apparent affinity. The paper does not attribute these four measurements specifically to the human-agent arm. These assays do not establish soluble-protein behavior, target-domain specificity, inhibition, therapeutic activity or clinical utility.
  • For the aptamer study, the paper and supplement include selected outcomes, representative sequences and curated agent traces, but complete de-identified sequence-activity and differential-enrichment tables are deferred by the authors until peer-reviewed publication. The targeted review did not establish artifact availability for VERID/FAP elsewhere.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the FAP fixed-budget comparison, including protocol, intermediate computational hits, assay selection denominators and cost accounting.
  • Orthogonal soluble-expression and kinetic binding measurements for the selected FAP clones, along with domain specificity and FAP inhibition assays.
  • Test whether VERID-style persistent physical-state tracking and closed-loop strategy transfer to additional labs, instruments, biological targets and independent operators.
  • Independent confirmation that aptamer specificity/hit-rate gains hold on additional targets and against broader assay controls; keep motif-derived and strictly de novo outcomes separate.
  • Complete de-identified aptamer sequence-activity and differential-enrichment tables are not yet included; authors state these will be made available upon peer-reviewed publication. Independent reproduction of the published rounds and assay results remains open.

Why it matters

The reports connect agentic design to physical experimental feedback and show two distinct proposed levers: persistent physical-state tracking for safer execution and target-local computation plus agent tactical search for more efficient protein design. If independently reproduced and extended, these mechanisms could affect AI-enabled lab automation and binder-design economics. Current evidence is narrow, from one lab and several target-specific campaigns; the strongest affinity results concern selected clones in yeast display, while soluble characterization and functional inhibition remain future work.
