Source-aligned agent-memory units improve complete-evidence retrieval in a synthetic trace benchmark

A paper introduces Execution Provenance Graph Memory (EPGM) for retrieving complete supporting evidence from long agent execution histories under a fixed token budget. On 2,000 queries over 1,207 held-out ISETrace trajectories, the authors report that source-aligned provenance units improve Full Support@2048 by 19.07 points over flat 512-token windows and by 11.96 points over a per-metric oracle across four flat chunk sizes, with matched Dense-FT scoring. Holding candidate units and seed scores fixed, a graph-conditioned residual adds a smaller 4.55 points (95% CI 2.98–6.18), concentrated on queries whose evidence spans multiple events. The corpus is synthetic and the results are author-reported; no independent reproduction was performed.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors define an execution-provenance retrieval method and evaluate it on ISETrace, a synthetic corpus of agent/tool trajectories; the paper reports a human audit of 500 test queries.
  • Claimed — On 2,000 test queries over 1,207 held-out trajectories, matched Dense-FT scoring with source-aligned provenance units improves Full Support@2048 by 19.07 points over flat 512-token windows and 11.96 points over a per-metric oracle across four flat chunk sizes.
  • Claimed — With candidate units and seed scores fixed, the graph-conditioned residual adds 4.55 Full Support@2048 points (95% CI [2.98, 6.18]); the paper reports that the benefit is concentrated when evidence spans multiple events.
  • Demonstrated — The authors state that ISETrace is synthetic and that exact trajectory disjointness does not ensure zero overlap in normalized intent text; they do not claim that observable graph links establish causal dependency.

Strongest baseline

Matched Dense-FT scoring on flat 512-token windows, plus an oracle selecting the best of four flat chunk sizes per metric; for graph contribution, exact seed-score passthrough with the same provenance-unit candidates. These comparisons separate candidate construction from the learned graph residual.

Limitations

  • All quantitative findings are reported by the paper authors; this activation did not run the linked code or independently reproduce the benchmark.
  • ISETrace is a synthetic execution-grounded corpus, so the measured effects may not transfer to real, diverse agent histories, tools or memory queries.
  • Queries are model-authored and only a stratified subset of 500 receives human validation; the audit does not establish error-free labels for the full test set.
  • Trajectory-disjoint splits control exact trajectory overlap but do not guarantee zero overlap in normalized intent text.
  • The observed provenance graph records execution structure and lexical reuse; it does not establish semantic or causal dependency between events.
  • The reported full-support metric evaluates retrieval of annotated spans, not reliable downstream task completion or answer correctness outside a limited transfer analysis.

What we still don't know

  • The reported candidate-view and graph-residual gains have not been independently reproduced.
  • The benchmark uses synthetic trajectories; performance on naturally occurring agent histories and real downstream tasks is unknown.

Why it matters

If the reported result generalizes, representing tool arguments and outputs as source-aligned units may help agent memory return complete evidence within a context budget, while the matched controls suggest most measured improvement comes from candidate construction and a narrower portion from graph propagation. This could change memory-system evaluation and design, but the experiment uses synthetic execution traces and does not establish reliable downstream performance on real agents.
