LLM Inference in a Flash reports compressed KV caching and modeled Compute-in-Flash gains

The paper combines integer-only compute-in-flash inference with a static flash-resident dictionary that stores compressed KV-cache codes. On LongBench, the authors report that the combined pipeline retains 99% of the FP16 full-KV baseline score across Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B, with less than one average score-point degradation and nearly 15× dynamic KV traffic compression. Separately, an analytical model for Llama-3.1-8B at batch size 1 projects 3.1× lower decode latency and 2.7× lower energy at 1K context, and 4.4×/6.8× at 256K, against an NPU+DDR5 full-KV baseline. These system-level numbers are modeled, not measurements on physical Compute-in-Flash hardware; prefill is outside the study.

The paper combines integer-only compute-in-flash inference with a static flash-resident dictionary that stores compressed KV-cache codes. On LongBench, the authors report that the combined pipeline retains 99% of the FP16 full-KV baseline score across Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B, with less than one average score-point degradation and nearly 15× dynamic KV traffic compression. These system-level numbers are modeled, not measurements on physical Compute-in-Flash hardware; prefill is outside the study.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — On LongBench, the authors report that their combined integer-only quantization and dictionary KV compression retains 99% of the FP16 full-KV baseline score for Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B, with less than one average score-point degradation and nearly 15× dynamic KV-cache compression.
  • CLAIMED — For Llama-3.1-8B at batch size 1, the paper's analytical system model projects 3.1× lower decode latency and 2.7× lower energy at 1K context, and 4.4× lower latency and 6.8× lower energy at 256K, for configuration S2 relative to an NPU+DDR5 full-KV baseline. These are modeled values, not physical-hardware measurements.

Strongest baseline

LongBench compares the combined method to FP16 with full KV cache. System model configuration S2 is compared to B1, an NPU+DDR5 DRAM FP16 baseline with full KV cache moved each decode step; B2 and S1 are additional modeled ablations.

The caveat that matters

  • LongBench and KV compression results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced.
  • Latency and energy improvements are analytical projections, not measurements on physical Compute-in-Flash hardware.
  • The system-level model covers decode only; mapping prefill efficiently is explicitly left as future work.
  • The model assumes two copies of the value dictionary, one transposed and one non-transposed, to support pursuit and attention.
  • System-level projections focus on Llama-3.1-8B, batch size 1 and modeled 1K/64K/256K context lengths; transfer to other models, hardware, concurrency and service loads is unknown.

What remains unknown

  • LongBench accuracy and KV traffic compression have not been independently reproduced.
  • The projected latency/energy savings have not been validated on physical Compute-in-Flash hardware, and prefill is not modeled.

FCMO Lens

Long-context decoding can be limited by moving KV state between memory and compute. The paper reports a large reduction in dynamic KV traffic with small LongBench score changes, then models potential decode benefits on a Compute-in-Flash design. The modeled gains depend on assumptions about device bandwidth, dictionary placement and duplicate dictionary storage, and do not establish physical-device performance or prefill efficiency.
