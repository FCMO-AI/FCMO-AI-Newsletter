Complex KDA extends Kimi Delta Attention state-transition expressivity

Complex KDA (CKDA) combines two parameter-range extensions—signed channel gates and a delta coefficient up to 2—to make Kimi Delta Attention express 2D rotations using a single diagonal-plus-rank-one recurrent transition. The paper proves exact representability for orthogonal diagonal-plus-rank-one matrices and reports finite-group state-tracking, synthetic periodic-audio and language-model experiments. The authors report stronger length extrapolation on selected synthetic tasks and language-model results comparable to KDA, not a broad demonstrated language-model advantage. Open code and committed measurements aid inspection; no independent proof audit or training reproduction was conducted.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper extends Kimi Delta Attention’s gate range to [-1,1] and delta coefficient to [0,2], allowing a single delta-rule update plus a channel-wise gate reflection to represent 2D rotations while retaining a diagonal-plus-rank-one transition.
  • Claimed — The authors prove that every orthogonal diagonal-plus-rank-one matrix is exactly representable as a Complex KDA transition, and report finite-group state-tracking and periodic-waveform experiments with stronger length extrapolation than the tested KDA range settings.
  • Claimed — In language-model experiments, the authors report results comparable to a KDA baseline and promising scaling behavior; the public repository includes committed experiment tables and reproduction code, but this review did not rerun the training.

Strongest baseline

KDA range settings and DeltaProduct2-style composed transitions in the paper’s matched expressivity/state-tracking comparisons; KDA is the direct language-model baseline.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are author-reported; this review did not independently reproduce them or audit the proof.
  • The finite-group tasks use one recurrent layer and bounded sequence-length extrapolation; periodic audio is a synthetic fixed-groove task rather than general audio generation.
  • The paper reports language-model results comparable to its KDA baseline and only describes scaling behavior as promising; it does not establish a broad downstream advantage.
  • The public repository archives measurements and provides reproduction drivers, but rerunning large language-model training requires external compute.
  • The authors explicitly report that CKDA language-model results are similar to the KDA baseline, which limits any claim of current language-model performance gains.
  • The audio task is intentionally restricted to extrapolating circular shifts of one synthetic groove; the repository notes that a GRU remains strongest on that task.

What we still don't know

  • No independent review of the exact representability proof or reproduction of the reported finite-group, audio or language-model results was performed.
  • Practical effects on quality, long-context behavior and compute at larger language-model scales are not established by the bounded paper experiments.

Why it matters

The result identifies a way to expand the state transformations available to a linear recurrent layer without increasing its transition rank, which could change memory and recurrence design choices. The strongest support is a mathematical paper claim with open experiment artifacts; practical gains beyond the tested tasks and language-model setup remain unverified.
