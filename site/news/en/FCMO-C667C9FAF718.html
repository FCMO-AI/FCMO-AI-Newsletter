Controlled experiments report answer-prior bias across eight dense retrievers

A Nov 8 arXiv paper reports that, across eight dense retrievers, a model's preference for a canonical answer predicts higher retrieval scores for passages containing that answer. In controlled LAMA/TREx template contrasts, this relationship also appears when the answer-matching passage expresses the wrong relation. The authors report positive correlations in both settings; their selected examples are single-token cases where the diagnostic already favors the canonical object. The study does not measure prevalence in natural corpora or downstream RAG outcomes.

The study does not measure prevalence in natural corpora or downstream RAG outcomes.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report positive correlations between their answer-knowledge diagnostic and retrieval-score differences across eight dense retrievers in both object-contrast and relation–object-conflict template settings.
  • Claimed — In the relation–object-conflict setting, the authors report that passages containing the canonical answer but expressing a different relation can receive higher retrieval scores than passages matching the relation but containing an alternative object.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish how often answer-prior bias occurs in natural corpora, its prevalence across ordinary factual queries, or its effect on downstream RAG task outcomes.

Strongest baseline

Within-pair contrasts hold query and relation fixed while changing answer object; relation–object-conflict contrasts compare an answer-matching wrong-relation passage against a relation-matching alternative-answer passage.

Limitations

  • The passages are template-generated and do not capture the length, noise, entity interactions, or multiple relations of natural corpora.
  • Examples are limited to single-token answer pairs for which the diagnostic favors the canonical answer, then sampled conditionally; rates are not population estimates.
  • The alternative object in the main LAMA/TREx contrast is type-compatible but not asserted to be a true alternative fact for the subject; multiple valid answers are tested separately on QAMPARI.
  • No independent reproduction or downstream RAG outcome evaluation was performed here.

What we still don't know

  • The reported correlations and retrieval preferences have not been independently recomputed in this activation.
  • The paper does not estimate prevalence or magnitude on natural passages, multi-token answers, temporal questions or production RAG tasks.

Why it matters

If the reported effect transfers beyond controlled passages, dense retrieval may favor familiar or stale answers over evidence that matches the requested relation, which matters for evidence selection in RAG. The present results justify testing this failure mode, not assuming it occurs at the same rate in natural retrieval.
