TopKLoRA paper reports sparse causal circuits for removing known-trigger backdoors

A new preprint presents TopKLoRA, a sparse fine-tuning adapter, with a Contrastive Latent Circuit Discovery pipeline for locating behaviors introduced during fine-tuning. In synthetic sleeper-backdoor model organisms up to 7B parameters, the authors report that ablating a selected circuit removes nearly all held-out responses to a known syntactic trigger, while multi-layer adapters retain benign instruction-following. The study compares against dense LoRA and reports smaller, more specific circuits. These are author-reported results in a controlled setup; unknown or semantic triggers, larger models, and independent reproduction remain open.

What changed

A new preprint presents TopKLoRA, a sparse fine-tuning adapter, with a Contrastive Latent Circuit Discovery pipeline for locating behaviors introduced during fine-tuning. In synthetic sleeper-backdoor model organisms up to 7B parameters, the authors report that ablating a selected circuit removes nearly all held-out responses to a known syntactic trigger, while multi-layer adapters retain benign instruction-following. The study compares against dense LoRA and reports smaller, more specific circuits. These are author-reported results in a controlled setup; unknown or semantic triggers, larger models, and independent reproduction remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report TopKLoRA and their Contrastive Latent Circuit Discovery (CLCD) pipeline identify and ablate necessary-and-sufficient circuits for a synthetically implanted backdoor with a known syntactic trigger in Gemma-2-2B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B model organisms. In their setup, ablation removed at least 99.9% of held-out triggered responses in each organism, while multi-layer adapters retained instruction-following close to clean twins.
  • Claimed — In the authors' matched-placement, rank-matched study, TopKLoRA circuits were smaller than dense-LoRA circuits across the reported 30 seed-matched pairs; their reported minimum circuit fraction was 1.2–6.8% of the TopKLoRA latent pool for eliminating the backdoor, compared with 20.6–38.9% for dense LoRA.

Strongest baseline

Rank-matched dense LoRA adapters at corresponding placement and training data; the paper also uses random latent-order and random-ablation controls.

Limitations

  • All performance and causal-ablation results are author-reported in an arXiv preprint; independent reproduction was not performed.
  • Experiments use synthetic backdoors with explicit known syntactic triggers and contrastive trigger/control prompt pairs, unlike many realistic unknown-trigger cases.
  • The largest reported base model is 7B; generalization to larger or deployed systems is not established.
  • The paper does not interpret individual latents and acknowledges that multiple verified circuits may exist.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the authors' TopKLoRA/CLCD results was found or performed in this activation.
  • The paper's circuit search requires knowledge of the trigger and contrastive eliciting/control prompts; it does not test unknown or semantic triggers.
  • The reported base models reach 7B parameters and are controlled model organisms; transfer to larger or deployed models is not measured.
  • Different discovery orders can yield multiple verified circuits; the paper reports that circuits are minimal under a search grid rather than unique mechanisms.

Why it matters

If reproduced beyond this controlled setting, the method could provide a more targeted way to audit and remove certain fine-tuning backdoors without broadly degrading benign task behavior. The result concerns synthetic known-trigger model organisms and does not establish protection against real-world or unknown-trigger backdoors.
