A controlled study reports reduced diversity after training successive paper-review models on synthetic reviews

Ho, Liu and Huang train a Llama 3.1 8B reviewer on ICLR 2018–2023 reviews, use it to generate reviews for ICLR 2024 papers, then train four successors on mixtures containing 0%, 33%, 66% or 100% explicitly synthetic reviews. With other training settings held fixed, their paper reports that synthetic exposure compressed rating distributions and monotonically reduced same-paper and corpus-level semantic diversity in the tested one-step setup. This is a controlled author-reported experiment, not a measurement of real-world AI assistance in peer review: the official reviews may themselves include unobserved AI assistance, and the paper does not test whether the effect compounds over further generations.

What actually changed

Ho, Liu and Huang train a Llama 3.1 8B reviewer on ICLR 2018–2023 reviews, use it to generate reviews for ICLR 2024 papers, then train four successors on mixtures containing 0%, 33%, 66% or 100% explicitly synthetic reviews. With other training settings held fixed, their paper reports that synthetic exposure compressed rating distributions and monotonically reduced same-paper and corpus-level semantic diversity in the tested one-step setup. This is a controlled author-reported experiment, not a measurement of real-world AI assistance in peer review: the official reviews may themselves include unobserved AI assistance, and the paper does not test whether the effect compounds over further generations.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that, after one recursive training step, increasing explicit synthetic-review exposure across 0%, 33%, 66% and 100% settings reduced rating-distribution spread and semantic diversity relative to the official-review-only training baseline.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The authors state that this is a controlled synthetic-exposure experiment and not a measurement of naturally occurring AI assistance in peer review; they study one recursive step and leave compounding across later generations open.

Strongest baseline

Successor reviewers trained only on official reviews (0% explicitly synthetic exposure) provide the controlled baseline; initialization, filtering, and optimization are held constant across mixture conditions.

The caveat that matters

  • All empirical outcomes are reported in one arXiv preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The experiment varies explicitly generated review proportions in ICLR data; it does not estimate real-world AI-assisted review prevalence.
  • The official review corpus is labeled official rather than human-written because reviews may contain unobserved AI assistance.
  • Only one recursive training step is examined; further compounding or stabilization is unknown.
  • The semantic-diversity measures and rating distributions are proxies for judgment variation and do not establish review quality, fairness or decision accuracy.
  • The TrustReviewer interventions are author-proposed mitigation methods; their efficacy and external validity were not independently verified here.

What remains unknown

  • The experiment does not measure how much AI-generated or AI-edited review text actually enters scientific training corpora or whether real review pipelines show the same effect.
  • The study tests one recursive training step; cumulative effects over repeated generations are unknown.

FCMO Lens

If AI-generated reviews enter later reviewer-training corpora, they could narrow the judgments learned by successor models. This paper reports a controlled one-step test of that pathway and finds lower rating and semantic diversity as explicitly synthetic review exposure rises. The result identifies a risk worth measuring in AI-assisted evaluation, while leaving natural prevalence, the effect of multiple generations and performance of the proposed mitigation methods open.
