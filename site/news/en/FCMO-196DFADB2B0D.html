What changed

Li Auto’s ME-Brain technical report describes an embodied system combining an external multimodal memory, a cognitive module and an action model, and reports comparisons on RoboMME, RoboDojo and six physical manipulation tasks. These are author-reported results: the real-robot evaluation used a Piper dual-arm platform and 10 trials per task. No independent reproduction, released artifact or broad deployment result is established here.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors describe ME-Brain as a three-part embodied system with Evolvable Memory, Cognitive Core and Action Model, intended to reuse multimodal task experience across executions without updating model weights.
  • Claimed — On RoboMME, the report gives ME-Brain a 47.88% mean success rate, compared with 44.62% for its FrameSampling + Modulation comparator and 41.75% for MemER; on the reported RoboDojo leaderboard comparison, it gives ME-Brain a 21.51 mean score and 16.03% success rate.
  • Claimed — For six ME-RealBench manipulation tasks on a Piper dual-arm robot, the authors report mean success of 66.7% and mean score of 69.5, based on 10 trials per task.
  • Not established — The report does not establish independent reproduction, generalization beyond the stated benchmarks/tasks, robust deployment across sites, or the isolated causal contribution of each system module.

Strongest baseline

Reported comparisons include FrameSampling + Modulation and MemER on RoboMME, leaderboard models on RoboDojo, and DM0.5 and pi_0.5 on ME-RealBench. Baseline recipe parity and independent validation were not assessed.

Limitations

  • All performance values are reported by the paper authors and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • The real-robot evaluation is limited to six tasks and 10 trials per task on a Piper dual-arm robot.
  • No public code, checkpoint, independent evaluation or broad commercial deployment was verified in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported comparisons or module ablations was checked.
  • The physical evaluation covers six tasks on one Piper dual-arm setup at 10 trials per task; cross-site and broader deployment robustness are not established.

Why it matters

The report offers a concrete systems approach to carrying task experience across robot actions and reports gains on memory-oriented and manipulation benchmarks. If reproduced, that could matter for long-horizon physical tasks; the current evidence is a single vendor report with limited trials and no verified independent replication.
