Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v1 preprint exists and defines a contrastive evidence-gap score based on how a model's token probabilities change between question-relevant and irrelevant image crops; it separately tests answer-peakedness to select a crop at inference.
  • Claimed — Across three Qwen vision-language models on one V*Bench evaluation, the authors report that selecting from 16 candidate crops localized answer regions 4.4–5.1 times above chance and raised direct-attribute answer accuracy from about 70% to about 85%. The result is author-reported, unreproduced and uses one 191-example benchmark.
  • Claimed — The authors report that combining evidence-gap and model-confidence signals improved correctness prediction, with AUC up to 0.99, and that the evidence signal identified confidently wrong answers in a high-confidence subset. The results apply to the tested benchmark and split.
  • Claimed — On an HR-Bench pilot training comparison using Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA and 300 steps per arm, the authors report that ungated on-policy distillation and two gated variants all underperformed the base model; the per-token gate performed worst. This negative training result does not negate the separate inference-time crop-selection result.

Strongest baseline

For crop selection, the authors compare full-image, center, random, anti-selected, peakedness-selected and oracle-box conditions. For training, the base model and ungated OPD are compared with trajectory- and token-gated variants.

Limitations

  • One unreproduced preprint and one 191-example V*Bench evaluation; models are three Qwen vision-language variants, not a broad family sample.
  • The crop-discovery method uses 16 candidate crops and therefore 16 forward passes per example; end-to-end latency and cost advantages were not established.
  • Reported accuracy uses multiple-choice answer letters at a fixed maximum image size; broader grounding metrics such as intersection-over-union and tests across additional datasets are needed.
  • The training-time negative result is a pilot with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LoRA and 300 steps per arm; it does not establish that all evidence-gated training approaches fail.
  • The paper describes one selectivity curve as an illustrative reconstruction that should be replotted from the underlying data; it is not treated here as validated quantitative evidence.

What we still don't know

  • No independent replication on V*Bench or a disjoint visual-grounding benchmark was found.
  • Reported region discovery requires a 16-crop grid/16 forward passes per example, but the paper does not compare against efficient crop-search and attention/Grad-CAM baselines across multiple datasets.
  • The selectivity plot is described in the source as an illustrative reconstruction whose underlying data should be used to replot it; detailed validation of that figure is missing.

Why it matters

A cheap signal that identifies whether an answer changes when the relevant visual evidence is shown could help detect perception failures and guide inference-time crop selection without answer labels. The tested gains are preliminary and compute-bearing, and the paper's pilot training method did not improve over its base model.
