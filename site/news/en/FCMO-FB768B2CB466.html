A study finds BF16 and FP16 can produce different greedy LLM outputs

A September 2026 TMLR paper reports that BF16 versus FP16 inference produced different greedy-decoding sequences on 49–100% of prompts across six tested models and three benchmarks. The authors report that recomputing only the language-model output head in FP32 when the top-two logit margin is small improved exact agreement by 22–36 percentage points on an A10G and 12–21 points on L4/A100 GPUs, with under 4% latency overhead at batch sizes up to 4. They report that the intervention’s benefit disappears at batch size 8 or higher and under end-to-end FP8. These are author-reported results, not an independent reproduction.

What changed

A September 2026 TMLR paper reports that BF16 versus FP16 inference produced different greedy-decoding sequences on 49–100% of prompts across six tested models and three benchmarks. The authors report that recomputing only the language-model output head in FP32 when the top-two logit margin is small improved exact agreement by 22–36 percentage points on an A10G and 12–21 points on L4/A100 GPUs, with under 4% latency overhead at batch sizes up to 4. They report that the intervention’s benefit disappears at batch size 8 or higher and under end-to-end FP8. These are author-reported results, not an independent reproduction.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The TMLR paper was published in September 2026 and is available through its arXiv record and PDF.
  • Claimed — The authors report BF16/FP16 greedy-decoding sequences diverged on 49–100% of prompts across six 1.1B–7B models, four model families and three benchmarks; a separate 12.2B test characterized divergence.
  • Claimed — The authors report that selective FP32 LM-head recomputation at low top-two margins improved exact sequence agreement by 22–36 percentage points on A10G and 12–21 points on L4/A100 with less than 4% latency overhead at batch size <=4.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the intervention’s benefit vanishes at batch size >=8 and under end-to-end FP8; it is not a universal determinism guarantee.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the intervention against unmodified inference and broader-scope FP32 recomputation. The authors report broader FP32 scope can reduce agreement; no external reproduction was located.

Limitations

  • All numerical performance findings are the paper authors’ reports and were not independently reproduced.
  • Divergence and repair effectiveness vary by model, GPU, batch size and precision regime; the intervention is not universal.
  • The low-overhead repair applies only to low-batch single-stream settings in the paper; benefit disappears at batch size >=8 and under tested end-to-end FP8.
  • The paper studies selected 1.1B–7B models and benchmarks, with a separate 12.2B divergence characterization; it does not establish prevalence for closed models, production workloads or all numerical formats.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the cross-precision divergence rates or selective LM-head intervention was performed.
  • The paper does not establish how often this issue occurs in production serving configurations or on larger/closed models.

Why it matters

If the reported regime-specific effect generalizes, serving precision can change exact model behavior even under greedy decoding, complicating cross-system replay and evaluation. The paper also offers a targeted but explicitly partial mitigation; its bounded workload and batch limits determine where the result applies.
