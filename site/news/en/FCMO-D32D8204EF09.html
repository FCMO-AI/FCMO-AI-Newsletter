OpenAI expands GPT-6 globally in ChatGPT with model-generated interactive interfaces

OpenAI announced that GPT-6 with Intelligent UI is rolling out globally in ChatGPT, first to paid plans on October 7 and then to Free and Go on October 8. The company says GPT-6 can compose answers from text, visuals and interactive components such as charts, buttons and forms, and can progressively stream these interfaces. This ChatGPT update does not change the models used in Codex or ChatGPT Work. OpenAI's October system card reports stronger performance on some evaluations and statistically significant regressions on selected self-harm and teen-safety tests; these are provider-run results, not independent evaluations.

What actually changed

OpenAI announced that GPT-6 with Intelligent UI is rolling out globally in ChatGPT, first to paid plans on October 7 and then to Free and Go on October 8. The company says GPT-6 can compose answers from text, visuals and interactive components such as charts, buttons and forms, and can progressively stream these interfaces. This ChatGPT update does not change the models used in Codex or ChatGPT Work. OpenAI's October system card reports stronger performance on some evaluations and statistically significant regressions on selected self-harm and teen-safety tests; these are provider-run results, not independent evaluations.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — OpenAI says GPT-6 with Intelligent UI began rolling out globally to Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise ChatGPT tiers on October 7, with Free and Go beginning October 8; enterprise access depends on administrator settings. The announcement says Codex and ChatGPT Work remain on prior released models.
  • CLAIMED — OpenAI describes Intelligent UI as allowing GPT-6 to compose answers from text, visuals and interactive components, including graphics, buttons, forms, charts and task-specific experiences, with interfaces progressively rendered as the model generates them.
  • CLAIMED — OpenAI's October system card reports statistically significant regressions for GPT-6 Sol on standard self-harm evaluations and for GPT-6 Luna on standard self-harm, gore and sexual-content evaluations versus their GPT-5.6 counterparts. It also reports regressions on selected under-18 evaluations; the company says additional classifier-based blocks are not reflected in those scores.
  • CLAIMED — The system card classifies both October GPT-6 Sol and Luna as High capability but below Critical in cybersecurity and biological/chemical domains, and below the High threshold in AI self-improvement under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework. These are OpenAI's internal assessments and thresholds.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI compares October GPT-6 with GPT-5.6 on internal timing, task and safety evaluations. The product announcement reports GPT-6 Instant begins web-search answers 44% sooner on average than GPT-5.6 Instant; the system card includes selected safety regressions and improvements. These are provider-run comparisons with evolving evaluations, not independent matched results.

The caveat that matters

  • The rollout, interface functionality, timing comparisons and safety findings are disclosed by OpenAI; the research desk did not independently test them or measure user adoption.
  • The system card's challenging production-style evaluations are intentionally selected and not estimates of average production behavior; most underlying-model safety results omit system-level safeguards.
  • The system card reports statistically significant regressions in selected self-harm, gore, sexual-content and under-18 evaluations alongside improvements elsewhere. It says added classifier-based teen safeguards are not captured in the scores.
  • The October GPT-6 ChatGPT models are distinct from the September GPT-6 Sol/Luna versions still used in Codex and ChatGPT Work; do not transfer evaluation or availability statements across these model snapshots.
  • The release describes component generation and progressive rendering but provides no independent benchmark of interface correctness, accessibility, reliability or comparative user outcomes.

What remains unknown

  • No independent evaluation of generated interfaces, task completion, correctness, accessibility or comparative user outcomes was performed.
  • System-card safety and capability evaluations are OpenAI-run, and their external validity and deployment-level rates are not established by this review.
  • The global rollout schedule is announced, but no independent reach, availability or adoption denominator was checked.

FCMO Lens

A globally deployed assistant that generates task-specific interfaces could change how users work with software, while the system card shows that model updates can improve some safety measures and regress on others. The announcement establishes what OpenAI says it deployed and the stated rollout schedule; it does not independently establish interface quality, user benefit, adoption or real-world safety rates.
