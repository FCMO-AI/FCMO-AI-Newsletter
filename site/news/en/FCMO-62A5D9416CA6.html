Story · Agents and memory

A paper introduces a five-platform benchmark for hybrid computer-use agents and reports a large gap between aggregate scores and full behavioral tests

The authors introduce RecreationWorld, a framework for training and evaluating agents that alternate between exploring a running application through its interface, implementing a recreation in code, and executing it for feedback across Ubuntu, macOS, Windows, Android and Web. Their held-out RecreationBench has 250 tasks and reference-grounded hidden tests. In the authors’ evaluation of ten models, GPT-6 Astra scored 58.06% overall, but only 2.8% of tasks had a full programmatic pass; the paper reports that interface structure was reproduced more reliably than interactions and computed outputs. These are author-reported results from a new benchmark, not an independent capability comparison or a measure of general computer-use ability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors present a framework that combines GUI exploration, coding and execution/visual verification in reproducible Ubuntu, macOS, Windows, Android and Web environments.
  • Claimed — On the authors’ 250-task RecreationBench, they report GPT-6 Astra at 58.06% overall, ahead of Claude Opus 5 at 44.16%; only 2.8% of tasks had full programmatic passes for GPT-6 Astra.
  • Claimed — The authors report that agents reproduce static interface structure more reliably than interactions and computed outputs on their benchmark.

Strongest baseline

The reported model comparison includes ten frontier systems; GPT-6 Astra is compared with Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol under the paper’s benchmark configurations. This is not an independently matched evaluation across all inference stacks.

Limitations

  • Results are reported by the paper authors and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • RecreationBench comprises 250 reference-recreation tasks; its representativeness of real computer-use work and transfer to other task formulations remain uncertain.
  • The overall score is not equivalent to full task correctness; 2.8% full programmatic pass coverage for GPT-6 Astra is the paper’s reported value under its scoring protocol.
  • The benchmark compares the listed frontier models under paper-specific inference configurations; it does not establish general ranking across workloads, prompts, models or harnesses.
  • The reported association between broader exploration and stronger scores is descriptive and does not establish that exploration caused the performance difference.

What we still don't know

  • No independent team has rerun RecreationBench or checked whether its hidden tests and scores generalize to other computer-use tasks.
  • Transfer beyond reference-recreation tasks, the five supported platforms and the paper’s model/harness configurations is not established.

Why it matters

Computer-use agents often need to combine interface exploration with code changes and then verify the running result. RecreationWorld offers a way to train that loop and score behavior against an executable reference. Its reported results also show why an aggregate score can overstate full behavioral fidelity: the leading model’s score was 58.06%, while full programmatic passes covered 2.8% of tasks in this benchmark. Independent replication and transfer beyond the authors’ task set remain open.
