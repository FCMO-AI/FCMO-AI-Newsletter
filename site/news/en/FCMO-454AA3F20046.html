ProgramDistill reports a reference-guided benchmark where coding-agent success falls as web-app restoration depth grows

The arXiv v1 paper introduces ProgramDistill, a benchmark built from 26 interactive web applications. Its automated pipeline reports 1,975 replay-verified behaviors and 4,063 repair tasks. On the paper’s nine-agent partial-application evaluation, success on two leading agents falls as restoration depth grows from 1 to 8: GPT-6 Astra from 100% to 64.0%, and Claude Opus 5 from 96% to 32%. The authors report that this decline persists among runs with similar prompt lengths. These are author-run benchmark results; the benchmark was not independently reproduced and the authors say its public release is still in preparation.

What actually changed

The arXiv v1 paper introduces ProgramDistill, a benchmark built from 26 interactive web applications. Its automated pipeline reports 1,975 replay-verified behaviors and 4,063 repair tasks. On the paper’s nine-agent partial-application evaluation, success on two leading agents falls as restoration depth grows from 1 to 8: GPT-6 Astra from 100% to 64.0%, and Claude Opus 5 from 96% to 32%. The authors report that this decline persists among runs with similar prompt lengths. These are author-run benchmark results; the benchmark was not independently reproduced and the authors say its public release is still in preparation.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors introduce ProgramDistill, which derives replayable software-engineering tasks from interactive reference applications by mining behaviors, masking their implementations and measuring whether agents restore them.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that mine–craft–patch generated 1,975 replay-verified behaviors and 4,063 tasks across 26 web applications; they state that the public benchmark release is still in preparation.
  • CLAIMED — In partial-application reconstruction, the paper reports GPT-6 Astra success declining from 100% at restoration depth 1 to 64.0% at depth 8, and Claude Opus 5 declining from 96% to 32%. The authors report that a similar decline persists within comparable prompt-length bands.
  • CLAIMED — On full-application reconstruction, the authors report mean cumulative-workflow recovery of 49.15% for GPT-6 Astra, 28.81% for Claude Opus 5, and 21.07% for GPT-5.6 Sol across their 12-app evaluation.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction or that the benchmark results transfer to other application classes, production coding tasks, or different agent harnesses.

Strongest baseline

Within benchmark tasks, agents are compared using shared replay-verified behaviors and reference/current application setup. No independent benchmark or matched alternate-harness replication was inspected.

The caveat that matters

  • Single arXiv v1 preprint; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • The task corpus contains 26 interactive web applications, including OSWorld-derived applications and open-source projects or SaaS clones; this is not a broad sample of all software engineering.
  • The benchmark pipeline uses GPT-5.6 Sol for task construction; construction-model choices may shape the task distribution.
  • The paper reports nine agent configurations and selected harness/settings; effects may change with tools, observation budgets or model versions.
  • The authors state that the public benchmark release is still in preparation.
  • Success is replay-based behavioral recovery; it does not directly measure code quality, maintainability, user value or production safety.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of benchmark construction or model evaluations was conducted.
  • The paper reports results on 26 web applications and selected agent harnesses; transfer to other software domains and production workflows is unknown.
  • The paper compares selected frontier coding agents but does not establish that the restoration-depth effect is invariant to agent harness and observation-budget choices.

FCMO Lens

The benchmark tests a coding-agent demand that issue-based tasks often omit: infer behavior from a working application, then restore it in a partial implementation. If its replay verification and depth controls generalize, it could help distinguish agents’ information gathering and multi-step repair ability. The evidence is confined to the paper’s web applications and experimental harness.
