What changed

A Luma-affiliated arXiv preprint introduces ZAP, which starts from a 27B vision-language model and teaches robot motion through supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning entirely in simulation. The authors report 96.7% success over 30 real-robot trials across 10 tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in their harness, with lower average time and cost; the same checkpoint also reports transfer from a bimanual robot to a single-arm Franka. These results are author-reported measurements from a bounded evaluation and have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — A version 1 preprint introducing Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP) was submitted to arXiv on October 26, 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report that ZAP, a Qwen 3.6 27B vision-language model taught motion using simulation-only supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on 40 tasks, succeeded on 96.7% of 30 real-robot trials across 10 tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in the same harness; reported mean task cost and time were USD 0.29 and 146 seconds for ZAP versus USD 4.87 and 397 seconds for Astra.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the same checkpoint trained on a bimanual YAM transferred without further training to a single-arm Franka and reached 55.9% on RoboLab-120, compared with 51.0% for Lakeside and 42.9% for FLUX 3 Action.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, broad transfer beyond the tested tasks and embodiments, robust performance under distribution shift, deployment reliability, or safety for extended real-world operation.

Strongest baseline

GPT-6 Astra at maximum reasoning in the same authors' harness reports 96.7% across 30 trials; MolmoAct2 reports 33.3%. The same GPT-6 Astra reaches 36.7% in a different Robocurve harness, so results are sensitive to interface and evaluation setup. On single-arm Franka RoboLab-120, the authors compare against leaderboard figures for Lakeside (51.0%) and FLUX 3 Action (42.9%).

Limitations

  • All performance, cost and transfer results are from the authors' preprint; no independent replication or artifact audit was performed.
  • The headline real-robot result covers 10 selected tasks with three trials per task per model on one bimanual YAM setup; this does not establish broad real-world task reliability.
  • Harness choice materially changes the reported GPT-6 Astra score: 96.7% in the authors' harness versus 36.7% in Robocurve. Comparisons across differing harnesses should not be read as model-only differences.
  • The paper reports checkpoint transfer to a single-arm Franka on RoboLab-120, but task selection, baseline protocol and uncertainty were not independently assessed.
  • No long-duration deployment, safety failure rate, recovery from hardware faults or evaluation on independently selected sites and object distributions is established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent group has reproduced the real-robot success, time/cost or embodiment-transfer results.
  • The training/evaluation code, checkpoint and exact task-selection/scoring artifacts were not audited in this activation.
  • Robustness across broader objects, environments, operators, hardware units, long-horizon operation and safety-relevant failures remains unknown.

Why it matters

The work tests whether a pretrained model's semantic knowledge can be paired with motion learned in simulation, reducing reliance on scarce real-robot demonstrations. If the reported transfer holds under independent and broader tests, it could change the cost and data requirements for embodied agents; the current task set and single-source evaluation do not establish generality.
