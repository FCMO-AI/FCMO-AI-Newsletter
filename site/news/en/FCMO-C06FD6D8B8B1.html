Anthropic publishes detailed recommendations for designing and reporting AI safety evaluations

Anthropic’s 13 November 2026 engineering post presents recommendations for designing, running and reporting AI safety evaluations. It emphasizes matching the design to the question, measuring harm-related behavior rather than refusal alone, testing multi-turn and realistic scenarios, validating graders against independent human labels, preregistering analyses, and reporting uncertainty and configuration. The post reports that appropriate-response rate in one Claude Fable 5.1 multi-turn suicide and self-harm evaluation was 60% (±14%) on the API without a system prompt and 94% (±7%) on claude.ai with the product system prompt. Anthropic’s later official system card reproduces those rates and uncertainty. These are first-party results; the comparison does not isolate one mitigation, and the review did not establish external generalization.

Why it matters

Safety evaluations inform model release, safeguard and training decisions, so construct validity, grader reliability and configuration comparability can materially affect conclusions. The post gives practitioners a concrete framework and reports a large API/product configuration difference in one self-harm evaluation. Its practical value is methodological; the reported example is first-party and under-specified here, and the guidance’s external effectiveness has not been tested in this review.
