Paper reports that emulated NVFP4 pretraining uses more exposures to learn rare facts than BF16

Lu and Zhang's Nov 9 preprint reports that transformer models trained with emulated NVFP4 matrix multiplications need more exposures than BF16 models to reach the same recall on controlled rare facts, even when validation loss is similar. In the primary 40M-parameter comparisons (six seeds), matched-token exposure multipliers are 1.32× for synthetic biographies and 1.52× for a FineWeb-Edu mixture; matched-loss estimates are 1.25× and 1.22×. The authors attribute part of the effect to gradient noise interacting with AdamW. Experiments use emulated FP4 GEMMs on one RTX 4090, models up to 96.6M parameters and up to 1B tokens. Native FP4 throughput, production-scale generalization and independent reproduction are not established.

What changed

Lu and Zhang's Nov 9 preprint reports that transformer models trained with emulated NVFP4 matrix multiplications need more exposures than BF16 models to reach the same recall on controlled rare facts, even when validation loss is similar. In the primary 40M-parameter comparisons (six seeds), matched-token exposure multipliers are 1.32× for synthetic biographies and 1.52× for a FineWeb-Edu mixture; matched-loss estimates are 1.25× and 1.22×. The authors attribute part of the effect to gradient noise interacting with AdamW. Experiments use emulated FP4 GEMMs on one RTX 4090, models up to 96.6M parameters and up to 1B tokens. Native FP4 throughput, production-scale generalization and independent reproduction are not established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — arXiv records the v1 submission on 2026-11-09.
  • Claimed — At matched training tokens, the authors report NVFP4/BF16 exposure multipliers of 1.32× (95% CI 1.16–1.50) for synthetic biographies and 1.52× (1.35–1.71) for the natural-text mixture.
  • Claimed — At matched validation loss, authors report 1.25× (1.13–1.38) on synthetic biographies and 1.22× (1.10–1.36) on natural text; the latter BF16 point is during learning-rate decay and biases the comparison toward 1.
  • Not established — Frontier-scale transfer, naturally occurring long-tail facts, native FP4 throughput and independent reproduction are not established.

Strongest baseline

Paired BF16 models use the same fact set and data order; main NVFP4/BF16 comparisons use six seeds.

Limitations

  • Author-reported results; not independently reproduced.
  • Main comparisons use six seeds and models up to 96.6M parameters; longest run is 1B tokens.
  • FP4 GEMMs are emulated via operand quantization and FP32 multiplication on one RTX 4090; no native throughput or end-to-end cost is measured.
  • Rare facts are controlled synthetic biographies, alone or mixed into one FineWeb-Edu corpus; natural long-tail knowledge is not tested.
  • Natural-text matched-loss BF16 checkpoint occurs during LR decay after 5–6% fewer tokens, biasing this comparison toward 1.

What we still don't know

  • Primary matched-token exposure results and AdamW mechanism have not been independently reproduced.
  • Transfer to frontier scale, natural long-tail facts, native FP4 kernels and production economics is untested.

Why it matters

Validation loss can underweight rare facts. If this result reproduces in larger models and naturally occurring corpora, exposure-controlled recall could change low-precision recipe comparisons by revealing a quality cost that aggregate loss hides. The current evidence is limited to small author-run experiments with emulated arithmetic.
