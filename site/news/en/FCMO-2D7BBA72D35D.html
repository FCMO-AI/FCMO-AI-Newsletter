WhatWorkedBench reports that AI agents can outperform matched statistical baselines on some small-budget experiment-reconstruction tasks, with inconsistent results across models and cohorts

A September 2026 arXiv paper introduces WhatWorkedBench, a benchmark for recovering conditional effects and selecting configurations from small experimental budgets. The authors report 36 conditions across 30 sources and eight workflow families, including 1,248 configuration records and 4,206 numerical controls. In a prospective typed study on 12 four-factor sources at eight purchases, DeepSeek Flash submitted valid tables for all 12 and had mean recovery 0.147 above a Gaussian-process fit to the same observations; DeepSeek Pro delivered 11 of 12, with a -0.001 all-attempt difference and +0.063 among delivered artifacts. The six-new-source subgroup reported +0.149 for Flash and +0.074 for Pro, while the all-source source-bootstrap range was [0.055, 0.261] for Flash and [-0.177, 0.153] for Pro, so only Pro’s range included zero. On four code-visible six-factor sources, seven of eight typed tables met verified code equivalences. These are paper-reported benchmark results, not independent replications or evidence of universal research-agent advantage.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that, at eight observations per source, DeepSeek Flash direct tables exceeded a same-observation Gaussian-process baseline by mean recovery 0.147 over 12 four-factor sources; Pro’s all-attempt difference was -0.001 and delivered-only difference +0.063. Six new sources produced +0.149 and +0.074, respectively. The reported paper analyses are not independently reproduced.

Strongest baseline

The prospective typed comparison uses same-observation Gaussian-process fits; additional comparisons include frozen pair-effect designs and main/pair-effect ridge estimators. Agent/model comparisons include DeepSeek Flash and Pro. The paper reports delivery failures and distinguishes all-attempt from delivered-only scoring.

Limitations

  • All numerical outcomes were inspected in the author-submitted paper; no independent rerun or artifact audit was performed.
  • The all-source descriptive bootstrap interval excludes zero for Flash ([0.055, 0.261]) but includes zero for Pro ([-0.177, 0.153]); source selection and benchmark construction still limit external validity.
  • Model performance depends on output delivery and estimator contract: Pro has one non-delivery, and delivered-only and all-attempt contrasts differ.
  • The tasks reconstruct benchmark response surfaces; real-world experimental costs, noisy measurements, and downstream scientific discoveries were not measured.

What we still don't know

  • The core prospective result has not been independently reproduced from the public benchmark repository and raw episode artifacts.
  • Generalization from selected benchmark surfaces to noisy, costly laboratory or AI-training experiments is unknown.

Why it matters

Small-budget experimental reconstruction is relevant to AI research automation because the agent must both choose measurements and infer conditional effects. The reported outcomes vary with model, delivery, cohort and estimator, and the all-source descriptive source-bootstrap range excludes zero for Flash but includes zero for Pro; preserving those distinctions avoids turning a benchmark result into a general claim about agents.
