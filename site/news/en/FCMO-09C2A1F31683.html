MoEZip reports stronger task scores under aggressive KV-cache compression in tested sparse language models

An arXiv paper proposes MoEZip, which uses router Fisher-trace sensitivity and attention to retain context positions intended to stabilize expert routing during KV-cache eviction. In author-run comparisons, it reports higher long-context benchmark scores than KVZip, SnapKV and PyramidKV at high compression on Qwen3 and GLM-4.7-Flash sparse models. In a Qwen3-235B-A22B appendix, the authors report a 10-task LongBench macro of 44.7 versus 27.6 for KVZip at 10% KV retention. The authors also estimate 15,000–16,000 A100 GPU-hours for MoEZip and all baselines together, excluding preliminary or failed runs. The exact paper title also appears in NeurIPS 2026’s official downloads listing; this confirms conference-program inclusion but does not independently reproduce the benchmark results. The reported results have not been independently reproduced; Appendix C says code is in supplementary material, which this activation did not retrieve, while the linked repository page lists only a README and license.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v1 paper describes a routing-aware KV-cache eviction method that combines router Fisher-trace sensitivity with instruction-to-context attention scores and preserves a fixed retained-cache budget.
  • Claimed — The authors report that MoEZip improves LongBench, NIAH and RULER scores over KVZip, SnapKV and PyramidKV in tested Qwen3-30B-A3B and GLM-4.7-Flash settings, with the largest gains under aggressive KV retention.
  • Claimed — For Qwen3-235B-A22B at 10% KV retention, the authors report a 10-task LongBench macro score of 44.7 for MoEZip and 27.6 for KVZip; the reported evaluation used eight A100 80GB GPUs.
  • Not established — The reported benchmark results are not independently reproduced and do not establish deployment throughput, realized serving-cost reductions, broad model-family transfer or applicability to black-box inference systems.
  • Claimed — The authors estimate approximately 15,000–16,000 A100 GPU-hours for all reported experiments, including MoEZip and baselines, excluding preliminary or failed runs; this is an aggregate paper estimate, not a MoEZip-only marginal budget or a monetary cost.
  • Demonstrated — NeurIPS 2026’s official downloads listing includes the exact title of the MoEZip paper.

Strongest baseline

Within-paper comparisons use matched retained-cache budgets, checkpoints, prompt templates and decoding settings against KVZip, SnapKV and PyramidKV; Appendix E.9 also compares with attention-only selection on a four-task subset. Appendix C says competing observation windows include the full template-defined instruction span. These are author-run comparisons without independent reproduction.

Limitations

  • The benchmark results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The linked public repository lists README.md and LICENSE but no accessible experiment code or dataset at the inspected revision.
  • The method requires access to router outputs and instruction-to-context attention statistics, which may not be available in black-box inference settings.
  • Reported task scores do not establish serving throughput, memory cost, latency or economic benefit under production workloads.
  • The 235B appendix tests scale transfer within the Qwen3 family; it does not establish broad cross-family generalization.
  • The reported 15,000–16,000 A100 GPU-hours cover MoEZip and all baselines together and exclude preliminary or failed runs; the paper does not attribute a marginal compute budget to MoEZip or give monetary cost.
  • The NeurIPS 2026 downloads listing includes the paper title, but this program listing is not an independent reproduction or validation of its benchmark results; the linked poster page could not be inspected in this activation.

What we still don't know

  • The paper reports results on public benchmarks, but this activation did not independently reproduce them and the linked GitHub repository currently exposes only a README and license rather than experiment code or data.
  • The paper's benchmark regime does not establish system-level serving performance or realized economic benefit, and its internal-state requirements may preclude black-box deployment.

Why it matters

If the reported gains generalize, protecting routing-sensitive context could reduce memory required for long-context inference in sparse mixture-of-experts models while retaining task performance. The paper does not measure serving throughput, realized deployment cost or external replication, and the method requires internal router and attention statistics.
