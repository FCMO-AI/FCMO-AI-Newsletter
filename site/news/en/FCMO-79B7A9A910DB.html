ForecastGym separates search evidence, model reasoning, and memory in live-agent forecasting evaluation

A November 17 preprint introduces ForecastGym, a protocol that prospectively captures search results for unresolved prediction-market questions and replays the frozen evidence to compare forecasting models, search providers and memory strategies under controlled conditions. The first reported batch contains 150 questions, 614 evidence states and 131,542 search results. In the authors' tests, rerunning queries after settlement leaked the outcome for up to 92% of questions despite date filters; all tested language models trailed market prices on the frozen evidence, and changing the search API shifted Gemini 3.8 Flash's last-state Brier score by up to 0.043, exceeding the 0.027 spread among four models. These are paper-reported findings from one sports-heavy batch and have not been independently reproduced; the provider effect bundles retrieval, follow-up queries and formatting.

What changed

A November 17 preprint introduces ForecastGym, a protocol that prospectively captures search results for unresolved prediction-market questions and replays the frozen evidence to compare forecasting models, search providers and memory strategies under controlled conditions. The first reported batch contains 150 questions, 614 evidence states and 131,542 search results. In the authors' tests, rerunning queries after settlement leaked the outcome for up to 92% of questions despite date filters; all tested language models trailed market prices on the frozen evidence, and changing the search API shifted Gemini 3.8 Flash's last-state Brier score by up to 0.043, exceeding the 0.027 spread among four models. These are paper-reported findings from one sports-heavy batch and have not been independently reproduced; the provider effect bundles retrieval, follow-up queries and formatting.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors submitted arXiv:2611.23045v1 on 17 November 2026, presenting ForecastGym as a prospective search-evidence capture and controlled-replay benchmark for forecasting agents.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that its first batch contains 150 prediction-market questions, 614 evidence states and 131,542 search results collected from four commercial search APIs; the appendix reports 144 settled questions and 576 scored states at the reported cutoff.
  • Claimed — In a post-settlement probe of 60 questions, the authors report that Exa date-filtered searches disclosed the outcome for 92% of questions; tested date filters therefore left substantial leakage in the queried results.
  • Claimed — On the frozen first-batch evidence, the paper reports that all tested language models trailed the market at the last state; for Gemini 3.8 Flash, changing providers moved last-state Brier score by up to 0.043, more than the 0.027 spread among the four main models.
  • Not established — The inspected preprint does not establish independent reproduction, a broad domain-balanced result, or that the reported provider effect isolates retrieval from follow-up policy and evidence formatting.

Strongest baseline

Market prices at each evidence state are the external forecasting baseline. Model comparisons use shared frozen evidence for four main models; provider comparisons hold Gemini 3.8 Flash fixed and use the authors' shared collection protocol. The paper reports confidence intervals paired by question.

Limitations

  • The quantitative results are reported in one preprint and have not been independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The first batch is dominated by sports and culture questions (109 of 150); only 41 selected questions are in other domains, so broad forecasting generalization is not established.
  • The paper reports failed search requests in part of the batch and attributes some diagnostic gaps using an LLM judge; these aspects may affect results and were not independently audited here.
  • The measured provider effect combines retrieval results with adaptive follow-up queries and evidence formatting under a shared initial collection policy; it is not a pure search-index causal effect.
  • The first batch's tested models trail the market; the paper does not demonstrate superior real-world forecasting or profitable trading.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the benchmark pipeline, outcome-leakage probe or forecasting results was inspected.
  • The first batch is sports-heavy and provider effects bundle retrieval with follow-up and result formatting; transfer to balanced domains and isolated provider mechanisms remains uncertain.
  • Although the paper says it releases the benchmark, this activation did not locate and inspect a versioned public code/data artifact.

Why it matters

The paper treats retrieved evidence as a controlled evaluation variable: it records what providers returned while questions were still open, then replays identical evidence trajectories so later comparisons can vary the model or memory without changing the search record. If the reported results hold, this makes benchmark scores more interpretable by exposing temporal leakage and provider sensitivity; it does not establish that the evaluated agents can forecast better than markets.
