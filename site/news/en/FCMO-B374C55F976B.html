RLVR rollout batches trade sample efficiency against training stability

An arXiv paper reports controlled GRPO experiments on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and related checkpoints. Across 21 unclipped runs spanning a 32-fold optimizer-batch range and nearly sixfold learning-rate range, the authors find that training failure timing is more consistent with consumed response samples than optimizer steps along square-root learning-rate scaling. They also report declining sample efficiency above a learning-rate-dependent critical batch, with clipping delaying failure but not removing the progress gap. The results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published a paper presenting a two-clock model and controlled GRPO experiments on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and related checkpoints.
  • Claimed — Across 21 unclipped runs spanning a 32-fold batch range and nearly sixfold learning-rate range, the authors report that failure points are approximately invariant in consumed response samples along square-root learning-rate scaling; the fitted failure exponents' 90% intervals include zero for the batch exponent adjusted by the square-root schedule.
  • Claimed — The authors report that larger batches above an estimated learning-rate-dependent critical scale require more response samples for comparable accuracy, while ratio clipping delays failure without eliminating the sample-efficiency gap.
  • Not established — The paper's empirical coverage is limited to one model family and a narrow task domain; it measures response samples rather than compute, token or wall-clock efficiency and does not establish a universal batch-scaling law.

Strongest baseline

Matched smaller and larger rollout batches under square-root learning-rate scaling, with clipped and unclipped comparisons and checkpoint robustness tests.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported and not independently reproduced.
  • The main experiments use one model family and a narrow task domain; cross-family transfer is not established.
  • Efficiency is measured in generated responses, not tokens, compute cost, throughput or wall-clock time.
  • Failure timing varies across seeds, and some runs are censored by the training budget.
  • The paper's local theory is evaluated through predictions; the curvature term is not directly measured at this scale.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the batch/rate sweep and the claimed sample-efficiency and failure-timing behavior.
  • Generalization beyond the tested Qwen2.5 family and mathematical reasoning task domain is unmeasured.

Why it matters

Large rollout batches are used to increase RLVR training throughput, but this paper reports that sample efficiency can worsen above a critical batch size while instability tracks consumed samples. If the pattern transfers, batch selection and schedule design may need to account for both clocks; the paper does not measure wall-clock or compute savings.
