A paper finds local serving protocols can distort tool-use fidelity scores

A September 2026 preprint reports that local serving behavior can be mistaken for model tool-use behavior. In the authors’ Ollama setup, static template flags reject some native tool-call requests before inference; their harness records retry exhaustion as a generic assistant turn, which can make an unrun model appear to have zero tool-call fidelity. Across four serving stacks, request handling differs. Prompt hints improve some accepted models, while forcing a uniform text protocol reduces fidelity for a model with native tool-call support. Turn-pooled and per-seed estimates differ by up to about 55 points when a looping episode dominates a thin sample. These are author-reported results from a small study, not a prevalence estimate or general model comparison.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published a September 22, 2026 preprint that studies serving-layer effects on local tool-call evaluation.
  • Claimed — In the authors’ Ollama case study, static template flags reject native tool requests for Phi-3 and Gemma-3 before model inference; their harness does not preserve refusal as structured failure metadata, allowing downstream analysis to count the request as a model non-call.
  • Claimed — Across the reported protocol controls and four-stack probes, fidelity depends on the serving path: adding a text hint helps some accepted models, a uniform text protocol lowers Llama-3.2’s measured fidelity, and turn-pooled versus per-seed estimates differ by up to about 55 percentage points on thin denominators.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The study is a small measurement study and does not establish a general scale law, model-family effect, reasoning/tool-training relationship, or prevalence across local deployments; the authors explicitly disclaim those inferences.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper, matched native, native-plus-text-hint and uniform text-tools conditions on the same models, plus a cross-stack probe. These are author-run protocol controls, not independent replications.

Limitations

  • All measurements are author-reported; no independent reproduction was performed in this activation.
  • The study is small and the largest pooled-versus-per-seed gaps arise on thin denominators and looping episodes.
  • The findings concern protocol fidelity and request handling, not downstream task success or general model capability.
  • The authors explicitly disclaim scale, model-family and reasoning/tool-training conclusions.
  • The deployment prevalence of these specific confounds is not established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent replay established request rejection, harness error classification and protocol-rate differences.
  • Prevalence of the reported failure mode across model templates, server versions and evaluation harnesses is unknown.

Why it matters

Tool-use evaluations can conflate model decisions with request acceptance, serialization and harness accounting. Separating transport rejection, non-response, malformed calls and model-selected non-calls could change how local agent results are interpreted. The paper does not show how common these errors are across deployments.
