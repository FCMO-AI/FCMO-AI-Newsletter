A conditional reliability model shows how shared failures erase nominal AI-control redundancy

An arXiv preprint models one published AI-control deployment route as access control, monitoring plus response, or visibility plus tracing plus containment. Under independent equal rare failures, its visible-component model has six size-three minimal cuts and failure probability asymptotic to 6ε³. Illustrative shared failure domains reduce the minimum underlying cut size to two or one, and a conditional derivation shows why stronger prevention can concentrate later recovery demands in harder failure regimes. The paper recommends explicit dependency mapping and recovery tests that preserve the initiating failure. These results are conditional analysis: the common-cause examples are not claims about Google DeepMind’s implementation, and no operational probabilities are calibrated.

What changed

An arXiv preprint models one published AI-control deployment route as access control, monitoring plus response, or visibility plus tracing plus containment. Under independent equal rare failures, its visible-component model has six size-three minimal cuts and failure probability asymptotic to 6ε³. Illustrative shared failure domains reduce the minimum underlying cut size to two or one, and a conditional derivation shows why stronger prevention can concentrate later recovery demands in harder failure regimes. The paper recommends explicit dependency mapping and recovery tests that preserve the initiating failure. These results are conditional analysis: the common-cause examples are not claims about Google DeepMind’s implementation, and no operational probabilities are calibrated.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — For the paper’s fixed route model φ = A ∨ (M ∧ R) ∨ (V ∧ T ∧ Q), the six minimal visible-component cuts each contain three failed functions. Under the additional assumption that all six component failures are independent with equal rare failure probability ε, the model gives system failure probability 6ε³ − 9ε⁴ + 5ε⁵ − ε⁶, asymptotic to 6ε³.
  • Demonstrated — In two explicitly illustrative failure-domain mappings that keep the visible route architecture fixed, one shared event disabling access control and monitoring reduces the minimum underlying failure cut size from three to two; a hypothetical shared event touching every success path reduces it to one. The paper says these mappings are not claims about Google DeepMind’s implementation.
  • Demonstrated — The paper’s conditional model shows that if prevention suppresses ordinary establishments faster than a hard/shared failure regime, the hard regime can dominate the population that reaches recovery. It therefore derives that recovery-test mixtures should reflect P(C|E), and that cleaning up the upstream compromised state before a recovery test can measure a different quantity.
  • Claimed — The author recommends mapping shared failure domains, tracking joint and conditional system-level assurance quantities, prioritizing interventions with model-specific importance measures, and exercising recovery while preserving or faithfully reproducing the failure mechanism. These are recommendations from an analytic preprint, not measured improvements to a deployed control system.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the same visible route under an idealized independent-component model and alternative hypothetical shared-failure-domain maps. It reports no empirical baseline.

Limitations

  • This is a single arXiv preprint applying established reliability theory to one fixed, static deployment route; it does not introduce a new general reliability formalism.
  • The third-order rare-failure result assumes independent, equally rare visible-component failures.
  • The common-cause examples are constructed illustrations, not claims about Google DeepMind’s actual architecture or failure rates.
  • The source offers no calibrated deployment probabilities, empirical dependence measurements or operationally measured recovery-selection effect.
  • Dynamic adaptation, resource constraints, attacker learning and multiple routes are outside the fixed-route model.

What we still don't know

  • The paper applies its route model to a public Google DeepMind control roadmap, but no independent implementation map establishes which models, identities, credentials, operators, code or control planes are actually shared across the safeguards.
  • The paper reports no calibrated failure probabilities, empirical dependence measurements, or validation that its conditional recovery-selection effect occurs in an operational control stack.

Why it matters

Counting visible safeguards can overstate redundancy when several depend on one model, identity service, codebase or control plane. The paper gives a concrete conditional framework for checking that dependence and for weighting recovery tests toward the failure cases that would actually reach them. Its value is as a design and measurement analysis; it does not estimate real system risk.
