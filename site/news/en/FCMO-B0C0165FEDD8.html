AgentHorizon tests whether judges catch constraint violations in computer-use agents

AgentHorizon is a benchmark for judging whether long-horizon computer-use agents followed precise user instructions, including paired near-miss failures where a trajectory completes the main task but violates a constraint. The authors report 1,373 instruction–trajectory pairs across three operating systems and six domains. GPT-5.5 through Codex reached 80.9 balanced accuracy on a difficult 528-task subset, but tooling effects varied across judges and exact recall for GPT-5.5 on bad side effects was 24.1%. Results are author-reported, and the English-only benchmark does not establish general reliability or production safety.

What actually changed

AgentHorizon is a benchmark for judging whether long-horizon computer-use agents followed precise user instructions, including paired near-miss failures where a trajectory completes the main task but violates a constraint. The authors report 1,373 instruction–trajectory pairs across three operating systems and six domains. GPT-5.5 through Codex reached 80.9 balanced accuracy on a difficult 528-task subset, but tooling effects varied across judges and exact recall for GPT-5.5 on bad side effects was 24.1%. Results are author-reported, and the English-only benchmark does not establish general reliability or production safety.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper and linked artifacts define AgentHorizon, a benchmark of 1,373 instruction–trajectory pairs (523 positive and 850 negative) spanning 166 hours, three operating systems and six domains. This establishes the benchmark artifact's reported scope, not its external validity.
  • CLAIMED — On a difficult 528-task subset, the authors report 80.9 balanced accuracy for GPT-5.5 used through Codex. This is a paper-reported result on the benchmark and has not been independently reproduced.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports inconsistent effects from agentic tooling across tested judges and configurations; GPT-5.5 direct prompting scored 68.6 balanced accuracy versus 80.9 through Codex, while Qwen 27B direct prompting scored 77.6 versus 70.4 through OpenCode.
  • CLAIMED — For GPT-5.5, the paper reports 24.1% exact recall on the benchmark's bad-side-effect category, indicating that a judge can miss an unrequested harmful action even when the task outcome appears successful.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that the 523 released positives were retained after review: two assessments agreed directly on 388 of 850 candidate positives, and arbitration retained 135 of the remaining 462 while excluding 327. They report no population inter-annotator agreement; a three-reviewer diagnostic covered 23 of 425 pairs, and cross-pass negative-label stability was Cohen’s kappa 0.802 over 850 negatives. A model-based residual-QA sample flagged an estimated 11.5% of tasks (18/156; Wilson 95% interval 7.4%–17.5%), not a human-validated error rate.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares direct model prompting and agentic harness configurations, reporting inconsistent effects across GPT-5.5 and Qwen 27B. This comparison does not isolate all harness confounds or establish a general benefit from tool use.

The caveat that matters

  • Empirical judge results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The benchmark emphasizes paired near-miss instruction violations and does not cover broad failure modes such as crashes or infinite loops.
  • The paper does not report an independent audit of annotator agreement; prior pilot exposure may weaken claims about an unseen test set.
  • Coverage is English-only and limited to the included applications, operating systems and task domains; screenshots and action logs do not capture every aspect of application state.
  • A high benchmark score is not a safety certification or evidence that a judge is suitable for production deployment.
  • Author documentation reports no population inter-annotator agreement. Its three-reviewer diagnostic covered 23 of 425 pairs; cross-pass kappa of 0.802 for negative labels is a different reliability measure. Model-based residual QA estimated 18/156 flagged tasks (11.5%; Wilson 95% interval 7.4%–17.5%), not a human-validated error rate.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of the benchmark scoring or reported judge results was performed; reported accuracy is not independently validated.
  • The authors report review and QA procedures but no population inter-annotator agreement; a diagnostic covered 23 of 425 pairs, cross-pass negative-label stability is not independent human agreement, and model-based residual QA estimated 18/156 flagged items (11.5%). Generalization to unseen applications, languages, operating systems and broad failures such as crashes or loops is unestablished.

FCMO Lens

An agent can appear to finish a task while violating a precise user constraint or taking an unrequested action. AgentHorizon makes those near-miss cases measurable, while its reported low side-effect recall shows why task success alone cannot establish safe or faithful behavior.
