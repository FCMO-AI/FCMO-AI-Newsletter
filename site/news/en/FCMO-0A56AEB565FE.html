What changed

The Institute for Strategic Dialogue (ISD) published a structured evaluation of six chatbot models answering US election-procedure questions across ten selected states. Its mid-2026 dataset contains 2,400 responses to generic and adversarial prompts. ISD reports that 29% of generic English answers were incomplete, inaccurate or outdated, while another 16% were correct but omitted important voter details; it reports a 16% overall accuracy decline for generic Spanish prompts. These are report-reported findings from a single response per test condition, with material API/UI and state-selection limits—not estimates of current nationwide product performance.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — ISD published a report published in 2026 describing a June 2026 evaluation of six chatbot models across ten selected US states, with 2,400 responses to generic and adversarial prompts.
  • Claimed — ISD reports that 29% of generic English answers were incomplete, inaccurate or outdated, while another 16% were correct but omitted information it considered important for voters.
  • Claimed — ISD reports an overall 16% accuracy decline on generic prompts in Spanish; four tested models did not provide accurate and complete responses to more than 40% of the tested Spanish questions.
  • Claimed — ISD reports that the six models largely refuted false or misleading statements in English adversarial prompts, with none affirming such claims in more than 4% of cases; Spanish adversarial performance was broadly similar in its test.
  • Claimed — ISD reports that citations leaned toward government sources on generic questions and news media on adversarial questions, without a clear correlation between source-citation patterns and answer accuracy.
  • Demonstrated — The report states that each prompt configuration was run once; five models were assessed through APIs while Meta was accessed manually through its consumer interface; the ten states were deliberately selected for higher-risk election contexts; and the data were collected mid-2026.

Strongest baseline

Within the report's tested sample, generic English responses were compared across six named model versions and against state-specific researched references; the same prompt set was also tested in Spanish. No repeated-run baseline or independent replication was reviewed.

Limitations

  • Each unique prompt/model/language/state combination was run once, so reported differences are a single snapshot of stochastic systems rather than stable estimates.
  • The ten states were deliberately selected partly for changing rules, litigation or prior controversy; results are not a national estimate.
  • Five systems were accessed through APIs, not public consumer interfaces; the report notes API endpoints may omit product-level prompts, filters and election-specific safeguards. Meta's manual UI procedure was not directly comparable and could have memory carryover.
  • Data were collected in mid-2026. Election rules and model behavior can change before the upcoming election.
  • The answer-level data and coding materials were not obtained or independently reproduced in this review. The report uses both model-assisted and human coding and reports a manual 5% sample check for adversarial-response coding; results remain report-reported.

What we still don't know

  • The answer-level responses and detailed coding artifacts were not obtained; ISD states that full data are available to researchers upon request.
  • One response per configuration cannot estimate run-to-run variability, and the API/UI mix complicates comparison with consumer product behavior.
  • Ten intentionally selected states and mid-2026 data do not establish current nationwide performance or performance in other languages.

Why it matters

Incorrect or incomplete answers about local election procedures can omit deadlines, eligibility exceptions or required steps, and ISD reports that Spanish prompts performed worse in its test. If similar failures persist in deployed systems, they could create an unequal information-quality problem for voters. The evaluation is a consequential signal, but its one-pass June snapshot and selected-state design bound what it establishes.
