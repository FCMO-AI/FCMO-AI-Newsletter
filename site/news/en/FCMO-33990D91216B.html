Agent research study reports a 10% average gain from periodically resetting search history

An October 14 preprint tests a simple reset rule for iterative agent research: after each round, discard every agent session and candidate history, then restart a communicating team from the best candidate and its score. Across four tasks in heuristic programming, kernel optimization and small-model training, four-agent teams with three resets reportedly improved mean best results by 10% over four parallel agents and 22% over one continuing agent, at the same total token cost. Each configuration used one model and was run three times per task. The authors attribute the gains to renewed exploration after sessions become anchored to prior attempts. These are author-reported benchmark results from a single agent/model harness, not an independent replication or evidence that forgetting is universally beneficial.

What changed

An October 14 preprint tests a simple reset rule for iterative agent research: after each round, discard every agent session and candidate history, then restart a communicating team from the best candidate and its score. Across four tasks in heuristic programming, kernel optimization and small-model training, four-agent teams with three resets reportedly improved mean best results by 10% over four parallel agents and 22% over one continuing agent, at the same total token cost. Each configuration used one model and was run three times per task. The authors attribute the gains to renewed exploration after sessions become anchored to prior attempts. These are author-reported benchmark results from a single agent/model harness, not an independent replication or evidence that forgetting is universally beneficial.

Evidence

  • Claimed — A four-agent communicating team that periodically discards all sessions and candidate history while retaining the best candidate and score reportedly improved mean best results by 10% over four parallel agents and 22% over one continuing agent, averaged across four tasks at the same total token cost.
  • Claimed — Each configuration used the same model and budget and was run three times per task. The four tasks were AtCoder Heuristic Contest 014, Frontier-CS Q171, a simulated VLIW kernel and nanochat-autoresearch.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that periodic forgetting improves other agent models, reset schedules, real-world research tasks or settings where discarded history contains useful constraints; the research desk did not reproduce the results.

Strongest baseline

Four parallel agents with the same model and total budget, but without communication or resets; the paper reports a 10% average advantage for the communicating team with forgetting. A single continuing agent is also compared and trails by 22% on average.

Limitations

  • All results are author-reported; the research desk did not reproduce them.
  • The comparison uses four tasks, one agent/model harness and three runs per configuration/task.
  • A four-agent team with forgetting is compared against a single continuing agent and four agents without communication; transfer to different team sizes, models or reset schedules is unmeasured.
  • The reported matched budget is total token cost; other deployment costs and wall-clock tradeoffs are not established.
  • The protocol retains only the best candidate and score; it does not show that discarding all other history is safe or beneficial when failed attempts encode important constraints or provenance.

What we still don't know

  • The reported gains have not been independently reproduced; each task/configuration has three author-run repetitions.
  • The study uses one agent/model harness, four optimization tasks and a fixed schedule of three resets; transfer to other agent architectures, tasks and reset schedules is unknown.
  • The protocol preserves only the best candidate and score; it does not measure when discarded failed-attempt details, safety constraints or provenance are necessary for later search.

Why it matters

Agent research systems can carry failed attempts forward until they become conservative and stop making useful changes. If the reported results generalize, periodically retaining only the best verified candidate while resetting context could be a low-complexity way to extend open-ended search under a fixed budget. The paper covers four benchmark tasks, one model and three runs per configuration; it does not establish the right reset schedule across workloads or show that all historical information should be discarded.
