KAPPO derives adaptive policy pressure for pass@k and max@k post-training

The authors derive closed-form optimal policies for KL-regularized pass@k and max@k objectives and identify prompt-adaptive pressure that can be implemented by modifying standard @1 optimization. They report experiments on mathematical reasoning and code generation using OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT and Qwen3-4B-Base: KAPPO variants reduce the paper's measured sharpening pattern, and @k-A*PO using one new completion per prompt is reported comparable to PKPO, which requires at least k on-policy completions. These are paper-reported results, not independent reproductions.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper derives closed-form optimal policies for KL-regularized pass@k and max@k objectives and expresses their optimization pressure through prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformations.
  • Claimed — The authors report that KAPPO adaptations reduce sharpening in experiments on mathematical reasoning and code generation with OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT and Qwen3-4B-Base.
  • Claimed — The authors report that @k-A*PO using one new completion per prompt reaches validation performance comparable to PKPO, which uses at least k new on-policy completions per prompt.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that KAPPO improves production training cost or transfers to other model families, tasks, or reward regimes.
  • Claimed — The authors state that Qwen's pretraining cutoff is undocumented and contamination of the LiveCodeBench evaluation cannot be ruled out completely.

Strongest baseline

For binary-reward mathematics, the paper compares k-adjusted A*PO with k=1 A*PO/GRPO and PKPO on OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT and Qwen3-4B-Base. For continuous-reward code generation, it compares k=1,2,4 variants on Qwen3-4B-Base.

Limitations

  • All experimental results are reported by the paper authors and have not been independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The mathematics experiments use two model families and AIME 2026; the code experiments use Qwen3-4B-Base and the hard subset of LiveCodeBench v6.
  • The reference calibration uses a static bank of M completions per prompt; the paper says this can make estimates noisier.
  • For continuous rewards, experiments optimize a win-rate surrogate rather than directly evaluating raw-reward max@k optimization.
  • The authors state that applying the win-rate solution to discrete rewards with ties is an approximation.
  • The paper states that Qwen's pretraining cutoff is undocumented, so LiveCodeBench contamination cannot be ruled out completely.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of KAPPO's reported sharpening and rollout-cost results was located or performed; the supplementary code and checkpoints were not inspected.
  • Generalization beyond the two model families and selected mathematics/code tasks remains untested in the inspected paper.

Why it matters

If reproduced, adaptive pressure could let existing single-completion policy optimizers target pass@k/max@k behavior with fewer new rollouts than methods that collect k completions per prompt. The paper also gives an optimizer-independent derivation connecting @k objectives to prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformation. The evidence is limited to two model families and the reported task setups; it does not establish production cost savings or transfer to arbitrary models and tasks.
