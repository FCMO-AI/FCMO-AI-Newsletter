Cairn tests community reputation as shared memory for agent tools and services

A September 2026 preprint presents Cairn, a community reputation service where agents check a tool, data source or peer before use and add a rubric-based rating afterward. Its time-decayed Beta scorer shrinks uncertain ratings toward neutral. In simulations averaged over 100 seeds, reported scores tracked responder accuracy with mean absolute deviation 0.017, but pooled ratings failed sharply when dishonest raters approached or exceeded a majority. A live-corpus retrieval test reached R@5 0.65 overall and only 0.34 for mostly negative entities, so failure reports were harder to retrieve. A judge-rated sample of 5,000 active Moltbook agents produced scores uncorrelated with platform karma. These are paper-reported system and judge results; the paper does not establish improved downstream task outcomes, and Sybil attacks remain an open threat.

What changed

A September 2026 preprint presents Cairn, a community reputation service where agents check a tool, data source or peer before use and add a rubric-based rating afterward. Its time-decayed Beta scorer shrinks uncertain ratings toward neutral. In simulations averaged over 100 seeds, reported scores tracked responder accuracy with mean absolute deviation 0.017, but pooled ratings failed sharply when dishonest raters approached or exceeded a majority. A live-corpus retrieval test reached R@5 0.65 overall and only 0.34 for mostly negative entities, so failure reports were harder to retrieve. A judge-rated sample of 5,000 active Moltbook agents produced scores uncorrelated with platform karma. These are paper-reported system and judge results; the paper does not establish improved downstream task outcomes, and Sybil attacks remain an open threat.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper presents Cairn as a community reputation service with a check-use-rate loop, append-only event log and time-decayed Beta scoring with confidence shrinkage for data sources, capabilities and peer agents.
  • Claimed — Across 100 simulation seeds, the authors report that scores tracked responder accuracy with mean absolute deviation 0.017. Collusion and slander became unreliable when dishonest raters approached parity; under a hard trust filter, liar detection F1 fell to 0.07 because low-rated entities stopped receiving traffic.
  • Claimed — On a live corpus of 933 events across 218 tool/data-source entities, the paper reports task-query retrieval R@5=0.65 overall (0.84 when related endpoints are grouped) but 0.34 for mostly negative entities versus 0.72 elsewhere; absence from retrieval is therefore not evidence of safety.
  • Claimed — A paper-reported judge study rated 14,966 comments from 5,000 active Moltbook agents. Mean review scores were uncorrelated with upvotes (r=0.03) and karma (r=0.02); a second judge re-rated 3,306 comments with Pearson r=0.85 and MAE 0.23, with weakest agreement near mixed evidence. This measures judge-assigned traits, not downstream agent performance.
  • Demonstrated — The paper identifies Sybil attacks as a fundamental unresolved threat to open-contributor reputation systems and says pooled memory fails when dishonest raters lose their minority status.

Strongest baseline

No matched study of agents using Cairn versus no reputation memory. The field study compares judge scores with platform karma/upvotes, not task outcomes.

Limitations

  • All reported system evaluations are paper-authored; no independent implementation or reproduction was performed here.
  • The adversarial simulator assumes a particular interaction/rating setup; majority-honesty behavior is conditional on those assumptions.
  • Trust-filter routing starves low-scored entities of new evidence and reduces simulated liar detection F1 to 0.07.
  • Negative reports are harder to retrieve: R@5 is 0.34 for mostly negative entities versus 0.72 elsewhere, so an empty or positive-only retrieval view cannot establish safety.
  • The Moltbook sample and judge scores do not demonstrate real-world agent capability, honesty or downstream decision quality; Sybil resistance is unresolved.

What we still don't know

  • The paper evaluates a scorer, retrieval and judge-rated corpus but does not test whether agents using Cairn make safer or more successful downstream decisions than agents without it.
  • The paper identifies Sybil attacks and a dishonest-rater majority as unresolved; simulations show pooled evidence degrades sharply near parity.
  • The Moltbook field study uses rubric-anchored LLM judges; second-judge agreement is weaker around mixed evidence, and ground-truth task quality is not independently established.

Why it matters

Cairn turns post-use observations into a shared, decaying reputation prior for tools, data sources and agents, a possible trust layer for agent ecosystems. Its evaluation exposes an operational tradeoff: reputation can track reliability under stated assumptions, but it collapses near dishonest-rater parity and retrieval is weakest for negative reports. The field study measures judge-assigned agent quality, not whether using Cairn improves agent decisions or outcomes.
