ScienceIDE paper reports scientific-code environments for agent evaluation and training

The arXiv v1 paper describes an infrastructure for packaging scientific codebases with executable checks and generating tasks for agent evaluation, supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. It reports 64 environments from 27 scientific codebases, 2,812 tasks and 1,076 executable checks. On an 85-task hard subset, 15 model–harness systems received one-hour budgets; the highest reported success estimates were 67.1% for Fable 5.1, 64.6% for Opus 5 and 63.1% for Astra. Fable was measured once and the Opus/Astra intervals overlap, so the paper does not establish a stable ordering. The paper also reports selected supervised-learning gains on held-out scientific-code repair and public benchmarks. These are author-reported results; independent reproduction and broader external validation remain open.

What actually changed

The arXiv v1 paper describes an infrastructure for packaging scientific codebases with executable checks and generating tasks for agent evaluation, supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. It reports 64 environments from 27 scientific codebases, 2,812 tasks and 1,076 executable checks. On an 85-task hard subset, 15 model–harness systems received one-hour budgets; the highest reported success estimates were 67.1% for Fable 5.1, 64.6% for Opus 5 and 63.1% for Astra. Fable was measured once and the Opus/Astra intervals overlap, so the paper does not establish a stable ordering. The paper also reports selected supervised-learning gains on held-out scientific-code repair and public benchmarks. These are author-reported results; independent reproduction and broader external validation remain open.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors describe ScienceIDE as infrastructure that packages versioned scientific code with executable scientific checks and task factories, allowing verified interactions to support evaluation, supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports 64 environments across 27 scientific codebases, 2,812 manufactured tasks and 1,076 executable checks. The task inventory is dominated by repair and implementation; the authors state that the 1,000-environment release remains a development target.
  • CLAIMED — On the 85-task ScienceIDE-Hard subset, the authors compare 15 model–harness systems from eight providers under one-hour task budgets. The highest reported success estimates are Fable 5.1 at 67.1%, Opus 5 at 64.6% and Astra at 63.1%; Fable has a single-attempt estimate and the Opus/Astra repeat intervals overlap, so their ordering is not established.
  • CLAIMED — For selected supervised fine-tuning comparisons, the authors report higher repair reward on held-out tasks within three scientific codebases and gains on selected public code, reasoning and knowledge benchmarks. Confirmation comparisons use disjoint items and unadjusted intervals; one reported comparison declines.
  • CLAIMED — A reinforcement-learning experiment reports higher held-out verifier reward after 30 training steps on LAPS and MITgcm-biogeo tasks, but the MITgcm run’s logged validation contains 21 of 23 tasks while a rebuilt 66/21 split is not substituted. Treat this result as provisional.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, open-ended scientific discovery, broad performance transfer beyond its task families, or that scientific checks cover all scientifically valid implementations.

Strongest baseline

Model–harness systems are compared under a common task interface and one-hour budgets; selected SFT comparisons pair initial and tuned checkpoints on the same items. No independent platform or reproduction comparison was inspected.

The caveat that matters

  • Single arXiv v1 preprint; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • Evidence concerns reference-verifiable software tasks, predominantly repair and implementation in computational physics and geoscience, not open-ended discovery.
  • The hard evaluation subset is 85 tasks under one-hour budgets; the paper states that the 1,000-environment release remains a development target.
  • Factory-generated variants may share code paths and assumptions, so task count alone does not measure independent scientific coverage.
  • Fable 5.1 has a single-attempt estimate; Opus 5 and Astra repeat intervals overlap, so fine-grained ordering is not established.
  • The paper says scientific checks cover selected observables and tolerances and do not establish correctness outside those checks; independent external audits and adversarial reward-hacking evaluations remain open.
  • For one MITgcm reinforcement-learning result, the logged validation has 21 of 23 tasks, while the rebuilt 66/21 split was not substituted.

What remains unknown

  • No independent replication of the environment inventory, benchmark results or SFT/RL comparisons was conducted.
  • The ScienceIDE-Hard leaderboard has uneven repeat coverage; Fable is a single attempt and the Opus/Astra intervals overlap. Fine-grained model ordering is unresolved.
  • For the MITgcm RL run, the logged validation has 21 of 23 tasks, while the paper notes a rebuilt 66/21 split but does not substitute it for the reported run.
  • The current tasks are predominantly repair and implementation in computational physics and geoscience; independent external audits and adversarial reward-hacking tests remain open.

FCMO Lens

If the reported environment and verifier approach generalizes, scientific codebases could supply reusable, outcome-checked training experience rather than only static text or hand-built benchmark tasks. The paper demonstrates a substantial computational-science testbed and selected model-learning results, but its current evidence is mostly repair and implementation in computational physics and geoscience, not open-ended scientific discovery.
