Relore combines repository decision history with current-code search; reported retrieval gains remain narrow

A September 21, 2026 Hugging Face community article and the public relore repository describe a tool that indexes issue and pull-request history with author and trust metadata, then joins that history to current-code search in a maintained clone. Its project-authored, model-judged retrieval evaluation reports Recall@10 rising from 0.938 to 1.000 for 48 Transformers failure queries after query expansion, and from 0.375 to 0.475 for 40 precedent queries. The larger pooled rationale result is contaminated by same-thread answer leakage; the leak-free rationale slices are only 7 and 9 examples. In one author-reported Transformers case, relore found two existing fix threads and the agent avoided writing another patch; a code-browsing overview reportedly used about 5% of the tokens needed to read a whole file. These results support bounded accounts of retrieval and one field case, not a general reduction in duplicate work, better patches or higher coding-agent productivity.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — A public Apache-2.0 repository describes relore, which indexes issue and pull-request history with author/trust metadata and serves current-code search against a working clone.
  • Claimed — Project-authored, model-judged retrieval results report Recall@10 for a Transformers sample of 48 failure queries rising from 0.938 to 1.000 with query expansion, and 40 precedent queries rising from 0.375 to 0.475; the same report identifies contamination in pooled rationale results and supplies leak-free rationale slices of 7 and 9 examples.
  • Not established — The repository evaluation does not establish a general reduction in duplicate work, improved patch success, or end-to-end coding-agent productivity across repositories.
  • Claimed — In one reported Transformers case, relore found two existing fix threads and the agent avoided writing another patch; a separate author-reported code-browsing comparison says one file overview used roughly 5% of the tokens needed to read the whole file.

Strongest baseline

The project compares relore retrieval with GitHub search and grep on the same query windows. Results vary by query type; rationale outcomes are sensitive to answer leakage, and the leak-free subsets are small.

Limitations

  • The reported benchmark is project-authored and model-judged; no independent reproduction or human-label audit was performed.
  • The Transformers benchmark uses 48 failure, 40 precedent and 51 pooled rationale queries; leak-free rationale subsets contain only 7 and 9 examples.
  • The pooled rationale result is contaminated because a reviewer finding can appear in the same thread as the answer; the repository separates leak-free subsets and notes the limitation.
  • The project reports one field case in which an agent avoided a duplicate patch and one token-use comparison, not a controlled measurement of repeated task success or general agent productivity.
  • The index is per repository and may lag later GitHub changes by its polling interval; cross-repository search and second-fresh answers are outside its design.

What we still don't know

  • The retrieval benchmark and the single reported field case have not been independently reproduced or audited.
  • The reported retrieval metrics do not measure reduced duplicate work, patch success or end-to-end agent task outcomes.
  • The leak-free rationale slices are small (7 and 9 examples), and the larger pooled rationale slice includes same-thread answer leakage.

Why it matters

Coding agents can repeat work when they miss an in-flight change or the rationale behind existing code. Relore combines historical discussion, authorship and trust metadata with current-code search, and reports retrieval gains on limited benchmark slices. The evaluation also exposes same-thread leakage and small leak-free samples; it does not establish better patches or general productivity.
