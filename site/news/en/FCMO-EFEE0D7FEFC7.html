What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces KIR, a typed intermediate representation and compiler for agents authoring building information models in Revit. On a pinned KIR 0.8.1 snapshot, the author reports offline checks across 83 operation contracts, including typed refusals for 29 of 42 stress-test programs, 38 named absences among 377 witness obligations, and no vacuous certificate in 219 program-version runs. These are paper-reported results from project-authored artifacts; native Revit runs are separately reported from project records. The paper specifies but has not run a same-model, same-budget comparison against agents writing Revit code directly, and it demonstrates only Revit as an execution backend.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper presents KIR as a typed intermediate representation and compiler for agent-authored building information models, making refusals, defaults, witness obligations, transaction uncertainty and digest-bound decisions explicit.
  • Claimed — For pinned KIR 0.8.1, the author reports 29 of 42 stress-test programs refused with diagnostic codes and no uncaught exception, three silent defects accepted, 38 of 377 witness obligations named as absences, and no vacuous result across 219 certificate runs. These are author-reported offline results, not an independent reproduction.
  • Claimed — The preprint separately reports project-record Revit executions (26/28 named operations on Revit 2023 and 17/21 on Revit 2026); the paper says these runs were not rerun for the paper.
  • Demonstrated — The paper states that its same-model, same-budget comparison of KIR with direct host-code agents is specified but not executed; Revit is the only demonstrated backend.
  • Demonstrated — A separate 2026 CADIR preprint describes an editable intermediate representation and reports cross-backend reconstruction in FreeCAD, SolidWorks and Fusion 360. It addresses adjacent CAD representation and backend transfer; the inspected abstract does not establish a matched comparison with KIR or the same Revit BIM transaction/witness semantics.

Strongest baseline

No executed direct-code baseline. The paper specifies a same-model, same-budget comparison against agents writing host code directly but marks it not executed.

Limitations

  • Offline evaluation is on one pinned project snapshot; no agent task-success comparison against direct host code was run.
  • Three silent defects were accepted in the reported 42 stress-test programs.
  • The paper reports project-authored test artifacts and Revit operations; this activation did not execute or independently reproduce them.
  • Only Revit is demonstrated; portability to IFC or other hosts remains prospective.
  • The reported 329× representation-size comparison is KIR versus emitted C#, not a matched comparison to a hand-written direct-code agent.
  • Related work already includes CADIR, a 2026 CAD intermediate representation reporting editable reconstruction across three CAD backends; the distinction here is KIR’s Revit-focused BIM transaction, provenance and witness semantics. This is adjacent work, not evidence of KIR superiority.

What we still don't know

  • The paper specifies but has not executed the principal same-model, same-budget comparison against direct host-code agents; without it, relative task success, reliability and cost are unknown.
  • Offline outcomes and native Revit run records are authored or reported by the project; no independent reproduction was located.
  • The paper demonstrates Revit only; backend independence is a design property, not an evaluated result.

Why it matters

KIR makes ambiguity, defaults, missing checks, transaction uncertainty and stale-state decisions explicit in an agent-to-host representation. If the reported design holds up, it offers a concrete pattern for auditing agents that mutate stateful engineering models. The current evidence does not show that KIR improves task success or safety over direct-code agents, nor that its design transfers beyond the evaluated Revit snapshot.
