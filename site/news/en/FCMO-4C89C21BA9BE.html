TopK-Guided reports bounded activation-sparsity gains over TEAL and WINA on two 7–8B models

Agarwalla and Lin introduce TopK-Guided, a training-free modification of WINA that combines bounded per-token sparsity with sensitivity-aware allocation of sparsity across transformer blocks. In the paper's reported C4 perplexity and eight-task zero-shot accuracy tables, it outperforms TEAL and WINA at each tested target sparsity on Llama-2-7B and Llama-3-8B. These are author-reported results from a single A100 SXM4 40 GB setup, with hyperparameters selected on WikiText-2; the study reports FLOP-level savings and explicitly leaves kernel-level wall-clock performance for future work.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Agarwalla and Lin published the TopK-Guided activation-sparsity preprint, arXiv:2610.01763v1, dated 2026-10-01.
  • Claimed — On Llama-2-7B and Llama-3-8B at target sparsities 30%, 50% and 70%, the authors report that TopK-Guided has the best C4 perplexity and average zero-shot accuracy among TopK-Guided, WINA and TEAL in all twelve model/metric/sparsity comparisons in Table 1.
  • Claimed — The authors state that TopK-Guided preserves sparsity-dependent projection FLOP savings comparable to WINA and adds only an O(d) per-token mask; they do not report wall-clock speedup or energy measurements.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, kernel-level serving speed, energy or cost gains, or transfer beyond decoder-only models up to 8B parameters.

Strongest baseline

WINA, with TEAL as a threshold-masking comparison; paper reports baseline setups and target sparsity in §3.

Limitations

  • Author-reported single-paper results; no independent reproduction established.
  • Experiments use Llama-2-7B and Llama-3-8B on a single NVIDIA A100 SXM4 40 GB GPU, with C4 perplexity over 500 validation samples and zero-shot accuracy on eight tasks.
  • Hyperparameters are selected on WikiText-2; reported comparisons do not establish robustness across parameter settings or random-seed variation.
  • FLOP-level sparsity is not equivalent to wall-clock or energy savings; the authors leave kernel-level implementation for future work.
  • The paper's appendix shows per-task results are mixed even when average accuracy improves; transfer to larger models and other architectures is unknown.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the paper's quality or sparsity results was identified in this review.
  • No official implementation or pinned code revision was established in this review.
  • The paper reports FLOP-level savings rather than measured wall-clock latency, throughput, memory, energy or serving cost; kernel behavior is explicitly future work.
  • The evaluation covers two decoder-only models up to 8B parameters, and the largest reported gains occur at 70% sparsity; transfer and variance remain uncertain.

Why it matters

Activation sparsity can reduce nominal projection work, but token adaptivity and exact sparsity budgets create competing design constraints. The paper reports one method that aims to retain both and improve tested quality metrics at matched target sparsity. Whether the approach yields faster or more energy-efficient serving depends on efficient kernels and broader reproduction, which remain unestablished.
