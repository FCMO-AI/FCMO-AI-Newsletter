MechSparse finds causal sparse-adapter selection loses to activation norms on schema-heavy extraction

Xuan et al. test whether activation-patching scores can select LoRA/QLoRA sites better than random, magnitude, activation-norm and gradient/Fisher heuristics. Across three Ministral-8B/NF4 task cells, their causal selectors do not win the primary metric. On the headline three-seed Swahili span-JSON extraction cell, MechSparse-C trails activation-norm by 0.028 span+type F1 (95% pooled-prediction bootstrap CI [-0.048,-0.007]), is statistically unresolved against magnitude, and beats random and gradient/Fisher. The single-seed English-to-Swahili translation cell shows no reliable pairwise separation within 0.30 BLEU. The authors interpret selector quality as task-shaped, but the recommendation to use causal selection on content-dominated tasks remains unproven: no tested cell provides a positive multi-seed result. The study is one model and three narrow cells; probe-based selection costs about 1.7 L40S GPU-hours per cell before reuse.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Xuan et al. describe a sparse PEFT selector that ranks attention heads and MLP blocks by normalized clean/corrupt activation-patching recovery and trains adapters only at selected sites; MechSparse-C adds bounded within-layer joint-subset credit.
  • CLAIMED — On the three-seed Swahili span-JSON IE headline cell, authors report MechSparse-C trails activation-norm by 0.028 span+type F1 with pooled-prediction paired-bootstrap 95% CI [-0.048,-0.007], remains statistically unresolved against magnitude, and exceeds random and gradient/Fisher by 0.079 and 0.174, respectively.
  • CLAIMED — In the single-seed English-to-Swahili MT cell, authors report all four selectors within 0.30 BLEU and every pairwise bootstrap CI includes zero.
  • SPECULATIVE — Causal or probe-delta selectors improve sparse adaptation whenever content sensitivity rather than output format is the bottleneck.

Strongest baseline

Activation-norm leads on the headline span+type F1 metric (0.539 versus MechSparse-C at 0.511). Magnitude is statistically unresolved against MechSparse-C in the reported pooled-prediction comparison. The single-seed MT comparisons do not establish a winner.

The caveat that matters

  • All experimental outcomes are author-reported. The linked anonymous artifact endpoint was not accessible in this review and the research desk did not reproduce results.
  • Only one base model and three narrow task cells are measured. The paper itself says its content-dominated causal-selector branch has no positive primary-metric witness.
  • The headline paired bootstrap pools 600 examples across three seeds and resamples examples; the authors state that a seed-level test is not powered and example-level intervals can understate between-seed uncertainty.
  • Supplementary Swahili IE and English-to-Swahili MT cells are single-seed; the MT result at BLEU around 11 may reflect a floor effect, and every pairwise interval crosses zero.
  • Patching costs about 1.7 L40S GPU-hours per cell before mask reuse, compared with seconds for activation-norm and magnitude selectors.
  • The authors report selector-asymmetric task exposure: activation-norm and causal selectors see 64 task prompts, while random sees none. AdaLoRA and Taylor-importance comparisons remain future work.
  • The study does not establish broad multilingual transfer, superiority over rank-allocation methods, or a universal causal-selector decision rule.
  • The paper's headline result favors activation-norm over MechSparse-C on the primary span+type F1 metric; this contradicts a general claim that causal localization improves sparse PEFT selection.
  • No tested task cell gives the proposed causal selector a primary-metric win, and the authors explicitly characterize the content-dominated branch as unconfirmed.

What remains unknown

  • The paper reports masks, scores, predictions and per-seed files as released, but the linked anonymous artifact endpoint could not be inspected through the available web tool; no independent replay was performed.
  • The recommendation to use causal/probe-delta selectors for content-dominated tasks has no positive multi-seed primary-metric result in the paper's three cells.

FCMO Lens

The negative result cautions against assuming that mechanistic localization improves sparse fine-tuning: a cheap activation-norm heuristic wins the paper's schema-heavy headline metric, while the expensive causal method does not win any primary metric. If the result generalizes, it changes a local adapter-placement choice and the cost accounting for interpretability-guided PEFT. The proposed task-shaped selector rule remains preliminary.
