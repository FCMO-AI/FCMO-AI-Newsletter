What changed

OpenAI introduced BioBasicsBench, a 100-task benchmark for AI systems interpreting raw wet-lab evidence such as gel images, microscopy, instrument exports and protocol records. OpenAI reports that GPT-6 Astra averaged 66.7% of available credit at its xhigh setting, versus 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol, across a provider-run evaluation of seven models and 37 settings. The announcement describes expert task review but also shows errors in interpreting controls, reconciling conflicting observations and counting colonies. These are first-party benchmark results; they do not establish dependable laboratory use or real-world research gains, and no independent reproduction was found in the inspected release.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI published BioBasicsBench on 6 November 2026, describing a benchmark of 100 routine wet-lab tasks that test interpretation of raw experimental data, error detection and recognition when evidence cannot support a conclusion.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports evaluations of seven models over 37 model and reasoning-effort settings, with five attempts on each task per setting. At the reported xhigh setting, GPT-6 Astra averaged 66.7% of available credit (95% CI 60.3–73.1%) and GPT-5.6 Sol averaged 43.8% (95% CI 37.6–50.0%). These results have not been independently reproduced.
  • Claimed — OpenAI says task authors paired experimental data with expert-written rubrics and each task received review from a separate Ligo scientist, an external PhD-level wet-lab scientist and a final internal reviewer. This is the provider’s account of benchmark construction, not an independent audit.
  • Not established — The announcement does not establish reliable autonomous laboratory operation or improved real-world research outcomes. It documents model errors in control interpretation, contradictory image evidence and colony counting, and does not provide an independent benchmark audit in the inspected material.

Strongest baseline

GPT-5.6 Sol is the directly reported comparison for the xhigh headline result. The article also lists GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Luna, GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.2; effort settings differ by model.

Limitations

  • Model results and task-review claims are first-party and were not independently verified.
  • The release describes failures by GPT-6 Astra in control interpretation, handling contradictory image evidence and colony counting.
  • Benchmark performance does not establish safe or reliable autonomous laboratory action, and no deployment outcome was reported in the inspected article.

What we still don't know

  • The 100-task corpus, grading rubrics and model scores were not independently audited or reproduced in this activation.
  • The announcement does not establish transfer from benchmark tasks to dependable, supervised lab workflows or improved scientific outcomes.

Why it matters

Raw experimental data, control interpretation and uncertainty handling are routine parts of wet-lab work that chart-based benchmarks can miss. A benchmark built around those inputs could help measure a meaningful capability gap, but the reported comparison still needs independent task review and reproduction before it supports claims about dependable lab assistance.
