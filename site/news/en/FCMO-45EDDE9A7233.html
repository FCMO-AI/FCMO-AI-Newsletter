What changed

In a 18 November research release, OpenAI says models helped redesign blocks in its Jalapeño inference chip and contributed RTL changes to AMD GPU and CPU designs, reporting up to 14% PPA improvement on a flagship GPU IP baseline and single-digit gains on selected CPU blocks. It also reports 9,881 RISC-V evaluation runs in which GPT-6.2 Astra led at comparable token budgets. The manufacturing and performance figures are first-party claims; the controlled evaluation covers two cores and omits physical-synthesis timing.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI published a research release on 18 November 2026 describing model-assisted chip design work, an AMD collaboration and two RISC-V model evaluations.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports up to 14% power-performance-area improvement against a heavily optimized baseline on AMD flagship GPU IP, single-digit gains on selected blocks of a next-generation flagship CPU, and says some model-generated changes were included in a design AMD sent for manufacturing or in its production design files.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports 9,881 scored runs across optimization and specification-to-design tasks on PicoRV32 and Ibex; at comparable budgets near 112,000 tokens per run, GPT-6.2 Astra had the highest average score on both cores, with about twice GPT-6 Astra's score on PicoRV32.
  • Not established — The release does not establish general autonomous chip-design ability or industry-wide productivity gains. The RISC-V evaluation covers two embedded cores, the specification-driven task can omit original features, and timing was estimated with Yosys rather than checked after physical synthesis.

Strongest baseline

For AMD, OpenAI says results are relative to heavily optimized baselines but the public release does not disclose enough design and measurement detail for independent reconstruction. RISC-V results compare model generations under the same task constraints and token budgets; best observed score on each core is normalized to 1.0.

Limitations

  • PPA results and manufacturing status are first-party reports; no independent partner evidence or design artifacts were reviewed.
  • The controlled capability evaluation covers two 32-bit embedded cores, not large production SoCs or accelerators.
  • Specification-driven tasks can gain throughput by omitting features present in the reference core; physical-synthesis timing is not checked.
  • The release does not quantify end-to-end engineering labor, total EDA costs, failure rates or realized manufacturing impact.

What we still don't know

  • No AMD/Synopsys independent confirmation or public design-level data were located in the bounded check.
  • The 9,881-run RISC-V evaluation does not provide a linked complete artifact bundle in the inspected release, and the tasks omit some production-core requirements.

Why it matters

If the bounded results hold, model-assisted RTL design may shorten parts of advanced chip development and broaden practical AI use in electronic design automation. The release does not establish general autonomous chip design, cross-vendor results or independently measured product impact.
