  • Demonstrated — The guide includes proposed uses such as port safety-risk monitoring, vessel/waterway safety, port automation, rail-water coordination, intelligent shipping operations and maritime carbon accounting. Limitations

  • The first-party ministry notice establishes formal guidance and the ministry's stated priorities, not that any listed system is deployed.
  • No funding commitments, mandatory deadlines, procurement, adoption rates or project owners are specified in the notice reviewed.
  • Potential safety, productivity, cost and emissions benefits are not measured here; the scenarios span heterogeneous transport tasks and would require separate evaluations.
  • This review covers one national ministry document and its same-ministry interpretation, not provincial uptake or global policy coverage.

What we still don't know

  • The issued guide names scenarios and asks organizations to choose priorities, but does not identify funded projects, procurement, deployment status, timelines or implementation owners for each scenario.
  • No measured safety, throughput, logistics cost, service reliability or emissions outcome is reported for these AI applications.

Why it matters

The guidance gives ports, shipping firms and public transport units a national ministry's scenario list for directing AI pilots and technical work across a strategically important logistics sector. Whether it changes procurement, adoption or operational outcomes remains unmeasured.
