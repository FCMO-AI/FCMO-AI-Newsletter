SELF-INDEX reports search and memory retrieval gains from revisable keys

The authors of SELF-INDEX describe an optimizer that diagnoses retrieval shortfalls, revises document keys, validates changes and uses simulated queries. Their paper reports improvements across BRIGHT with three retrievers, BrowseComp-Plus across four backbones and LongMemEval V2. For GPT-OSS-120B with BM25, reported BrowseComp-Plus accuracy rises from 31.08% to 58.92% while search calls decrease. LongMemEval results vary by task: several improve by 9.2–13.9%, while Gotchas is unchanged or lower. These are paper-reported results; independent replication and matched full optimization-cost comparisons remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that SELF-INDEX improves BRIGHT retrieval across three retrievers and BrowseComp-Plus search across four backbones, with fewer search calls in reported configurations. On GPT-OSS-120B with BM25, BrowseComp-Plus accuracy increases from 31.08% to 58.92%.
  • Claimed — On LongMemEval V2, the authors report gains of 9.2–13.9% on several tasks; the task-specific Gotchas result is unchanged or lower, so the reported effect is not uniform.
  • Not established — The reported evaluations are author-run; simulated optimization queries are used, and this review did not independently reproduce the results or establish a matched total index-construction and inference-cost comparison.

Strongest baseline

Paper-reported retriever, search-agent and memory baselines using the stated backbones; a matched total index-optimization budget was not established here.

Limitations

  • All quantitative results cited here are reported by the authors and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • Search-call or retrieval gains do not by themselves establish lower total compute or latency because optimization and index-construction costs must be included.
  • Performance varies by task; the reported LongMemEval Gotchas result is unchanged or lower.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported retrieval and search-agent gains was found in this review.
  • Reported search-token use does not establish total optimization and index-construction cost relative to a fixed-index baseline under an equalized end-to-end budget.

Why it matters

Retrieval is a central bottleneck for agents that need to use growing stores of prior work. Revising retrieval keys could improve access to relevant information, but total optimization cost and task-level failures determine whether this approach is useful in practice.
