Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that their AI coding agent reproduced 1,694 of 2,190 atomic claims (77.4%) across 20 selected SEER-based papers; 76 were close and 420 were not reproduced.
  • Claimed — The authors report that their agentic robustness pipeline flagged 55 of 579 fully reproduced findings (9.5%) for expert review; one coauthor reviewed 10 cases, with nine not establishing a consequential change under a defensible alternative and one requiring further review.
  • Inferred — Claim-level reproduction and alternative-analysis screening may help direct scarce expert attention toward research findings that merit closer review, if the reported process transfers beyond this curated corpus.

Strongest baseline

Prior adjacent benchmarks include CORE-Bench (90 papers/270 tasks across computer science, social science and medicine), REPRO-Bench (112 social-science reproducibility-assessment tasks), and RECLAIM (100 NeurIPS 2025 papers with fixed claims and GPU-hour budgets). These are not matched baselines for the reported SEER reproduction rate; this review compared primary abstracts/metadata, not artifacts or full protocols.

Limitations

  • The results are author-reported in a preprint and have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The benchmark covers 20 selected SEER studies, not a representative sample of all medical or scientific literature.
  • The authors classify 420 non-reproduced claims; data issues are the largest category and 48 cases (11.4%) are attributed to agent-side errors.
  • The robustness system's 55 flagged cases are model-assessed leads, not established errors in the original papers; a coauthor reviewed only 10 and one remained unresolved.
  • No exact public implementation or benchmark release was located or tested in this review.
  • The prior-art comparison inspected primary abstracts/metadata for CORE-Bench, REPRO-Bench and RECLAIM, not their full protocols or artifacts; it narrows the novelty claim but is not an exhaustive literature review.

What we still don't know

  • The reported 77.4% rate and robustness-screening outcomes have not been independently reproduced; this review did not establish public access to the exact benchmark/code package.
  • Only 10 model-flagged cases received coauthor review in this paper, and the corpus covers 20 SEER studies; clinical validity and transfer beyond this setting remain unknown.

Why it matters

Claim-level reproduction and explicit testing of alternative analytic choices could make some scientific review cheaper and more systematic. The paper’s own results show why expert review remains necessary: its 55 model-flagged findings were leads, and only one of ten examined cases remained potentially consequential.
