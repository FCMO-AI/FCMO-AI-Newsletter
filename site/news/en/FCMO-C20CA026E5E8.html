What changed

A September 17 preprint compares shared-workspace teams with independent parallel agents on 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games. Using Claude Sonnet 4.6, the authors report full-game solve rates of 4.6% for teams of three and 8.0% for teams of five, versus 1.4% and 2.2% for the best of three and five independent attempts. Matching the reported team solve rates required 13 and 33 independent attempts, respectively. The paper also reports gains on two research-oriented tasks, but says communication can lose under limited compute or unclear feedback. These are author-reported results from one model and benchmark setup; the research desk did not reproduce them.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games using Claude Sonnet 4.6, the authors report full-game solve rates of 4.6% for team@3 and 8.0% for team@5, versus 1.4% for best@3 and 2.2% for best@5; matching those team rates required 13 and 33 independent attempts, respectively.
  • Claimed — The paper reports transfer gains on polyomino packing and MNIST classifier compression, and reports no advantage over independent pass@2 on Terminal-Bench 2.0 when intermediate feedback does not reliably rank progress.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish that the reported advantage generalizes to other models, tasks, larger teams, deployment workloads, or settings without objective progress feedback; the research desk did not reproduce the results.

Strongest baseline

Best-of-k independent attempts using Claude Sonnet 4.6 on the same 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games: 1.4% at k=3 and 2.2% at k=5, versus team rates of 4.6% and 8.0%. The paper also compares team@k with larger independent pools of 13 and 33 attempts.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported from a single preprint; the research desk did not reproduce them.
  • The primary ARC result is limited to Claude Sonnet 4.6, 25 public games and team sizes of three and five.
  • The paper describes a coordination tax when compute is limited or progress is hard to verify; Terminal-Bench 2.0 team@2 did not outperform independent pass@2.
  • A polyomino packing score differs between the introduction and Table 1 (0.922 versus 0.945); that transfer result is not treated as resolved.

What we still don't know

  • The ARC-AGI-3 team and independent success rates are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The principal comparison covers one model, public ARC games and team sizes three and five; generalization to other models, task regimes and larger teams is unknown.
  • The paper reports different polyomino packing scores in its introduction and Table 1 (0.922 versus 0.945).

Why it matters

If the reported advantage survives independent evaluation, sharing verified partial discoveries could change how inference budgets are allocated for open-ended agent tasks. The evidence covers one model on 25 public games and team sizes of three and five, and the paper itself identifies a coordination tax when compute or progress feedback is inadequate.
