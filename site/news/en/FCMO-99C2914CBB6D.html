APort Vault reports zero unauthorized transfers behind a deterministic payment authorization layer in a human-attack replay benchmark

In arXiv:2609.22076v1, APort’s author replays 4,371 human-written attacks from a live payment-agent capture-the-flag event against 14 models from eight labs, comparing model-only execution with a deterministic pre-action authorization check. Across restricted Levels 2–4, the paper reports 140 transfers to disallowed recipients in 76,842 model-only evaluations and none in 69,297 evaluations behind the layer; 105 versus zero also occurs on 68,970 matched model, prompt and track triples. The zero is bounded by 790 source sessions (session-clustered rule-of-three upper bound 0.38%). The result concerns one simulated payment tool and a locally implemented policy engine; the paper discloses a Level 4 confirmation-code mismatch that makes its measured layer stricter than the published policy pack. No inference to other tool domains, live deployment rates, or benign-task overblocking is supported.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper reports replaying 4,371 human-authored attacks from a public payment-agent CTF against 14 models from eight labs, with 225,964 completed evaluations across policy levels, replay tracks and architectures.
  • Claimed — At restricted Levels 2–4, authors report 140 disallowed-recipient transfers among 76,842 model-only evaluations and zero among 69,297 evaluations behind a deterministic authorization layer; the matched subset reports 105 versus zero on 68,970 triples.
  • Claimed — Authors bound the zero across 790 source sessions with a session-level rule-of-three upper bound of 0.38%; they also report 25,370 successful payments behind the layer, so the result was not produced by refusing all transfers.
  • Claimed — The paper discloses that its local Level 4 engine additionally required a confirmation code not required by the published policy pack; therefore the Level 4 zero does not alone establish behavior under the published pack.
  • Inferred — No live-deployment rate, cross-tool transfer rate, representative attacker-population estimate or benign-traffic false-positive rate is established by this benchmark.

Strongest baseline

Within-paper matched comparison holds model, prompt and replay track constant while changing whether the deterministic pre-action layer is present. Aggregate comparisons also include unequal coverage and are not used as causal estimates.

Limitations

  • The tested system is one simulated bank and one payment tool; the paper explicitly does not claim transfer to code execution, data access or agent-to-agent delegation.
  • The attacks are self-selected CTF submissions, not a representative user or state-grade attacker sample; no population risk rate follows.
  • The local policy engine is stricter than the published Level 4 policy pack because it required an extra confirmation code, so the Level 4 zero is not evidence about the published pack’s Level 4 behavior.
  • The policy engine was checked against its hosted verifier on a reported 100-case parity sample; the research desk did not inspect or rerun that test.
  • The corpus contains no benign-task evaluation, so false-positive or legitimate-traffic overblocking rates cannot be inferred.
  • 18,812 planned evaluations did not complete; multi-turn coverage is incomplete for some models and cells.
  • Cross-provider API defaults such as top_p and penalties were not normalized.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the replay counts, session-clustered bounds or matched comparisons has been completed.
  • The local engine’s parity against the hosted verifier is reported on 100 cases only, and a disclosed Level 4 rule differs from the published policy pack.
  • The corpus has no benign traffic, so the practical trade-off between rejecting unauthorized actions and blocking legitimate requests is unmeasured.
  • Transfer beyond the simulated payment tool and attacker population is not established.

Why it matters

If the reported comparison generalizes, a deterministic authorization check at the tool boundary can prevent some disallowed high-consequence actions without relying on the model to refuse requests. The paper provides an unusually detailed, released replay corpus and separates requests, payments, decisions and unauthorized transfers. Its single simulated payment domain, attack-selected corpus and disclosed policy mismatch sharply limit transfer claims.
