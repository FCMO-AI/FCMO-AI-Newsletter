Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The v2 paper specifies StarVLA-α as a compact VLA policy using a Qwen3-VL-4B vision-language backbone and an MLP action head, and describes both per-benchmark specialist training and one jointly trained generalist model.
  • Claimed — Authors report competitive specialist and generalist results across LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboCasa-GR1. Their Table 5 reports the generalist StarVLA-α results alongside specialist systems and baselines; performance is not uniformly best on every benchmark column.
  • Claimed — On the ARX5 RoboChallenge 11-task set, authors report StarVLA-α at 33.6% success and 54.5 progress, versus π0.5 at 12.7% and 27.6. The paper also reports three physical-robot OOD tasks and LIBERO-Plus perturbation results in appendices.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproducibility, general superiority of minimal architectures, or transfer to materially different robot/task distributions beyond its stated regimes.

Strongest baseline

In Table 5, the generalist StarVLA-α is compared with per-benchmark specialist systems including π0.5, GR00T-N1.6 and specialist StarVLA-α variants. The ARX5 RoboChallenge comparison reports π0.5 at 12.7% success / 27.6 progress against StarVLA-α at 33.6% / 54.5; these are author-run matched task-set results, not independent cross-lab measurements.

Limitations

  • All capability and benchmark measurements are author-reported; no independent reproduction or artifact audit was performed.
  • The reported generalist comparison covers four named benchmark families and uses the paper's data and training pipeline; it is not a universal comparison across VLA stacks.
  • The RoboChallenge result cited in the abstract is for an ARX5 11-task set; other appendix results use distinct platforms, tasks and settings.
  • Physical-robot trial counts and statistical uncertainty are incomplete in the inspected summaries; no deployment-level reliability or safety conclusion follows.
  • The paper's architectural simplicity does not establish that added components are unnecessary in other data, embodiment or control regimes.

What we still don't know

  • Reported simulation, RoboChallenge and OOD results have not been independently reproduced; the author-linked code/artifacts were not audited here.
  • Several physical-robot results and appendix summaries do not expose complete trial denominators or uncertainty estimates in the inspected sections.
  • The evidence does not establish transfer beyond the named benchmarks and robot setups or matched cost against more complex policies.

Why it matters

Vision-language-action results often entangle architecture changes with data pipelines, embodiment setup and benchmark-specific engineering. A simple, openly described baseline with cross-benchmark and physical-robot results can help researchers compare those choices and determine when extra architecture is useful. The paper does not establish that simpler policies generally win or that its reported gains transfer beyond the named tasks and setups.
