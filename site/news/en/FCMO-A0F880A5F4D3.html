Preprint reports that outcome-routed lessons can carry chance into later agent decisions

A preprint submitted to arXiv on October 12, 2026, reports a registered synthetic study of whether model-written lessons can transmit random outcomes into later agent choices. Across 256 two-tool worlds, the authors report that after a more reliable tool failed by chance, readers shown its outcome-routed lesson chose the worse tool in 11.6% of next-run choices, compared with 0.4% with irrelevant memory and 0.4% with a success-written lesson. The reported panel interaction was +0.112 (95% interval +0.097 to +0.126) across 11 served model endpoints. The authors report that two writing-instruction variants reduced the panel effect but did not meet their harm-reduction rule at every affected endpoint. The study tests one stored lesson and one constrained synthetic choice; it does not measure deployed self-improvement, accumulated memory, or real-system prevalence, and it cannot separate lesson advice from a repeated failure report. These are paper-reported results, not an independent reproduction.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — A paper by Kazuki Nakayashiki, submitted to arXiv on October 12, 2026, reports a registered study of outcome-written memory and later agent choices.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that, across 256 synthetic worlds and 11 model endpoints, readers shown a lesson written after a chance failure of the more reliable tool chose the worse tool in 11.6% of next-run choices, versus 0.4% with irrelevant memory and 0.4% with a success-written lesson; it reports a panel interaction of +0.112 with a 95% interval of +0.097 to +0.126.
  • Claimed — The paper reports lower panel-level worse-tool rates after adding a decision-quality note or using an instruction with a no-change option, but neither remedy met the registered harm-reduction rule at every endpoint that carried the effect.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that the effect occurs in deployed agents, accumulated or retrieved memory, or situations where an outcome signals a genuine change in tool reliability; it also cannot separate lesson advice from the lesson repeating the failure outcome.

Strongest baseline

Information-matched readers with an irrelevant memory item or a success-written lesson; the paper's direct missing control is a memory item that repeats the same failure as a fact without interpretation or advice.

Limitations

  • The study uses one stored lesson, read once, and a scripted previous run; it does not execute a self-improvement loop or measure accumulated/retrieved memory.
  • Synthetic worlds constrain the reader to choosing between two tools, and the study does not estimate how often analogous cases occur in real deployments.
  • The reader sees the authoritative record, and the paper does not test readers without it.
  • The study does not separate advice in the lesson from the lesson repeating the failure outcome; the authors identify a neutral failure-fact memory as the most direct missing control.
  • The remedies are tested only with transient failures that do not signal a reliability change; transfer to changed or broken tools is unknown.
  • The paper states that endpoint readings are exploratory, endpoints were tested on one day, and the study has not been replicated.
  • The arXiv record reports 90,084 valid reader answers; the paper reports 90,106 reader requests, including 22 unparsed responses. These are different denominators, not a contradiction.

What we still don't know

  • The registered paper's reported results have not been independently reproduced from its public code, protocol and records.
  • The experiment cannot separate lesson advice from a repeated report of the same failure and does not test accumulated memory or outcomes that signal a true reliability change.

Why it matters

If the effect transfers beyond the synthetic setup, systems that turn run outcomes into persistent instructions could encode chance failures as advice and alter later decisions. The paper also shows that simple writing-instruction changes did not reliably remove its measured effect across endpoints. The study's constrained one-lesson design and missing control limit any conclusion about production agents.
