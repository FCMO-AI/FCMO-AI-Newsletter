Anthropic’s preliminary S-1 reports rapid growth alongside compute financing exposure

Anthropic’s October 26, 2026 preliminary Form S-1 reports $11.6 billion in revenue and $1.0 billion in operating income for the three months ended June 30, 2026, compared with $787 million in revenue and a $1.2 billion operating loss in the same 2025 quarter. The filing says consumption-based services made up 80% of first-half 2026 revenue and 48% of revenue came through cloud-provider marketplaces. It reports $112.4 billion in cash and $19.3 billion in short-term investments at June 30, 2026; the six-month 2026 figures are unaudited. The filing also describes multi-year minimum-purchase and take-or-pay compute obligations, a shift toward leased accelerators and dedicated facilities, and conditional infrastructure financing. The filing describes capacity up to $42 billion under a Broadcom arrangement for 2026 lease obligations, up to $30 billion from Google (none issued by June 30, 2026), and up to $20 billion from Amazon; separately, it reports $10.2 billion aggregate principal of notes issued to two accredited investors by the filing date. These facility ceilings do not establish cash drawn, amounts paid or compute delivered. Several aggregate compute-commitment totals are left blank in the preliminary filing. Large reported net losses include non-cash fair-value remeasurement of convertible notes and related instruments. These disclosures describe the company’s reported results and financing structure; they do not establish compute utilization, future revenue, total realized compute cost or a completed IPO.

Limitations

  • The S-1 is preliminary; the proposed share count and offering price are blank, and the registration had not become effective in the reviewed filing.
  • Six-month 2026 financial figures are unaudited; results are issuer disclosures.
  • The filing attributes much of the reported net loss to non-cash fair-value remeasurement of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs; net loss must not be treated as operating cash burn.
  • The filing describes conditional partner facilities and up to amounts that are not equivalent to actual issuance, cash draw, spending or delivered compute.
  • Aggregate long-term compute commitment and payment amounts are blank in reviewed preliminary passages.
  • Cloud-provider marketplace revenue share is not the same as compute-provider share or compute utilization.
  • No evidence reviewed establishes future revenue persistence, total return on compute investment, or the final offering outcome.

What we still don't know

  • The preliminary prospectus leaves aggregate compute purchase and lease commitment totals and schedules blank in reviewed passages.
  • Conditional note-purchase and equity facilities are not reconciled here to actual issuance, drawdown, conversion, debt maturity or payment obligations.
  • The filing provides no supplier-level compute delivered/used, utilization, workload economics or matched unit-cost comparisons.
  • The registration and proposed IPO were not complete in the reviewed preliminary filing.

Why it matters

The filing gives a rare, detailed view of how one frontier AI company links model demand, usage-based sales, cloud distribution, long-dated compute commitments and capital access. If sustained, those relationships could materially shape compute procurement and the economics of frontier model development. The numbers are issuer disclosures, much of the 2026 period is unaudited, aggregate commitment totals are unavailable in the reviewed preliminary text, and no compute-utilization or future-return result follows from them.
