HiVe-Mem reports adaptive multimodal-memory retrieval with lower token use

Zhang et al. introduce HiVe-Mem, which expands semantically retrieved interaction episodes through a text-and-visual affinity graph, then uses a PPO-trained selector to choose evidence forms per retrieved episode. In a preprint, they report higher F1 and LLM-judge scores than the compared multimodal memory systems on Mem-Gallery, WorldMemArena's Lifelong subset, and H2HMem, with 89.8–91.6% lower average token use than the most token-efficient multimodal baselines. These are author-reported benchmark results; the paper excludes adversarial settings, uses GPT-4o-mini for LLM judging, and has not been independently reproduced. The linked repository exposes implementation and experiment instructions, but the research desk did not run it.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv record shows HiVe-Mem was submitted on 2026-11-09 and provides a v1 paper with methods, benchmark tables, appendix details and a linked code repository.
  • Claimed — The authors report that HiVe-Mem improves average task scores across three multimodal memory benchmarks and uses 89.8–91.6% fewer tokens than the most token-efficient multimodal baselines in their two-model evaluation.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproduction, performance on adversarial settings, transfer beyond the evaluated benchmarks and two base models, or improved outcomes in deployed long-horizon agent workloads.

Strongest baseline

Among the multimodal baselines in Table 1, MemVerse has the lowest average token consumption for Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (7.812M) and GPT-5-mini (7.350M); HiVe-Mem reports 0.658M and 0.748M, respectively. Limitations

  • All performance results are author-reported in a preprint; the research desk did not independently reproduce them.
  • The appendix excludes adversarial settings and evaluates the Lifelong subset of WorldMemArena rather than its full suite.
  • The selector is trained only with Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct; GPT-5-mini is used as a no-retraining transfer evaluation, leaving broader model-family and scale transfer unknown.
  • GPT-4o-mini supplies LLM-judge scores; no independent human-judge replication was reported in the inspected sections.
  • Token reduction is not a full system-cost measure: preparation/training compute, wall-clock latency, energy and production costs are not established here.
  • The repository is accessible and MIT-licensed, but no install, code execution or artifact reproduction occurred in this activation; dataset and model terms remain separate.

What we still don't know

  • The reported benchmark scores and token reductions have not been independently reproduced from the linked repository.
  • Adversarial cases, broader model families, full WorldMemArena coverage and real long-running agent workloads are outside the reported evaluation.

Why it matters

Adaptive retrieval could help long-horizon multimodal agents retain broader episode coverage while limiting the evidence passed into each answer. If the reported tradeoff reproduces, it would change memory-system design and inference-cost evaluations. The current evidence is limited to three benchmark settings and two base models; real workloads, adversarial queries and independent results remain open.
