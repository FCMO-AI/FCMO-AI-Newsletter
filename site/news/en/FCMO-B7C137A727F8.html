Stiefel-constrained attention paper attributes CIFAR-10 gain to scale-free optimization step

A v3 paper studies Stiefel-constrained query/key projections trained with a corrected Riemannian Adam update. On one CIFAR-10 image-patch benchmark at 10,000 examples, authors report a 6.79 percentage-point gain over AdamW across 12 paired starts; their 12-seed ablation attributes the improvement to a scale-free, step-capped update rather than to the tangent projector or equivariance. The optimizer took 1.82 seconds per epoch versus 0.73 seconds for the baseline. A separate modular-arithmetic grokking experiment finds no benefit for the constrained method; the paper’s positive grokking result is a single-seed pilot and remains unconfirmed. Independent replication and transfer to language-model training are open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On a CIFAR-10 patch benchmark with 10,000 examples, corrected Stiefel Riemannian Adam improves accuracy by 6.79 percentage points over matched AdamW across 12 paired starts (t=38.33; 12/12 starts).
  • Claimed — A 12-seed ablation attributes a +4.63-point gain to the scale-free step-capped rule and no measured gain to the tangent projector or equivariance. A targeted epsilon sweep reports a 2.6-point loss at epsilon=0.1 (p<0.001).
  • Claimed — The authors report optimizer epoch time of 1.82 seconds versus 0.73 seconds for the baseline, a 2.5× overhead.
  • Demonstrated limitation — In two five-seed modular-arithmetic grokking studies the constrained arm does not outperform the baseline; the claimed stable grokking under slingshot conditions is a single-seed pilot. The paper does not establish benefit on language-model training.

Strongest baseline

Matched AdamW under the same architecture and training schedule, plus the paper’s component ablation.

Limitations

  • Results are from one CIFAR-10 image-patch task; language-model or broad vision transfer is untested.
  • All empirical outcomes are paper-reported; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • The corrected optimizer takes 1.82 seconds per epoch versus 0.73 seconds for AdamW, a reported 2.5× overhead.
  • The earlier fixed-step Riemannian SGD result is mostly explained by orthonormal initialization, while the corrected Adam gain is attributed to the scale-free step; these variants must not be conflated.
  • Grokking benefit is not established: controlled multi-seed studies find no gain, and the positive slingshot result is a single-seed pilot.
  • The positive modular-arithmetic grokking signal does not persist: the paper reports reruns reversing the initial single-seed result, two five-seed studies where the constrained arm does not grok better, and no effect-size claim for the pilot.

What we still don't know

  • The reported paired CIFAR-10 patch result and scale-free-step ablation have not been independently rerun or audited against released code in this review.
  • Generalization beyond the small image-patch benchmark, especially to language-model attention training, is unknown; measured optimizer epoch overhead is 2.5×.

Why it matters

The paper asks whether constraining attention query/key projections improves learning and which part of that method earns any gain. Its seed-matched ablation argues that scale-free step normalization, rather than the geometric projector itself, explains the reported CIFAR-10 improvement. The result is narrow and slower to train, and controlled grokking tests are negative, so it is a training-method signal rather than evidence of broad language-model gains.
