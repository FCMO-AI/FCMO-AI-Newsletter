Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI published a report on 3 November 2026 describing AI-supported mechanical, electrical and physical-product engineering workflows.
  • Claimed — The BenchCAD leaderboard publicly lists an OpenAI internal unreleased model in a provider-credited Codex custom-harness result at 0.875 using the BenchCAD 2.0 scorer, with estimated cost of about $38 per case; it separately reports 0.594 for GPT-6 Astra at maximum effort on the standard 2.0 harness.
  • Demonstrated — BenchCAD 2.0 describes a 100-case held-out Core set spanning six tasks for CAD parts, assemblies and PCB connectivity, using reference-based scoring without an LLM judge in an offline sandbox.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that its hardware and infrastructure teams use Codex and ChatGPT for CAD, manufacturing drawings, simulations and design reviews, and that token usage across those teams has grown 23-fold since February.
  • Claimed — OpenAI says Chip Ganassi Racing engineers are reviewing scan-to-CAD outputs and that Mazda engineers are collaborating on model-assisted aerodynamic and crash-safety design exploration.
  • Not established — The newer model remains unreleased and unidentified; the 0.875 result is listed on a provider-credited custom-harness board with estimated cost, but no task-level run artifact or independent reproduction was inspected. The inspected sources do not establish measured workplace productivity gains or production-ready/manufacturable outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The board separately reports 0.594 for GPT-6 Astra at maximum effort on the standard 2.0 harness, while the 0.875 belongs to an OpenAI-credited Codex custom harness; because harnesses differ, this is not a matched or directly comparable baseline.

Limitations

  • The 0.875 result and the 23-fold internal hardware-team token-use increase are first-party reports.
  • The model is identified only as an unreleased OpenAI internal model. The 0.875 appears on BenchCAD’s separate custom-harness board, is credited to OpenAI and uses the 2.0 scorer; the listed ≈$38/case cost is an estimate. It is not an independent reproduction, and custom-harness scores are not comparable with the standard 2.1 board.
  • The benchmark measures bounded engineering tasks, not the full engineering lifecycle, field productivity, costs, safety or manufacturability.
  • OpenAI explicitly reports that prototype preparation required review and iteration and that Astra did not consistently account for manufacturer production limits and assembly details.
  • Collaborator examples are early exploratory work with engineer review; no measured time, cost, quality or production outcome is reported.

What we still don't know

  • The leaderboard attributes the 0.875 to an unreleased OpenAI internal model and a Codex custom harness using the 2.0 scorer, but no model identity, task-level run artifact or independent reproduction was inspected.
  • The article describes internal and collaborator workflows but provides no controlled measures of engineering time, cost, design quality or manufacturability.

Why it matters

CAD and engineering workflows combine geometry, code, simulation and manufacturing constraints. BenchCAD’s public listing gives the reported 0.875 a specific but bounded context: an unreleased OpenAI model, Codex custom harness, 2.0 scorer and estimated cost, separate from standard 2.1 results. This makes attribution clearer without independently validating the result or measuring end-to-end quality, time saved, costs, safety or manufacturability. OpenAI’s collaborator examples remain early engineer-reviewed work.
