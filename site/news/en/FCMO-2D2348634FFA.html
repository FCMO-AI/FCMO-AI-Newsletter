PaperFormalizer preprint reports author-approved repairs to proof gaps in three research papers

A Stanford-authored preprint introduces PaperFormalizer, a coding-agent pipeline that transcribes research statements, checks them for ambiguity and correctness, asks authors to decide claim-changing repairs, and formalizes reviewed results in Lean. The authors report using it on three of their papers: in a treatment-effects paper, five of seven results were false as printed and repaired with author approval; all seven revised results were then proved in Lean without `sorry` or axioms beyond Lean's standard ones. They report that 15 of 29 results needed revision in a second paper, while all 12 results in an optimal-transport paper were proved as printed. These are author-reported case studies, not an independent error rate or evidence of general accuracy.

Why it matters

The case study reports material proof and assumption gaps in published research and demonstrates a workflow that keeps authors responsible for changes to what a theorem claims. If the approach generalizes, it could make formal verification more usable in research practice. The reported cases are selected from the authors' own papers; independent reproduction, general effectiveness, time and token cost, and uptake beyond the authors are not established.
