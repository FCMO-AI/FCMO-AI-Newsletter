FSB issues 12 nonbinding AI governance practices for financial institutions

On October 15, the Financial Stability Board published a final report with 12 nonbinding practices for responsible AI adoption by financial institutions. The practices span board-level oversight and organization-wide risk governance, plus lifecycle controls for use-case assessment, model selection, data, transparency, performance, human oversight, cyber/ICT and third-party dependencies. The FSB says the report was revised after public consultation, including broader documentation and third-party-risk treatment and additional discussion of agentic and multi-agent systems. What changed

On October 15, the Financial Stability Board published a final report with 12 nonbinding practices for responsible AI adoption by financial institutions. The practices span board-level oversight and organization-wide risk governance, plus lifecycle controls for use-case assessment, model selection, data, transparency, performance, human oversight, cyber/ICT and third-party dependencies. The FSB says the report was revised after public consultation, including broader documentation and third-party-risk treatment and additional discussion of agentic and multi-agent systems. It calls the practices an adaptable toolkit rather than a binding standard; the report says agentic-specific guidance remains nascent.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The FSB's final report sets out 12 sound practices for AI governance in financial institutions, covering organization-wide governance and risk management across the AI lifecycle, including use-case materiality, technology selection, data governance, explainability, performance evaluation, human oversight, cyber/ICT and third-party risk.
  • Demonstrated — The FSB strongly encourages boards and senior management to consider the practices as a proportionate, adaptable toolkit; it expressly states that they are non-prescriptive, do not constitute a binding international standard, and do not replace local legal or regulatory requirements.
  • Claimed — The FSB says the final report responds to consultation feedback with expanded treatment of documentation, transparency, third-party AI risk and advanced agentic or multi-agent risks; it also says agentic and multi-agent-specific practices remain nascent and could warrant fuller treatment later.
  • Not established — The report does not establish that the practices have been adopted by financial institutions or supervisors, have reduced AI risk, or are binding requirements in any jurisdiction.

Strongest baseline

The final report builds on the FSB's June 2026 consultation draft and existing financial-sector AI work. The report identifies changes made in response to consultation but remains broadly compatible with ongoing work by the FSB and other standard setters; this review did not conduct a line-by-line draft comparison.

Limitations

  • The FSB states that its decisions and activities are not binding and that this report is not a binding international standard.
  • The practices are non-prescriptive, should be applied proportionately and do not replace local legal duties.
  • The report says agentic and multi-agent-specific practices remain nascent and may warrant more comprehensive future treatment.
  • Institutional case studies are illustrative, reflect benefits reported by participating institutions and are not independently established outcomes or benchmarks.
  • No national implementation, institutional uptake or resulting safety/financial-stability effect was measured in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No national supervisory implementation, adoption guidance, enforcement or institution-level uptake following the final report was checked.
  • The case studies illustrate institution-reported benefits and practices but are not independently audited outcomes or expected-performance benchmarks.

Why it matters

The report gives financial institutions and supervisors a common reference for evaluating AI governance across organizational and lifecycle controls, including model dependencies and increasingly autonomous systems. Its practical effect will depend on whether national authorities and firms incorporate the practices into local expectations and operations. The FSB expressly leaves them nonbinding and recognizes that agentic-AI guidance remains incomplete.
