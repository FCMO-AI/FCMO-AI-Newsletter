A SkillsBench study reports that agent-skill utility depends on model–harness fit and artifact organization

Cheng et al. report a controlled study of agent skills on 87 SkillsBench tasks across three models and three harnesses. The same benchmark-supplied skills help some configurations and hurt others on 32 tasks (36.78%). On 23 single-skill tasks, operation-support reranking raises first-choice pass rates by 4.35–5.80 points across three selected configurations; on multi-skill tasks, stage-plan and dependency-DAG organization generally outperform simple ordering. These are author-reported preprint results based on three runs per condition. The study does not measure runtime, token or maintenance costs, and independent transfer beyond the selected task and configuration sets remains unknown.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — Cheng et al. report controlled evaluations using 87 SkillsBench tasks, three models and three agent harnesses, with three executions per task and condition; reported outcomes are task-verifier pass rates.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that the same benchmark-supplied skills produce both gains and losses across model–harness configurations for 32 of 87 tasks (36.78%).
  • CLAIMED — For three selected configurations, reranking five marketplace candidates by support for atomic task operations improves first-choice pass rate over relevance-first ranking by 4.35–5.80 percentage points in the study’s existing execution records.
  • CLAIMED — For the same supplied skill sets, stage-plan or dependency-DAG instructions outperform simple use order in the reported overall results; DAG improves over flat organization by 6.50–11.38 percentage points across the three configurations.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The preprint does not establish that these gains transfer to other tasks, skill libraries, languages, models or harnesses, nor that they justify runtime, token and maintenance costs.
  • DEMONSTRATED — A direct retrieval attempt of the paper-linked anonymous artifact endpoint returned HTTP 401, so no artifact files or execution records were accessed.

Strongest baseline

Within-task comparisons use the same task verifier and model–harness configuration against No-Skill; organization comparisons retain the same supplied skills/resources while varying instructions. No independent held-out replication was inspected.

The caveat that matters

  • This is a single-group arXiv v1 preprint; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • Task pass/fail is the outcome measure; partial completion and runtime, token and maintenance costs are outside scope.
  • Three runs per condition leave task-level estimates sensitive to execution randomness.
  • The operation-support ranking uses LLM-assisted operation extraction and support labels with heuristic scores; the paper says alternative scoring or operation granularity could change rankings.
  • The reranking results use the existing candidate set and execution records, and were motivated by the studied task set; independent-task stability is open.
  • The study centers on English-language skills, 87 SkillsBench tasks and selected model–harness configurations; generalization to other skill corpora and workflows is unknown.
  • The paper-linked anonymous data/code endpoint returned HTTP 401 on direct retrieval; its contents were not inspected.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of the reported reranking or organization gains was conducted.
  • The paper links an anonymous repository for data, execution records and scripts; direct retrieval of its file endpoint returned HTTP 401, so contents remain inaccessible.
  • The study does not establish whether pass-rate gains justify added execution, token and maintenance costs.

FCMO Lens

Reusable procedural instructions can help or hinder depending on what an agent can execute and how the harness coordinates multiple skills. The study offers bounded evidence that checking operational support and preserving dependencies between intermediate artifacts may matter more than topic relevance or simple ordering alone; the gains need independent, cost-aware replication.
