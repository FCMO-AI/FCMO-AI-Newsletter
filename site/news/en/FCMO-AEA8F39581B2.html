What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces SWE-Flux, a benchmark for predicting dynamic code behavior in real Python repositories. It contains 480 questions across 12 repositories, with answers harvested from instrumented test executions. In the authors’ five-model evaluation, GPT-5.4 scored 37.71% overall; dataflow questions were especially difficult. An input-perturbation pipeline generated valid variants for 52 of 58 selected instances, which were harder for the tested models. These are paper-reported results; the linked benchmark repository and evaluations were not independently inspected or reproduced, and coverage is limited to Python and the selected repositories.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce SWE-Flux, a benchmark with 480 execution-grounded instances from 12 Python repositories, covering single-test and multi-test questions across seven dynamic-behavior categories. The gold answers are harvested from instrumented executions rather than written by annotators or judged by an LLM. The paper links a public repository, which was not inspected in this review.
  • Claimed — Across five evaluated models, the paper reports GPT-5.4 as the highest-scoring model at 37.71% overall accuracy. Its reported accuracy on dataflow questions is 6.78% for single-test questions and 0% for multi-test questions; the authors report substantial semantic rather than formatting errors in a manual analysis of 299 GPT-5.4 incorrect answers.
  • Claimed — In a selected input-perturbation study, the authors report that 52 of 58 instances yielded valid variants. GPT-5.4 accuracy fell from 100% on those selected originals to 43.1% on the perturbed instances; two models not used to guide perturbation also fell from 48.3% to 24.1% and from 46.6% to 24.1%. These results are confined to the paper's selected Python instances and evaluation setup.

Strongest baseline

GPT-5.4 reports the highest overall score at 37.71%, followed by GPT-5.3-Codex at 33.75%. On dataflow, GPT-5.4 reports 6.78% for single-test and 0% for multi-test questions. Results are author-reported and benchmark-specific.

Limitations

  • The evaluation covers 480 instances from 12 Python repositories and five models; other languages and broader repository populations are not established.
  • The linked repository and model evaluations were not independently inspected or reproduced in this review.
  • Evaluated models could inspect repositories and use navigation tools but could not execute code; the benchmark therefore measures the stated no-execution prediction setup.
  • The 58-instance perturbation study is selected from items GPT-5.4 initially answered correctly and does not estimate performance on a representative deployment workload.
  • Paper-reported benchmark accuracy is not a direct measure of end-to-end coding-agent success or safety.

What we still don't know

  • The linked benchmark repository, instances and reported model runs were not independently inspected or reproduced.
  • All benchmark instances are drawn from 12 Python repositories; transfer to other languages, repository types and deployment workloads is unknown.
  • Models were evaluated without code execution; retrieval and tool-use constraints may contribute to observed errors and the results do not directly measure full coding-agent outcomes.
  • The 58 perturbed instances were selected from examples GPT-5.4 answered correctly, so their difficulty increase is conditional on that selection and does not establish a broad distributional shift.

Why it matters

Coding agents must often predict what repository code will do across calls, state changes and test suites, including when they cannot safely or practically execute it. SWE-Flux offers an execution-grounded way to measure that task and reports weak results on several dynamic reasoning categories in its tested setup. Independent artifact inspection, broader language coverage and reproduction are needed before treating its scores as general capability estimates.
