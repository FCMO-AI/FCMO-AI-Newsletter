OpenAI launches Grid Protectors and cites a large flexible-load agreement for its planned Georgia campus

OpenAI announced Grid Protectors on 2 November 2026 as a program to arrange flexible power use at future compute campuses. It cites a Georgia Power agreement for a planned Effingham County campus expected to require about 3,200 MW, with up to 1,000 MW of demand response under a 25-year term. Georgia Power said its contract to serve OpenAI was approved through the Georgia PSC regulatory process. A joint PSC Staff/Georgia Power statement records staff review, compliance with prior orders and non-objection subject to stated terms; the PSC filing describes a 3,210 MW contract as deemed approved but does not identify its customer, and its public-summary ZIP was not inspectable. Georgia Power projects about $950 million in annual portfolio savings beginning in 2029 and at least $180 per year for a typical residential customer; these are future projections. The campus is not operating, and actual curtailment and customer outcomes remain unmeasured.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI announced the Grid Protectors program on 2 November 2026, describing a plan to develop future campus arrangements that reduce electricity use when local grids are under strain.
  • Claimed — Georgia Power says a 25-year agreement for OpenAI’s planned Effingham County facility, expected to require about 3,200 MW, permits up to 1,000 MW of flexible demand response during high-demand periods.
  • Claimed — OpenAI describes the Georgia arrangement as a Grid Protectors pilot; Georgia Power said on 26 August 2026 that its contract to serve OpenAI’s Effingham project had been approved through the Georgia PSC regulatory process.
  • Claimed — In an August 2026 filing, NRDC, Sierra Club and Southern Alliance for Clean Energy asked Georgia PSC staff to object to the OpenAI contract unless enforceable customer protections were provided and sought public disclosure; this was an advocacy request, not a Commission finding.
  • Demonstrated — A 26 August joint statement by Georgia PSC staff and Georgia Power says staff reviewed the July-filed large-load contract, confirmed compliance with prior orders and protections for non-large-load customers subject to stated conditions, and did not object; the statement is not a Commission order.
  • Claimed — Georgia Power reports that the approved OpenAI contract contributes to projected portfolio savings of about $950 million annually and $2.847 billion over 2029–2031, equivalent to at least $180 annually for a typical 1,000-kWh/month residential customer; these are future portfolio projections, not realized savings.
  • Not established — The inspected evidence does not establish actual curtailment, avoided generation or transmission investment, realized customer savings, or the unredacted contract terms. A PSC docket filing identifies a 3,210 MW contract as deemed approved but does not name its customer; the linked public-summary ZIP could not be inspected.

Limitations

  • Grid Protectors remains a program announcement; the Effingham campus is planned, not operational.
  • Georgia Power reports that its OpenAI contract was approved through the PSC process, and a joint PSC Staff/Company statement documents staff review and non-objection; neither is an independently measured operating outcome.
  • The PSC cover letter says an anonymous 3,210 MW contract was deemed approved; the linked public summary ZIP could not be inspected and the cover letter does not name the customer.
  • The reported $950 million annual portfolio savings, $2.847 billion over 2029–2031 and at least $180 per year for a typical customer are future projections, not realized customer savings.
  • Actual demand-response dispatch, avoided infrastructure investment and system reliability/customer effects remain unmeasured.
  • The August intervenor filing raised requests for safeguards and disclosure; it is not evidence that the Commission found the contract inadequate.

Why it matters

A large flexible compute load can either add pressure to grid planning or become a resource if dispatch is enforceable and delivered. The Georgia process includes staff review, non-objection and utility-reported customer-protection terms, while projected savings remain unmeasured and the contract summary is not inspectable here. Actual dispatch and customer outcomes will determine whether the model delivers its claimed grid and ratepayer benefits.
