OpenAI announced Grid Protectors on 2 November 2026 as a program to arrange flexible power use at future compute campuses. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI announced the Grid Protectors program on 2 November 2026, describing a plan to develop future campus arrangements that reduce electricity use when local grids are under strain.
  • Claimed — Georgia Power says a 25-year agreement for OpenAI’s planned Effingham County facility, which it expects to require about 3,200 MW, permits up to 1,000 MW of flexible demand response during high-demand periods.
  • Claimed — OpenAI says it will pay the project’s full infrastructure and electric-service costs and describes the Georgia arrangement as a pilot for its Grid Protectors program; the campus is still in development.
  • Claimed — In an August 2026 filing recorded by the Georgia PSC, NRDC, Sierra Club and Southern Alliance for Clean Energy asked Commission staff to object to the OpenAI contract unless enforceable protections cover costs and requested greater public disclosure; this is an advocacy filing, not a Commission finding.
  • Not established — The inspected evidence does not establish actual curtailment, avoided generation or transmission investment, realized customer savings, the full terms of the redacted contract, or the Georgia PSC’s final disposition. The official docket page still lists docket 44280 as open; its calendar lists an October 29, 2026 hearing on petitioners’ challenge to trade-secret information and request for access, but the page provides no hearing outcome.

Why it matters

Large compute campuses can create very large grid loads. A binding ability to curtail load could make some demand a system resource, while long-lived generation and transmission investments create cost-allocation and stranded-asset questions. The program’s value depends on the contract’s enforceable terms, deployment and measured grid outcomes.
