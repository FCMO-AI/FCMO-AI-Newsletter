What changed

The authors combine a closed-loop engineering-change-order workflow with a Qwen3.5-9B model trained on repair demonstrations and action preferences. Across 22 chip designs and 594 runs, they report a mean repair score of 79.65 for INT8 and 79.63 for BF16, versus 77.44 for GPT-6 Astra under a shared execution protocol. Evidence

  • Claimed — Across 22 designs and three repeats per model-design pair, the authors report WaveletECO-Policy INT8 and BF16 repair scores of 79.65 and 79.63, respectively, compared with 77.44 for GPT-6 Astra under the same execution protocol and a 1% area-growth limit.
  • Claimed — The authors estimate INT8 inference at $0.00226 per task and GPT-6 Astra at $0.33188 per task, or about 1/147 the cost, based on measured local throughput and dated API and GPU-rental prices; the estimate excludes EDA and idle costs.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that the cost ratio or score advantage transfers to different EDA backends, larger design populations, other technology nodes or end-to-end production costs; the deployments used for cost estimation were not re-evaluated for repair quality.

Strongest baseline

GPT-6 Astra scored 77.44 under the same ECO execution interface across the same design set. The authors also compare local Qwen3.5-9B direct and thinking variants, WaveletECO SFT, GPT-5.6 Sol and DeepSeek V4. The quality score and cost estimate are not a matched deployment comparison because cost-estimation deployments were not re-evaluated for repair quality.

Limitations

  • All experimental findings and costs are author-reported; the research desk did not reproduce the benchmark or cost model.
  • The benchmark has 22 designs, nine held out; the paper does not establish transfer to larger or more diverse designs and technology nodes.
  • The shared execution interface uses one EDA backend, one candidate repair action per round and a 1% area-growth limit.
  • The 1/147 ratio is estimated inference cost only. It uses fixed API prices and GPU-rental/throughput estimates and excludes EDA and idle costs; the cost-estimation deployments were not re-evaluated for repair quality.

What we still don't know

  • Repair-quality and cost claims have not been independently reproduced; the cost model is limited to inference and specified September 2026 prices/rental proxies.
  • Benchmark uses 22 designs from one setting and one EDA backend; transfer across nodes, design types and toolchains is unmeasured.

Why it matters

If the reported quality and cost estimates transfer to larger design sets and production workflows, a specialized local model could make multi-round physical chip repair cheaper while reducing dependence on paid frontier APIs. The reported cost ratio concerns inference only and uses estimated local GPU rental and throughput; it does not establish end-to-end EDA economics or a general advantage over frontier models.
