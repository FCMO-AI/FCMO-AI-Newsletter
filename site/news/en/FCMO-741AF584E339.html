Differentiable soft-symbolic planning links visual uncertainty to rule-constrained action planning

A September 18 preprint presents a differentiable planner that keeps visual scene relations as continuous symbolic beliefs, propagates actions through soft logical transition rules, and lets the task objective update both action choices and perception parameters. On Blocksworld, the authors report 40/40 valid solutions on LatPlan-40 versus 33/40 for LatPlan and 596/600 on PlanBench-600 versus the published o1-preview score of 587/600. On a fixed 100-instance blurred-image subset, planning gradients raise valid plans from 59/100 with frozen perception to 83/100. The approach also reports results on Logistics and Sokoban. These are paper-reported, domain-specific results; solve-time and estimated-cost comparisons use different systems and are not a matched economic comparison.

On Blocksworld, the authors report 40/40 valid solutions on LatPlan-40 versus 33/40 for LatPlan and 596/600 on PlanBench-600 versus the published o1-preview score of 587/600. Evidence

  • Claimed — On image-based Blocksworld, the authors report 40/40 valid plans on LatPlan-40 versus 33/40 for LatPlan and 596/600 on PlanBench-600 versus the published o1-preview result of 587/600; their method uses three independent planning seeds.
  • Claimed — On a fixed 100-instance blurred-image subset of PlanBench-600, valid plans increase from 59/100 with frozen perception to 83/100 when planning gradients also update perception parameters.
  • Claimed — The paper reports 196/200 valid plans on Logistics versus 188/200 for the cited o1-preview result, and 33/55 on Sokoban versus 7/55. The remaining failures are non-goal-reaching rather than invalid action sequences.
  • Not established — The reported latency and estimated cost advantages do not establish a matched compute or economic advantage over reasoning models; the method was timed on an NVIDIA A100 and model comparison numbers are sourced from prior benchmark reports.

Strongest baseline

LatPlan on LatPlan-40; the published PlanBench o1-preview result on PlanBench-600; frozen perception on the blurred-image ablation. Baseline runs were not all conducted on the same hardware or under one matched compute budget.

Limitations

  • Paper-reported results have not been independently reproduced; the three planning seeds are internal replications by the authors, not an external reproduction.
  • Main results use compact benchmark domains; transfer to open-world visual perception and richer embodied actions is not established.
  • Blurred-image perception benefit was measured on one fixed 100-instance subset using a synthetic 6-pixel Gaussian blur.
  • The comparison of solve times and estimated costs uses different systems and previously published model results, so it is not a matched economic comparison.
  • Task-and-motion execution was demonstrated in PyBullet simulation, not on a physical robot.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the paper’s planning and perception results was inspected.
  • The experiments focus on compact relational domains and synthetic/benchmark images; the authors identify larger entity counts, richer action schemas, partial observability and geometric constraints as future work.
  • Reported solve-time and estimated-cost comparisons mix an A100-based local planner with prior model-evaluation results and different execution systems.

Why it matters

The method addresses a real interface problem in embodied planning: a hard perception-to-symbol handoff prevents later task reasoning from correcting uncertain scene relations. Its soft rule-constrained rollout preserves uncertainty and passes task gradients back into perception. The reported gains are promising within compact symbolic domains, but they do not establish performance on open-world perception, complex action schemas or physical robot execution.
