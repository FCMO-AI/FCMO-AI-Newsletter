A trained concept generator raises reported pass@128 on filtered hard-math sets

A paper trains a 7B concept generator with reinforcement learning to propose reasoning strategies for a frozen larger answer model. On a 1,000-item DeepMath hard subset selected for zero Qwen2.5-32B success in 128 prior rollouts, the authors report pass@128 of 39.2% versus 19.0% for repeated sampling at the same answer-rollout allocation; on a similarly filtered Omni-MATH 2 set, they report 18.60% versus 11.29%. A limited transfer evaluation reports a smaller gain when steering Llama-3.3-70B. These remain author-reported, task-filtered benchmark results: evaluation reuses the Qwen answer model as judge, training reward estimation is compute-heavy, and no independent reproduction or broad cost advantage is established.

What changed

A paper trains a 7B concept generator with reinforcement learning to propose reasoning strategies for a frozen larger answer model. On a 1,000-item DeepMath hard subset selected for zero Qwen2.5-32B success in 128 prior rollouts, the authors report pass@128 of 39.2% versus 19.0% for repeated sampling at the same answer-rollout allocation; on a similarly filtered Omni-MATH 2 set, they report 18.60% versus 11.29%. A limited transfer evaluation reports a smaller gain when steering Llama-3.3-70B. These remain author-reported, task-filtered benchmark results: evaluation reuses the Qwen answer model as judge, training reward estimation is compute-heavy, and no independent reproduction or broad cost advantage is established.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In the paper’s model-filtered hard-set evaluations at matched answer-rollout allocation, an RL-trained Qwen2.5-7B concept generator steering a frozen Qwen2.5-32B answer model reports pass@128 of 39.2% versus 19.0% repeated sampling on 1,000 held-out DeepMath problems, and 18.60% versus 11.29% on a separately filtered Omni-MATH 2 set.
  • Claimed — A trained concept generator evaluated on the same DeepMath set with Llama-3.3-70B as answer generator reports 34.3% pass@128 versus 26.2% for repeated sampling, although that set was filtered for the Qwen2.5-32B model and the Qwen model remains the judge.
  • Claimed — The paper’s re-evaluation of an earlier iterative concept-guided sampling method reports that its gains largely disappear against exploratory repeated sampling across 25 Qwen2.5 size pairs on MATH500; the paper attributes the trained method’s results to single-trajectory, multiple-concept generation and RL training of the concept generator.
  • Not established — The reported results do not establish gains on ordinary or broad mathematical reasoning workloads, deployment cost savings, or independently reproduced performance; the training reward loop is compute-heavy and benchmark subsets are selected for poor baseline performance.

Strongest baseline

Naive repeated sampling with the same answer-rollout allocation and exploratory decoding; the paper also compares generic prompting, untuned concept generators, and its predecessor’s iterative guided-sampling protocol.

Limitations

  • All results are author-reported; no independent artifact audit or reproduction was conducted.
  • The principal DeepMath and Omni-MATH 2 sets are model-specific filtered hard subsets with zero observed baseline success in 128 rollouts; they do not represent general benchmark performance.
  • The main judge is Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, also the answer generator; cross-family transfer uses the same judge.
  • Training reward estimation is compute-heavy; the paper does not establish a deployment-scale total-cost break-even.
  • The paper reports a modest cross-family inference-time gain in a limited subset, but it does not demonstrate general reasoning gains across models, tasks or ordinary workloads.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the reported concept-generation training and evaluation was conducted.
  • The primary DeepMath and Omni-MATH 2 sets retain only problems with zero observed repeated-sampling success in 128 draws; performance on ordinary benchmark distributions is not established.
  • The authors describe reward estimation as compute-heavy, using eight concept trajectories and 128 answer-generator rollouts per trajectory during training; full training-to-inference break-even is not established.
  • Main evaluation correctness judgments reuse Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, the answer generator, and remain subject to judge error.

Why it matters

The paper studies whether a small trainable policy can steer a larger frozen model toward diverse problem-solving strategies instead of spending inference compute on repeated unconditioned answers. Its hard-subset results are substantial but carefully bounded; training cost, subset selection and the same-family judge leave deployment economics and general reasoning impact unresolved.
