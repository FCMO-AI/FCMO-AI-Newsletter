ADIndex reports faster sparse-attention indexing for long-context inference

Qi et al. introduce ADIndex, which calibrates token-bank size by layer and distills a lightweight group indexer to share candidate tokens across neighboring queries in DeepSeek Sparse Attention. In author-reported SGLang experiments on NVIDIA B300 GPUs with DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5.1, ADIndex stays within one percentage point of standard DSA on RULER at 16K–128K contexts, while reported indexing-kernel speedups reach 6.22× for prefill and 3.40× for decode at 256K; the paper reports 1.54× end-to-end speed at 128K. Some shorter-context configurations are slower than DSA, and code or independent reproduction was not established in this review.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Qi et al. published ADIndex, a layer-adaptive sparse-attention indexing method, as arXiv:2611.12956v1 on 7 November 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report that on DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5.1, ADIndex remained within one percentage point of the original DSA on RULER at each tested context length from 16K through 128K. At 128K, the reported scores were 93.34 versus 93.69 for DeepSeek-V3.2 and 94.49 versus 94.97 for GLM-5.1. These results were not independently reproduced here.
  • Claimed — The paper reports custom indexing-kernel speedups over DSA of 3.15×–6.22× for prefill and 2.46×–3.40× for decode at 256K across four reuse configurations, and 1.54× end-to-end speed at a 128K KV-cache length. It also reports end-to-end slowdowns at shorter contexts for some configurations, including 0.69× at 16K for one configuration and 0.86× for its layer-wise setting.
  • Claimed — The appendix analytical model counts each score evaluation at equal cost regardless of indexing-head count and excludes pooling, projections, candidate selection and kernel-launch overhead; its scoring-operation reductions are not themselves end-to-end latency measurements.
  • Not established — The inspected paper does not establish independent reproduction, performance on hardware other than the reported NVIDIA B300 setting, or realized serving-cost savings in production workloads.

Strongest baseline

Original DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) is the main baseline; the paper also compares HISA, MISA, IndexCache and PIVOT.

Limitations

  • All task-performance and efficiency measurements are author-reported; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • The reported experiment uses SGLang and NVIDIA B300 GPUs with two models; cross-stack, cross-hardware and production transfer are unestablished.
  • ADIndex can be slower than DSA at shorter contexts; the paper reports configuration-specific end-to-end slowdowns at 16K and 32K.
  • The appendix operation-count model assigns equal cost to different indexer heads and excludes pooling, projection, candidate-selection and kernel-launch overheads; these ratios are not wall-clock latency.
  • The 1.54× end-to-end result is at a 128K KV-cache length and does not establish general production savings.

What we still don't know

  • No author-linked implementation or pinned code artifact was found in the arXiv record or the earlier bounded search.
  • The benchmark and efficiency results remain author-reported; no independent matched reproduction was conducted.

Why it matters

The paper targets a systems bottleneck in sparse-attention inference: repeated full-prefix scoring can remain costly even after attention is sparsified. Its bounded results suggest layer-aware sharing may improve long-context throughput while retaining benchmark performance, which could affect serving choices if independently reproduced on relevant hardware and workloads.
