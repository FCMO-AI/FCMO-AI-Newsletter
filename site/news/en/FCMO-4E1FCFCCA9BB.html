VA-Bench measures the gap between spatial diagnosis and successful robot action

Zhang et al. introduce VA-Bench, a simulator benchmark requiring general-purpose multimodal models to use RGB-only demonstrations, actively choose camera views, issue metric Cartesian robot commands and revise actions from execution feedback. Across 14 base task families and 12 primary model conditions, the authors report three runs over the same 20 physically verified seeds per task. The strongest tested condition has a three-run macro-average terminal task success of 53.93±3.17%, despite 100.0% target localization and 78.9% spatial-relation scores in its annotated run. In one matched active-camera versus passive five-view comparison, success rises from 27.86% to 57.50%; this comparison covers four model conditions and does not identify whether view relevance, cross-view integration or longer visual context explains the gain. No tested model completes the strict five-object long-horizon episode. These are author-reported benchmark results, not an independent reproduction.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report a 14-family simulator benchmark with 12 primary model conditions and three runs over the same 20 verified seeds per base task; the strongest tested condition averaged 53.93±3.17% terminal success.
  • CLAIMED — In the strongest condition’s annotated run, the paper reports 100.0% target-localization and 78.9% spatial-relation scores, while no tested model completes the strict five-object long-horizon episode.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports one matched active-camera versus passive five-view comparison increasing success from 27.86% to 57.50%; the four-condition protocol comparison does not identify which aspect of active viewing causes the difference.

Strongest baseline

For active evidence acquisition, the paper compares camera control with passive observation that supplies five predefined views at each observation while preserving the same seeds, demonstrations, robot execution and terminal-success accounting. It also reports held-out geometry/layout transfer against the base-task regime.

The caveat that matters

  • All measurements are author-reported simulator results; no independent execution was performed.
  • The annotated behavioral diagnostics use one run per condition; terminal success is repeated across three runs.
  • The active/passive protocol comparison is limited to four model conditions and combines possible view relevance, cross-view integration and context-length effects.
  • Held-out geometry/layout variants are finite and do not establish arbitrary open-set or real-robot generalization.
  • The strict long-horizon result covers one five-object composition family.
  • The paper does not intervene on perception, planning and execution components to identify which causes each observed failure.

What remains unknown

  • VA-Bench results and the active/passive comparison have not been independently executed.
  • The simulator task set and one five-object composition family do not establish open-set or real-robot transfer.

FCMO Lens

The benchmark separates describing or locating objects from acquiring useful visual evidence, grounding metric actions and completing manipulation. Its reported gap between strong intermediate diagnostics and terminal success, plus the bounded active/passive comparison, gives embodied-agent evaluation a way to test the full observe–reason–act–revise loop rather than perception alone. The simulator, finite task families and untested real-robot transfer limit generalization.
