On-policy power distillation trains a model to absorb sequence-level answer sharpening

Potraghloo and colleagues present on-policy power distillation (OPPD), which uses sequential Monte Carlo samples from the model being trained and a frozen teacher’s sharpened sequence distribution to reweight a maximum-likelihood update. The authors report single-generation accuracy increases of up to 23.0 points on MATH500 and 27.3 points on GSM8K over the untrained model at the same temperature, and report gains in a same-checkpoint GRPO comparison. Their abstract also claims performance above published 64-candidate power-sampling results; the existing candidate review found that this comparison uses a different evaluation protocol, while the same-paper 16-candidate comparison is close and does not show OPPD ahead. These are author-reported preprint outcomes, not independent validation or a matched total training-plus-inference cost result.

FCMO Lens

The method aims to absorb some benefit of answer-distribution sharpening into training, potentially reducing inference-time sampling for reasoning tasks. The key decision is whether single-sample gains persist under same prompts, checkpoints, seeds, and total compute budgets, and whether they transfer beyond mathematics.
