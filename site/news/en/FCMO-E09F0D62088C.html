On-policy distillation improves reported reasoning retention at sub-3-bit weights

A September 2026 preprint studies why quantization-aware distillation can preserve short-form answers yet leave long mathematical and code generations impaired at sub-3-bit weight precision. The authors add on-policy distillation (OPD), which samples through the deployment quantized path and applies teacher guidance to the student's own generated prefixes, alongside task verifiers. Across Qwen3 and Falcon3 models at 2.79 and 1.88 effective weight bits, they report higher math and code benchmark performance than the QAD starting checkpoints, including gains over a matched-budget continued-QAD control in all 16 reported comparisons. The released QAOPD repository links checkpoints and reproduction instructions. These remain author-reported benchmark results; independent reproduction is open. The paper also reports a material implementation caveat: OPD uses a 16-bit KV cache in the principal arms rather than the released 8-bit KV-cache configuration, a deviation the authors say favors their results.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published a preprint and linked a public QAOPD repository with evaluation checkpoints and instructions for evaluating released weights and reproducing OPD phase 1.
  • Claimed — Across four models at 2.79 and 1.88 effective weight bits, the authors report that OPD raises average BF16 performance retention on MATH-500 from 35% to 70% and on HumanEval from 66% to 91%, while preserving short-form performance.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that OPD outperforms continued teacher-forced QAD in all 16 matched-budget comparisons shown for GSM8K, MATH-500, MBPP and HumanEval, with shared starting checkpoints, corpora, phase schedules, step counts, learning rates and samples per step.
  • Claimed — For selected configurations, the authors report OPD recovery using fewer GPU-hours than QAD initialization, including 57 versus 820 GPU-hours for Qwen3-4B at 1.88 bits and 11 versus 253 GPU-hours for Falcon3-1B-Instruct at 2.79 bits; these figures compare different stages and do not establish total-cost superiority over alternative deployment pipelines.
  • Demonstrated — The principal OPD arms use a 16-bit KV cache, while the released EdgeRazor configuration referenced for comparison uses an 8-bit KV cache; the authors state this deviation favors their results.
  • Demonstrated — The paper reports reproducing its QAD initialization from the released recipe, with a smaller effective batch than the recipe specifies and the optional offline regeneration step omitted; the reported QAD and OPD comparisons inherit these stated deviations.

Strongest baseline

The paper's strongest direct baseline is continued teacher-forced QAD from a shared QAD checkpoint under matched corpora, phase schedules, optimizer steps, learning rates and samples per step, evaluated on GSM8K, MATH-500, MBPP and HumanEval (Table 4). Its additional quantization-method comparison uses Qwen3-1.7B at aligned low-bit settings, but some methods use wider whole-bit fallbacks and generative benchmark head/embedding precision differs.

Limitations

  • All performance and training-budget results are reported by the paper authors; this activation did not run the repository or reproduce any table.
  • The principal OPD experiments use a 16-bit KV cache rather than the released 8-bit configuration, so they do not directly demonstrate the same results with a fully 8-bit KV deployment path.
  • The paper's reproduced QAD starting point uses a smaller effective batch than the released recipe specifies and omits optional offline teacher regeneration; comparisons inherit those implementation choices.
  • The matched control and quantization baselines are author-run; independent baseline alignment, seed variation and hardware replication remain open.
  • Benchmark results cover four model checkpoints and fixed math, code and QA tasks; transfer to larger models, other architectures, other tasks and real serving workloads is not established.
  • GPU-hour comparisons are for selected training stages, not total pipeline cost, inference latency, energy, or a complete deployment cost model.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of OPD results, matched-QAD control, or released-checkpoint evaluation has been completed.
  • The principal OPD results use a 16-bit KV cache rather than the referenced released 8-bit KV configuration; performance under a deployment-matched 8-bit KV cache is unknown.
  • Transfer beyond four small-to-mid-sized checkpoints and the selected math, code and short-form tasks has not been established.

Why it matters

If the reported gains reproduce, training on the trajectories a quantized model actually visits could recover some long-form reasoning and coding capability at very low weight precision, potentially improving the quality-cost frontier for constrained inference. The result is limited to four small-to-mid-sized model families/configurations and the paper's benchmark protocols; it does not establish a general low-bit solution or end-to-end deployment advantage.
