BOOST reports concurrent host-memory and HBM gains for LLM serving

The September 11 preprint presents BOOST, a runtime that places model weights and KV pages so GPU threadblock waves can access host memory and HBM concurrently in proportion to their bandwidth. On one Grace Hopper system with vLLM, the authors report 4.3% lower time-per-output-token than HBM-only serving at fixed batch size. In capacity-scaled high-throughput tests they report 31% higher average throughput than HBM-only and a 15% advantage over prefetching. The throughput result includes additional usable capacity and should not be interpreted as a latency reduction; when capacity is held constant, the authors isolate about four percentage points from concurrent bandwidth use. These claims have not been independently reproduced here.

What changed

The September 11 preprint presents BOOST, a runtime that places model weights and KV pages so GPU threadblock waves can access host memory and HBM concurrently in proportion to their bandwidth. On one Grace Hopper system with vLLM, the authors report 4.3% lower time-per-output-token than HBM-only serving at fixed batch size. These claims have not been independently reproduced here.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that BOOST's wave-aware page placement enables concurrent proportional access to host memory and HBM without kernel changes. On one Grace Hopper system integrated with vLLM, they report 4.3% lower time-per-output-token than HBM-only serving at fixed batch size, and 31% higher throughput on average than HBM-only serving in capacity-scaled high-throughput tests, 15% ahead of prefetching. These are author-reported results and have not been independently reproduced in this review.

Strongest baseline

HBM-only serving and prefetching are named in the abstract; detailed configurations and model-specific comparisons require full-paper review.

Limitations

  • All performance results cited here are reported by the preprint authors and were not independently measured in this activation.
  • The paper reports experiments on a Grace Hopper system with vLLM; generalization to other interconnects, hardware, models and serving stacks is unknown.
  • The abstract distinguishes fixed-batch TPOT from capacity-scaled throughput. The 31% throughput result should not be read as a 31% latency reduction.
  • Full tables, code availability and detailed baseline settings were not inspected in this review.

What we still don't know

  • The paper's performance measurements are from a single Grace Hopper configuration; no independent reproduction or evidence of transfer to other CPU-GPU links, accelerator generations, serving stacks or workloads was recovered.

Why it matters

HBM capacity and bandwidth constrain model serving. If BOOST's results hold, direct concurrent use of host-memory and HBM bandwidth could raise throughput while expanding the runnable working set. The reported capacity-scaled throughput gain bundles more capacity with a smaller contribution from concurrent bandwidth use, so it does not imply the same percentage reduction in latency or universal gains across systems.
