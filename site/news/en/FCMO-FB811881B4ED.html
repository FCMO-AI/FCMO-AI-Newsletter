CARE reports improved recovery from robot failures using staged corrective demonstrations

CARE trains robot policies to recover from failures drawn from their own execution patterns. It collects unsuccessful rollouts, models stage-conditioned deviations, synthesizes failure states and corrective demonstrations, and uses a staged planner with geometric monitoring before a unified vision-language-action executor. On FSR-Bench, the authors report improved recovery over comparison methods across five simulation tasks and four real dual-arm tasks. The real-robot evaluation uses a 12-DoF SO-101 platform and 100 trials per task; the paper reports a 15.9 percentage-point gain in its real-world comparison and a 7.5-point gain on the recovery-focused FSR measure. These are author-reported results in a single paper; the research desk did not independently reproduce them.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On four real dual-arm tasks using a 12-DoF SO-101 platform, the authors report 100 trials per task and a 15.9 percentage-point gain in their overall comparison; their recovery-focused FSR measure improves by 7.5 points. These are results from the authors’ evaluation, not an independent reproduction.
  • Claimed — On FSR-Bench, comprising 36 recovery scenarios across five tasks, the paper reports improved recovery, with train/test failure distributions separated. It also evaluates seven hard bimanual RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and three multi-arm RoboFactory tasks. The reported results are benchmark-specific and do not establish transfer to other platforms or failure distributions.

Strongest baseline

Limitations

  • The quantitative findings are reported by the paper authors; no independent reproduction was inspected.
  • Real-robot evaluation covers four dual-arm tasks on one 12-DoF SO-101 platform with 100 trials per task; this does not establish cross-platform transfer.
  • FSR-Bench and the paper’s simulation suites are bounded benchmark regimes; performance on other failure distributions and deployment conditions remains unknown.
  • The reported gain depends on the full system, which combines failure mining, stage-conditioned correction, geometric monitoring and a staged planner; component-level attribution requires matched ablations.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction was inspected. Real-robot evidence covers four tasks on one SO-101 platform; transfer to other embodiments and failure distributions is unknown.

Why it matters

Robots often fail after leaving the nominal demonstration path, while arbitrary perturbations may not resemble a policy’s actual failures. CARE reports gains from mining failures produced by the policy and training stage-specific corrections. If independently confirmed, this could shape how long-horizon robot recovery is trained and evaluated; current evidence is limited to the authors’ benchmark and one real-robot platform.
