NIST’s new adversarial-ML draft adds a taxonomy for agentic-system attacks

NIST’s 9 November initial public draft SP 2400-102e2026 extends its adversarial machine-learning taxonomy to agentic systems. Section 4 classifies attacks across workflow stages and attacker capabilities, including attacks using untrusted data and memory, malicious tools and skills, compromised agents in multi-agent systems, and long-horizon task persistence. NIST says the taxonomy is intended to inform future standards and practice guides; public comments are open through 18 December. This is a voluntary draft, not evidence of adoption or of defense effectiveness.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — NIST published SP 2400-102e2026 as an initial public draft on 9 November 2026, with comments due 18 December.
  • Demonstrated — The draft includes a dedicated agentic-systems taxonomy covering attacks across workflow stages, untrusted data, malicious tools and skills, multi-agent systems, and long-horizon tasks.
  • Inferred — Relative to NIST AI 100-2 E2025’s predictive and generative system coverage, the draft’s separate agentic taxonomy is a scope expansion for NIST’s AML reference.

Strongest baseline

NIST AI 100-2 E2025, which classified predictive and generative AI adversarial-ML risks but did not have this dedicated agentic-system taxonomy.

Limitations

  • This is an initial public draft and voluntary reference, not a binding rule or evidence of adoption.
  • The taxonomy’s completeness and practical usefulness have not been independently evaluated in this activation.
  • The report does not demonstrate that listed mitigations prevent agent attacks or reduce incidents.

What we still don't know

  • The report is open for comment; the final taxonomy, adoption by standards groups, and use in evaluation practice remain unknown.

Why it matters

NIST’s 2025 taxonomy covered predictive and generative systems; this draft explicitly treats agents’ tools, memory, peers and persistent state as security surfaces. That gives evaluators and standards groups an official, agent-specific vocabulary while the field works through how to assess these risks. The draft remains open for comment and does not establish that the proposed taxonomy or mitigations are adopted or effective.
