Scientist World reports a benchmark of AI research agents across 1,000 executable paper environments

A 5 November arXiv paper introduces Scientist World, which turns selected empirical papers into executable research environments. The authors report releasing 1,000 environments with reproduced baselines, hidden evaluation slices and separate verifier-agent checks. In their benchmark, agents improved on the reproduced method in 84% of improvement attempts and 74% of attempts that designed a method from scratch. The paper also reports raising Qwen3-8B’s median idea score across ten held-out papers after supervised fine-tuning and offline reinforcement learning; a fixed GPT-5.6 Sol implementer executed all ideas. These are author-reported results from the paper’s selected tasks and setup. They do not establish general autonomous scientific discovery, and were not independently audited or reproduced here. The linked project page currently describes a friends-only preview, shows zero papers in its progress counters, and elsewhere gives a 76% result instead of the paper’s 74%; the cited GitHub organization showed two unrelated public repositories and the trace dataset returned HTTP 401. Public artifact access and this score discrepancy remain unresolved.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — A paper submitted to arXiv on 5 November 2026 describes Scientist World, a system that converts empirical research papers into runnable environments by reproducing a paper method, specifying an improvement task and visible/held-out evaluations, packaging the workspace, and passing it to separate verifier agents.
  • Claimed — The authors report a first release of 1,000 environments selected from 124,816 indexed papers and say the released environments reproduce source-method results within margin in 99% of cases and pass a separate verifier in all 1,000 cases. These validation figures have not been independently audited here.
  • Claimed — In the authors’ benchmark, AI-scientist agents beat the reproduced paper method on held-out evaluation in 84% of improve attempts and 74% of innovate-from-scratch attempts. The paper describes a single controlled trial per paper, model and mode; results are benchmark-specific and not external replications of the underlying research papers.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that supervised fine-tuning and offline GRPO on research traces improved the median held-out ideation score of Qwen3-8B from 41.2 to 106.3 across ten papers. A fixed GPT-5.6 Sol implementer carried out all 30 ideas under the same conditions, so the result concerns ideation quality under this setup, not end-to-end autonomous research capability.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that agents make novel, durable scientific discoveries in deployed research programs, that the benchmark represents scientific work beyond the selected empirical papers, or that its reported results independently reproduce.
  • Not established — The linked project page describes itself as a preview for friends, its progress panel shows zero papers indexed or selected, one page block reports a 76% benchmark result rather than the paper’s 74%, the cited GitHub organization lists two unrelated public repositories, and the linked trace dataset returned HTTP 401 in this check. This does not establish whether the claimed environments and traces are absent or available through another route, but their public accessibility and the 74%/76% relationship remain unresolved.

Strongest baseline

Each task compares a proposed method with the corresponding paper method reproduced by the builder pipeline. The paper’s reported percentages measure attempts exceeding that reproduced method on held-out slices, not the proportion of papers whose original scientific conclusions were independently validated.

Limitations

  • The main benchmark and training results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced here.
  • Papers were selected by an automated quality/feasibility process from four arXiv categories; the reported results do not cover all scientific fields or all research tasks.
  • The 74% result is for innovate-from-scratch attempts in this benchmark. It does not mean that 74% of scientific discoveries or papers are independently improved in real research programs.
  • The Qwen3-8B training result isolates ideation by having a fixed GPT-5.6 Sol implement all generated ideas; it does not test a small model completing the full research loop.
  • The paper reports 100% verifier approval, but the verifier is another agent in the same automated pipeline rather than an external independent audit of the released corpus.
  • The paper-linked project page presents a friends-only preview, shows zero in its progress counters, and elsewhere reports 76% rather than the paper’s 74%; the linked GitHub organization lists two unrelated public repositories and the trace dataset returned HTTP 401. These observations leave artifact accessibility and metric reconciliation unresolved, but do not prove that no artifacts exist elsewhere.

What we still don't know

  • No independent audit of the released 1,000 environments, reproduction figures or verifier outcomes was performed.
  • The reported 84%/74% held-out benchmark rates and ideation-training gain have not been reproduced by an independent team.
  • Generalization beyond selected empirical papers in four arXiv categories and transfer to real research outcomes are not established.
  • The paper-linked project and dataset artifacts could not be accessed publicly in this check; the project page also reports 76% in one abstract block where the paper reports 74%.

Why it matters

Executable environments built from prior research could provide a common way to test whether AI agents improve methods through measured experiments and could supply outcome-linked training data. The paper reports a substantial benchmark and training pipeline, but its results depend on author-selected papers, constructed tasks and a particular model/implementer setup. Independent audits are needed before inferring broad research productivity or autonomous discovery.
