A controlled robot benchmark reports instruction-grounding declines under scene changes

RoboFollow is an author-designed benchmark intended to test whether embodied policies use language to select a task when the visual scene supports multiple valid actions. Across four controlled scene families, nine evaluated VLA/WAM policies and L0–L3 visual or semantic perturbations, the paper reports sharp declines in selected policies’ Intent Scores—for example, pi0 falls from 98.2% to 0.0% on a Scene 1 layout change, and pi0.5 falls from 100.0% to 34.2% on a Scene 2 combined perturbation. A small pi0.5 robot pilot reports 20/40 successes on training instructions versus 6/40 on a different held-out instruction set, but the comparison is unmatched. These paper-reported results diagnose sensitivity in this short-horizon controlled benchmark; they do not establish general deployment failure rates or broad embodied-agent unreliability.

What changed

RoboFollow is an author-designed benchmark intended to test whether embodied policies use language to select a task when the visual scene supports multiple valid actions. Across four controlled scene families, nine evaluated VLA/WAM policies and L0–L3 visual or semantic perturbations, the paper reports sharp declines in selected policies’ Intent Scores—for example, pi0 falls from 98.2% to 0.0% on a Scene 1 layout change, and pi0.5 falls from 100.0% to 34.2% on a Scene 2 combined perturbation. A small pi0.5 robot pilot reports 20/40 successes on training instructions versus 6/40 on a different held-out instruction set, but the comparison is unmatched. These paper-reported results diagnose sensitivity in this short-horizon controlled benchmark; they do not establish general deployment failure rates or broad embodied-agent unreliability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce RoboFollow, a controlled diagnostic benchmark with four scene families, 75 training task labels across six task-independent initial configurations and 3,750 training episodes. Nine VLA and WAM policies are scored across L0–L3 perturbation levels using stage-wise Intent and Execution measures.
  • Claimed — Under the paper’s fine-tuning and benchmark setup, authors report pi0 Scene 1 Intent Score declines from 98.2% at in-distribution L0 to 0.0% after the L1 layout change; pi0.5 Scene 2 declines from 100.0% at L0 to 34.2% under the joint visual-semantic L3 perturbation.
  • Claimed — In a preliminary real-robot pilot with pi0.5, the paper reports 20/40 successes (50%) on eight training instructions and 6/40 (15%) on eight held-out instructions, five trials each. The instruction sets and task compositions were unmatched.
  • Not established — These results do not establish general real-world instruction-following failure rates, long-horizon or contact-rich transfer, or broad unreliability of embodied agents; the pilot is small and unmatched, and the benchmark uses short-horizon controlled scenes and author-reported measurements.

Strongest baseline

Within each policy, in-distribution L0 is compared with the specified L1 layout, L2 semantic recombination and L3 combined perturbations. These are diagnostic comparisons, not cross-study matched baselines or prevalence estimates.

Limitations

  • All benchmark and pilot results are author-reported; no independent code, data or score audit was performed.
  • The benchmark uses four short-horizon controlled scene families and actions in the trained repertoire; it does not establish long-horizon, contact-rich or broad cross-robot transfer.
  • The real-robot pilot uses eight training and eight held-out instructions, five trials each, but compares unmatched instruction sets and task compositions.
  • The measured Intent Score is a benchmark diagnostic under specified layout and semantic perturbations, not an estimate of deployed robot failure prevalence.

What we still don't know

  • The paper’s score tables, datasets and scoring scripts were not independently audited or reproduced.
  • The benchmark uses four short-horizon, controlled scene families, simple objects and actions within the trained repertoire; transfer to longer, contact-rich tasks and other robots is unknown.
  • The 16-instruction real-robot pilot compares different instruction sets and task compositions, so it is not a matched estimate of performance loss.

Why it matters

A robot can complete a familiar task while relying on visual shortcuts instead of the requested instruction. RoboFollow constructs scenes in which multiple actions are visually plausible and separates choosing the requested intent from executing it. The reported perturbation results make that distinction testable for selected models, while the benchmark and pilot do not establish how often deployed robots fail.
