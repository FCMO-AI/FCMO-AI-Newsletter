NVIDIA releases Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and Bolt models for multimodal agents and local deployment

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — NVIDIA announced Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and two Bolt variants on Oct. 15, stating that Bolt E4B was scheduled for Oct. 23.
  • Claimed — NVIDIA describes Super VL as a 120B multimodal model, Bolt 74B-A9B as a model for 24GB deployment, and Bolt E4B as a small model targeting roughly 4GB deployment.
  • Claimed — NVIDIA reports up to 5x faster task completion/throughput for Super VL versus its comparison class; this review did not independently assess the comparison.
  • Claimed — NVIDIA lists Bolt E4B as released Oct. 23, 2026 and reports separate BF16 and Q4_K_M benchmark results plus hardware-specific latency, memory and throughput measurements. Results are mixed across named tasks; this review did not independently reproduce them.

Strongest baseline

NVIDIA's Hugging Face card compares Q4_K_M Bolt E4B with Q4_K_M Gemma 4 E4B-it, Qwen 3.5 4B and Nemotron 3 Nano 4B in a shared NeMo Gym harness. Reported Qwen 3.5 4B scores are higher on BFCL-v3 (70.56 vs 67.31), Tau2-Bench (61.74 vs 52.32), RULER (95.37 vs 85.76), and LongBench v2 (58.28 vs 48.67); Bolt E4B scores higher on other rows, including MultiChallenge and RoleBench. The card says reasoning mode is consistent across models within each benchmark, but varies by benchmark. The results are provider-run and not independently reproduced.

Limitations

  • All model and benchmark claims come from NVIDIA; no independent reproduction, matched external evaluation or application-level deployment test was performed.
  • The research page's detailed benchmark table is labeled BF16; the linked Hugging Face model card separately provides BF16 and Q4_K_M columns across 20 benchmark rows. These provider-run results are mixed: Q4_K_M Bolt E4B trails Qwen 3.5 4B on several tool-use and long-context rows, including BFCL-v3, Tau2-Bench, RULER and LongBench v2.
  • NVIDIA states that the benchmark harness is consistent across models but reasoning mode varies by benchmark. The report says Brevity Bench and When2Call-MT address evaluation gaps and were not yet released; leaderboard results do not establish better behavior in target applications.
  • The claimed up-to-80% output-token reduction is a provider comparison and does not by itself establish equal task quality, lower end-to-end latency or lower total inference cost.
  • Serving figures are specific to NVIDIA's named hardware, llama.cpp/CUDA and AIPerf setup, batch size one, quantization and input/output lengths; performance can differ with software, hardware and configuration.
  • Partner customization and integration examples are reported by NVIDIA and do not establish adoption scale or outcomes.
  • This development combines the Oct. 15 model-family announcement with the Oct. 23 E4B release; event dates remain separate within the source records.

What we still don't know

  • Independent evaluations under matched quality, memory, throughput and task-completion conditions are absent, including for the newly released Bolt E4B checkpoint.
  • The linked E4B training recipe and evaluation artifacts were not retrieved or audited; benchmark prompt sets and full configuration details remain unverified.

Why it matters

A small open model with tool-use post-training and a roughly 4GB deployment target could widen the hardware range for local agents. The Oct. 23 technical report provides an inspectable model card, recipe and NVIDIA-run measurements, but does not establish independent quality, latency or deployment advantages.
