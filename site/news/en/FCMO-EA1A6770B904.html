What changed

The International Federation of Robotics reports about 7,000 sales of full-size humanoid robots above 140 cm worldwide in 2025. Its release says current uses remain specialized and often involve human teleoperation, while its presentation says the segment is still moving from laboratories to pilots. This is an industry-reported statistic, not an independently verified census: the IFR says its World Robotics service-robot figures use a 238-company sample, are not projected to the whole industry, and vary in sample composition each year.

Evidence

  • Claimed — IFR's World Robotics 2026 materials report that about 7,000 full-size humanoid robots (above 140 cm) were sold worldwide in 2025. The release describes current applications as specialized and often teleoperated; the accompanying deck says the category remains on the verge of moving from laboratories to pilots, let alone real deployments.
  • Demonstrated — IFR states that World Robotics 2026 service-robot figures are based on a sample of 238 companies, are not projected to the whole industry, and have year-varying sample composition.

Strongest baseline

This is the first quantitative count in IFR's humanoid chapter, according to its press presentation; no prior comparable IFR annual humanoid count was identified in this bounded review.

Limitations

  • The approximately 7,000 figure is an IFR report claim, not an independently verified global census; the same release says the annual service-robot sample is not projected to the whole industry and changes composition year to year.
  • The phrase 'mostly research/pilot demand' is not a quantified split in the inspected IFR sources. The presentation says the sector is still moving from laboratories to pilots, let alone real deployments, while the release says the robots are intended for commercial/professional applications; these statements concern different aspects and do not establish a unit allocation.
  • No inference about autonomy, labor displacement, revenue, shipment-to-deployment conversion, or year-over-year growth follows from this count.

What we still don't know

  • The IFR's humanoid-specific inclusion rules, reporting suppliers, response rate, geographic coverage, category-overlap treatment, and uncertainty are not exposed in its release; the release's 238-of-1,001 supplier sample is explicitly not projected to the whole industry.
  • No supplier-level or independent industry dataset was inspected to reproduce the reported 2025 count or determine the share used in pilots, laboratories, or production.

Why it matters

This is an early quantitative reference point for comparing humanoid-market claims with observed supplier reporting. The sampling caveat materially limits its use: it does not establish a complete global unit total, productive deployment volume, or a stable year-over-year growth rate.
