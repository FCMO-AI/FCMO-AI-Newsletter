Study finds task reward can hide inaccurate latent world dynamics

A new arXiv preprint proposes latent rollout consistency (LRC) to test whether a learned model’s action-conditioned latent predictions track the states inferred from realized observations. Across DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 and LeWM on three deliberately simple DeepMind Control Suite tasks, the authors report that strong task reward can coexist with inaccurate latent rollouts. Pixel inputs generally worsen rollout prediction, while adding velocity partly improves results for some models. The finding is an author-reported result in controlled low-dimensional environments; it does not establish that deployed or real-world world models generally fail to represent their environments.

What changed

A new arXiv preprint proposes latent rollout consistency (LRC) to test whether a learned model’s action-conditioned latent predictions track the states inferred from realized observations. Across DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 and LeWM on three deliberately simple DeepMind Control Suite tasks, the authors report that strong task reward can coexist with inaccurate latent rollouts. Pixel inputs generally worsen rollout prediction, while adding velocity partly improves results for some models. The finding is an author-reported result in controlled low-dimensional environments; it does not establish that deployed or real-world world models generally fail to represent their environments.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors define latent rollout consistency (LRC) as action-conditioned agreement between autoregressive latent predictions and latent representations inferred from the corresponding realized observations.
  • Claimed — Across DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 and LeWM on Pendulum, CartPole and Reacher, the authors report that task reward can remain high while pixel-input latent rollouts diverge from representations of realized trajectories. They report four model seeds per model, modality and environment.
  • Claimed — In controlled CartPole interventions, the authors report that providing ground-truth velocity improves latent rollout consistency for some models, while nonlinear invertible observation mixing degrades learned dynamics despite preserving input information.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 and LeWM across the specified tasks; results are paper-reported, and no external reproduction is available.

Limitations

  • Author-reported preprint; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The authors explicitly use three low-dimensional control tasks as minimal test cases, not a comprehensive benchmark.
  • Findings do not establish failure in high-dimensional, robotic or deployed world models.
  • The relationship between LRC and downstream planning reliability beyond the studied environments remains untested.

What we still don't know

  • The LRC findings have not been independently reproduced from a pinned implementation.
  • The paper tests three deliberately simple low-dimensional control environments; generalization to more complex visual or embodied settings is unmeasured.

Why it matters

Task success is often used to evaluate world models, but it may not show whether internal dynamics predictions are accurate. If independently validated, LRC could add a useful measurement axis for research claims. Current evidence is a single paper in three simple environments.
