What changed

A May 2026 version of a Cerebras-authored preprint reports that its Router-weighted Expert Activation Pruning (REAP) method retained coding benchmark accuracy after removing 50% of experts from Qwen3-Coder-480B and Kimi-K2-Instruct. The authors summarize the non-agentic coding accuracy decreases across those two models as 0.16% at 25% compression and 1.2% at 50%; model-specific benchmark scores are reported separately in Table 3. These are author-reported results, not independently reproduced serving or economic outcomes.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that REAP, a pruning criterion combining router gate values and expert activation norms, outperforms the compared pruning and merging baselines on the paper’s generative benchmarks, particularly at 50% expert compression.
  • Claimed — The authors summarize their two-model non-agentic coding results as mean accuracy decreases of 0.16% at 25% compression and 1.2% at 50% compression. Their Table 3 reports benchmark scores separately for each model.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, general quality preservation outside its evaluated tasks and configurations, or real serving savings in memory residency, throughput, latency, power, or cost.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares REAP with frequency-based pruning, Expert Activation Norm pruning, and expert merging methods at matched compression ratios on its listed models and benchmarks.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported in a preprint by authors affiliated with Cerebras Systems and the University of Calgary; no independent reproduction was reviewed.
  • The strong large-model result is specific to the study’s two listed checkpoints and benchmark setup; other tasks show larger quality losses.
  • A reduction in stored experts does not by itself establish proportional runtime, power, or cost savings.
  • The text’s percentage summary and model-specific values in Table 3 use different aggregation levels; the paper’s reported aggregate should not be mixed with individual table-cell differences.
  • The authors’ repository reports a material implementation/evaluation update: top-k router-logit renormalization improved a five-model non-agentic coding mean decrease from 2.6% to 1.9%; it says prior large-scale results used the normalized setting. These first-party figures were not independently reproduced.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark results are from a single author team and have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • Quality retention is task- and model-dependent; the paper itself reports weaker results on some agentic/tool-use and multiple-choice metrics, and the result does not cover broad production traffic.
  • Removing experts does not demonstrate reduced end-to-end serving cost or latency; no matched hardware/runtime measurements were reviewed.

Why it matters

Large MoE checkpoints carry substantial memory overhead. If the reported task-specific quality retention generalizes, selective expert removal could make some large models easier to store or serve. The paper does not show proportional reductions in wall-clock latency, power, or total serving cost.
