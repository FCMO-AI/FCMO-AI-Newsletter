TasteVal reports a 2.30× experimental-compute multiplier for Opus 5.5 on eight bounded AI R&D tasks

Jaffe and Sherburn introduce TasteVal, an author-built benchmark of eight text and vision tasks intended to measure experimental design and interpretation when a fixed research problem is given. They report that Opus 5.5 (max) reaches a 2.30× experimental-compute multiplier against the best expert attempt per task (95% CI 1.15–4.37). The authors also estimate that frontier-model multiplier performance doubled about every 3.0 months after December 2025, while final normalized performance shows no corresponding trend break. These are preprint results on withheld tasks, not evidence that models choose valuable research questions or outperform experts across broad research work.

What actually changed

Jaffe and Sherburn introduce TasteVal, an author-built benchmark of eight text and vision tasks intended to measure experimental design and interpretation when a fixed research problem is given. They report that Opus 5.5 (max) reaches a 2.30× experimental-compute multiplier against the best expert attempt per task (95% CI 1.15–4.37). The authors also estimate that frontier-model multiplier performance doubled about every 3.0 months after December 2025, while final normalized performance shows no corresponding trend break. These are preprint results on withheld tasks, not evidence that models choose valuable research questions or outperform experts across broad research work.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED_PUBLICATION_EVENT — The authors posted the TasteVal preprint, describing an evaluation of experimental research behavior on eight author-built tasks.
  • CLAIMED_BY_AUTHORS — The authors report that Opus 5.5 (max) achieved a 2.30× experimental-compute multiplier against the task-level best expert attempt (95% CI 1.15–4.37).
  • CLAIMED_BY_AUTHORS — The authors report that the compute multiplier for frontier models doubled about every 3.0 months after December 2025 (95% CI 1.7–5.0), while final normalized performance doubled every 14.6 months without a significant trend break.
  • DEMONSTRATED_SCOPE_LIMIT — The paper defines its measured construct as iterative experimental design and interpretation for a fixed problem; it does not measure problem selection or broad autonomous research.

Strongest baseline

Best recruited human expert attempt on each of eight tasks, as defined by the authors. No external audit of this baseline was performed.

The caveat that matters

  • The results are author-reported in one preprint and have not been independently reproduced.
  • Eight deliberately uncontaminated tasks are author-built; the authors withhold task content.
  • TasteVal isolates experiment design and interpretation for fixed research problems, not valuable problem selection, novel ideas or complete autonomous research.
  • The abstract's reported 2.30× multiplier compares with the best recruited expert attempt per task and does not establish broad superiority over human research teams or at equal dollar cost.
  • The reported $282 versus $9,123 per-run costs use author-selected API, GPU and salary assumptions; they do not show performance at equal dollar cost. The AI-R&D forecast scenarios are explicitly naive extrapolations that assume rate transfer from TasteVal to broader frontier work.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction or external assessment of the reported multiplier was identified in this activation.
  • The eight benchmark tasks are withheld to reduce contamination, preventing direct replication of the identical task set; task-level raw outcomes and a public executable artifact were not inspected in this activation.
  • The best expert attempt per task and chained compute-multiplier references may affect the estimate; no independent baseline audit was performed here.

FCMO Lens

If the reported efficiency gain transfers to larger and less scripted research settings, it could reduce compute per experimental result and change research-program planning. The benchmark measures iterative experiment design for fixed problems; it does not measure problem selection, novel idea generation, open-ended project execution, or real-time multi-agent research.
