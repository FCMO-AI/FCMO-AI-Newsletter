Feedback coding reports reliable black-box covert messages in bounded LLM tests

The paper introduces Burnashev Adaptive Posterior Matching (BAM), which uses transcript feedback, posterior matching and a confirmation phase to embed messages in generated text without giving the receiver the model’s weights or prompt. On C4 RealNews, the authors report roughly 0–0.1% error for an 8-bit payload in about 46–50 tokens across three open-weight models and 1,000 trials, versus roughly 10.5–16.8% for the strongest listed black-box baseline at 50 tokens. In separate conversational experiments, error varied by model and task: Llama-3.1 and Phi-4 stayed below 3% message error, while Qwen3-A3B-30B had 19–22% dialogue error. These are one preprint’s results; no independent reproduction was found, the implementation has computational costs, and transcript editing is outside the stated security model.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Across three open-weight models on C4 RealNews, the authors report that BAM carries an 8-bit payload with approximately 0–0.1% empirical message error at about 46–50 tokens over 1,000 trials; the strongest listed black-box baseline, ArcMark, reports roughly 10.5–16.8% at 50 tokens.
  • Claimed — In author-run conversational experiments with 100 dialogues per cell, reported error varied by model and payload: Llama-3.1 and Phi-4 had message error below 3% across three tasks, while Qwen3-A3B-30B showed 19–22% dialogue error.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper’s security analysis assumes a specified shared-secret/API threat model and excludes an adversary that edits, deletes or paraphrases the transcript; the authors do not claim classical asymptotic optimality for this LLM channel.

Strongest baseline

ArcMark at a fixed 50-token length in the paper’s Table 1; this baseline is author-run and the reported comparison is not independently validated.

Limitations

  • All performance results are reported by one preprint; this review did not independently reproduce them.
  • The 8-bit primitive result is measured on C4 RealNews over 1,000 author-run trials and three named open-weight models; it does not establish performance on proprietary models or deployed conversations.
  • Conversational tests use 100 dialogues per cell and show material model and task dependence, including 19–22% dialogue error for Qwen3-A3B-30B.
  • The security analysis depends on its formal shared-secret and API threat model; transcript editing, deletion and paraphrasing are outside scope.
  • BAM has exponential decoding cost as message length grows; the paper’s 24-bit scalability appendix reports this as a practical constraint.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the reported reliability advantage was found.
  • The analysis excludes transcript editing and does not establish detection or recovery under paraphrase, truncation or platform transformations.

Why it matters

A feedback-based method that materially reduces decoding errors in the tested black-box setting could change how researchers assess covert communication through model-generated conversations. The paper also shows dependence on model entropy, task and transcript integrity, so it motivates threat-model-specific testing rather than a general claim that hidden communication is reliable across deployed agents.
