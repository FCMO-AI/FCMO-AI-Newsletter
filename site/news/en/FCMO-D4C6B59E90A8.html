Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report an 80B-total/13B-active MoE trained on a 20T-token corpus, and Table 2 reports higher scores than Hunyuan-Large on 12 of 14 benchmarks and than Qwen3-A22B on 7 of 12; rankings vary by benchmark. These first-party scores are not independently reproduced.

Strongest baseline

Table 2 compares base models Hunyuan-Large-1116, Qwen2.5-72B and Qwen3-A22B across 14 named benchmarks. Tables 3–4 compare post-trained models including Qwen3-A22B, DeepSeek R1 and OpenAI o1 in separate fast/slow settings. The paper does not provide a matched independent evaluation or hardware-matched throughput comparison.

Limitations

  • The model was released before this technical report; the September 2026 arXiv date is the report event, not the model release date.
  • Benchmark scores and training details are first-party claims; no independent rerun or contamination audit was performed.
  • Model rankings are mixed across individual benchmarks and reasoning modes; the paper does not establish a uniform quality advantage.
  • Reported throughput is not a hardware-matched comparison and cannot by itself establish lower serving cost or energy use.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark scores have not been independently reproduced or audited for contamination and protocol comparability.
  • The throughput table lacks a hardware-matched comparison against comparable models, so it does not establish a relative efficiency advantage.

Why it matters

An open model reporting 13B active parameters with competitive scores against some larger models could matter for deployment choices where access and inference resources constrain use. The report’s mixed per-benchmark rankings and absence of matched throughput comparisons mean the results support only a bounded vendor-reported model profile, not a general efficiency or quality lead.
