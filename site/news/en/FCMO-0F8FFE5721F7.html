NostrAgent combines decentralized agent identity, scoped delegation, trust attestations and Lightning payment

The paper proposes NostrAgent, which places agent identity declarations, narrowing delegation chains and peer attestations in signed Nostr events, with Lightning HTTP 402 payment bound to agent identity. Its Python prototype reports depth-4 delegation verification at 0.0619 ms median (900 measurements) and a full L402 flow at 157.4 ms median (90 runs) on a local Docker regtest setup. The authors say 17 of 19 failure modes passed empirical tests, one was bounded analytically and one remains an architectural limitation. These are author-reported prototype results: experiments used a fixed seed on Apple Silicon/macOS with Python 3.11, and the linked replication package was not run independently. The paper also reports trust-query latency rising to 2.42 seconds at 500 synthetic nodes and identifies public attestation metadata and relay-consistency limits.

Strongest baseline

The reported evaluation compares configurations and failure modes within the prototype; it does not establish a head-to-head production comparison with centralized identity, OAuth/DID composition or more expressive capability systems.

Limitations

  • All measured results are from one author-run prototype and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The evaluation uses fixed seed 42, one Apple Silicon/macOS host and Python 3.11; Docker flows use a private regtest network, not Bitcoin mainnet or production relays.
  • Trust-query median latency reaches 2.422 seconds on 500-node synthetic graphs under the reported settings.
  • The attestation graph is public and exposes relationship metadata; this privacy cost is a design trade-off, not a measured deployment outcome.
  • The paper identifies relay-only consistency/divergence limits, a pre-rotation propagation window and a single-level key-rotation equivocation gap.
  • The authors report 17 of 19 failure modes pass empirically; one is bounded analytically and one remains an architectural limitation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent execution of the linked replication package was performed, and no external implementation or deployment was inspected.
  • The paper does not establish interoperability with deployed agent frameworks or standardized Nostr event kinds, and the comparison against centralized/more expressive systems is architectural rather than head-to-head operational.
  • The largest reported trust graph is synthetic and query latency rises sharply at 500 nodes; real deployment graph shape and privacy acceptability are unmeasured.

Why it matters

Cross-agent delegation needs identity continuity, least-authority scopes, revocation, trust and payment across organizational boundaries. NostrAgent offers one concrete composition and measures some local verification costs, which can inform architecture choices for decentralized agent services. Its constrained setup and disclosed scaling, privacy and consistency costs mean it does not establish production readiness or that the design is preferable to centralized or more expressive alternatives.
