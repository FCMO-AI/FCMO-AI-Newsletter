Manacá-1B-Instruct-2 reports PT-BR safety results and an LLM-judge refusal failure

What changed

An October 14 arXiv report describes Manacá-1B-Instruct-2, a 1.72B-parameter model trained from scratch for Brazilian Portuguese, with a new safety-SFT alignment and unusually detailed negative results. The authors report 97.2% held-out safety on 218 harmful prompts against four external PT-BR models tested on the same items and harness; the lower Wilson confidence bound is 94.1%, above all four baselines. The paper also reports that its Claude judge refused to score 2 of 6 sensitive MT-Bench-PT prompts regardless of the evaluated answer, reducing the effective sample for this category across eight historical project reports. The authors say their repository now exposes this missingness. These are author-reported results; model scale, benchmark scope, low absolute general capability and absent independent reproduction sharply limit wider claims.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The October 14 arXiv v1 report documents Manacá-1B-Instruct-2, its post-training experiments, safety evaluation, and stated limitations; the project repository README in Portuguese and English identifies an experimental v0.2 release and says it is not production-grade safety.
  • Claimed — The authors report 97.2% held-out safety on 218 harmful prompts for v4, with a 94.1% lower Wilson confidence bound above the reported results for four external PT-BR baselines tested on the same items and harness. They report 9.2% over-refusal on 184 items; the statistical comparison is author-run and has not been independently reproduced.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the Claude judge refused to score 2 of 6 sensitive MT-Bench-PT items regardless of the answer being evaluated, reducing the effective sample across six models and eight historical project reports; the repository says its reporting was corrected to expose per-cell sample size and judge refusals.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The authors state that the controlled 10–60% general-data composition scan used one training round per point and small N=16/34 probes; its non-monotonic over-refusal pattern and 40–50% “knee” may be partly noise and are untested at another model scale. They also report low absolute general capability and chance-level results on several exams for models at this scale.

Strongest baseline

Four external PT-BR models were run on the same items and harness. Reported safety rates were 3.2%, 15.1%, 14.7%, and 70.6% versus 97.2% for v4. The paper reports Wilson intervals and paired McNemar tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction, but the analysis is not independently reproduced.

Limitations

  • All comparative results are author-reported; the current repository README was read but the model weights, code and evaluation pipeline were not independently executed.
  • The model is one 1.72B Brazilian-Portuguese system; these results do not establish safety for other languages, scales, model families or deployed use.
  • The 10–60% composition scan uses one training seed per point and small N=16/34 probes; the authors state the apparent knee may partly be noise.
  • The safety percentage depends on the authors’ prompt set, classification rules, generation settings and matched baseline choice.
  • The repository labels this an experimental research release and explicitly disclaims production-grade safety.
  • The judge refusal mechanism is documented within one project and one Claude evaluation workflow; broader prevalence is unknown.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the 218-item safety and 184-item over-refusal comparisons and the reported paired statistical tests is absent.
  • The relationship between the released v4 weights, the exact repository commit and the paper’s reported final checkpoint was not independently verified.
  • Generalization of the judge-refusal finding beyond the Claude judge, this MT-Bench-PT safety category and the project’s eight historical reports is unknown.
  • The controlled general-data composition scan has one training seed per point and N=16/34 iteration probes; the 40–50% knee may reflect training or sampling noise.

Why it matters

The work contributes two bounded signals: a reproducible-looking safety comparison for an underrepresented language, and a concrete failure mode in which the evaluator’s own safety layer silently removes sensitive items from a score denominator. If independently checked, the latter could improve reporting practice for LLM-judge evaluations. The paper does not show general safety, production readiness, or that the observed judge failure is widespread beyond this project.
