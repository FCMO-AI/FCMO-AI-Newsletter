Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors present OpenFrontiers as a corpus of 12,151 research-problem records from 62 contributing source collections and a 200-item mathematics benchmark: 110 resolved problems with located solutions and 90 problems treated as open at the authors’ status check. Every benchmark item has a Lean 4 statement; the HQ-50 is a selected evaluation subset.
  • Claimed — The authors report evaluating seven LLM agents on HQ-50 under closed-book, frozen-retrieval and live-web conditions, with proof acceptance requiring two independent Lean checkers. Under closed-book continuation, agents solved at most 7 of 40 resolved items; among 181 live-web solves, 167 followed retrieval of a located solution source. These are results reported by the paper’s authors and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the broader corpus is mathematics-heavy (11,349 of 12,151 records, 93.4%), labels 5,591 records unassessed, and includes 1,946 Lean 4 attachments, of which 1,562 are flagged for lexical placeholders; they report no canonical accepted proof or locally verified Lean proof for the general corpus snapshot.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that its reported results generalize beyond this mathematics-focused benchmark, the selected models and access conditions, or the curated problem subset; it also does not provide independent reproduction of the reported agent evaluation in this review.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the same selected models across three access conditions, with closed-book as the principal rediscovery condition and live web treated as retrieval-sensitive. This within-paper comparison is author-reported, not an independent matched reproduction.

Limitations

  • The LLM evaluation is author-reported and has not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • HQ-50 contains 50 selected mathematics problems and results may not transfer to other fields, problem types, model lineages or tool budgets.
  • Live-web success is not a rediscovery measure: the paper reports that most live-web solves followed retrieval of located published solutions.
  • The 12,151-item corpus is source-record based and not deduplicated by problem statement; it is 93.4% mathematics, and 46.0% of records are labeled unassessed rather than currently open.
  • Of 1,946 corpus Lean attachments, 1,562 are flagged for lexical placeholders; the authors report no canonical accepted or locally verified Lean proof in the general corpus snapshot.
  • The linked dataset and code artifacts were not independently retrieved or inspected here.

What we still don't know

  • The paper-reported agent evaluation and benchmark curation have not been independently reproduced or audited in this review.
  • The linked Hugging Face collection could not be retrieved during this review, so the dataset snapshot, executable code, licensing and artifact contents were not independently inspected.
  • OpenFrontiers is heavily concentrated in mathematics, while 46.0% of its corpus records are unassessed and most Lean attachments carry placeholder flags; the work does not establish broad coverage or current open status for every record.

Why it matters

Research-agent evaluations can mistake finding a published result for independently deriving it. OpenFrontiers proposes a way to separate those behaviors using time-anchored problems, lineage exclusions and kernel-checked proofs. Its current data and model evaluation remain bounded to mathematics and a small curated sample, so it supplies an evaluation method to examine rather than evidence that agents can or cannot conduct research broadly.
