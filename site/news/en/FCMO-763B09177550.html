Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI published an October 29 system-card addendum identifying GPT-6 Luna (October 29) as an updated ChatGPT model. The card says the update is more concise and less overly agreeable and retains the October 7 model's training types and safeguards stack.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that GPT-6 Luna (October 29) is High in cybersecurity and biological/chemical capability under its Preparedness Framework, and reports 99.91% robustness on its instruction-hierarchy evaluation.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports improvement over the October 7 Luna snapshot on four under-18 safe-completion categories: sexual content, eating disorders, emotional reliance and gore. The reported scores are evaluation-specific and provider-run.
  • Claimed — The October 29 addendum lists lower displayed values than the October 7 Luna column on HealthBench, HealthBench Hard, HealthBench Consensus and overall, non-acute and high-acuity MentalHealthBench, while the emergent MentalHealthBench value is slightly higher. These are provider-reported table comparisons, not a controlled longitudinal estimate: OpenAI says earlier-model values may reflect later model versions and can vary from previously published scores.
  • Not established — The provider-published card does not independently establish the reported safety or health-evaluation changes, their performance in ordinary deployment, or a broad improvement in model safety.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI compares against GPT-6 Luna (October 7) and other listed GPT-5.5/GPT-5.6/GPT-6 snapshots. Several selected under-18 metrics improve, while HealthBench and most MentalHealthBench comparisons are mixed or lower. OpenAI cautions that earlier-model values can reflect later versions and vary from previously published scores; the table is descriptive provider reporting, not a controlled temporal comparison.

Limitations

  • All comparative evaluation results and capability assessments are OpenAI-reported; no independent replication was performed.
  • The October 29 card inherits detailed evaluation-method descriptions from the October 7 card, which was not re-audited in this activation.
  • Provider metrics do not establish performance on ordinary production traffic or broad user safety outcomes.
  • Cross-version table differences are not a controlled longitudinal estimate: OpenAI warns that previous-model values may reflect later versions and vary from previously published scores; its October 7 card also says evaluation methods evolve and difficult safety-set error rates do not represent average production traffic.

What we still don't know

  • Safety, prompt-injection and health-evaluation values are provider-reported and not independently reproduced. The October 7 card says the difficult safety evaluations are not representative of average production traffic and that evolving policies, datasets and graders limit comparisons with earlier cards; the October 29 addendum also warns earlier-model values may reflect later versions.
  • The system-card comparisons do not establish safety or health performance under ordinary production traffic, deployment prevalence or downstream user outcomes.

Why it matters

Dated model revisions can change the behavior and risk profile of widely deployed assistants. OpenAI's card gives a version-specific account of selected gains and trade-offs, but external evaluators still need to verify those measurements before drawing broader safety conclusions.
