What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces ActGov, which checks each proposed tool call against a task-scoped policy before execution. The authors report lower indirect prompt-injection success on AgentDojo and AgentDyn across four model backends, with task utility retained to varying degrees. Their cross-domain transfer test also shows severe over-restriction: an AgentDojo-trained policy has 0.100 clean-task utility on AgentDyn versus 0.767 without defense. These are paper-reported benchmark results, not independent reproduction or evidence of deployment effectiveness.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors propose an offline policy-generation stage with Z3 checking and a deterministic runtime monitor that intercepts each candidate tool call before external execution.
  • Claimed — On the displayed AgentDojo and AgentDyn test splits, the authors report ActGov attack-success rates from 0 to 0.7% across four model backends, with varying reductions in task utility.
  • Claimed — In cross-domain policy transfer, the authors report zero attack success but severe over-restriction; an AgentDojo-trained policy has 0.100 clean utility on AgentDyn compared with 0.767 for no defense.
  • Not established — The results do not establish that the policy abstraction or semantic extraction is reliable in open-world deployments, nor that attack reductions generalize beyond these benchmark tasks and assumptions.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares against no defense and CaMeL, Progent, DRIFT and ACE. It reports attack-success and clean/attacked task-completion metrics; cross-domain transfer substantially reduces utility despite low attack success.

Limitations

  • All attack-success and utility measurements are author-reported; the research desk did not run code or reproduce results.
  • The threat model trusts the user task and enforcement layer and assumes every external action passes through pre-execution validation.
  • Policy abstraction depends on finite records and semantic extraction; errors in mapping runtime context can affect enforcement.
  • The main evidence comes from two benchmark suites and specific task splits; cross-domain transfer shows severe utility loss.
  • The strongest policy-generation setting uses human review, so it does not demonstrate fully autonomous policy synthesis in that setting.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the AgentDojo/AgentDyn split or author-reported attack-success and task-utility values was performed.
  • Evaluation assumes trusted user tasks, an immutable enforcement layer, and complete pre-execution interception; open-world tools and unseen task domains are not established.
  • Cross-domain policy transfer preserves low reported attack success through severe conservatism, including 0.100 clean utility on AgentDyn for an AgentDojo-trained policy.

Why it matters

ActGov tests a concrete security boundary: authorization is checked at each proposed external action rather than delegated to the model's judgment. The paper's held-out benchmark results make that mechanism worth tracking, while the cross-domain utility collapse shows that low attack success can result from blocking legitimate work. Deployment value depends on whether policy coverage and semantic extraction generalize beyond the tested task splits.
