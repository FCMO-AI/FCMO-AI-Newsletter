A preprint reports that separating environmental facts from task procedures can help evolving agents

A September 21, 2026 arXiv preprint proposes TTSE, which evolves environmental facts (FACT) separately from task-conditioned procedures (TIP), using interaction evidence to revise or retire both kinds of rule. The paper frames representation errors and execution errors as distinct contributors to agent failure and evaluates the framework on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, GDPevo, SOPBench and PinchBench. Authors report gains over selected baselines and an end-to-end PinchBench score of 0.7822 versus 0.7439 across three runs. In the SOPBench comparison, the reported dual-track gain over a single-track Bayesian-memory ablation is 2.99 percentage points over 892 tasks, but warehouse inspection contributes 78% of the gain; without that domain the reported gap is 0.67 points, and dangerous-goods tasks slightly favor single-track. The preprint reports no independent reproduction; its GDPevo dual-versus-TIP ablation is not formally significance-tested, and induced rules lack a structured verification gate.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v1 preprint exists and specifies a two-track online self-evolution framework that stores environmental facts (FACT) separately from task-conditioned procedures (TIP), with evidence tracking, retirement and contradiction-conditioned synthesis.
  • Claimed — On ALFWorld, ScienceWorld and GDPevo, the authors report improvements over selected baselines or ablations. For example, they report ALFWorld success rates of 97.8% with DeepSeek-V3.2 and 98.51% with GLM-5; the GDPevo dual-track mean was 0.2616 versus 0.2380 for TIP-only, but the authors state that gap was not formally significance-tested.
  • Claimed — In the SOPBench Bayesian-fusion comparison, the authors report a dual-track score of 94.58% versus 91.59% single-track across 892 tasks (+2.99 percentage points; paired bootstrap 95% CI +2.34 to +3.65; permutation p<10^-4). Warehouse inspection contributed 78% of the total gain; excluding it left +0.67 points, while dangerous-goods tasks slightly favored single-track. These analyses are author-reported and unreproduced.
  • Claimed — On PinchBench's 147 end-to-end tasks, the authors report mean scores of 0.7822 for TTSE and 0.7439 for baseline across three runs. This tests integration in the reported harness and is not an independent deployment validation.

Strongest baseline

The most decision-relevant dual-track comparison is against a unified single-track Bayesian-memory ablation on SOPBench with matched experience tracking; the GDPevo ablation compares FACT+TIP, TIP-only and FACT-only, though the authors state the full-vs-TIP gap is not formally significance-tested.

Limitations

  • One author-reported preprint; the research desk did not independently reproduce the benchmark findings.
  • The SOPBench aggregate advantage over the single-track ablation is heavily concentrated in warehouse inspection; without warehouse, the reported gain is much smaller, and a domain-level regression is present.
  • The paper states that the GDPevo full-versus-TIP-only difference lacks a formal significance test.
  • The authors identify blank-bank cold start and LLM rule induction without structured execution verification as limitations.
  • PinchBench scores come from the authors' integration and evaluator across three runs; this is not evidence of production reliability or cross-organization transfer.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of TTSE's benchmark results or public replication of the reported PinchBench integration was found.
  • The 2.99-point SOPBench gain is concentrated in warehouse inspection (78%); without it the reported difference is 0.67 points, while one domain slightly favors single-track.
  • The authors state that LLM-induced rules are post-hoc filtered but lack structured execution verification before entering the rule bank; cold-start behavior also depends on zero-shot ability.

Why it matters

If the reported results transfer, agent memory systems may benefit from separately tracking what appears true about an environment and which conditional procedures work there, making context-dependent failure and stale assumptions easier to identify. The evidence is author-reported and varies by domain; a large fraction of one aggregate gain comes from a single benchmark domain.
