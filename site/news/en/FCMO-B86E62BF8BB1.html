NgramRL uses prior rollouts to accelerate structured RL post-training generation

NgramRL proposes per-prompt CPU-side n-gram caches built from recent policy rollouts, with target-model verification and an adaptive fan-out prefetcher that prepares likely next drafts during GPU work. In author-reported experiments across Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-LLM-7B on GSM8K, Spider and CodeContests, it commits 3.60–7.77 tokens per target forward in the final 30% of training, depending on model/task, versus 2.32–4.15 for SuffixDecoding. On Spider, prefetch improves rollout throughput over synchronous NgramRL by 1.38x for Qwen3-8B and 1.21x for DeepSeek-7B at budget N=16. These are paper-reported results, not independent replication or end-to-end training-cost measurements.

What changed

NgramRL proposes per-prompt CPU-side n-gram caches built from recent policy rollouts, with target-model verification and an adaptive fan-out prefetcher that prepares likely next drafts during GPU work. In author-reported experiments across Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-LLM-7B on GSM8K, Spider and CodeContests, it commits 3.60–7.77 tokens per target forward in the final 30% of training, depending on model/task, versus 2.32–4.15 for SuffixDecoding. Evidence

  • Claimed — Across Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-LLM-7B on GSM8K, Spider and CodeContests, the paper reports 3.60–7.77 committed tokens per target forward in the final 30% of training; corresponding SuffixDecoding figures are 2.32–4.15.
  • Claimed — On Spider at prefetch candidate budget N=16, the authors report throughput of 1.38x synchronous NgramRL for Qwen3-8B and 1.21x for DeepSeek-7B; higher-entropy workloads gain 1.05–1.06x.
  • Claimed — The paper's proposed acceptance verification preserves the target policy's output distribution; the research desk did not independently inspect an implementation or validate this property.

Strongest baseline

Matched-budget SuffixDecoding for accepted tokens per target forward; synchronous NgramRL on the same model/task setup for prefetch throughput.

Limitations

  • This is a single author-reported preprint; the research desk did not independently reproduce results or inspect source code.
  • Accepted tokens per target forward are a decoding proxy, not an end-to-end training throughput, wall-clock or economic result.
  • The 1.38x/1.21x wall-clock results are Spider-specific comparisons against synchronous NgramRL; reported higher-entropy task gains are 1.05–1.06x.
  • Acceptance results emphasize the final 30% warm-cache regime; the paper reports cold-start and policy-drift effects, including declining Spider acceptance later in training.
  • Timing is reported on one RTX PRO 6000 96GB and Ryzen Threadripper 9970X host profile; broader hardware, batch and concurrency behavior is unestablished.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction, public implementation check, or run-level timing audit was completed. All empirical values remain paper-reported.
  • The paper does not establish realized end-to-end training time/cost savings, training quality effects, broad hardware transfer or production behavior under different prompt-reuse and concurrency regimes.

Why it matters

RL post-training rollout generation can consume much of training time. If the reported mechanism generalizes, reusing prompt-conditioned history and hiding host scheduling could improve an important training-efficiency bottleneck without a separate draft model. The observed gains vary by task and are strongest on Spider; their value depends on repeated prompts, cache warm-up and hardware/host timing.
