What changed

A September 18 arXiv paper proposes Interrupt and Silent Modeling (ISM), which adds <interrupt> and <silent> decisions to an audio-language model so one watch-out intent can drive continuous alerts without a new query for every event. On ESC-50, the authors report 99.6% interrupt F1 and 100.0% recall for suppressing duplicate alerts. In a zero-shot Epic-Sounds evaluation, interrupt F1 was 67.5 and onset recall 96.7%, but irrelevant-silence recall was 10.1% and sustained-relevance recall 41.2%; duplicate suppression was not measured there. The reported 3.5-second mean latency comes from 400 constructed streams with the event onset fixed at five seconds. These are author-reported benchmark results, not evidence of wearable deployment or improved outcomes for Deaf and Hard of Hearing users.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Using Qwen2-Audio-7B with Interrupt and Silent Modeling (ISM), the authors report 99.6% interrupt F1 and 100.0% de-duplication recall on ESC-50 under their proactive evaluation. ESC-50 uses balanced proactive examples derived from a 2,000-clip dataset; reactive classification uses five-fold cross-validation. These are author-reported results.
  • Claimed — On Epic-Sounds, which was used only for evaluation, the authors report 67.5 interrupt F1 and 96.7% onset recall for ISM, compared with 66.9 F1 for zero-shot prompting and 65.2 for reactive fine-tuning. ISM's irrelevant-silence recall was 10.1% and sustained-relevance recall was 41.2%; de-duplication was not evaluated on Epic-Sounds because the protocol did not preserve conversation history across samples.
  • Claimed — In a controlled streaming test of 400 constructed 15-second samples spanning 50 ESC-50 classes, with relevant audio placed at a fixed 5–10 second interval, the authors report 3.5 seconds average response latency and suppression of repeated alerts through conversation history. This is not a wearable deployment or a user study.
  • Demonstrated — The paper defines a four-state proactive audio task and a training method that adds <interrupt> and <silent> tokens to autoregressive decoding. The reviewed source documents this design; it does not establish that performance generalizes to other AudioLLMs or real assistive devices.

Strongest baseline

On ESC-50, reactive SFT is the closest reported baseline for interrupt F1 (79.8 versus 99.6 for ISM), while zero-shot prompting nearly always interrupts and has only 0.2% duplicate-suppression recall. On Epic-Sounds, ISM's 67.5 F1 narrowly exceeds zero-shot's 66.9 and reactive SFT's 65.2; the behaviors trade off onset detection against silence decisions.

Limitations

  • All numerical results are author-reported; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The experiments use one Qwen2-Audio-7B backbone and controlled benchmark datasets; transfer to other audio-language models is proposed but not tested.
  • On Epic-Sounds, irrelevant-silence recall is 10.1% and sustained-relevance recall is 41.2%, despite 96.7% onset recall. De-duplication is not evaluated on Epic-Sounds because sample histories do not carry over.
  • The 400-sample streaming test uses constructed clips with a fixed event onset and duration. The paper identifies randomized timing and latency tradeoffs as future work.
  • The paper reports no deployment on wearable hardware and no study with Deaf or Hard of Hearing participants; accessibility benefit, alert fatigue and user preference remain unknown.
  • Only one zero-shot Epic-Sounds evaluation set is reported; the paper does not show broader environmental generalization or real-world false-alert rates.

What we still don't know

  • The paper's ESC-50, Epic-Sounds and streaming results have not been independently reproduced from a pinned implementation and complete test protocol.
  • Real-world utility and burden for Deaf and Hard of Hearing users are unknown; the paper reports no wearable deployment or participant study.

Why it matters

Reactive audio models wait for a query for each event, while sound-awareness systems can become tiring if they repeat alerts or notify on irrelevant sounds. ISM makes event onset, continued relevance, irrelevance and prior notification history explicit parts of the model's decision. The paper offers a bounded first evaluation of this design, but its poor irrelevant-silence score on noisy Epic-Sounds and the absence of a DHH user study leave real-world usefulness unresolved.
