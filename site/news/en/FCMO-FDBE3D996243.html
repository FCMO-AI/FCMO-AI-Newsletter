Anthropic reports Claude leads 26% of measured AI R&D while none is fully autonomous

Anthropic published a prototype R&D Automation Index based on roughly 15,000 sampled granular R&D tasks organized into 378 leaf categories and rated on Epoch AI's AL0-AL5 automation scale. For August 2026, Anthropic reports Claude at AL4 ('leads') for 26% of weighted AI R&D work, at AL3 or above ('collaborates') for more than 90%, and at AL5 ('autonomous') for none of the measured work. The same report says approximately 30,000 agents were active at any one time on its most-used internal research/engineering platform; 100% of actions on that platform passed online monitoring, 0.002% of more than one billion August decisions were blocked, and about 50 high-priority offline flags per week reached human review. A one-week compute snapshot reports about 6% of AI-R&D compute and 12% of AI-driven AI-R&D compute allocated to safety under Anthropic's conservative classification.

What actually changed

Anthropic published a prototype R&D Automation Index based on roughly 15,000 sampled granular R&D tasks organized into 378 leaf categories and rated on Epoch AI's AL0-AL5 automation scale. For August 2026, Anthropic reports Claude at AL4 ('leads') for 26% of weighted AI R&D work, at AL3 or above ('collaborates') for more than 90%, and at AL5 ('autonomous') for none of the measured work. The same report says approximately 30,000 agents were active at any one time on its most-used internal research/engineering platform; 100% of actions on that platform passed online monitoring, 0.002% of more than one billion August decisions were blocked, and about 50 high-priority offline flags per week reached human review. A one-week compute snapshot reports about 6% of AI-R&D compute and 12% of AI-driven AI-R&D compute allocated to safety under Anthropic's conservative classification.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — Claude leads 26% of Anthropic's measured AI R&D work as of August 2026.
  • CLAIMED — More than 90% of measured AI R&D is at AL3 or above, while no measured subset is fully autonomous at AL5.
  • CLAIMED — Approximately 30,000 agents were active at any one time on Anthropic's most-used internal R&D platform, with all actions passing the online monitor and 0.002% of more than one billion August decisions blocked.
  • INFERRED — The results establish rapid partial automation of frontier R&D but do not establish autonomous recursive self-improvement.

Strongest baseline

The most relevant baseline is Anthropic's own February 2026 AL4 share, reported as under 1%; cross-lab comparison is not yet valid because methodology is not standardized.

The caveat that matters

  • Automation ratings and much of the evidence extraction use Claude itself, creating correlated-judge risk.
  • Underlying internal work records and telemetry are unavailable for independent audit.
  • Person-time weighting is a crude proxy for contribution to model progress and may underweight rare bottleneck tasks.
  • The 30,000-agent oversight figures cover only Anthropic's most-used internal platform, not all company agent activity.
  • Cross-lab comparisons are currently invalid without common task definitions, weighting and independent verification.
  • A high AL4 share does not imply equivalent acceleration in end-to-end model capability because compute and non-automatable bottlenecks can dominate.

What remains unknown

  • No independent third party has yet verified the reported automation levels, weighting or monitor telemetry.
  • The 378-leaf task tree, per-category ratings, weights and raw sampled evidence are not publicly available.

FCMO Lens

This is unusually concrete first-party evidence about the production process of frontier-model R&D rather than a benchmark proxy. If the methodology survives independent audit and becomes comparable across labs, it gives the field a measurable way to track AI-driven acceleration, oversight load and safety-compute allocation before fully autonomous recursive self-improvement. The reported 26% AL4 share is material, but the explicit zero-AL5 result is equally important: it argues for rapid partial automation, not autonomous self-improvement today.
