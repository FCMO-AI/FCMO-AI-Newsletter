SAFE paper measures whether four frontier models seek safety evidence before deployment decisions

Omer Tafveez introduces SAFE, a two-stage benchmark in which models can inspect optional safety evidence before making deployment decisions and then respond to findings. What actually changed

Omer Tafveez introduces SAFE, a two-stage benchmark in which models can inspect optional safety evidence before making deployment decisions and then respond to findings. The paper evaluates GPT-5.5, o3, Claude Opus 4.8 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 on 900 controlled environments with five rollouts per scenario. The authors report inspection responds more to severity and retrieval cost than to stated risk probability: raising probability from 10% to 70% changes inspection by at most 21 percentage points. A linked repository provides scenarios, saved outputs and analysis notebooks. This is a prompted, stylized benchmark, not a measure of latent motivation or autonomous open-ended evidence search; no independent rerun was found.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The paper introduces SAFE, a 900-environment, two-stage benchmark across five domains. It varies issue probability, severity, retrieval cost and evidence-channel presentation; models first choose whether to inspect and then choose a response if a finding is revealed.
  • CLAIMED — Across GPT-5.5, o3, Claude Opus 4.8 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 with five rollouts per scenario, the authors report inspection rises with issue severity and falls as numeric retrieval cost rises. Raising stated issue probability from 10% to 70% changes inspection by at most 21 percentage points in the reported main conditions.
  • CLAIMED — In a targeted 50-scenario cost–obligation decomposition, adding a 60-point retrieval cost to the free/no-obligation baseline reduces inspection rates to 33% (GPT-5.5), 18% (o3), 72% (Opus 4.8) and 32% (Sonnet 4.6); the authors report explicit cancellation threats account for more of the obligation effect than remediation duty alone.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper links an author GitHub repository whose README describes code, scenarios, saved model outputs and analysis notebooks for SAFE. Repository availability does not independently validate the reported experiments.

Strongest baseline

No competing benchmark baseline is central to the paper; its main comparison is within-model behavior across controlled scenario conditions. The article compares channel formats and counterfactual variants rather than external evaluation methods.

The caveat that matters

  • The study measures prompted observable choices and generated explanations, not latent motivation. Absence of a strategic-ignorance rationale does not rule out internal information avoidance.
  • The 900 scenarios are a stylized single-turn benchmark. Models are explicitly offered an inspection action with stated costs, probabilities and payoffs; autonomous open-ended evidence search is not tested.
  • The study evaluates four models from two providers; results may differ under less evaluation-like deployment conditions.
  • The high-severity condition combines increased harm with protected-group bias and legal exposure, so it is not a pure severity scalar.
  • Evidence Discovery changes wording because it has no named report, so the channel contrast is not a minimal presentation-only intervention.
  • Counterfactual cases are selected for diagnostic sensitivity; their flip rates do not estimate population-wide treatment effects.
  • Rationale-monitor labels are imperfect: Stage 2 agreement is 81.1% with one annotator and 67.8% with another on a stratified sample; labels are approximate and not direct evidence of causal reasoning.
  • The author repository exposes saved outputs and analysis materials, but no independent rerun or external validity study was performed here.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of the model inspection patterns or controlled interventions was located. Author-reported saved outputs and code do not establish independent validation.
  • SAFE uses a prompted single-turn setting that explicitly offers evidence gathering with numeric payoffs. It does not test autonomous recognition of missing information or open-ended search.
  • The high-severity manipulation changes harm type by adding systematic demographic bias and legal exposure; severity is not a pure scalar. Evidence Discovery also requires different wording, limiting attribution to channel framing alone.
  • Rationale labels use an ordered monitor ontology; Stage 2 monitor agreement is below one human annotator and selected counterfactual cases are diagnostic, not population estimates. Rationale text does not directly establish latent motivation.

FCMO Lens

Safety evaluations often provide risk information up front. SAFE tests an earlier decision: whether a model chooses to gather optional evidence before acting, separately from what it does after seeing a finding. If validated in broader settings, this distinction could improve evaluation design. Current results are bounded to a controlled single-turn task and four models.
