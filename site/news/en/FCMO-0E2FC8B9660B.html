A paper reports prompt-based manipulation of LLM serving length estimates, with fixed-output completion gains near parity

The authors of JIL report a white-box attack that appends an optimized suffix to reduce the output-length estimate used by a TRAIL-style scheduler. In their end-to-end serving experiments, the system moved target requests earlier in queue order. The paper’s controls show that the headline 1.53× completion advantage combines scheduling and changes in generated output length: when output is fixed at 512 tokens, target completion speedup is 1.05 [0.92, 1.18] at 16 slots and 1.00 [0.97, 1.03] at 64; the fixed-output signal comparison against FCFS is 0.97 [0.95, 1.00] and 1.01 [0.99, 1.03]. The reported setup uses one model for serving, one GPU, and five small workloads; attack construction assumes white-box model/probe access and per-request optimization. These are paper-reported results, not an independent reproduction or evidence of deployed prevalence.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv:2610.03430, a paper describing JIL, an attack on prediction-based LLM request scheduling.
  • Claimed — The authors report that an optimized prompt suffix lowers the length estimate used by a TRAIL-style scheduler and can move attacked requests earlier in queue order in their tested serving experiments.
  • Claimed — In the paper’s 512-token fixed-output controls, target completion speedup is 1.05 [0.92, 1.18] at 16 slots and 1.00 [0.97, 1.03] at 64; the fixed-output signal contrast against FCFS is 0.97 [0.95, 1.00] and 1.01 [0.99, 1.03]. This indicates queue-position manipulation in the tested setup but little or no isolated completion-time benefit in these controls.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproducibility, black-box attack feasibility, prevalence in deployed systems, production-scale effects, or generality across schedulers and serving setups.

Strongest baseline

The paper’s own fixed-output controls and FCFS comparison isolate scheduling effects from changes in generated response length; they show queue-position movement but completion-speedup estimates close to parity.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported and not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • End-to-end serving results use one model, one GPU and five small workloads; broader scheduler and production generality is not established.
  • The headline natural-output completion ratio combines scheduling effects with output-length changes; fixed-output controls put completion effects near parity with uncertainty intervals spanning parity.
  • The attack assumes white-box access and per-request optimization, so black-box feasibility and deployment prevalence remain unknown.

Why it matters

The paper identifies a potential integrity weakness in serving systems that use prompt-derived length predictions to assign request priority. Its controlled results support a narrow queue-order finding, while fixed-output completion measures are near parity. Independent reproduction and real-world prevalence remain open.
