MIGU reports improved pointing-and-language grounding and clarification gains on a mobile-manipulation robot

The September 21 preprint introduces MIGU, which fuses a VLM-derived semantic prior with a 3D pointing likelihood and can choose between proceeding and asking a binary clarification question. On a 100-scenario instruction-grounding benchmark, the authors report 83% object-level accuracy, beating the tested baselines. In a separate real-world mobile-manipulation study, eight tasks were repeated three times: planning success is reported at 83.3% with semantic-geometric fusion, versus 45.8% for semantic-only and 41.7% for geometry-only grounding; adding clarification raises the fused condition to 91.7%. The results are paper-reported. The study does not supply independent reproduction, broad task coverage or evidence of reliability in deployment.

What changed

The September 21 preprint introduces MIGU, which fuses a VLM-derived semantic prior with a 3D pointing likelihood and can choose between proceeding and asking a binary clarification question. On a 100-scenario instruction-grounding benchmark, the authors report 83% object-level accuracy, beating the tested baselines. Evidence

  • Claimed — MIGU fuses a 3D geometric likelihood from pointing with semantic priors from a vision-language model, then uses an attribute-based one-round decision to proceed or ask a binary clarification question.
  • Claimed — On a 100-scenario multimodal instruction-grounding benchmark, the authors report 83% object-level accuracy for MIGU, the strongest tested result; the paper reports 83% accuracy for the full model in its ablation table.
  • Claimed — On eight real-world mobile-manipulation tasks repeated three times, paper-reported planning success is 83.3% with semantic-geometric fusion, 45.8% with semantic-only grounding and 41.7% with geometry-only grounding; adding clarification to the fused system raises reported success to 91.7%.
  • Not established — These results do not establish independent replication, reliable calibration, robust performance across broader tasks or settings, multi-round clarification, or deployment reliability.

Strongest baseline

On the real-robot mobile-manipulation comparison, semantic-geometric fusion without clarification is reported at 83.3%, versus semantic-only 45.8% and geometry-only 41.7%; adding clarification is 91.7%. On the 100-scenario benchmark, the authors say MIGU outperforms all evaluated methods, but the extracted text exposes table values primarily for ablations rather than all baseline rows.

Limitations

  • All outcome measurements are reported in the paper; no independent reproduction or robot-log audit was performed.
  • The physical mobile-manipulation evaluation covers eight tasks repeated three times; it does not establish broad task or environment robustness.
  • The vision-language model's semantic confidence is used as a relative prior, not a calibrated probability; the paper identifies calibration as future work.
  • Clarification is limited to a single binary interaction round and does not jointly optimize later manipulation steps.
  • The grounding benchmark has 100 scenarios; paired per-task outcomes, uncertainty intervals and released raw logs were not inspected.
  • Tabletop task-and-motion planning is demonstrated through examples without aggregate success-rate evidence.
  • The system uses specific sensors, models, robot platforms and planning components; transfer to other robots or deployment settings is unknown.

What we still don't know

  • The grounding benchmark, mobile-manipulation task success and clarification gains have not been independently reproduced or checked against raw robot logs.
  • The mobile-manipulation evaluation includes only eight tasks repeated three times, with no reported uncertainty interval or broad environment sweep.
  • VLM semantic confidence is treated as a relative prior rather than calibrated uncertainty, and clarification is limited to one round.

Why it matters

Robots that combine pointing, language and visual context need to know when their target estimate is uncertain enough to ask a person before acting. MIGU links an explicit uncertainty model to a clarification decision and task planning, with reported outcomes on both a labeled grounding set and a small repeated mobile-manipulation study. Its calibration assumptions, single-round clarification, limited tasks and lack of independent reproduction bound the result.
