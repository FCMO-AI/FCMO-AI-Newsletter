OpenAI paper encodes Turing-machine halting in forced Navier–Stokes particle motion

An OpenAI-authored paper constructs a smooth external force that encodes any finite Turing machine and input into a Navier–Stokes flow: from zero initial velocity, one fixed particle enters a fixed open region exactly if the machine halts. The core construction uses fixed positive computable viscosity, compact spatial support and bounded kinetic energy. These are source-reported mathematical results, not independently reviewed here. The construction is forced and remains globally smooth; it does not show unforced blowup or resolve the Clay Millennium problem.

Why it matters

If the construction is correct, it shows a precise halting-dependent event can be embedded in a smooth forced fluid flow under a fixed flat geometry. This is relevant to computability in mathematical fluid dynamics and to the scope of algorithmic guarantees for forced-flow prediction. The paper does not give a complexity lower bound, establish practical simulation hardness, or generalize to arbitrary initial data or unforced dynamics.
