MLExpert reports post-training gains on long-horizon machine-learning engineering

The Oct 15 arXiv paper introduces MLExpert, a 35B-total/approximately 3B-active MoE initialized from Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and post-trained for long-horizon machine-learning engineering using hierarchical agentic SFT and Windowed Interaction RL. On a 22-task MLE-Bench Lite subset, the authors report 72.7% any-medal versus 37.9% for the base under their unified harness; under Claude Code they report 69.7% versus 42.4%. A 10-task NatureBench Lite comparison also reports gains over the base. These are paper-reported benchmark results, not an independent demonstration of autonomous AI research or recursive self-improvement.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that MLExpert, a post-trained 35B-total/approximately 3B-active MoE initialized from Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, improves any-medal rate on the 22-task MLE-Bench Lite subset from 37.9% to 72.7% under their unified harness.
  • Claimed — Under the Claude Code general-purpose harness, the paper reports 69.7% any-medal for MLExpert versus 42.4% for the same-scale base model.
  • Claimed — The paper reports NatureBench Lite Surpass-SOTA on 5/10 tasks for MLExpert versus 0/10 for its base under Claude Code; other evaluated frontier systems also score strongly on this small subset.

Strongest baseline

The same-scale Qwen3.6-35B-A3B base under the same reported evaluation harness; a Claude Code general-purpose harness comparison is reported separately.

Limitations

  • The 37.9% to 72.7% comparison is author-reported on only 22 historical competition tasks; task-level variance and broader benchmark transfer need independent examination.
  • The Claude Code and paper harness results are separately reported; they should not be conflated with the unified-harness result.
  • NatureBench Lite uses only ten tasks; the reported 5/10 result does not establish broad scientific discovery capability.
  • No independent reproduction, released-artifact audit, complete training-compute accounting, or benchmark contamination audit was performed.
  • The paper's recursive-self-improvement framing is a motivation; the measured tasks do not demonstrate autonomous development of a stronger foundation model.

What we still don't know

  • The paper's benchmark and generalization results are author-reported. Released code/model, task generation and train/test separation were not audited or independently run.
  • Complete training-compute and cost accounting is not provided in the reviewed paper sections, limiting efficiency comparison.

Why it matters

The study tests whether an agent can learn to choose experiments, revise executable solutions and use intermediate execution feedback over sustained machine-learning workflows. If the reported improvement transfers to broader tasks and independently reproduced setups, it could affect how AI engineering agents are trained. The present evidence is bounded to historical competition tasks and author-run evaluations.
