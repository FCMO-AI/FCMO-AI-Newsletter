Paper formalizes safety and retry costs for stateful agent execution harnesses

The paper models an agent's tool-execution harness as a stateful Petri-net supervisor: approvals, capabilities and budgets become consumable marks, and an external kernel admits calls only when interface, validation, state and supervisor checks pass. Its proofs show safety for the specified model and give four assumptions needed to transfer that result to a running system. It also models the extra inference cost when an agent retries after refusal and derives a proposer-aware safe supervisor that can reduce that cost. The release-agent example is a synthesis illustration, not deployment evidence. Allowed-call argument attacks, bypasses and system correspondence need separate validation.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors formalize an agent execution harness as an Agentic Execution Petri Net. A call is admitted only when its proposal maps to a modeled transition, a validator accepts its arguments, the transition is enabled by the current marking, and the supervisor permits it; consumable marks encode capabilities, approvals and budgets.
  • Claimed — The authors prove safety for the modeled net and state four assumptions for transferring that guarantee to a running system: faithful state tracking, step correspondence between the interface and net, complete mediation of relevant effects/events, and coverage of relevant forbidden states. The guarantee does not itself inspect malicious arguments on otherwise admitted calls, and the paper excludes direct bypass of the admission kernel.
  • Claimed — In a replanning model where each refusal can trigger another inference call, the authors show that the conventional maximally permissive safe supervisor can incur arbitrarily more refusal cost than a stricter safe supervisor. They characterize a least-intrusive refinement using a proposer model; the refinement only removes actions from the safe supervisor, so the stated safety guarantee does not depend on proposer-model accuracy.
  • Claimed — In a synthesized autonomous-release example, the paper reports a condition under which a least-intrusive supervisor orders a security scan before a release request: for its illustrative proposer, the order changes when the premature-deployment proposal probability exceeds 43/60. This is a model example, not an observed deployment rate.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the conventional maximally permissive supervisor with a stricter safe supervisor under its refusal-cost replanning model and analytically characterizes the proposer-aware refinement; this is a within-model formal comparison, not a comparison with deployed agent scaffolds.

Limitations

  • Results are mathematical claims for the specified Petri-net model; no deployed harness was measured or independently verified here.
  • Safety transfer requires the four stated modeling/mediation assumptions; an omitted system event or unmodeled alternate route can invalidate correspondence.
  • The net supervisor does not decide whether arguments to an allowed action are malicious; separate argument validation and workflow controls are required.
  • The release-agent example uses an illustrative proposer model and does not estimate real agent behavior or attack frequency.
  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the threshold, safety or least-intrusive results was conducted in this activation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent formal proof review or implementation-level replication was performed; all technical results here are attributed to the authors' v1 paper.
  • The safety transfer depends on faithful state mapping and complete mediation, and the paper does not validate that real agent systems satisfy these assumptions. Malicious arguments on allowed calls, approval laundering and bypasses remain outside the guarantee unless separately modeled and validated.
  • The least-intrusive supervisor's optimization uses a proposer model, and practical synthesis cost/scaling is not established for large harness state spaces.

Why it matters

Tool-using agents can perform actions whose order and repetition matter. The paper gives a formal way to express approvals and budgets as state that an external supervisor can consume, and explicitly prices the retries caused by refusals. This could inform safer and less intrusive runtime controls if real systems satisfy its mapping and mediation assumptions. It does not establish that deployed harnesses currently satisfy those assumptions or prevent malicious payloads on allowed calls.
