System One decision models show lower wrong-pick rates on a constructed legal-authority abstention test

A University at Albany preprint compares four System One decision models with six generative language models on the CaseHOLD legal-reasoning benchmark. In a constructed abstention condition, the authors replace the correct holding with a similar real holding in 1,670 of 5,314 test items and add a “none” option. They report wrong-pick rates of 37.8–85.6% for generative models and 30.1–60.8% for decision models; the most cautious decision model also abstains on 26.2% of items where the correct holding remains. An agreement gate between a decision model and a generative model reportedly halves wrong citations on a held-out split. These are author-reported results from one English-language U.S. case-law task, not independent reproduction or evidence of legal reliability in practice.

What changed

A University at Albany preprint compares four System One decision models with six generative language models on the CaseHOLD legal-reasoning benchmark. In a constructed abstention condition, the authors replace the correct holding with a similar real holding in 1,670 of 5,314 test items and add a “none” option. They report wrong-pick rates of 37.8–85.6% for generative models and 30.1–60.8% for decision models; the most cautious decision model also abstains on 26.2% of items where the correct holding remains. An agreement gate between a decision model and a generative model reportedly halves wrong citations on a held-out split. These are author-reported results from one English-language U.S. case-law task, not independent reproduction or evidence of legal reliability in practice.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that when the correct holding is replaced by a similar real holding, six generative models select a wrong holding in 37.8–85.6% of affected test items, while four decision models do so in 30.1–60.8%.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the most cautious decision model has a 30.1% wrong-pick rate on affected items and falsely abstains on 26.2% of items where the correct holding remains.
  • Claimed — On a held-out split, the authors report that accepting a generative model answer only when a decision model agrees halves wrong citations for the strongest tested generative model.
  • Not established — The results do not establish practical legal reliability, citation correctness, or transfer beyond this English U.S. CaseHOLD task and constructed missing-holding condition.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares ten model versions on the same CaseHOLD test items with fixed short prompts, reports bootstrap confidence intervals and paired tests, and reserves a random 30% of items to tune combination thresholds before reporting on the remaining 70%. No independent reproduction, live retrieval test or legal-practice benchmark was performed.

Limitations

  • This is a single author-run preprint accepted to a workshop; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The test uses English-language U.S. case law and a constructed missing-holding condition, not live legal research workflows.
  • Model agreement is not proof that a citation supports a proposition; source reading and legal verification remain necessary.
  • The wrong-citation versus manual-review cost ratio is illustrative and user-specific.
  • The paper states that contamination checks cannot rule out that closed models saw some public passages during training.

What we still don't know

  • Reported metrics and the agreement gate have not been independently recomputed or reproduced in this review.
  • The constructed absence condition replaces a holding with a similar real holding and does not test other common gaps such as overruled authority, wrong jurisdiction, nonexistent cases, or retrieved evidence that does not support a proposition.
  • The relative cost assigned to a wrong citation versus an unnecessary human review is illustrative; it varies by legal setting and user.

Why it matters

A low-cost abstention check could help identify unsupported authority claims before they reach legal workflows, and the study shows why wrong citations and unnecessary review should be measured separately. The result is bounded to one constructed CaseHOLD condition; it does not establish that model agreement makes a citation correct, or that the gains transfer to real retrieval, overruled cases, jurisdiction mismatch or other legal systems.
