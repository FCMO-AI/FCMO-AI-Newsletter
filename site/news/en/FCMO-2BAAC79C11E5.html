VRR uses verification feedback to expand candidate search, with mixed reported benchmark results

Tomihari and Ichikawa introduce Verify–Repair–Reselect (VRR), which feeds verifier diagnostics back into generation: after ranking an initial pool, it retains the winner, conditionally creates repairs of the winner and runner-up plus a new-approach candidate, filters invalid/duplicate outputs and reselects under the original criteria. Across two models and eight code/reasoning benchmarks, the authors report VRR beats fixed-pool LAV selection in 9 of 16 settings. Table 1 includes improvements as well as null and negative cells. On LiveCodeBench, the authors report recovery on some problems where all initial candidates were incorrect. These are paper-reported results using the same model for generation, verification and repair; matched total-inference-budget comparison with best-of-N and independent reproduction remain open.

What actually changed

Tomihari and Ichikawa introduce Verify–Repair–Reselect (VRR), which feeds verifier diagnostics back into generation: after ranking an initial pool, it retains the winner, conditionally creates repairs of the winner and runner-up plus a new-approach candidate, filters invalid/duplicate outputs and reselects under the original criteria. Across two models and eight code/reasoning benchmarks, the authors report VRR beats fixed-pool LAV selection in 9 of 16 settings. Table 1 includes improvements as well as null and negative cells. On LiveCodeBench, the authors report recovery on some problems where all initial candidates were incorrect. These are paper-reported results using the same model for generation, verification and repair; matched total-inference-budget comparison with best-of-N and independent reproduction remain open.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — Across two models and eight benchmarks, the authors report VRR improves over fixed-pool LAV selection in 9 of 16 settings; Table 1 also contains null and negative settings.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report VRR can recover correct LiveCodeBench solutions when every candidate in the initial pool is incorrect; the recovery result is not independently reproduced.
  • CLAIMED — The same model performs generation, verification, repair and reselection; no matched total-inference-budget comparison against best-of-N was established in this review.

Strongest baseline

Fixed-pool LAV selection over the same frozen initial candidates, plus a Single first-candidate baseline. The paper does not establish a matched total-token/latency comparison with best-of-N independent sampling.

The caveat that matters

  • All results are paper-reported; the research desk did not execute code or independently reproduce the experiments.
  • VRR improves over fixed-pool LAV in 9 of 16 model-benchmark settings; the table includes null and negative cells.
  • The same model performs generation, verification, repair and reselection, so verifier errors may be inherited or amplified.
  • The paper does not establish that gains survive an equal total generation-plus-verification token or latency budget versus best-of-N sampling.
  • Passing public tests or format filters establishes candidate eligibility, not correctness; reselection can still choose an incorrect answer.
  • Table 1 reports VRR below fixed-pool LAV for some settings, including -0.61 percentage points on HumanEval+ with Gemma 4 31B IT and -0.27 on MBPP with Qwen3-32B; other cells are tied.

What remains unknown

  • Reported accuracy results and all-incorrect recovery cases have not been independently reproduced.
  • Equal-total-inference-compute comparison against best-of-N sampling is missing.

FCMO Lens

Verifier feedback can describe why candidates fail, not just which candidate ranks highest. Reusing that diagnostic information to generate alternatives could improve test-time search when every initial candidate is wrong. The paper’s mixed results, same-model verifier/generator and missing compute-normalized best-of-N comparison mean the gain should be treated as a bounded method result rather than general evidence that repair beats more sampling.
