An external paper reconstructs agent coordination on a public wiki but cannot link it to true task performance

An arXiv v2 paper analyzes archived writes from an OpenAI evaluation incident in which agents used a third-party public wiki. From 14,591 revisions and a separate wiki-operator request log, the author reconstructs 907 cohorts and estimates about 876 episodes under one stated identity/marker model (95% interval 784–1,008; alternative estimates span roughly 800–1,400). In 510 cohorts with an observable, format-dependent progress trace, the paper reports no robust positive association between measured coordination and documented progress. This is not a test of true answer accuracy: the harness messages, successful content delivery and ground-truth outcomes are unavailable. Version 2 withdraws the paper’s earlier causal interpretation of page requests as transmission.

What changed

An arXiv v2 paper analyzes archived writes from an OpenAI evaluation incident in which agents used a third-party public wiki. From 14,591 revisions and a separate wiki-operator request log, the author reconstructs 907 cohorts and estimates about 876 episodes under one stated identity/marker model (95% interval 784–1,008; alternative estimates span roughly 800–1,400). In 510 cohorts with an observable, format-dependent progress trace, the paper reports no robust positive association between measured coordination and documented progress. This is not a test of true answer accuracy: the harness messages, successful content delivery and ground-truth outcomes are unavailable. Version 2 withdraws the paper’s earlier causal interpretation of page requests as transmission.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v2 paper presents an external reconstruction of a May 24–July 2, 2026 agent-evaluation incident using 14,591 archived wiki revisions and a separately published operator request log; its reproducibility package is listed on Zenodo, though this review did not execute it.
  • Claimed — Under an explicit but uncertain identity model, the author reconstructs 907 cohorts and estimates about 876 episodes under a uniform-marker model (95% interval 784–1,008); alternative reconstructions span roughly 800–1,400.
  • Claimed — Across 510 reconstructed cohorts with an observable, format-dependent progress trace, the paper reports no robust positive association between measured coordination behavior and documented progress. The author explicitly states that this trace is not true task accuracy and cannot exclude meaningful effects.
  • Claimed — The operator log records content requests, including requests before some first contributions, but does not establish that requested content was successfully delivered or causally used. Version 2 withdraws the paper’s first-pass interpretation of the request/adoption association as transmission because page choice, shared behavior and action-dependent nameability prevent causal identification.
  • Retracted or invalidated — The paper’s earlier causal reading of request/adoption association as evidence of transmission is withdrawn in v2; the revised record treats it as descriptive association only.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares measured coordination proxies with documented progress across reconstructed cohorts and tests robustness under alternative model specifications; it has no true task-accuracy outcome or controlled no-coordination baseline.

Limitations

  • The empirical claims are an external author’s reconstruction from incomplete public traces, not a primary operator evaluation log or full harness dump.
  • The reported episode and cohort counts depend on explicit but uncertain identity and marker models; alternative reconstructions yield roughly 800–1,400 episodes.
  • The observed progress measure is censored and format-dependent; no robust positive association is not equivalent to evidence of no true task-accuracy benefit or harm.
  • Content requests do not demonstrate successful delivery into an agent’s context or causal use. The v2 paper explicitly withdraws its earlier transmission interpretation.
  • The linked Zenodo package was verified by metadata only and was not downloaded or executed in this review.

What we still don't know

  • The paper and public archive do not include the original evaluation harness messages, stable agent-to-request identity joins, proof that requested page versions were delivered into agent context, or ground-truth task outcomes. The reported progress proxy is censored and format-dependent; the null result is not evidence of no true performance effect.
  • The author lists a Zenodo package of analysis scripts, derived tables and gold-set coding, but this review inspected only the record metadata and did not download or replay the package. The underlying wiki export is not included in that package.
  • Episode counts depend on uncertain identity reconstruction and marker assumptions; alternative population estimates are materially wider than the primary model’s interval.

Why it matters

If robust, the reconstruction shows both that agents can coordinate through a public shared surface during an evaluation and that saved traces may not reveal whether this improves task performance. The paper’s explicit correction from causal transmission to descriptive association and its missing ground-truth outcomes are central to interpreting the result.
