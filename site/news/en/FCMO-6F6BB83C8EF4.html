DRYLAB-Bench reports elevated assay-based biosecurity risk scores in tool-augmented model evaluations

A new preprint reports DRYLAB-Bench, an in-silico evaluation of biological-design outputs from 15 frontier language models across 30 policy-derived scenarios, scored against 187,695 pre-existing deep-mutational-scan measurements. The authors report that 67.6% of one-shot designs exceeded the average measured variant and 21.4% fell in the assay-specific highest-risk decile; under their tool-augmented iterative workflow, the top-decile share rose to 23.5% and the pooled Biosecurity Risk Score increased by 0.116 standard deviations. These are author-reported results from single-substitution assay proxies, not evidence of multi-variant organism-level outcomes, physical construction, or real-world harm. The paper’s sequential design does not isolate whether tool outputs or the additional deliberation cause the full increase, and no independent reproduction was inspected.

Strongest baseline

The strongest within-paper baseline is the same model’s pre-tool one-shot submission in the same sequential trajectory. The authors also report single-round and repeated no-tool controls, but the no-tool iteration arm is under-sampled and noisy.

Limitations

  • Results are reported by the paper authors in a single preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The Biosecurity Risk Score is an assay-relative proxy based on single-substitution measurements; it does not establish multi-variant phenotypes, organism-level behavior, transmission or real-world harm.
  • The study’s sequential conditions do not fully separate tool-output effects from extra deliberation; the authors identify further controls that are not run.
  • The paper includes sensitive adversarial prompts and biological examples; this public record retains only high-level methods and aggregate results.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the benchmark scoring, model runs or aggregate risk results was inspected.
  • The score is bounded to single-substitution assay measurements and does not establish multi-variant effects, host-context biology, transmission, or operational real-world risk.
  • The within-trajectory comparison does not isolate the causal contribution of tool-output content from extra deliberation; the paper describes two unrun mechanism controls.

Why it matters

If the reported assay-bounded results replicate, this evaluation moves biosecurity assessment beyond knowledge and refusal tests toward empirical measurement of model-generated designs and tool-augmented workflows. That could change how developers evaluate access to computational design tools. The measured single-substitution assay outcomes do not establish organism-level effects or operational threat.
