MorFiC reports critic-conditioned transfer across quadruped morphologies

The March 15 preprint presents MorFiC, which conditions a shared policy's critic on robot morphology using feature-wise modulation. The authors report improved zero-shot quadruped transfer after simulation training with morphology randomization, including speed gains of 16.1% on A1, about 2× on Cheetah and about 5× on B1 versus morphology-conditioned PPO baselines. They also report deployment on Unitree Go1 and Go2 without fine-tuning. Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that MorFiC, a morphology-conditioned critic using feature-wise modulation in a shared actor-critic policy, improves zero-shot quadruped transfer after simulation training with morphology randomization. The abstract reports speed gains of 16.1% on A1, about 2× on Cheetah and about 5× on B1 relative to morphology-conditioned PPO baselines, plus deployment on Go1 and Go2 robots without fine-tuning. These are paper-reported results, not independent measurements in this review.

Strongest baseline

Limitations

  • All quantitative performance results are author-reported in a preprint and were not independently reproduced here.
  • The headline simulation velocity gains do not show task-level success, safety, robustness or economic performance across robot platforms.
  • The physical deployment claim is limited in the abstract to Unitree Go1 and Go2 without fine-tuning; the test conditions and failure rates were not inspected.
  • The result concerns quadruped locomotion and does not establish general morphology transfer to other robot types.

What we still don't know

  • The abstract gives selected speed gains and says the policy was deployed on two Unitree platforms without fine-tuning, but this review did not inspect task counts, robot test conditions, failure rates, uncertainty, or a replication.

Why it matters

Robots with different body dynamics can invalidate a shared policy's value estimates. If the reported results hold, morphology-conditioned critic features may offer a practical way to transfer locomotion policies without retraining each robot. Current evidence is limited to the authors' stated quadruped simulations and two Unitree deployments, and does not establish safe general-purpose transfer.
