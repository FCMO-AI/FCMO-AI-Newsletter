Video DeltaNet combines local softmax and linear memory for faster long-video diffusion

Xi et al. present Video DeltaNet (VDN), a hybrid attention design instantiated on MiniMax H3 for video diffusion. It keeps exact softmax attention within a local temporal window and at first/last-frame anchors, while bidirectional linear memory handles distant video context; a frame-wise joint delta update couples correlated spatial writes. The authors report that, at 50 denoising evaluations on one B200, VDN-H3 reduces backbone latency from 799.6 to 307.9 seconds (2.6×) while remaining close to the dense baseline on their automatic quality metrics. Their optimized eight-step, eight-B200 path reports 6.7 seconds of denoising for a 14.3-second 768p clip. These are author-reported measurements, not independent reproductions; the final speed combines architecture, distillation and multi-GPU inference.

What actually changed

Xi et al. present Video DeltaNet (VDN), a hybrid attention design instantiated on MiniMax H3 for video diffusion. It keeps exact softmax attention within a local temporal window and at first/last-frame anchors, while bidirectional linear memory handles distant video context; a frame-wise joint delta update couples correlated spatial writes. The authors report that, at 50 denoising evaluations on one B200, VDN-H3 reduces backbone latency from 799.6 to 307.9 seconds (2.6×) while remaining close to the dense baseline on their automatic quality metrics. Their optimized eight-step, eight-B200 path reports 6.7 seconds of denoising for a 14.3-second 768p clip. These are author-reported measurements, not independent reproductions; the final speed combines architecture, distillation and multi-GPU inference.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper presents Video DeltaNet as a hybrid that retains local-window and first/last-frame softmax attention while using bidirectional linear memory for distant video context; its Video Delta Attention update jointly solves frame-level spatial writes.
  • CLAIMED — For fixed prepared features and gates under the paper’s stated nonnegative write-gate and bounded decay assumptions, the authors derive a non-expansive inherited-state transition for the frame update.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that VDN-H3 reduces 50-evaluation single-B200 backbone latency from 799.6 seconds to 307.9 seconds (2.6×) relative to dense H3, while remaining close on the paper’s automatic quality metrics.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report 6.7 seconds of denoising for a 14.3-second, 768p video on eight B200 GPUs after eight-step distillation; the paper’s 119.3× cumulative speedup is against single-GPU, 50-step dense H3 and combines architecture, fewer steps and distributed inference.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The authors released training and inference code under Apache-2.0 and distribute the derivative model weights separately under the MiniMax H3 Community License Agreement; the repository says its full checkpoint bundle is about 82 GB.

Strongest baseline

Dense MiniMax H3 at matched 50 denoising evaluations for the architecture-only single-GPU timing comparison; the paper also compares Radial Attention at matched latency and FastH3 v2 for eight-evaluation quality. Its headline cumulative speedup uses a different one-GPU/50-step baseline than the eight-GPU/eight-step VDN endpoint.

The caveat that matters

  • All reported runtime and quality results are author-reported; no independent reproduction or external video-quality panel was completed.
  • The 103-prompt automatic evaluation set, model, resolution, clip length and hardware define a narrow regime; transfer to other video models, tasks, lengths and production traffic is unknown.
  • The 6.7-second denoising figure excludes model load, warm-up, VAE decoding and MP4 encoding. The reported eight-GPU result does not establish lower total compute, energy or cost than a dense baseline.
  • The 119.3× cumulative figure compares an eight-step/eight-B200 VDN configuration with single-GPU/50-step dense H3; it combines architectural acceleration, sampler-step reduction and multi-GPU inference. The matched single-GPU, 50-step architectural speedup is 2.6× on B200 and 3.2× on H200.
  • GitHub README reports a 6.9-second denoising and approximately 9-second end-to-end setup, while arXiv v3 reports 6.7-second denoising in its table; the configurations differ and were not independently reconciled beyond their stated timing scopes.
  • The model weights are derivative of MiniMax H3 and use its Community License Agreement; Apache-2.0 applies to the repository code, not the weights.
  • The paper’s non-expansive-state result is conditional on fixed prepared features and gates and does not bound the full input-dependent network.

What remains unknown

  • No independent run confirmed the paper’s B200/H200 latency or automatic video-quality results; public weights and code were not executed in this review.
  • The paper reports latency but not full accelerator-hours, energy or cost per accepted video; its cumulative headline compares different GPU counts and denoising steps.
  • Transfer beyond the MiniMax H3 checkpoint and fixed 103-prompt, 14.375-second evaluation regime is unmeasured.

FCMO Lens

If the reported quality and latency hold beyond the authors’ fixed prompt set, combining local exact attention with compressed long-range memory could lower the inference cost of long video generation without relying on a full dense attention map. The evidence is one MiniMax H3 implementation using high-end accelerators and substantial optimization; transfer to other models, hardware and production workloads is unmeasured.
