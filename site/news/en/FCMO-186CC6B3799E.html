Conduit turns distributed RL experience handling into a runtime optimization surface

Zhang et al. present Conduit, an Experience Data Plane that separates reinforcement-learning experience ingestion, placement and delivery from framework execution. The authors report up to 97% lower exposed experience-path latency and up to 38% lower end-to-end iteration latency in RLlib tests spanning DQN, SAC and PPO workloads on an eight-actor/eight-learner setup. They also report scaling from 8 to 1,024 AMD MI250X GPUs on a fixed workload, compared with Conduit without adaptive placement, and similar convergence curves in two tested tasks. These are author-reported results from one preprint, not an independent reproduction; transfer to other workloads and full cost/energy effects remain unmeasured.

What changed

The authors report up to 97% lower exposed experience-path latency and up to 38% lower end-to-end iteration latency in RLlib tests spanning DQN, SAC and PPO workloads on an eight-actor/eight-learner setup. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The paper introduces an Experience Data Plane abstraction that exposes experience ingestion, placement and delivery as explicit runtime control points separate from framework-specific execution logic.
  • Claimed — In its RLlib experiments, the paper reports up to 97% lower exposed experience-path latency and up to 38% lower end-to-end iteration latency across tested DQN, SAC and PPO workloads with eight actors and eight learners.
  • Claimed — The paper reports scaling from 8 to 1,024 AMD MI250X GPUs at fixed workload size, with 50 ms end-to-end iteration time at 1,024 GPUs; the comparison at that scale is against Conduit without adaptive placement, not Gear or Reverb.
  • Claimed — In two tested RLlib tasks, the paper reports similar reward-versus-iteration curves with and without Conduit, but this does not establish convergence preservation across algorithms or workloads generally.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The inspected preprint does not independently establish the reported latency, scale or convergence results, nor their transfer to other frameworks, later systems or production workloads.

Strongest baseline

For the end-to-end headline, RLlib's built-in experience buffer with the same stated algorithms, environments and actor/learner counts. Gear and Reverb appear in separate placement/scaling comparisons where compatible; they are not comparators for the 1,024-GPU AMD result.

Limitations

  • All latency, scale and convergence outcomes are author-reported; this activation did not run the implementation or independently reproduce them.
  • The reported 38% maximum end-to-end improvement is workload-specific. The paper says on-policy freshness constraints bound gains and rollout/update time can dominate.
  • The 1,024-GPU AMD scale result compares Conduit with and without adaptive placement, while Gear and Reverb are omitted for lack of AMD support; it is not a 1,024-GPU cross-system victory claim.
  • Convergence comparisons cover DQN/MountainCar and PPO/Meta-World only. Transfer to other algorithms, models, datasets and longer training regimes is unknown.
  • The paper does not report full energy, accelerator-hour or dollar costs, nor matched production-scale utilization and throughput.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the §7.2 RLlib latency results or convergence curves has been completed.
  • The 1,024-MI250X scaling result and adaptive-placement comparison have not been independently replicated.
  • Transfer beyond the selected environments and frameworks, and full energy/cost consequences, remain unmeasured.

Why it matters

If the reported effects generalize, treating the experience buffer as a schedulable data plane could reduce a significant training bottleneck in large distributed RL and post-training systems. The paper reports the strongest end-to-end gains where experience handling is a large part of iteration time, while on-policy freshness constraints limit gains; independent replication and broader transfer are still open.
