INSPECT learns robot inspection views from assembly-assistant records

A September 2026 preprint introduces INSPECT, which learns robot view preferences from egocentric assembly-assistant records by separating evidence needed to resolve a claim from observation changes a robot camera can reproduce, then calibrating relative views to robot pose. In offline fixed-lattice replay on gearbox assemblies, the authors report mean human-rated view utility of 1.205 and full verifiability of 41.7% across 132 matched trials, versus 1.045 and 34.8% for the current-view baseline. On 1,068 IMPACT trials, its relative-view selector reports a 54.3% correct-decision rate versus 50.6% for current view. These are author-reported results in two constrained replay settings, not a live closed-loop deployment or independent reproduction.

What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces INSPECT, which learns robot view preferences from egocentric assembly-assistant records by separating evidence needed to resolve a claim from observation changes a robot camera can reproduce, then calibrating relative views to robot pose. In offline fixed-lattice replay on gearbox assemblies, the authors report mean human-rated view utility of 1.205 and full verifiability of 41.7% across 132 matched trials, versus 1.045 and 34.8% for the current-view baseline. On 1,068 IMPACT trials, its relative-view selector reports a 54.3% correct-decision rate versus 50.6% for current view. These are author-reported results in two constrained replay settings, not a live closed-loop deployment or independent reproduction.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In offline fixed-lattice replay on 132 matched gearbox trials, the authors report INSPECT mean human-rated view utility of 1.205 and Verifiable@1 of 0.417, compared with 1.045 and 0.348 for Current View. Two annotators graded completed selections; this does not establish a live robot effect.
  • Claimed — On 1,068 IMPACT trials using a frozen state head and four static camera views, the authors report a correct-decision rate of 0.543 for INSPECT's relative-view selector versus 0.506 for Current View. Inspection was triggered on abstention and occurred on 7.7% of trials; the 82 inspections yielded 40 correct commitments, 29 incorrect commitments and 13 continued abstentions.
  • Demonstrated — The preprint describes a method that learns claim- and evidence-role-conditioned relative view preferences from egocentric assistant records, filters observation changes a robot camera can reproduce, calibrates preferences to robot pose, and screens candidate views against the evidence needed for the complete claim. The method description does not establish generalization beyond the paper's evaluated settings.

Strongest baseline

On 132 matched gearbox trials, Current View scored 1.045 mean utility and 0.348 Verifiable@1 versus INSPECT at 1.205 and 0.417. On 1,068 IMPACT trials, Current View had a 0.506 correct-decision rate versus 0.543 for the relative-view selector. Results are paper-reported and not independently reproduced.

Limitations

  • The metrics are reported by the authors of a single arXiv preprint; no independent reproduction was inspected.
  • Gearbox evaluation is offline replay with 132 matched trials from 22 setups assigned steps 2–4, six start views per setup; two annotators graded informativeness. The oracle reached 100% Verifiable@1, so 41.7% remains well below that evaluated upper bound.
  • The IMPACT result uses a frozen state head, four static camera views and abstention-triggered movement on 7.7% of 1,068 trials; 29 of 82 triggered inspections produced incorrect commitments.
  • The paper states that improved human-rated verifiability on gearbox replay did not yield additional correct commitments after view changes. Its view utility and correct-decision metrics answer different questions.
  • Code is linked by the authors, but code availability is not a reproduction or evidence of practical deployment.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the 132-trial gearbox replay or the reported IMPACT transfer result was inspected.
  • The experiments use offline one-step replay and constrained assembly settings, not repeated live closed-loop inspection with physical camera motion, execution disturbances and deployment-level time or safety costs. The gearbox evaluation covers 22 of 60 setups; in IMPACT, only 7.7% of trials triggered a move and those moves included 29 incorrect commitments among 82 inspections.
  • On gearbox replay, higher human-rated verifiability did not produce additional correct commitments after view changes. The distinct effects of some components remain unresolved: removing requirement weighting left both headline metrics unchanged, while smaller motion-supervision budgets were non-monotonic.

Why it matters

Industrial inspection depends on seeing evidence that distinguishes correct part identity and installation state. INSPECT reports a way to learn claim-relevant robot viewpoints from assistant-use records, with gains over current-view replay baselines in two settings. The results remain paper-reported, offline and not independently reproduced.
