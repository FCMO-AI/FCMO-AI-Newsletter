A paper models GPU energy per output token from active bytes and decode time

A new arXiv paper proposes a locally calibrated model for GPU-board energy per useful output token during dense, full-history decoding. It combines shared-weight bytes, per-request KV-history bytes and observed decode time. The authors report 374 held-out energy executions in total: 270 static workload runs across 54 conditions (maximum condition-mean error 3.73%) and 104 controlled request-departure runs (all condition-mean errors below 1%). A separate 177 HBM profiles examine whether the logical byte coordinates track physical reads. Results cover three BF16 models and specified GH200/GB300 configurations; they do not establish independent reproduction, portable coefficients, facility energy or monetary savings.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors propose a GPU-board energy-per-output-token model for dense, full-history decoding. It combines logical dense-weight bytes amortized across the realized batch, per-request KV-history bytes, and observed decode time; fitted coefficients are specific to each model and hardware/runtime configuration.
  • Claimed — In the static-workload validation, the authors report 270 held-out energy executions across 54 cohort-specific conditions, with all condition-mean predictions within 3.73% of measured GPU-board energy. This is a subtotal, not the paper’s total validation count.
  • Claimed — Across six single-GPU model/platform pairs in the paper’s tested range, the largest tested batch has 33.2–46.8% lower measured mean GPU-board energy per useful output than the smallest tested batch at matched model and history; measured mean energy rises with longer tested histories at fixed batch.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper studies dense full-history BF16 decode on three models and NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell GPUs. Its coefficients are calibrated per model/platform/runtime, energy is measured at the GPU-board boundary, and the paper does not establish facility energy or monetary cost.
  • Not established — No independent reproduction, portable coefficient set, facility-level energy result, or general serving-cost reduction is established by this paper-level review.
  • Claimed — The authors report 104 additional GH200 request-departure energy executions across Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B, with every condition-mean error below 1%. Together with the 270 static executions, these make 374 held-out energy executions. A separate 177 HBM profiles assess correspondence between the logical read coordinates and measured HBM traffic; they are not energy trials.

Strongest baseline

Within each listed model/platform, the paper compares measured board energy across batch and history settings and validates model predictions on held-out conditions. These within-study results do not compare providers, model quality, facility energy, or total serving costs.

Limitations

  • The paper’s results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The model predicts GPU-board energy for dense full-history BF16 decoding; it does not measure facility energy, prefill, or end-to-end monetary costs.
  • Energy-model coefficients are calibrated per model and hardware/runtime configuration and must not be treated as universal or portable without validation.
  • The evaluation includes three models on GH200 and GB300 plus a two-GH200 tensor-parallel configuration; broader architectures, workloads and hardware are untested.
  • Reported held-out error compares condition means in the authors’ design; it does not establish out-of-domain predictive accuracy or production savings.
  • No author-linked reproducibility artifact or independent reproduction was located in this bounded review; search absence does not prove no artifact exists.
  • The paper’s 374 held-out energy executions comprise 270 static-workload runs and 104 controlled request-departure runs; the separate 177 HBM profiles measure memory traffic. These are author-reported studies, not independent replications.
  • The energy model uses observed decode time. Prospective energy estimates also require a separately validated duration model.

What we still don't know

  • The reported error bound is author-reported across 270 static workload executions and 104 controlled request-departure executions; none has been independently reproduced. The separate 177 HBM profiles do not reproduce the energy model.
  • Measured boundary is GPU-board energy, and the model covers dense full-history BF16 decode rather than prefill, facility overhead, or every serving architecture.
  • No author-linked measurement scripts or raw energy traces surfaced in the bounded artifact search.

Why it matters

Batch size and context length jointly shape inference energy, but token counts alone do not describe the work or time spent generating each output. If independently reproduced within real serving regimes, this model could make hardware and batching efficiency comparisons more informative. Its present evidence is a configuration-specific, author-run GPU-board study, not a general energy or cost forecast.
