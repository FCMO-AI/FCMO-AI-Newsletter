Repeated materials campaigns report a shared frontier and a common-mode structure error

A preprint compares 16 one-week autonomous materials-discovery campaigns given the same 12,499-entry metal-organic-framework database, methane-storage objective and pinned protocol. The authors report divergent search strategies but convergence near 200 cm³/cm³, with an independent calculation placing its nine best structures among agent reports. However, 15 of 16 campaigns also selected an audit-excluded incomplete structure whose missing anions created artificial pore volume. Enforced checks improved fresh-run reproduction from 1/8 to 8/8 in the tested subset but did not detectably improve conclusion validity.

Strongest baseline

Limitations

  • The preprint reports author-run results; the research desk did not reproduce the campaigns or validate structures.
  • All campaigns share one model-harness configuration, frozen database, objective and protocol, so the results do not establish cross-model robustness.
  • The abstract reports convergence in a database search regime, not synthesized materials or measured methane-storage performance.

What we still don't know

  • The campaign results and structural audit have not been independently reproduced.

Why it matters

Repeated runs can reveal both stable signals and errors inherited from shared data. This bounded study shows why replication across agents does not by itself establish that a candidate is physically valid when all runs use the same database and protocol.
