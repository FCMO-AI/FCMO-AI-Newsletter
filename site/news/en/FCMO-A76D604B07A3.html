Tencent Hunyuan releases open Hy-SimulMT for Chinese–English real-time simultaneous speech translation

Tencent Hunyuan's official repository dates Hy-SimulMT's release to Oct 26, 2026 and links six model variants with inference and training tools for bidirectional real-time Chinese-English speech translation. The Simplified-Chinese README reports about 3 seconds StreamLAAL on the long-form RealSI comparison and favorable benchmark results, including a small comparison against professional interpreters. These outcome claims are first-party and unverified; StreamLAAL is not time to first speech output, and the human comparison uses selected high scores from six interpreters on 18 recordings. Tencent states that its publicly available inference-enabling code, parameters and weights are under Apache-2.0, while listed third-party components retain their own terms. The base variant’s model card lists 30B BF16 parameters and 60.9 GB across 17 shards; Tencent’s serving guide requires an NVIDIA GPU with enough memory. Peak VRAM, throughput and realized inference cost are unmeasured. The documented deployment also requires a configured TTS service for speech output and voice cloning.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Tencent Hunyuan's official repository dates the Hy-SimulMT release to 2026-10-26, describes bidirectional Chinese-English streaming speech translation, links six model variants, and provides SimulInfer inference and SimulTrain training tools.
  • Claimed — Tencent reports approximately 3 seconds StreamLAAL on the long-form RealSI comparison, the lowest among systems shown, and a GEMBA score above the compared open-source models and four of five commercial APIs.
  • Claimed — Tencent reports a comparison on 18 online audio recordings appearing from September 2026 onward, each about six minutes long, against six professional interpreters; the repository says the comparison uses the top score among three interpreters at each certification level and limits conclusions to these samples and evaluators.
  • Not established — The repository does not establish independent replication, performance across broader language pairs or deployment conditions, or real-world speech quality and voice similarity; speech output and voice cloning require a configured TTS service in the documented setup.
  • Demonstrated — Tencent states that the inference-enabling code, parameters and weights it makes publicly available are licensed under Apache-2.0, while listed third-party components retain their original licenses; the license file lists Qwen3-ASR-0.6B under Apache-2.0. The linked Hugging Face Interleave card also labels its model Apache-2.0.
  • Demonstrated — The publisher-owned Hugging Face card identifies the base Hy-SimulMT-30B-A3B variant as 30B parameters in BF16 and its file listing totals 60.9 GB across 17 model shards. Tencent’s deployment guide requires an NVIDIA GPU with enough memory and documents vLLM BF16 serving; no peak VRAM, throughput, energy or realized inference cost is established.

Strongest baseline

Tencent's README compares RealSI results with listed open-source systems and five commercial APIs and describes a same-recording comparison with six interpreters; evaluation details and independent reruns were not inspected.

Limitations

  • Benchmark and human-comparison outcomes are reported by Tencent Hunyuan and were not independently reproduced.
  • The approximately 3-second StreamLAAL result is not time to first speech output and does not by itself measure overall conversational quality.
  • The human-comparison set contains 18 recordings and six interpreters; choosing the highest score among three interpreters at each certification level does not establish broad parity with professional interpreters.
  • No independent comparison across other language pairs, accents, noisy conditions, compute budgets, hardware configurations or service loads was performed.
  • The documented deployment says speech output and voice cloning need a configured TTS service; the repository says llama.cpp support for this model had not yet merged upstream at the time of the inspected README.
  • The inspected Tencent license scopes Apache-2.0 to the publicly available inference-enabling code, parameters and weights and preserves original terms for listed third-party components; not every variant, payload or dependency notice was independently audited.
  • The 60.9 GB listing applies to the base BF16 variant; it is not a measured peak-memory requirement or a cost estimate, and requirements for all quantized/interleaved variants were not compared.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported RealSI, short-form or human-interpreter comparisons was performed, and raw benchmark outputs were not audited.
  • The README's human comparison is limited to 18 recordings and six interpreters with highest-score selection within certification levels; deployment performance and voice similarity across representative conditions remain unmeasured.
  • The model files and dependency notices were not downloaded or exhaustively audited; the license scope was read from Tencent’s repository and the Interleave model-card label.
  • The base model listing exposes a 60.9 GB BF16 artifact, but peak VRAM, concurrency, throughput, latency, energy and per-task cost under serving conditions are unknown.

Why it matters

An openly released streaming speech-translation system with linked model variants and tooling may broaden who can inspect, evaluate and adapt low-latency Chinese-English interpretation. The reported latency and quality do not establish independent performance or suitability outside the source's benchmarks and limited human-comparison sample. The base BF16 artifact is substantial at 60.9 GB, while actual serving memory and operating cost remain unmeasured.
