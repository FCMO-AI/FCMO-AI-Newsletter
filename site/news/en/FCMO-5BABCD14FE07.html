DiscoveryRSI paper reports iterative gains in evidence-bounded hypothesis development

The authors introduce DiscoveryRSI, which develops linked research hypotheses and investigation plans, then learns from scored research histories through model training, workflow replay and new investigations. In four life-science domains, they report automated hypothesis-quality scores rising from 4.86 for the base model to 5.39 after two rounds. A blinded human review of 292 proposals by 27 biomedical or clinical research participants reports improved revision scores, while the paper’s literature-correspondence comparison is descriptive and sensitive to retrieval coverage. Results are author-reported; no independent reproduction or scientific outcome is established.

What changed

The authors introduce DiscoveryRSI, which develops linked research hypotheses and investigation plans, then learns from scored research histories through model training, workflow replay and new investigations. In four life-science domains, they report automated hypothesis-quality scores rising from 4.86 for the base model to 5.39 after two rounds. Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors describe DiscoveryRSI as a multi-agent research workflow that preserves linked hypothesis revisions and learns from assessed research histories through hypothesis-credit training, harness replay and rediscovery.
  • Claimed — Across four life-science domains, the paper reports automated mean hypothesis-quality scores of 4.86 for the base model, 5.18 after one round and 5.39 after two rounds; falsifiability, alternative-explanation, data-resolvability and feasibility scores rise, while importance and novelty decline.
  • Claimed — A blinded assessment of 292 proposals by 27 biomedical or clinical research participants reports that 41% of DiscoveryRSI revisions improved mean human ratings, compared with 31% for GPT-5.6 Sol and 25% for GPT-6 Luna; mean changes were +0.07, +0.03 and −0.01.
  • Claimed — The paper reports 74 of 1,176 DiscoveryRSI hypothesis versions (6.3%) with a qualifying correspondence to post-cutoff research, versus 5.4% for DeepSeek, 2.8% for GLM, 2.5% for GPT and 2.3% for Claude; the authors characterize these as descriptive comparisons with unequal group sizes, cutoffs, retrieval coverage and dependent versions.
  • Demonstrated — The linked public code repository documents a research harness and tests, and the Hugging Face task-example dataset exposes three illustrative tasks with synthetic fixtures. The inspected DiscoveryRSI-27B main-branch listing contained a 9 kB README only; no loadable checkpoint appeared in that listing.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent replication, that generated hypotheses led to completed discoveries or clinical benefit, prospective researcher adoption, or a causal advantage from the model separate from the workflow and evaluation choices.
  • Claimed — PhAI Labs' report page, dated Oct 19, says its technical report, code, models and dataset are public; the separately inspected Hugging Face model repository listing showed no checkpoint files, so downloadable weight availability was not established.

Strongest baseline

Same-system base model for the round-to-round automated score; GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna for the reported human revision comparison. The paper also compares literature-correspondence rates, but retrieval, sample composition and time cutoffs differ across model groups.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are reported by the paper authors; no independent code execution, checkpoint inference or reproduction was performed.
  • The automated quality evaluations use GPT-5.6 Sol as judge. The blinded human panel contains 27 participants with mixed biomedical/clinical training and reviews 292 proposals; this is not a clinical outcome study.
  • Automated quality gains concern investigation design more than perceived importance or novelty; importance falls 6.48 → 6.04 and novelty 4.87 → 4.58 in the reported comparison.
  • The literature-correspondence rates use unequal groups, model-specific time cutoffs and a lexical retrieval gate. Only 15/131 Claude proposals passed its retrieval gate versus 355/1,176 DiscoveryRSI proposals; among retrieved proposals, positive fractions were similar. Versions and reused judgments are dependent, so rates are descriptive and not significance-tested.
  • The inspected model repository lists no checkpoint files, exact base-model identity, training-data manifest or complete evaluation configuration. The three public task examples and synthetic fixture checks are not the complete benchmark or the original evaluation inputs.
  • No prospective researcher adoption, completed experiments, scientific discoveries or clinical benefit is established.
  • PhAI Labs' Oct 19 report page says code, models and dataset are public, but the inspected Hugging Face model repository listing contains no checkpoint files; the public model page does not establish downloadable weights.

What we still don't know

  • The reported round-to-round hypothesis-quality gains and human revision comparisons have not been independently reproduced.
  • The inspected model repository does not expose checkpoint files or the base-model and training-data metadata needed to reproduce the published model.
  • The literature-correspondence comparison is sensitive to retrieval coverage and uses different cutoff dates, sample composition and reused automated judgments; it does not show researcher adoption or causal influence.
  • No evidence shows that improved hypothesis quality led to completed experiments, validated discoveries or clinical outcomes.

Why it matters

A workflow that learns from the quality of prior research plans could help scientific agents improve experiment design and evidence discipline over repeated investigations. The paper reports gains in falsifiability, alternative explanations, data resolvability and feasibility, but not in question importance or novelty; actual discovery, adoption and clinical benefit remain unmeasured.
