NSA, FBI and CISA issue joint advisory alleging industrial-scale frontier-model distillation by six China-based AI firms

On 2026-09-08, NSA, FBI and CISA jointly issued an 18-page TLP:CLEAR cybersecurity advisory asserting that DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI extracted billions of tokens across millions of requests/exchanges from U.S. frontier models since at least late 2024. The agencies describe distributed access through native APIs, cloud providers, third-party aggregators and gray-market proxy 'transfer stations', plus bulk premium subscriptions, automated failover and quality-evaluation loops. This is unusually detailed government attribution and operational guidance, but it is not a public causal measurement of how much capability each alleged campaign transferred or how much of any named Chinese model's performance was caused by distillation.

What actually changed

On 2026-09-08, NSA, FBI and CISA jointly issued an 18-page TLP:CLEAR cybersecurity advisory asserting that DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI extracted billions of tokens across millions of requests/exchanges from U.S. frontier models since at least late 2024. The agencies describe distributed access through native APIs, cloud providers, third-party aggregators and gray-market proxy 'transfer stations', plus bulk premium subscriptions, automated failover and quality-evaluation loops. This is unusually detailed government attribution and operational guidance, but it is not a public causal measurement of how much capability each alleged campaign transferred or how much of any named Chinese model's performance was caused by distillation.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — NSA, FBI and CISA jointly issued an 18-page 2026-09-08 advisory alleging industrial-scale distillation by DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI against U.S. frontier-model providers.
  • GOVERNMENT_CLAIM — The advisory states that the named firms collectively extracted billions of tokens across millions of requests/exchanges since at least late 2024 and distributed traffic across APIs, clouds, aggregators and proxy transfer stations to reduce traceability.
  • GOVERNMENT_CLAIM — The advisory attributes specific teacher-model use and capability targets to named Chinese firms, including reasoning, coding, SFT/RL, agentic and domain-specific functions; the underlying attribution evidence is not fully public.
  • INFERRED — If effective at the asserted scale, adversarial distillation can partially substitute model-output access for some otherwise expensive data generation, experimentation and training effort, weakening a compute-only view of frontier capability control.
  • DEMONSTRATED_LIMITATION — Public sources inspected here do not establish the marginal capability gain per teacher-query dollar, the exact share of any named model's performance caused by the alleged distillation, or that API-derived data can substitute for frontier pretraining wholesale.
  • COUNTERPOSITION — A July 2026 Chinese Ministry of Commerce response rejected U.S. accusations and sanctions threats as lacking factual/legal grounds, emphasized indigenous/basic research and open cooperation, and noted that U.S. firms also use Chinese models for legitimate distillation. This is a government counter-position, not a technical falsification of the Sep. 8 attribution.
  • DEMONSTRATED_RESEARCH_BASELINE — Independent research suggests individual extraction queries can resemble benign use while aggregate query distributions become more detectable; one 2026 benchmark reports MMD traffic-window testing at 0.3% benign FPR, 100% pure-attacker TPR and 90.5% average TPR across attacker fractions, but real industrial campaigns may adapt around such detectors.
  • DEMONSTRATED_RESEARCH_BASELINE — Independent anti-distillation research finds many output-only defenses weak or task-specific: DistillGuard reports paraphrasing barely harms student quality and poisoning mainly degrades conversational fluency, while removing chain-of-thought sharply reduces math transfer but not code transfer in its tested same-family setup.
  • DEMONSTRATED_COUNTEREVIDENCE — Watermark radioactivity may help detect training on protected outputs, but watermark inheritance is not unique proof of authorship or uncompromised attribution: peer-reviewed DITTO shows a distilled model can spoof a victim model's watermark signal.

Strongest baseline

Prior U.S. policy and provider allegations established the broad concern; the Sep. 8 advisory adds named firms, alleged teacher/student model relationships, access-path TTPs and concrete mitigations. Independent research provides a useful defense baseline: traffic-window distribution testing can detect extraction-like query distributions at low reported benign FPR in synthetic/benchmark settings, whereas several output-only anti-distillation defenses remain weak or task-specific.

The caveat that matters

  • The underlying intelligence, provider telemetry and full attribution evidence supporting the six-company claims are not public in the inspected sources.
  • The advisory gives counts at the broad 'billions of tokens / millions of requests' level but not a firm-by-firm traffic ledger, exact acquisition cost or independently reproducible training-data lineage.
  • The advisory does not provide a matched causal estimate of capability gain attributable to distillation versus each target lab's own pretraining data, architecture, training, reinforcement learning, tools or compute.
  • Distillation is a standard legitimate ML technique; the security issue here is alleged unauthorized access, terms circumvention and distributed capability extraction, not distillation as a method in itself.
  • The U.S. and Chinese government sources contain geopolitical and policy judgments; neither should be treated as an independent ML reproduction.
  • No public evidence recovered here establishes that model-output access can replace frontier pretraining wholesale; transfer is likely capability-, data- and student-regime-dependent.
  • Published extraction detectors and anti-distillation defenses are benchmark results; an adaptive industrial actor can alter query mixtures, accounts, providers and downstream training strategy.
  • Watermark/fingerprint evidence can support provenance investigations but should not be treated as sole-source attribution because removal, suppression and spoofing attacks exist.
  • China's Ministry of Commerce publicly rejects the U.S. allegations/sanctions framing, says Chinese companies conduct indigenous basic research and open cooperation, and points out legitimate reciprocal use of Chinese models for distillation by U.S. firms.
  • Public open-source evidence does not quantify how much of observed Chinese model progress is causally explained by alleged U.S.-model distillation rather than indigenous research, open research transfer, architecture improvements, other synthetic data or domestic compute.
  • The White House's own April policy memorandum says distilled models do not replicate the full performance of originals, narrowing any inference that distillation alone transfers an entire frontier model.
  • Independent defense research shows both sides of the attribution problem: traffic-level extraction can be detectable, but output perturbation/watermark/fingerprint strategies have meaningful bypasses or utility costs.

What remains unknown

  • No public matched experiment quantifies capability transferred per teacher-query dollar across frontier model families.
  • Underlying multi-agency/provider attribution telemetry and full campaign-level evidence are not public.
  • No standardized benchmark combines distributed-account evasion, realistic benign high-volume use and downstream capability-transfer measurement.
  • Current watermark/fingerprint methods do not yet provide a universally robust, unforgeable lineage proof under adaptive distillation.

FCMO Lens

If the central attribution and effectiveness claims are substantially correct, frontier-model access becomes part of the geopolitical compute boundary: concentrated teacher inference can transfer some valuable behavior even when advanced-chip access is constrained. The advisory also moves anti-distillation defense from per-account throttling toward cross-provider traffic intelligence, behavioral distribution monitoring and provenance/attribution. Its strongest claim—that distillation is the 'critical core' of the named companies' development—is much less independently demonstrated than the existence of large-scale suspicious traffic and should remain labeled as a government attribution rather than repository fact.
