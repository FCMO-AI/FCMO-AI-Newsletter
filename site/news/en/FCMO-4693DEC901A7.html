A targeted SFT and RL routing study reports gains on a bounded set of enterprise analytics skills

A preprint studies how to allocate supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) for long-horizon analytics agents using production-mirroring beta APIs. It separates retrospectively observed trajectories into imitation, lift and discovery regimes, then uses teacher support and reward-observable headroom to route features to SFT, RL or further environment work. Across 18 subsequent feature-specific experiments, the diagnostic reportedly predicted 15 trajectories. On GPT-OSS 120B, targeted SFT-then-RL produced positive point estimates on seven of eight advertiser skills versus a frontier control; five gains had paired 95% confidence intervals excluding zero and one skill had a supported regression. A matched comparison reports a higher seven-skill mean delta with 43% less incremental RL compute than uniform routing.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In the authors’ experiments, the routing diagnostic predicted 15/18 feature trajectories; targeted SFT-then-RL on GPT-OSS 120B had positive point estimates on 7/8 advertiser skills, with five paired 95% intervals excluding zero, and the matched targeted-versus-uniform comparison reported a seven-skill mean delta of +3.57 versus +1.62 using 43% less incremental RL compute.
  • Not established — The paper’s author-reported results do not establish independent reproduction or broader deployment impact.

Strongest baseline

Frontier control for the eight advertiser skills; uniform SFT/RL allocation with shared rewards and optimization for the matched compute comparison.

Limitations

  • The experiments concern advertiser skills in one enterprise analytics environment and do not establish transfer to other agent tasks.
  • Results are author-reported; the research desk did not reproduce the API environment or training runs.
  • The paper reports a supported regression on one of eight skills; averages do not imply uniform benefit.

What we still don't know

  • The feature-routing results and reported compute comparison have not been independently reproduced outside the authors’ analytics environment.

Why it matters

The paper offers a potentially useful way to decide where RL is worth its extra compute in tool-using workflows. The evidence comes from one enterprise analytics setting and author-run experiments, so it does not establish a general training recipe or field-wide efficiency gain.
