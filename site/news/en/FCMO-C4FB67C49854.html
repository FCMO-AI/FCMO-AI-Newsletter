Google discloses opt-in Workspace data use for AI personalization and training

In an October 16, 2026 update to an older AI safeguards post, Google says users can choose to connect Workspace apps to other Google services, and that data processed through these features may personalize other Google experiences and, depending on settings, be used to train generative-AI models or reviewed by people in limited circumstances. Limitations

  • This record covers a dated public disclosure about new user-enabled connections; it does not say that all Workspace content is used for model training or review.
  • The Help Center describes Search AI Mode and Ask Maps; applicability to other products, account types and regions is not established here.
  • Google's claims about data use and privacy safeguards have not been independently audited in this review.
  • The Help Center warns that settings are being updated and may not match each user's current experience.

What we still don't know

  • Google's Help Center says the settings are being updated and may not match every user's current experience; rollout, eligibility and settings may vary by service, country and account.

Why it matters

If implemented as described, connecting private productivity content to AI search and assistant features creates a consequential, opt-in data boundary spanning personalization, model improvement and limited human review. The scope depends on user choices and account settings, so this does not establish that all Workspace content is used for training or review.
