What changed

An Oct 26 preprint introduces SmoothScore, an LLM-assisted method for checking whether research-report claims preserve the scope and strength of their cited evidence. On ResearcherBench, the authors report that 27.4–93.4% of claims from four selected systems score at least 3 on its 1–5 smoothing scale, with higher rates for some other systems and datasets. In a 73-claim human check, exact agreement between the judge and one annotator was 56%. A 61-question experiment reports less smoothing when tested systems receive cited documents plus instructions than with WebSearch alone. These are author-reported results from selected benchmarks; the judge is not established as ground truth, and the linked code/data repository currently has no implementation or dataset available.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The preprint introduces SmoothScore, a claim-level pipeline that extracts report conclusions, gathers supporting and qualifying evidence from cited documents, groups evidence by aspect, and asks an LLM judge to score whether claim scope and strength match the evidence. The authors report applying it to seven research systems across four selected datasets.
  • Claimed — On ResearcherBench, the paper reports that 27.4% of Sol 5.6 claims, 28.9% of Luna 5.6 claims, 59.5% of Sonnet 5 claims and 93.4% of Gemini Deep Research claims received SmoothScore >=3. Gemini's mean was 3.27, compared with 1.93 for Sol and 1.95 for Luna.
  • Claimed — For 73 claims rated by annotators, the paper reports 56% exact agreement and 93% agreement within one score between the SmoothScore judge and one annotator; two annotators agreed exactly on 58% and within one score on 96%.
  • Claimed — In the paper's regeneration study, providing cited documents together with instructions reduced reported smoothing relative to WebSearch alone for tested Luna and Sonnet configurations; documents or instructions in isolation were less effective.
  • Demonstrated — The linked SmoothScore GitHub repository currently contains a README saying that the pipeline and data are forthcoming; no implementation or dataset was available there for this activation.
  • Not established — The inspected evidence does not establish that the reported score distribution is a general rate of research-agent error, that the scoring pipeline is independently reliable across domains, or that the document-reading intervention improves real-world report quality beyond the tested benchmark and configurations.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper's ResearcherBench table, SmoothScore >=3 rates vary by system: 27.4% for Sol 5.6, 28.9% for Luna 5.6, 59.5% for Sonnet 5 and 93.4% for Gemini Deep Research. These judge-derived measurements are not directly comparable to external ground-truth error rates.

Limitations

  • SmoothScore relies on an LLM-mediated extraction, aspect grouping and scoring pipeline; exact judge-to-annotator agreement was 56% on the reported human-validation sample.
  • The paper uses selected queries from four research-report datasets; the inspected evidence does not establish representative coverage of all research-agent workloads or domains.
  • All measured score distributions and intervention effects are reported by the authors and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The linked repository README says the pipeline and data are forthcoming, so the implementation, prompts, dataset and detailed outputs could not be inspected.

What we still don't know

  • The linked code repository exposes no implementation or dataset, preventing inspection of the prompts, data, scoring pipeline and report-level outputs.
  • Human validation covers 73 claims and exact agreement between the judge and one annotator is 56%; domain breadth, annotation protocol and score reliability need stronger independent assessment.
  • The mitigation experiment is limited to 61 ResearcherBench questions and tested Luna/Sonnet configurations; it does not establish transfer to other tasks or production systems.

Why it matters

When research systems compress many sources into a confident report, readers need to know whether the conclusion preserves the cited evidence's limits and disagreements. The preprint offers a way to measure that failure mode and reports a document-reading intervention, but its LLM judge has only a small human check and the code/data are not yet available. The findings are a reason to test claim-level evidence fidelity, not a general estimate of research-agent error.
