What changed

The Bank of England and Financial Conduct Authority’s 2026 survey of 196 responding firms reports that 86% currently use AI and 80% have AI agents deployed. Among reported agent deployments, decision-support use is common while broad autonomy is rare: 80% of respondents report decision-support agents and 2% report agents with broad autonomy. Third-party systems account for 39% of reported AI systems and 53% of those classed as high materiality. These are self-reported survey results, not a census or measured productivity and safety outcomes.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In the 2026 joint Bank of England/FCA survey, 86% of respondents reported currently using AI; 196 firms responded across the full and short-form questionnaires.
  • Claimed — The report says 80% of respondents reported current AI-agent deployment; for agent autonomy, 80% reported decision-support agents and 2% reported broad-autonomy agents.
  • Claimed — Third-party implementations accounted for 39% of reported AI systems and 53% of reported high-materiality AI systems in the survey results.
  • Not established — The survey does not establish causal productivity or revenue gains, realized safety or consumer outcomes, sector-wide adoption beyond respondents, or independent audit of the reported deployments.
  • Claimed — The 2024 Bank/FCA survey had 118 responses and reported 75% of respondent firms using AI; the 2026 survey reports 86% among 196 respondents. The 2026 report cautions that respondent composition and question scope differ, so this is not a clean like-for-like or causal trend.

Strongest baseline

The Bank/FCA 2024 report had 118 responses and reported 75% current AI use; the 2026 report reports 86% among 196 respondents. The 2026 report warns that respondent composition and question scope differ, so the change is descriptive context, not a like-for-like panel estimate or causal trend.

Limitations

  • The 196 respondents are not a census of the whole UK financial sector; response rates and representativeness are not established by this report.
  • Question-level denominators vary. The 105 full-questionnaire completions and the full/short-form response design must be kept distinct; do not infer unreported denominators.
  • Comparison with 2024 is qualified because respondent composition and questionnaire scope changed; some technology categories are not directly comparable.
  • Reported adoption and governance measures are self-reported aggregate data, not independent verification of deployments, controls, or outcomes.
  • The 80% agent deployment figure does not mean broad autonomy: reported levels overlap by respondents and only 2% reported broad-autonomy agents.
  • The survey measures reported use and perceptions, not causal productivity, safety, revenue, member or customer effects.

What we still don't know

  • The report aggregates 196 responding firms, but the evidence does not establish representativeness of the full financial-services sector or permit firm-level verification.
  • The survey reports adoption, perceived benefits/risks and governance measures but does not estimate causal productivity, financial returns, incidents, safety or consumer outcomes.
  • The report warns that respondent composition and question scope differ from 2024, limiting some trend comparisons.

Why it matters

This is a regulator-reported snapshot of AI’s operating footprint in a major financial sector. It distinguishes widespread AI and agent use from the still-limited reported use of broad autonomy, and quantifies third-party dependency and governance context. The results can inform deployment and oversight questions, but do not establish causal benefit, realized harm, or adoption across the whole UK sector.
