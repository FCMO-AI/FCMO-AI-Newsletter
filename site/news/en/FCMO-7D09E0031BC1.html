Continual AutoResearch finds persistent state speeds early research, but does not reliably improve final results

A 3 November 2026 preprint introduces Continual AutoResearch, a matched benchmark for comparing persistent and reset AI agents across nine machine-learning task families. The authors report that persistent state improved fixed-budget search efficiency for all five evaluated CLI agents, mainly by producing a valid solution sooner or a stronger first solution. Final held-out performance was much less consistent: only Sonnet 4.6 showed a substantial aggregate outcome gain, and most agents lost their efficiency advantage after controls for first-solution latency and candidate count. An explicit memory ablation and trace cases show that relevant prior insights can improve initialization, while overly specific prior state can anchor search and yield worse final selections. These are author-reported results from short task streams; the paper says its code will be released with the camera-ready version.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors introduce Continual AutoResearch, which compares persistent and reset versions of the same CLI agent over matched ordered ML task streams, using hidden held-out evaluation to measure first-solution quality, search efficiency and final outcomes.
  • Claimed — In the reported experiments, persistent state improves mean fixed-budget time-convergence for all five evaluated agents, while the magnitude and duration of the advantage vary by model, task family and controls.
  • Claimed — The authors report that final held-out performance gains are inconsistent and that relevant memory or persistent state can alter initialization without reliably improving final performance; selected trace cases show anchoring and poor final selection.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish that the measured effects generalize to longer research streams, deployed agents, tasks outside its ML benchmark, or multi-agent research teams; the research desk has not independently reproduced the results.

Strongest baseline

Within-agent paired comparison against a reset instance using the same model, task stream, data, wall-clock budget and evaluation procedure. Hidden test scores are computed after runs and are not exposed during search.

Limitations

  • The streams contain only three to five tasks and generally use 30-minute budgets; the paper itself calls for longer streams and longer research tasks.
  • Only GPT-5.6-Sol has three repeated stateful-reset pairs. Other model comparisons use a single matched run pair, limiting uncertainty estimates for stochastic agents.
  • Each model uses its native CLI scaffold. Within-agent comparisons are matched, but differences between agents may reflect both model and scaffold.
  • Persistent state bundles session history, code, results and execution experience; the study does not fully separate these components. The explicit insight-card ablation is limited to one forecasting stream.
  • Selected trace case studies illustrate mechanisms but are not a systematic or causal decomposition of aggregate results.
  • The authors state that benchmark code will be released with the camera-ready version; no code artifact or independent reproduction was inspected.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the matched persistent-versus-reset results was performed, and the authors have not yet released the promised camera-ready code.
  • Results cover short streams of three to five tasks and do not establish transfer across long-duration research programs, deployed workflows or multi-agent teams.

Why it matters

Long-running research agents can carry code, hypotheses and procedures from one task to the next. This paper separates earlier candidate discovery from final solution quality and reports both gains and failures under matched persistent-versus-reset comparisons. If the results hold, they argue that agent systems should measure search efficiency, final selection and transfer safety separately, and should verify prior assumptions before reusing them. The study does not establish the effect in deployed systems or long-horizon research programs.
