What changed

A Nov 10 arXiv preprint from Output Labs reports Bonbon, a 1.6B-parameter model trained on about 3.2T paired protein-ligand sequence tokens. The author reports three zero-shot interaction-property capabilities and sharp gains at the largest of six tested model sizes, plus 131 dose-response-confirmed inhibitors among 213 compounds across nine targets. Bonbon ranked compounds generated by a separate method; assays were conducted by an unnamed external CRO. The reported findings are not independently reproduced, each model scale has one training run, and clinical efficacy is not established.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The author introduces Bonbon, a 1.6-billion-parameter molecular-interaction model trained on approximately 3.2 trillion paired protein-ligand sequence tokens. The paper says the training corpus combines proprietary screening data with synthetic interaction data and that the synthetic-data method is described separately.
  • Claimed — The author reports three zero-shot capabilities from frozen representations—mechanism-of-action classification, binding-site localization including orthosteric/allosteric discrimination, and covalent versus non-covalent recognition—without task-specific labels or structural coordinates. In a six-size scaling study, the reported continuous metrics rise sharply at 1.6B parameters; each size used one training run, so seed variability was not measured.
  • Claimed — The paper reports 131 dose-response-confirmed inhibitors among 213 tested compounds across nine targets in seven protein families (61.5%). Bonbon ranked de novo compounds produced by a separate generative method; up to 24 top-ranked compounds per target were synthesized and assayed by an external contract research organization whose identity the paper withholds.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent replication, clinical efficacy, improvement over alternative compound-generation or medicinal-chemistry workflows, or generalization beyond the evaluated targets, assays and reported experimental setup.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares its frozen representations with reported molecular-interaction prediction methods and co-folding/physics-based approaches on selected datasets, and reports five-orders-of-magnitude inference-speed advantages. No independent matched reproduction or end-to-end drug-discovery workflow comparison was inspected; this record therefore does not treat those comparisons as established external performance.

Limitations

  • All capability and assay results are reported in one author-led preprint and were not independently reproduced here.
  • Bonbon ranked compounds from a separate generative method; the paper does not show that Bonbon generated the tested molecules.
  • The training corpus includes proprietary screening and synthetic interaction data; synthetic-data generation is outside this paper's scope.
  • The six-scale experiment has one run per model size and does not measure training-seed variability.
  • The CRO identity is withheld. The assay study reports 213 selected compounds over nine targets and does not establish clinical activity or population-level success rates.
  • The reported hit fraction is not a comparison against alternative compound rankers or complete drug-discovery workflows.

What we still don't know

  • No independent replication of the molecular-interaction evaluations or the prospective assay results was inspected or performed.
  • The training corpus includes proprietary screening data and synthetic interaction data; the paper says synthetic-data generation is covered in a separate technical report. Public model, code, weights and training data were not inspected.
  • Each of the six model sizes was trained once, so variability across training seeds is unknown and the sharp reported threshold could be run-specific.
  • The wet-lab experiment tests model ranking on compounds generated by a separate method and selected from the top-ranked sets; it does not isolate gains over competing ranking or end-to-end medicinal-chemistry workflows.

Why it matters

If replicated, learning useful interaction properties from paired sequences and prioritizing compounds that yield prospective assay hits could shift AI-assisted screening research. The present paper reports one bounded study, with proprietary and synthetic training data, one scaling run per size, and no comparison against competing end-to-end discovery workflows.
