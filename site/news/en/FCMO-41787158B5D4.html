BootLoops manuscript reports 15 new Feynman-integral results among 30 computed integrals

A preliminary author-hosted manuscript presents analytic forms or differential equations with boundary data and standalone evaluation code for 30 multi-loop Feynman integrals across polylogarithmic, elliptic, and K3/Calabi–Yau classes. The authors identify 15 as known results recomputed by the method and 15 as new at the kinematics stated in each entry. The claimed set includes results for exact-top-mass diboson processes and massive three-loop light-by-light integrals, but several are restricted to fixed mass ratios, kinematic slices or paths, and one W-pair result is evaluated on an unphysical line.

  • Claimed — The manuscript says each computed result is accompanied by standalone evaluation code and is checked against independent numerical values at kinematic points not used in fitting or boundary data; it states a threshold of at least 30 digits. This review did not execute the code or reproduce the checks.
  • Claimed — The paper says a large language model operated the BootLoops toolkit for the computations; the guest post identifies the work as Matthew Schwartz’s independent project rather than an Anthropic research result.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares each result with prior published analytic results where known and with auxiliary-mass-flow numerical values at selected independent points; no broad model or collider-prediction benchmark is reported.

Limitations

  • The source is an author-hosted preliminary manuscript, not independently peer reviewed in this review.
  • Novelty is reported by the author and was not independently adjudicated.
  • The stated 30-digit criterion and comparisons are manuscript-reported; no code was run here.
  • Several results have restricted kinematic domains; this does not establish general physical-region coverage.
  • No measured improvement in collider observables, turnaround time, or experimental prediction accuracy is reported.
  • LLM contribution is author-reported and does not establish autonomous scientific discovery.
  • The repository README says Matthew Schwartz maintains BootLoops and it is not officially supported by Anthropic, while its license notice carries an Anthropic, PBC copyright line; these distinct provenance statements should not be collapsed into a claim of Anthropic-led research.

What we still don't know

  • No independent group reproduced the new integral results or checked the paper’s standalone evaluation code during this review.
  • The claim of novelty is the authors’ literature assessment, and several results apply only at a fixed mass ratio, slice, path, or unphysical line.

Why it matters

If the reported expressions and checks hold, adding reusable analytic representations for previously untreated multi-loop integrals can reduce a technical bottleneck in precision collider calculations. The result is a methods and integral-coverage advance; the manuscript does not establish an improvement in measured collider predictions or broad physical impact.
