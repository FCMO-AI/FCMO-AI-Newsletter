What changed

An Oct. 29 arXiv preprint presents Soei, a provenance and verification observability design, alongside a prototype study of 45 real VS Code Copilot agent-mode sessions from one developer. The authors report 102,233 records and 20,577 tool executions. Their audited rule-based classifier yields a bias-corrected estimate that 23.4% of code-editing episodes ended without an independent check after the last edit (95% CI 17.1–35.7); 57.8% of edit-backed to-dos marked complete had no recorded proof between the last edit and completion. The authors also report that their content-free, hash-chained ledger occupied about 20% of the raw transcript size and detected a tamper test. The private corpus is not released. These are author-reported single-user pilot findings, not population estimates or evidence that a classified check establishes correctness.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report analyzing 45 real VS Code Copilot agent-mode sessions from one developer, spanning 23 April to 5 October 2026, with 102,233 transcript records and 20,577 tool executions. The source corpus is private and was not inspected here.
  • Claimed — Using their final audited rule-based classifier, the authors report a bias-corrected estimate that 23.4% of code-editing episodes ended without a recorded independent check after the last edit (95% CI 17.1–35.7). This describes the paper's corpus and classifier, not population prevalence.
  • Claimed — The authors report that 186 of 322 edit-backed to-dos (57.8%) marked complete had no recorded verification between the last code edit and the completion status. Their definition of verification includes execution and UI checks, not only formal tests.
  • Claimed — On the same corpus, the authors report a content-free hash-chained ledger at about 20% of raw transcript size, deterministic replay and detection of a tamper test. No independent implementation or performance reproduction was conducted here.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that the reported rates generalize to other developers, agent products or work domains; that its verification label implies correctness; or that displaying the proposed evidence changes supervisor decisions. The private source corpus is not released.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper, the authors compare four successive classifier versions and report blind audits of 30 episodes per sampled label; the unverified rate shifts from 42.1% in v1 to 25.1% in v4, then to an audit-corrected 23.4% estimate. No independent team baseline was inspected.

Limitations

  • The corpus contains 45 sessions from one developer and one VS Code Copilot agent-mode product; findings do not establish population prevalence.
  • The proof classifier is rule-based. Its final precision audit sampled 30 episodes per label, and the reported estimate is sensitive to the classifier version and definition of verification.
  • The authors define verification as an independent check after the last edit; this does not establish that the change is correct.
  • The session corpus is private and was not released; no independent replication of empirical or prototype results was performed here.

What we still don't know

  • The study uses one developer's VS Code Copilot session corpus. No independent multi-developer replication was found; the authors frame it as a pilot rather than a population estimate.
  • The paper describes prototype scripts and frozen aggregate artifacts, but this review did not run the prototype or reproduce ledger size, throughput, classification or tamper-detection results.
  • A recorded independent check is not equivalent to correctness, and the paper does not measure whether its proposed interface improves supervisory decisions.

Why it matters

If replicated across developers and agent products, evidence linked to artifact versions could help supervisors distinguish an agent's completion claim from a check that actually ran. The study provides a concrete measurement and prototype in one person's workflow, but does not establish prevalence across teams or that the proposed system improves human decisions.
