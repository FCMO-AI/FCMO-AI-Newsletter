What changed

The Office for Budget Responsibility’s October 2026 outlook retains its uncertain central assumption that AI will add 0.2 percentage points a year to UK productivity growth by the medium term. It reports that 30% of businesses said they used AI in September 2026, up from 15% two years earlier, and describes faster recent output-per-worker growth in some AI-associated sectors. The ONS identifies the adoption estimate as a voluntary survey result and official statistic in development, with a 25.7% response rate. The OBR cautions that sector growth was broad-based and short-period measures volatile. These are a forecast and descriptive observations, not evidence that AI caused an economy-wide productivity gain.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The OBR's October 2026 central forecast assumes AI will add 0.2 percentage points per year to productivity growth by the medium term, while explicitly saying the size and timing are highly uncertain; it says its previous underlying trend-productivity judgement was unchanged.
  • Demonstrated — The report says 30% of UK businesses reported using at least one AI technology in the September 2026 Business Insights and Conditions Survey, compared with 15% two years earlier. It also reports annualized output-per-worker growth from 2024Q4 to 2026Q2 of 7.6% in information and communication and 3.7% in professional, scientific and technical services.
  • Demonstrated — The ONS identifies the 30% estimate as the final result from Wave 165 of its voluntary fortnightly Business Insights and Conditions Survey, fielded from 21 September to 4 October 2026. ONS labels these official statistics in development; the wave received 9,933 responses from a sample of 38,592 businesses (25.7%).
  • Not established — The report does not attribute the sector productivity changes to AI or establish that AI has caused an economy-wide productivity gain; it notes that growth accelerated in sectors likely less affected by AI and cautions that short sectoral series can be volatile.

Strongest baseline

The OBR compares survey-reported AI use with September 2024 and sector productivity with prior periods; the 0.2 percentage-point assumption is unchanged from earlier OBR material. ONS marks the BICS estimate as official statistics in development.

Limitations

  • The 0.2 percentage-point contribution is a forecast assumption, not a measured realized AI effect, and is not a new estimate in this October report.
  • Business AI use is self-reported survey evidence; this activation did not inspect the underlying ONS tables or independently assess survey definitions and sampling.
  • Sector output-per-worker changes are descriptive, annualized over a short period, and not causally linked to AI; the report notes broad-based acceleration and volatility.
  • The OBR landing page returned HTTP 403 in this activation; the official GOV.UK HTML publication copy was accessible and used.
  • ONS describes BICS as a voluntary survey and official statistics in development; Wave 165 response was 25.7%, and its industry frame excludes several sectors, including agriculture, public administration, public education and public health. The exact OBR sector table source workbook was not reconciled here.
  • The OBR forecast was published on 28 October 2026; the report page was later updated on 30 October. Event date follows the publication date, not the later page-update date or this activation’s recording time.

What we still don't know

  • The exact ONS/Resolution Foundation source workbook and series underlying the OBR Table 2.2 sector output-per-worker comparison were not downloaded and reconciled in this activation.
  • No causal evidence connecting AI adoption to the reported sector output-per-worker changes is presented in the inspected report.

Why it matters

The outlook shows how an independent fiscal forecaster incorporates expected AI effects into medium-term projections while treating measured productivity as uncertain. Its accompanying adoption and sector data offer useful observability context, but cannot establish AI's causal contribution or realized economic effect.
