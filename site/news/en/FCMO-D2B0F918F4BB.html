OpenAI details Habitat’s multi-tenant storage and global latency design at 500 PB scale

OpenAI’s Oct 23 engineering post describes the storage layer of Habitat, its online platform supporting products used by more than 1 billion people weekly. What changed

OpenAI’s Oct 23 engineering post describes the storage layer of Habitat, its online platform supporting products used by more than 1 billion people weekly. OpenAI reports returning to a co-managed multi-tenant Azure Cosmos DB design using an improved serverless mode with request-unit-based partition splits, and says it observed no provisioning or noisy-neighbor incidents for months after the change. It also reports that nearby replication, in-region caching and a Rust service reduced p50 read latency from roughly 120 ms to 10 ms. The article describes a change-data-capture system processing millions of changes per second. These are provider-reported production results; the post does not independently audit the telemetry or isolate each intervention’s contribution. Microsoft Learn’s public standard serverless documentation describes a different single-region, 5,000-RU/s-per-partition contract; OpenAI characterizes Habitat’s mode as an improved version built for it, and public availability is unclear.

Evidence

  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that Habitat supports products used by more than 1 billion people weekly and serves more than 500 petabytes of data through a globally distributed storage platform.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports adopting an improved Azure Cosmos DB serverless mode that supports request-unit-based partition splits, allowing a return to a shared multi-tenant container; the company says it has seen no provisioning or noisy-neighbor incidents for months after the change.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports p50 Habitat read latency falling from roughly 120 ms to roughly 10 ms after nearby regional replication, in-region caching and a Rust service launch; the article does not separate the contribution of each change.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that its change-data-capture platform processes millions of database changes per second across thousands of containers and millions of container partitions, feeding more than one hundred Kafka topics.
  • Not established — The post does not establish independently audited reliability, cost efficiency, generalization to other workloads, or the separate causal effect of replication, caching and Rust on latency.
  • Demonstrated — Microsoft Learn documents the standard Azure Cosmos DB serverless account as single-region and states a maximum of 5,000 RU/s per physical partition. OpenAI describes a separate improved mode built for Habitat with up to 10,000 RU/s per partition and RU-based splits; public availability or equivalence to the standard account is not established.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI compares the design to its prior shared-container partition-throughput limits and subsequent dedicated-container capacity-management burden. For latency, the post reports roughly 120 ms before and 10 ms after a combined sequence of replication, caching and Rust deployment, without a matched public baseline table.

Limitations

  • The operational scale, incident history and latency figures are reported by OpenAI; the post does not provide independently auditable telemetry.
  • The roughly 12x p50 change is attributed jointly to regional replication, in-region caching and the Rust service; their separate effects are not measured in the post.
  • The post provides no normalized cost comparison or full p50/p95/p99 distribution for the described changes.
  • Habitat is an application online-storage platform; the results do not establish transfer to model training or inference workloads.
  • Microsoft Learn’s public standard serverless documentation, last updated 2026-04-27, describes a single-region account capped at 5,000 RU/s per physical partition and does not support shared-throughput databases. OpenAI says Azure built an improved mode for Habitat; public availability and equivalence to the standard account contract are not established.
  • Microsoft Learn’s public Azure Cosmos DB serverless documentation, last updated Apr 27, 2026, describes standard accounts as single-region, limited to 5,000 RU/s per physical partition and unable to use a shared-throughput database. OpenAI’s Oct 23 post describes an improved Azure-built-for-Habitat mode allowing 10,000 RU/s per partition and request-unit-based splits. This is a scope/availability discrepancy rather than a demonstrated contradiction: the Microsoft pages document the standard service and do not address the reported custom mode. The custom mode’s availability beyond Habitat remains unknown.

What we still don't know

  • OpenAI’s scale, latency and reliability numbers are first-party production claims; independent telemetry or a customer-side corroboration has not been inspected.
  • The reported roughly 120 ms to 10 ms p50 read-latency change combines regional replication, in-region caching and the Rust service; separate causal effects and workload mix are not reported.
  • The account concerns OpenAI’s application storage workload and does not establish that the same design or quantitative outcomes transfer to model serving, training or other online systems.
  • Microsoft Learn’s public standard serverless documentation last updated 2026-04-27 describes single-region accounts with up to 5,000 RU/s per physical partition and no shared-throughput database. OpenAI describes a different improved mode built for Habitat, but its deployment scope, public availability and relationship to the standard account contract are not established.

Why it matters

The post gives a concrete account of how workload isolation, partition behavior, regional placement, caching and change-data capture interact in a large online storage service. Its reported 12x p50 change is operationally meaningful, but it combines multiple interventions and does not establish their separate effects, comparative cost or transfer to other workloads. The public Azure serverless contract documented as of Apr 27, 2026 differs from the Habitat-specific mode described here, so the reported configuration should not be treated as generally available.
