Preprint derives sharp force-space density thresholds for forced Navier–Stokes breakdown

Cao, Chi, and Nie report sharp thresholds for how close smooth forcing can be, in weak Sobolev norms, while producing classical breakdown by a prescribed time. For fixed positive viscosity and zero initial velocity, their preprint claims density exactly below s=1/2 in relative L1_t H^s_x on both T3 and R3; on R3 it gives the corresponding L2_t H^s_x threshold s<-1/2. The construction glues in a compact forced blowup block attributed to OpenAI while changing the external force. These are preprint claims not independently reproduced here, and they do not address the unforced Millennium problem or independently verify the building block.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Cao, Chi, and Nie posted v1 of this preprint on 2026-09-09; the inspected v4 manuscript is dated 2026-09-22 and presents a force-space density theorem for classical breakdown in three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations.
  • Claimed — The authors claim that for fixed positive viscosity and zero initial velocity, smooth external forces producing classical breakdown by T are dense in the relative L1_t H^s_x topology exactly for s<1/2 on both the three-torus and R3. On R3 they also claim the corresponding L2_t H^s_x threshold is exactly s<-1/2. The torus theorem additionally gives density below s=1/2 for each fixed admissible smooth initial velocity.
  • Claimed — The construction inserts a rescaled compact forced blowup solution into a region where a smooth cutoff of a local vector potential makes the surrounding velocity vanish; it adjusts the external force and preserves the initial velocity and earlier history.
  • Inferred — The result concerns force-dependent classical breakdown and weak force-space topologies; it does not resolve the unforced Navier–Stokes regularity problem or independently validate the compact blowup solution used as its building block.

Strongest baseline

No empirical baseline applies. The paper contrasts its classical-breakdown result with earlier force-density results for nonunique Leray–Hopf solutions and derives the stated threshold from its own proof and critical-regularity estimates.

Why it matters

If correct, the sharp thresholds characterize when smooth forcing can be made arbitrarily small in specified weak norms while still causing classical breakdown, clarifying the sensitivity of forced Navier–Stokes dynamics. The result is mathematically significant but conditional: the construction changes the force and imports a separate compact blowup solution, and neither proof has been independently checked by the research desk.
