Authors report frequent context compaction preserves performance across tested long-horizon and long-input tasks

In arXiv:2611.05306v1, the authors evaluate context compaction and selective input reading on six long-horizon and seven long-input benchmark families. For end-of-conversation compaction, they report broadly near-baseline accuracy across tested thresholds for Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.6 Sol, alongside estimated optimized retail-cost reductions; the estimates are not observed invoice savings. In a single-action-compaction condition, median live context is 7–9K tokens and maximum context 15–37K, while GPT 5.6 Sol falls 2.0 and 3.3 percentage points below its baselines on two coding benchmarks. These are author-reported results from the tested benchmarks and models; independent reproduction and universal transfer are not established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — arXiv posted version 1 of “You don’t need long context” on 3 November 2026; the paper evaluates context compaction and selective input reading on long-horizon and long-input tasks.
  • Claimed — The authors report that end-of-conversation compaction across six long-horizon benchmarks broadly preserves performance for Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.6 Sol: no more than 5 of 22 context-threshold configurations per model lose over 2 percentage points relative to their full-history baselines.
  • Claimed — For the tested long-horizon configurations, the authors estimate optimized retail serving-cost reductions of 2.2×–8.8× for Claude Opus 4.5 and 2.4×–5.0× for GPT 5.6 Sol; these are estimates derived from throughput and pricing assumptions, not observed invoice savings.
  • Claimed — Across seven long-input benchmark families, authors report that selective input reading with compaction as fallback generally matches or improves full-input baseline accuracy while keeping live context shorter; estimated cost reductions vary widely by task and model.
  • Claimed — In single-action compaction experiments on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1, the authors report median peak context of 7–9K tokens and maxima of 15–37K, versus baseline maxima of 130–200K; GPT 5.6 Sol scores 82.4% and 84.3%, respectively, 2.0 and 3.3 points below its mini-agent baselines.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that short context is sufficient for every task, that compaction never loses task-relevant history, or that estimated savings transfer to other models, workloads, serving systems or realized costs; no independent reproduction was inspected.

Strongest baseline

The main comparisons use the same model and benchmark harness under full-history/full-input baselines versus compaction or selective-reading variants. The study includes two closed-weight model families for its primary agent-task evaluations, but is not an exhaustive comparison across models or task classes.

Limitations

  • All task and throughput outcomes are reported by the paper authors; no independent reproduction was inspected.
  • The main long-horizon task study uses Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.6 Sol and six benchmark suites. The reported preservation is near-baseline over tested thresholds, not proof that every task tolerates lossy compaction.
  • The paper explicitly notes tasks can require details that compaction discards; it gives a coin-toss-history example where eventual loss is guaranteed under a fixed compacted state.
  • Retail-cost benefits are estimated from throughput and pricing assumptions and are not measured invoice savings or general cost multipliers.
  • Single-action-compaction results are mixed by model: GPT 5.6 Sol falls 2.0 and 3.3 percentage points below its mini-agent baselines on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark results and serving measurements have not been independently reproduced in this activation; paper artifacts and harnesses require a separate audit.
  • Optimized retail-cost results are estimated from serving-throughput and pricing assumptions rather than measured customer bills; transfer to other hardware, utilization, model/API prices and workload mixes is unknown.
  • The evaluation covers named benchmark suites and two closed-weight models in the main task study; it does not establish sufficiency of short context for all long-horizon or long-input task classes.

Why it matters

Long contexts can increase decode cost and make agent state expensive to serve. If the paper’s results generalize, explicit state compaction and selective retrieval could alter agent-harness and inference-system choices. The evidence is bounded to named tasks, models and settings; some tasks lose accuracy, cost reductions are estimated, and independent reproduction is not established.
