A compositional benchmark exposes the reuse–forgetting tradeoff in continual robot world models

A September 18, 2026 arXiv preprint introduces a benchmark for continual world models in robot manipulation. Six simulated task curricula end with compositions of earlier action and perception primitives, separating reuse on a new task from forgetting on earlier tasks. The authors evaluate DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 with fine-tuning, replay, EWC and PackNet, plus a modular Prismatic World Model. In a diagnostic with an encoder pretrained on demonstrations from all benchmark tasks and then frozen, PWM reports mean forgetting score 3.85 and forward-transfer score 36.18 across six suites, compared with 34.97 and 38.11 for TD-MPC2 using that same encoder; lower forgetting and higher forward transfer are better. Without this privileged encoder, PWM is reported on par with TD-MPC2. The study is limited to simulation, three seeds, selected model settings, and no controlled parameter or compute budgets; it does not establish physical-robot performance.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v1 preprint defines six simulated Meta-World manipulation curricula that end with tasks recombining preceding primitives along action, perception, or both axes. It measures forward transfer on the composition task and forgetting on earlier tasks.
  • Claimed — Across six suites, the authors report that modular Prismatic World Model (PWM) with a pretrained frozen encoder has mean backward-transfer forgetting score 3.85 and forward-transfer score 36.18, compared with 34.97 and 38.11 for TD-MPC2 using the same encoder. Values are means over three seeds. The encoder was pretrained on expert demonstrations from all benchmark tasks; without this privileged encoder, PWM is reported on par with TD-MPC2.
  • Claimed — For DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2, author-reported results show forgetting generally worsening from action to perception to full composition; TD-MPC2’s mean forward transfer becomes negative for perception composition. These results are from simulated curricula and are not real-robot validation.

Strongest baseline

TD-MPC2 with the same pretrained frozen encoder is the direct modularity diagnostic; however, that encoder was trained on expert demonstrations from all benchmark tasks. Without this privileged condition, PWM is reported on par with TD-MPC2. Conventional baselines include DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 using fine-tuning, experience replay, EWC and PackNet.

Limitations

  • The evaluation uses simulated curricula, online model-based RL and six Meta-World suites; no physical robot result is reported.
  • Only three random seeds are reported. The study tests selected model sizes and expert counts without controlled parameter or compute budgets.
  • The strongest modular PWM result depends on a frozen encoder pretrained on demonstrations from the full benchmark task distribution. Without that privileged setting, PWM is reported on par with TD-MPC2.
  • The fixed task-level router does not consistently recover reuse structure for temporal compositions; stage-adaptive routing and interactions between primitives remain open.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark and model results are author-reported; no independent replication across seeds or implementations was established.
  • The strongest PWM comparison uses a frozen encoder pretrained on demonstrations from all benchmark tasks, and the paper does not control parameter or compute budgets.
  • The benchmark is simulated; transfer to physical manipulation, sensor changes and imperfect resets remains unmeasured.

Why it matters

The benchmark makes a useful evaluation distinction explicit: rapid adaptation can reflect new learning rather than reuse of prior experience. Its simulation results suggest perception changes and full task compositions can make reuse harder, while also showing that a modular model’s apparent forgetting advantage depends on privileged encoder pretraining.
