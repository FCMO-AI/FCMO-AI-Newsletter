Auto-Research Arena reports multi-objective benchmark results for autonomous research systems

A UC San Diego and Adobe Research paper introduces Auto-Research Arena, a benchmark spanning nine language-model efficiency objectives. In a single reported testbed, the authors compare six LLM research harnesses and a non-agentic baseline using two foundation models. No harness dominates all objectives; simple Sequential Search ranks second overall in both model groups. In a separate one-hour follow-up search, the authors report lower training loss under matched data and FLOPs and lower held-out BPB at the same model size after combining selected discoveries. These are author-reported results from one setup, not evidence of general autonomous scientific-research capability.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The paper introducing Auto-Research Arena was submitted to arXiv on October 26, 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report comparing six LLM-based research harnesses and a tree-structured Parzen estimator baseline over nine language-model efficiency objectives, using Claude Opus 5 and GPT-6 Astra. They report no single harness dominated and simple Sequential Search ranked second overall with both foundation models.
  • Claimed — In a separate quality-focused search combining selected discoveries, the authors report training loss decreasing from 2.675 to 2.544 at matched training data and from 2.617 to 2.544 at matched training FLOPs, with held-out BPB decreasing from 0.9295 to 0.9112 at the same 50.33M-parameter model size.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that the benchmark rankings or reported gains generalize to other model families, hardware, datasets, scientific fields, or end-to-end research tasks; no independent reproduction was performed in this review.

Strongest baseline

The paper reports Sequential Search as second overall in both foundation-model groups; its reference starting program anchors the reported resource and quality comparisons. Cross-testbed superiority is not established.

Limitations

  • The benchmark is limited to language-model pretraining and does not evaluate the full end-to-end research process or scientific novelty.
  • Reported experiments use one fixed training testbed/data pool and an NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU with seed 42; external validity to other regimes is unknown.
  • The one-hour quality search is an author-reported follow-up, not an independent reproduction; no broad scientific discovery capability is established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the benchmark comparisons or the one-hour transfer experiment was located or performed.
  • Generalization beyond the single language-modeling testbed, two foundation models, training pool, A100 and seed is unmeasured.

Why it matters

The work tests whether autonomous research harnesses can be compared across multiple objectives and reports that a simple sequential method remains competitive with more elaborate orchestration. Its follow-up suggests some short-search discoveries can be reused to improve measured model quality within the same testbed. Independent reproduction and transfer beyond this setup remain open.
