GVPO is extended to sequence-level on-policy distillation objectives

An arXiv paper explicitly extends the authors’ NeurIPS 2025 Group Variance Policy Optimization (GVPO) work. Its new bounded contribution is a sequence-level formulation for on-policy distillation (OPD): the authors derive a GVPO loss for matching student and teacher distributions, then generalize it to positive response-weighted teacher/student distributions. The paper proves a consistency result for the transformed objective and shows how group-centering cancels partition-function terms, allowing sampling from the student or previous student policy without importance-sampling correction. The theory is author-reported; this activation did not audit proofs, experiments or code, and does not establish training gains in practice.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors extend GVPO to standard on-policy distillation by expressing the student-teacher reverse-KL objective as a group-centered sequence-level loss. The derivation removes dependence on an arbitrary reference policy and does not require token-level alignment between teacher and student.
  • Claimed — For positive response weight f(x,y), the paper defines weighted teacher and student distributions and proves that equality of those distributions implies recovery of the teacher policy (Theorem V.1). Its group-centered loss cancels partition-function terms, and the authors state that sampling from the student or previous student policy retains the theoretical guarantee without importance-sampling correction.
  • Demonstrated — The official NeurIPS 2025 record establishes the prior GVPO paper and its core claims about KL-constrained reward optimization, unique optimum and flexible sampling. It does not include the later paper’s OPD extension, which is the distinct contribution assessed here.

Strongest baseline

The official NeurIPS 2025 GVPO paper is the prior version: it establishes the base GVPO post-training method and its theoretical sampling/optimality claims. The distinct addition here is sequence-level standard and weighted OPD, rather than a new claim for the original GVPO method.
