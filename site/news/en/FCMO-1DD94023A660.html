A joint quality-and-diversity method for selecting reasoning rollouts

An arXiv paper introduces PKTP, which selects partial reasoning rollouts by directly estimating the chance that at least one retained rollout will finish correctly. The authors report higher pass@k than a quality-only linear probe and a diversity-only selector across eight open-weight math-reasoning models, especially at moderate selection budgets. Their main setup samples 128 rollouts per problem, observes 4,000-token prefixes and keeps 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16; results average 20 repetitions of eight-fold cross-validation over roughly 600 filtered problems per model. Correctness is judged by Qwen3-32B answer equivalence. These are author-reported benchmark results, not an independent reproduction or evidence of end-to-end inference savings.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The paper defines PKTP as a greedy selector that estimates joint rollout correctness with a latent Gaussian model and optimizes a submodular approximation to pass@k.
  • Claimed — Across eight open-weight math-reasoning models, the authors report that PKTP outperforms a quality-only linear probe and a diversity-oriented RepExp selector, with the clearest gains at moderate k.
  • Claimed — The main reported setup uses 128 sampled rollouts per problem, 4,000-token prefixes, selection budgets k=1,2,4,8,16, roughly 600 filtered problems per model, a Qwen3-32B answer-equivalence judge and 20 repeated eight-fold cross-validation runs.
  • Not established — The paper does not independently establish transfer beyond mathematical reasoning, robustness to answer-judge errors, independent reproducibility or end-to-end token, latency, compute or monetary savings.

Strongest baseline

Random selection, a hidden-state Linear Probe and representation-based RepExp are compared on the same sampled rollout pools. The preprint reports mixed baseline performance by model and k; no independent evaluator or reproduction was inspected.

Limitations

  • All performance outcomes are author-reported from one version-1 preprint and are not independently reproduced.
  • The evaluation covers eight open-weight mathematical-reasoning models; it does not establish gains for coding, general agents or other task families.
  • Correctness labels use Qwen3-32B to judge answer equivalence to references; judge error was not independently audited.
  • A 4,000-token prefix is generated for each of 128 candidate rollouts in the main setup; reported pass@k gains alone do not show lower total inference tokens, latency, compute or cost.
  • The authors note that partial-rollout embeddings can carry weak correctness signals and that PKTP approaches RepExp at larger k.

What we still don't know

  • The reported eight-model benchmark results were not independently reproduced and the author implementation/supplement was not inspected in this review.
  • The paper does not establish total inference cost or whether gains transfer beyond math reasoning; a Qwen3-32B judge supplies answer-equivalence labels.

Why it matters

The work frames test-time rollout pruning around the practical objective of retaining at least one successful continuation while balancing predicted quality against correlated failures. If the reported gains hold in reproduction, this could inform how reasoning systems spend inference compute. The paper also reports weak correctness signal in partial-rollout embeddings and does not measure total token, latency or cost savings.
