An exact site-frequency spectrum for selected alleles extends error-controlled calculations to biobank-scale samples

A preliminary manuscript by Matthew D. Schwartz and Michael M. Desai presents a rational-arithmetic method for evaluating the Sawyer–Hartl Poisson random field site-frequency spectrum. The authors report exact results across classical sample sizes and controlled-error evaluation at biobank scale, compare three common inference packages against the result, and fit the model to gnomAD v4.1 exomes. The reported biological inference remains conditional on the paper’s equilibrium/panmictic modeling and synonymous-calibration assumptions.

What actually changed

A preliminary manuscript by Matthew D. Schwartz and Michael M. Desai presents a rational-arithmetic method for evaluating the Sawyer–Hartl Poisson random field site-frequency spectrum. The authors report exact results across classical sample sizes and controlled-error evaluation at biobank scale, compare three common inference packages against the result, and fit the model to gnomAD v4.1 exomes. The reported biological inference remains conditional on the paper’s equilibrium/panmictic modeling and synonymous-calibration assumptions.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report exact rational evaluation of the stationary Sawyer–Hartl spectrum in the classical sample-size range and validated error bounds at biobank sample sizes.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that their fit to gnomAD v4.1 exomes places most high-confidence loss-of-function DFE mass in the strongest-selection window resolvable under their model and data treatment.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Anthropic’s Oct 1 guest post attributes this work to Claude plus BootLoops, while identifying BootLoops as Schwartz-owned and not an Anthropic project; it does not provide independent evaluation of model autonomy or scientific correctness.

Strongest baseline

POLYDFE v2.0, FITdadi with dadi 2.4.4, and FASTDFE 1.3.3 are compared with the proposed evaluator using matched parameter conventions and likelihood fits.

The caveat that matters

  • The manuscript is explicitly marked PRELIMINARY and compiled Oct 1, 2026; peer review/status has not been established.
  • The author’s manuscript index says all manuscripts were read by humans for accuracy, while also noting that some LLM-first drafts have not been fully checked; this item is watermarked preliminary.
  • The authors say paper-specific Popcorn code, analysis code and data package will be deposited on acceptance; the public BootLoops repository does not yet reproduce this paper’s empirical analysis.
  • The analysis assumes panmixia/equilibrium and does not fit a demographic model. Population structure and linked selection are handled only through synonymous calibration and robustness checks; error guarantees do not cover the demographic layer.
  • Simulation checks found the per-bin tail masses are not identifiable (a +0.03 interior-bin spike was undetected at p=0.46); only aggregate selection-window mass is reported as robust. The profile intervals include a measured model-error floor and depend on a single dispersion factor adequately describing residuals.
  • The manuscript says the dominance treatment currently covers the stationary spectrum only and cautions against combining heterogeneous linked-selection results into a causal claim.
  • The gnomAD result is an author-reported model fit, not a direct observation that each mutation has the inferred selection coefficient; identifiability and calibration choices bound its interpretation.
  • No independent numerical reproduction or evidence of Claude’s autonomous contribution to the scientific conclusion was found in the sources reviewed.
  • The manuscript explicitly says its closed-form equation is not new and was already implemented by POLYDFE and FASTDFE; novelty is claimed for the conditioning analysis and error-controlled computation/comparison, not for the underlying expression.

What remains unknown

  • The paper-specific exact evaluator, gnomAD fit scripts and data package are not public; the manuscript says they will be deposited on acceptance.
  • No independent reproduction of the exact numerical outputs, package comparison, or 730,947-exome fit was located in this scan.
  • The paper’s likelihood/error guarantee does not include a fitted nonequilibrium demographic model; results depend on the stated synonymous calibration.
  • The manuscript simulation does not identify individual tail-bin masses; the aggregate selection-window mass is more robust, and interval coverage depends on the quasi-Poisson model-error floor being represented by a single dispersion factor.

FCMO Lens

Site-frequency-spectrum methods support inference about how natural selection shapes genetic variation. If the reported numerical method and error controls hold, researchers can test likelihood accuracy in sample-size and selection regimes where floating-point implementations are difficult to audit, including large exome cohorts.
