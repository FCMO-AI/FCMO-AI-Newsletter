U.S. Treasury proposes prohibiting covered outbound investment in development of defined AI systems for countries of concern

The U.S. Treasury published a proposed outbound-investment rule on Oct 26, 2026. It would implement the COINS Act framework and classify development of a defined “super intelligence system” as prohibited, rather than notifiable, technology in covered U.S.-person transactions involving countries of concern. The proposed definition includes generative systems and certain software, hardware, applications, tools or utilities operating with them. Treasury estimates annual direct compliance costs of $2.772–$5.86 million and about 20,000 person-hours, based on an estimated 200 potentially covered transactions; for its notification-cost estimate, it assumes all 200 are notifiable. These are ex ante regulatory estimates, not observed costs. Separately, Treasury estimates $4–$5 million in government IT development costs, $2–$3 million in annual system maintenance, and under $1 million in FY2027 personnel costs; it has no specific estimates for future outreach and enforcement. The proposal is open for comment through Dec 7, 2026, not current law; final scope and realized effects remain unknown.
