Reflection releases Beam 1.0 as a 501B-parameter open-weight model

Reflection released Beam 1.0 weights on October 16 under Apache 2.0, with BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 variants. Its model card lists a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 501.3B total and 23.4B active parameters and a 1M-token context window. Reflection reports strong coding, reasoning and tool-use benchmark scores, but these remain vendor-run results without an independent reproduction. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Reflection's October 16 release post links publicly available Beam weights under Apache 2.0; the official model card lists BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 variants.
  • Claimed — The model card describes Beam as a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 501.3B total parameters, 23.4B active parameters, 52 layers and a 1M-token context window.
  • Claimed — Reflection reports scores of 81.6% on SWE-bench Verified and 80.1% on Terminal Bench 2.1, alongside results on reasoning and tool-use benchmarks. These are provider-reported results; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • Claimed — Reflection's safety report assigns Beam a Limited rating across four systemic-risk categories and includes evaluations from named external providers. The report also describes a 20-participant human biological-uplift study as statistically non-significant and not wet-lab validated, and reports elevated performance on a narrow text-only pathogen-knowledge test; neither finding establishes real-world operational capability or its absence.
  • Demonstrated — No independent execution of the released weights, reproduction of vendor benchmark scores, or independent audit of the safety report was conducted in this review.

Strongest baseline

The model card compares Beam across BF16, FP8 and NVFP4 variants on listed public benchmarks; the safety report compares with a selected set of open-weight systems. No independent matched benchmark or deployment-cost comparison was conducted in this review.

Limitations

  • All reported benchmark scores and the overall safety assessment are from Reflection or named providers cited in its report; this review did not independently reproduce them.
  • A 1M-token context specification does not establish quality or cost across that full context length.
  • The 501.3B total parameter count may impose substantial memory and serving requirements; actual deployment hardware, throughput, latency and cost were not measured here.
  • The safety report's Limited ratings apply to specific evaluation regimes. Its human biological-uplift trial used 20 participants, was explicitly not statistically significant and lacked wet-lab validation; selected knowledge scores measure narrow test performance, not operational capability.
  • No evidence here establishes real-world adoption, customer outcomes, or the generalization of benchmark performance across tasks and harnesses.

Why it matters

Publicly downloadable weights and a permissive license extend access to a very large model from a new provider, while its sparse active-parameter count and reported agentic results make it relevant to open-model and inference choices. Actual serving costs, independent capability levels and safety performance remain unmeasured here, so the release does not yet establish that its reported quality is economical or generalizes beyond the tested settings.
