A paper proposes auditability as a fourth machine-unlearning criterion and reports a two-stage method with dataset tradeoffs

The Nov. 4 arXiv preprint “Widen the Gap, Hide the Gap” treats auditability—whether a provider can distinguish requested-forget examples from retained examples under a chosen statistic—as a fourth criterion for machine unlearning. It proposes GapWiden, which trains an audit-statistic gap into the unlearned model, and GapVeil, an inference-time rejection sampler intended to reduce disclosure of that gap. The authors report evaluations over eleven unlearning methods on TOFU and MUSE. Results are paper-reported: auditability rises toward 0.99 on MUSE, but retain performance on MUSE News falls from 0.569 to 0.380. These benchmark results do not establish deletion from a deployed model or proof of deletion for an individual request.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper defines unlearning auditability as a statistic that separates requested-forget examples from retained examples, and proposes the GapWiden training stage plus the GapVeil inference-time rejection sampler.
  • Claimed — Across eleven unlearning methods on TOFU and MUSE, the authors report that their two-stage procedure improves auditability and leakage metrics; reported outcomes vary across datasets and methods.
  • Claimed — On MUSE News, the paper reports that retain performance decreases from 0.569 to 0.380 with the proposed procedure, which the authors attribute to overlap between forget and retain content.
  • Not established — The benchmark results do not establish deletion from deployed systems, compliance with an individual deletion request, or an operationally reliable user-facing deletion certificate.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares each method with and without the proposed stages and includes a retrained oracle benchmark; these are author-run evaluations, not an independent matched reproduction.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are author-reported; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • TOFU and MUSE benchmark results do not demonstrate deployed-model erasure, regulatory compliance or an individual user deletion certificate.
  • MUSE News retain performance falls from 0.569 to 0.380 in the reported result; tradeoffs differ across datasets.
  • The authors describe auditability as a starting operational definition and identify consensus criteria as unresolved.
  • Membership leakage metrics and the rejection sampler’s production latency/cost have not been independently validated here.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported TOFU/MUSE evaluation was performed; underlying baselines and audit/leakage metrics remain author-reported.
  • The paper formalizes a benchmark statistic, not a production deletion procedure or an individual-request certificate; it does not establish that trained information has been removed across prompts or deployment pathways.
  • Retain utility degrades materially on MUSE News, and the compute/latency cost of GapWiden retraining and repeated rejection sampling is not characterized here as a production tradeoff.
  • The authors state that standardized consensus criteria for demonstrable unlearning remain unresolved; the proposed audit statistic is one operational definition.

Why it matters

A deletion workflow needs evidence that requested material was removed, while an observable audit signal can itself reveal membership. The paper makes that tension an explicit evaluation target and tests a training-plus-decoding approach. Its dataset-specific retain tradeoff and unverified operational assumptions matter before treating auditability as a practical guarantee.
