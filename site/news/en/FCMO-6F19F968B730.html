A premise-first audit finds limited selection signal, modest realized speedup, and compute-dependent render corruption in one world-action-model stack

A preprint by Liu et al. audits test-time scaling on Cosmos Policy in RoboCasa. What changed

A preprint by Liu et al. audits test-time scaling on Cosmos Policy in RoboCasa. In that configuration, the authors report that their deployable value selector performed 1.66 percentage points below random selection; a hindsight oracle gained 1.40 points, with an episode-level interval spanning zero, while 82% of tested states had identical candidate outcomes. Token pruning removed up to 47.9% of per-forward FLOPs but delivered at most 1.152× measured end-to-end speedup on the deployed path (1.253× with future-image decoding excluded), below a pre-registered 1.5× target. A power gate halted the full adaptive-allocation experiment, so its effect remains unresolved. The authors also identify a silent OpenGL render corruption in the tested RoboCasa/robosuite stack, report a detector and fix, and find the corruption more frequent in a non-standard compute-heavy planning path; they say the official direct-policy benchmark is not materially affected. These are author-reported, configuration-specific findings, not evidence that test-time scaling generally fails.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On render-verified clean data in the tested Cosmos Policy–RoboCasa setup, the authors report that their value-based selector performed 1.66 percentage points below random. A hindsight oracle gained 1.40 points, but its episode-level 95% interval crossed zero; 82% of tested states showed identical outcomes across candidates. The oracle is an upper-bound diagnostic, not a deployable selector.
  • Claimed — On the tested stack, token pruning removed up to 47.9% of per-forward FLOPs but produced 1.152× measured end-to-end speedup on the deployed eager path and 1.253× with future-image decoding excluded, both below the pre-registered 1.5× target. The result is a latency-only measurement; action quality was not assessed.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish whether joint adaptive compute allocation improves task success: the preliminary effect was below the measured noise floor and failed the pre-registered power gate, so the full factorial experiment was not run. This is inconclusive, not a measured null.
  • Claimed — The authors report identifying and fixing a silent OpenGL-context rendering corruption in their RoboCasa/robosuite stack. They measured corruption at 0.67% per episode in the tested direct-policy arm and report up to 65% in a non-standard compute-heavy planning configuration. They say the official direct-policy benchmark was not materially affected; the 65% figure is not a standard-benchmark rate.

Strongest baseline

For candidate selection, the authors compare value-based selection with random choice and a non-deployable hindsight oracle under a shared candidate/outcome matrix. For latency, they directly time complete dense and pruned queries on the deployed eager path and on a decode-excluded path.

Limitations

  • One preprint and one Cosmos Policy–RoboCasa/robosuite configuration; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The selection results use a defined replay/continuation protocol; only two candidate repeats were used. Supplementary decomposition covers four tasks and three episodes and is exploratory, with limitations on independence and multiple comparisons.
  • The full adaptive-allocation factorial was not run after a pre-registered power gate failed; the reported preliminary effect is unresolved.
  • The maximum 65% rendering-corruption rate comes from a non-standard compute-heavy planning configuration, not an official benchmark. The authors attribute the bug to OpenGL context management but say maintainer confirmation was not sought.
  • The paper says it releases a detector, fix and scripts, but this activation did not inspect a repository, raw data or reproduce the result.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the candidate-selection, end-to-end latency, or render-integrity results was established.
  • The findings have not been tested on other world-action policies, robot tasks, simulator versions, backends or candidate-generation and continuation policies.
  • The identified OpenGL-context bug attribution lacks maintainer confirmation, and the non-standard compute-heavy planning path does not match an official benchmark configuration.

Why it matters

The study shows why inference-time planning claims depend on three separate premises: candidates must contain a usable decision signal, nominal compute savings must reduce complete-query latency, and evaluation must remain reliable under the compute path being measured. If reproduced in other robot-policy stacks, this audit design could change how researchers measure test-time scaling. This preprint establishes neither broad failure nor general transfer.
