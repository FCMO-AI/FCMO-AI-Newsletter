What actually changed

A new arXiv benchmark asks AI agent systems to build compact stochastic models of El Niño–Southern Oscillation from observations. The paper reports that six of twelve final agent submissions scored above its published reference overall and that some agent-built models better matched reconstruction or held-out forecast measures, while the reference led on statistical matching. The paper's two model families echo competing explanations of ENSO asymmetry, but do not resolve the physical debate. Results come from one six-hour run per system, with limited single-run access controls; independent reproduction is open.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors introduce SciExam for ENSO, a six-hour task in which agent systems construct low-order stochastic ENSO models from observations, freeze diagnostics, and are scored on statistics, hidden-variable reconstruction and forecasts over held-out years.
  • CLAIMED — In the paper's final submissions, six of twelve agent systems score above the published reference overall (0.378); the authors report 0.515 for Fable 5.1, 0.479 for Kimi K3 and 0.443 for GPT-5.6 Sol. Systems are evaluated as model–harness submissions, not isolated model weights.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports metric tradeoffs: the published reference performs best on statistical matching, while several agent submissions score better on reconstruction or forecasting. The strongest agent-built models fall into two families corresponding to nonlinear-drift and multiplicative-stochastic-forcing explanations of ENSO asymmetry.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that one controlled-access run of the top system under varied data access found that web access could distract from the task observations; the paper also reports that literature access shifted a model toward sparser theory-based couplings. Each controlled condition used a single run.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The benchmark does not establish repeatable agent capability, a causal effect of data access, or which physical explanation of ENSO asymmetry is correct; independent replication and repeated submissions remain open.

Strongest baseline

The published reference scores 0.378 overall. Six of twelve final agent submissions exceed it; the paper reports Fable 5.1 at 0.515, Kimi K3 at 0.479 and GPT-5.6 Sol at 0.443. The reference is strongest on statistical matching, while agent entries often do better on reconstruction or forecasting.

The caveat that matters

  • The headline is based on twelve final one-off agent-system submissions, not repeated trials; one run per system cannot establish variance or repeatability.
  • The published reference had observations covering all three periods, while agent systems used 35 years and were evaluated on a never-seen 2015–2024 period; comparison conditions differ.
  • The benchmark combines model, scaffold and tool choices, so scores cannot be attributed to model weights alone.
  • The paper's controlled-access ablations use one run per condition and do not establish causal effects of web or literature access.
  • Two families of fitted low-order models reflect candidate explanations of ENSO asymmetry; their appearance in this benchmark does not resolve the physical mechanism or establish new climate science.
  • The benchmark selects and scores simplified models using its own diagnostics; broader scientific validity beyond those diagnostics is not established.

What remains unknown

  • No independent rerun of the benchmark or verification of its code, data and scoring pipeline was conducted.
  • Agent systems and the published reference had different observation access, while each system's headline score is based on a single final run.
  • The candidate ENSO mechanism families are generated and judged within this benchmark and do not establish which physical explanation is correct.

FCMO Lens

This is a concrete test of whether agent systems can construct compact scientific models against hidden diagnostics and held-out observations, beyond answering a fixed question. If replicated, it could inform how research agents are evaluated for model building. The reported scores are one-off benchmark outcomes, and the candidate mechanism families are not confirmed discoveries about ENSO.
