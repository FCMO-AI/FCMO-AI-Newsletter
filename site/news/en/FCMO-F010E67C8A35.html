A paired-replay study finds weak history signals for predicting when agent context compaction will hurt

Pakhomov and Nijkamp use TRACE’s paired-replay corpus to test whether an agent’s pre-compaction history predicts the harm caused by summarizing that context. Across 590 harness-triggered boundaries from 275 trajectories on 147 AppWorld tasks, the prespecified contrast between histories with and without prior writes is a wide null (dU@3 −0.062; 95% interval −0.209 to +0.079); a naive “has written” label largely tracks trajectory phase. The best history-based trigger reaches held-out AUROC 0.66, below a same-boundary replay yardstick of 0.72. A frozen two-feature logistic trigger at 83.7% retention avoids 20.6% of positive-burden boundaries by count, but has no detected advantage on burden mass. The study does not establish a deployable compaction policy: it covers one agent/compressor, one AppWorld cohort and a 4,096-token window, and cannot compare with a token-budget rule at matched retention.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors analyze 590 harness-triggered compaction boundaries from 275 trajectories on 147 AppWorld tasks using paired replay before and after compaction, one minimax-m3 agent/compressor and a 4,096-token context.
  • CLAIMED — The prespecified contrast between prior-write and read-only histories is a wide null, while the naive “has written” label mostly tracks trajectory phase.
  • CLAIMED — The best reported history trigger has held-out AUROC 0.66 versus a same-boundary replicate yardstick of 0.72; a frozen two-feature trigger avoids 20.6% of positive-burden boundaries at 83.7% retention by count, but has no detected burden-mass advantage.
  • DEMONSTRATED_LIMITATION_OF_SOURCE — The study does not establish cross-model generality, a deployable compaction policy, or performance against a token-budget rule at matched retention.

Strongest baseline

A same-boundary split-half replay is used as a noise/replicability yardstick, not a deployable policy. The trigger is compared with a uniform random-rule expectation at equal-weight compaction opportunities; the paper cannot evaluate a token-budget rule at matched retention because token counts are not released.

The caveat that matters

  • All performance figures are author-reported; the research desk did not independently reproduce the analysis.
  • The cohort contains one agent model, one compressor and one deliberately tight 4,096-token window, with harness-triggered boundaries only on AppWorld.
  • Each boundary uses three to five replays per arm and the paired harm outcome is noisy; the same-boundary replicate AUROC is 0.72 while three-class sign agreement is 0.55 (κ=0.28).
  • The public release omits ordered actions, token counts, compression ratio and summary text, preventing a matched-retention comparison with a token-budget policy.
  • The source reports no data redistribution permission and the cited repository revision had no license file; independent replication must use authorized data access and must not rehost the corpus.
  • Trigger frontiers are offline plug-in estimates over logged outcomes, not a deployable fitted policy or measured task-success/latency benefit.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction or test across different models, compressors or harnesses was performed.
  • The public observables omit ordered histories, token counts, compression ratio and summary text; matched-retention comparison to token-budget compaction is unavailable.

FCMO Lens

Context compaction is often triggered by a global token threshold, but delaying it based on recent agent behavior only helps if the history predicts which boundaries will be harmed. This study reports weak prediction in one controlled corpus and shows that a simple “has written” signal can be confounded with trajectory phase. It gives agent-harness designers a counterfactual evaluation target, while leaving cross-model generality and matched-retention policy value unresolved.
