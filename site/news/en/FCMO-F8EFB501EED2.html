Fixed-SAE Track traces bounded, format-heavy RL shifts in a small reasoning model

A September 14, 2026 arXiv paper introduces Fixed-SAE Track: it trains one shared sparse autoencoder per analyzed layer on activations pooled across a base model and its RL checkpoints, then freezes the feature directions to track representation shifts. In the main Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct math-RL setting, the authors report gradual late-layer drift concentrated in formatting and reasoning scaffolds. Steering amplified features at dose α=10 raises strict accuracy from 0.050 to 0.192, close to the short-RL final model’s 0.175, while lenient reasoning accuracy changes more modestly. The paper also reports that all 29 selected GSM8K problems that shift from greedy-base-wrong to RL-right are solved by base sampling at k=64; however, 2000 additional RL steps raise lenient accuracy from 0.300 to 0.408, and directly injecting a necessary reasoning feature set recovers little. The approximately 80% headline therefore concerns a bounded short-RL extractability/format effect, not a general claim that RL only elicits existing capability. Results are author-reported and not independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce Fixed-SAE Track, which trains one shared SAE per analyzed layer on pooled activations from a base model and RL checkpoints, then re-encodes all checkpoints through the frozen dictionary to track feature shifts using fixed directions.
  • Claimed — In the main Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct mathematical RL setting, the authors report small, gradual, late-layer feature drift concentrated in formatting scaffolds and ladder tokens. At steering dose α=10, steering amplified features raises strict accuracy from 0.050 to 0.192 versus 0.175 for the short-RL final model, while lenient accuracy changes more modestly.
  • Claimed — For 29 GSM8K problems that are wrong under greedy base decoding but correct after RL, the authors report base pass@k rises to 1.00 at k=64; they also report that continuing training for 2000 steps raises lenient accuracy from 0.300 to 0.408. These results qualify the claim that short RL mainly elicits existing sampled capability: sustained training can add reasoning performance in this regime.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that RL generally only elicits pre-existing capabilities or that the headline steering recovery is a general fraction of RL’s gain; the ~80% framing concerns a short-training strict-accuracy/extractability effect, while directly injecting a reasoning feature set recovers little.

Strongest baseline

For steering sufficiency, the short-RL final checkpoint is compared with the base model under amplified-feature and random-direction interventions. For base capability, the comparator is greedy decoding versus sampling pass@k on the selected 29 GSM8K items. These comparisons answer different questions and should not be collapsed into one general RL effect.

Limitations

  • All evidence is from the author-reported arXiv v1 paper; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The main representational analysis focuses on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct mathematical RL and a fixed SAE at layer 27, with some layer comparisons; broader transfer is unknown.
  • The approximately 80% headline describes steering recovery of a short-RL strict-accuracy/extractability effect; lenient reasoning accuracy changes more modestly, and sustained training raises it further.
  • Among 29 GSM8K problems wrong under greedy base decoding but right after RL, base sampling solves all by k=64; this selected set does not establish general capability boundaries.
  • A reasoning feature set is reported as causally necessary under ablation, but direct static injection recovers only a small fraction of the RL gain.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of Fixed-SAE Track, feature interpretations or steering results has been inspected.
  • The main mechanistic claims center on one small model family, mathematical RL and a specific fixed SAE layer; broader model, task and algorithm transfer remains uncertain.
  • The approximately 80% steering recovery framing refers to the short-RL strict-accuracy/extractability effect; sustained training also improves lenient accuracy, and static injection of the reasoning feature set recovers little.
  • The base pass@64 result covers the 29 selected GSM8K problems that switch from greedy-base-wrong to RL-right; it does not establish broad capability presence across harder tasks or sampling budgets.

Why it matters

A shared fixed feature basis can make checkpoint-to-checkpoint interpretability less dependent on matching separately trained SAE features. The reported interventions suggest some short-run mathematical RL gains may reflect answer-format extractability, while the paper also finds genuine reasoning gains under sustained training. This distinction could change how post-training gains are measured and interpreted, but the evidence remains author-reported and narrow in model/task regime.
