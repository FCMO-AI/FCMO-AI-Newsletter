Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The European Commission published Flash Eurobarometer 578 on AI and algorithmic management in European SMEs and mid-caps on October 14, 2026. Its infographic says fieldwork ran March 20–April 13, 2026, using telephone interviews with 12,826 respondents drawn from privately owned SMEs and mid-caps with 1–749 employees across all 27 EU member states.
  • Claimed — For the combined category of AI or algorithmic management, the survey reports 42% current use (12% extensive, 30% limited or experimental), 21% planning to use, 35% with no plan, and 2% unsure.
  • Claimed — The infographic reports that 75% of surveyed companies expect AI and algorithmic management to improve job quality. It also says 90% of companies using the tools keep people in charge of decisions: 38% report human decisions without AI, 52% AI suggestions with human decisions, 7% AI decisions with possible human review, and 2% fully automated decisions without human review; 1% did not know.
  • Claimed — The infographic lists investment costs (50%), uncertainty about legal or regulatory rules (49%), and lack of internal expertise (48%) as the most common barriers to AI or algorithmic-management adoption.

Strongest baseline

This is a cross-sectional survey; no prior matched measurement or same-firm change estimate is provided in the reviewed infographic.

Limitations

  • The reported use categories combine artificial intelligence with algorithmic management; the infographic does not isolate AI-only uptake.
  • Results are telephone-interview responses from company heads, not independently verified system inventories, audited purchase or usage records, or representative measurements of all EU employers.
  • The infographic gives the target population, date range, and interview count but not the complete questionnaire, weighting, response rate, sampling error, or microdata; the linked Eurobarometer survey-detail page loaded only as a client-side shell in this review.
  • Plans and expectations are not realized deployment, productivity changes, job-quality outcomes, employment effects, or causal impacts.

What we still don't know

  • The report infographic does not expose the full questionnaire, country-level sample allocation and weighting, response rate, sampling error, or microdata in the inspected artifact.
  • The headline category combines AI with algorithmic management and combines extensive use with limited or experimental use; it cannot isolate AI adoption or production deployment.
  • Surveyed expectations of productivity, job quality, and adoption plans are not realized outcomes.

Why it matters

This offers a new cross-EU snapshot of reported workplace adoption, near-term plans, and employer expectations among privately owned SMEs and mid-caps. The split between current use, experimental or limited use, and planned use can inform adoption assumptions and labor-policy discussion. The categories combine AI with algorithmic management, however, and the survey does not measure installed systems, productivity, or realized effects on workers.
