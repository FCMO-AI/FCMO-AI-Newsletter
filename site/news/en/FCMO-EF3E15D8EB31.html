VLM-in-Sandbox reports bounded visual evidence state for multimodal agents

The preprint presents a training-free visual workspace that registers sandbox-generated images in a ledger, keeps active visual context bounded and lets the model choose which artifacts to re-inspect. Across seven benchmarks, the authors report a 2.52 percentage-point pooled accuracy gain for GPT-4.1-mini over an append-only sandbox on 6,350 examples (95% CI 1.83–3.21), with 302 rescues and 142 regressions. A compiler-matched study on 1,260 examples reports 66.27% accuracy with 18.6% fewer total tokens than an automatic retain-all control. These are author-reported results, with heterogeneous per-benchmark effects and no independent reproduction.

Evidence

  • Claimed — VLM-in-Sandbox stores generated visual artifacts in an addressable ledger and uses model-directed promotion with a bounded active visual context.
  • Claimed — The paper reports a 2.52 percentage-point pooled accuracy gain over Original Append-only on 6,350 GPT-4.1-mini examples, alongside 302 rescues and 142 regressions.
  • Not established — The author-reported benchmark results do not establish independent reproducibility or universal accuracy, compute or latency gains across multimodal agent deployments.

Strongest baseline

Original Append-only for the primary GPT-4.1-mini comparison; the matched 2×2 study compares model-directed visibility and bounded retention against an automatic retain-all control.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are paper-reported; the research desk did not run code or independently reproduce them.
  • The primary GPT-4.1-mini comparison reports both 302 rescues and 142 regressions; results vary materially by benchmark.
  • The 1,260-example matched ablation changes visual visibility and retention controls; it does not establish that token reduction alone causes the accuracy result.
  • A training-free system depends on the base VLM to decide when to use tools and which visual evidence to revisit.
  • Fewer visual tokens do not by themselves establish lower total inference compute or deployment cost.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark and matched-ablation results have not been independently reproduced.
  • Total serving cost and transfer across model families are not established by the reported token savings.

Why it matters

Multimodal agents can accumulate crops, masks and rendered analyses faster than they can fit them into the active context. An explicit evidence ledger and selective re-entry policy are a concrete alternative to either discarding artifacts or appending all of them indefinitely. The reported gains are modest in aggregate and include regressions, so the result supports testing the design rather than assuming universal benefit.
