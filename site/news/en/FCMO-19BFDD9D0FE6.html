What changed

The revised preprint reports 7,950 FSM-validated synthetic trajectories for 53 RoboCasa-derived kitchen tasks. In closed-loop simulation, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct reaches 65% success on 10 held-out tasks; adding rationale targets to the Thinking model reports up to 77%. These are author-reported simulator results, not physical-robot tests.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report constructing 7,950 finite-state-machine-validated synthetic trajectories across 53 RoboCasa-derived kitchen tasks.
  • Claimed — With 150 trajectories per task, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct reports 94% success on 43 training tasks and 65% on 10 held-out tasks over 10 episodes per task.
  • Claimed — The authors report up to 77% success on the 10 held-out simulator tasks for Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking trained with rationale targets at 120 trajectories per task.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares communication protocols and training variants; these are within-paper simulator comparisons and have not been independently reproduced.

Limitations

  • No physical robot or real-world environment was evaluated.
  • The held-out set contains 10 tasks with 10 episodes per task; reported success uses an FSM goal verifier.
  • Held-out success is not monotonic in training-set size.
  • Results are paper-reported; this review did not execute the public code or reproduce the results.

What we still don't know

  • No independent execution or reproduction of the simulator results was conducted.
  • Transfer from synthetic closed-loop simulation to physical robots and less scripted environments is untested.

Why it matters

If the reported results hold in broader settings, structured wait-release communication and exclusive-workspace rules paired with multimodal demonstrations may improve compact VLM coordination in constrained multi-robot tasks. Transfer to real robots and less scripted environments remains unshown.
