A staged LLM pipeline reports Lean-checked runtime proofs for designed optimization algorithms

An October 26 preprint reports a staged pipeline in which LLMs propose black-box optimization algorithms and construct potential functions and runtime analyses that are checked by Lean. The author reports that 12 of 15 frontier-model runs across eight problem classes completed the pipeline; nine produced polynomial query bounds without relying on uncharged offline computation. The paper reports an O(n²) result for CycleUncover and matching O(n²) order for LeadingOnes. These are preprint-reported results, not independent reproduction. The study did not empirically compare the algorithms, tested open-weight models did not complete the pipeline, the search space is restricted, and the bounds are not yet state of the art.

Strongest baseline

The paper reports exponential expected runtime for the classical (1+1) EA on its CycleUncover problem and an O(n²) designed algorithm; it reports matching O(n²) order on LeadingOnes. The claims are author-reported formal results and not independent performance comparisons.

Limitations

  • The evidence is one author-reported preprint; no independent reproduction or specialist review was located in this bounded check.
  • The paper reports no empirical comparison of algorithms; runtime claims are within the specified black-box query model, which does not charge offline computation between queries.
  • Only frontier models completed the pipeline in the reported study; the tested open-weight models did not complete it.
  • The search is restricted to bitstring problems and the paper states the bounds are not yet state of the art.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or mathematical review of the reported Lean-checked algorithms and runtime claims was found or performed in this review.
  • The inspected preprint describes Lean checks and gives extensive appendices, but this activation did not inspect the linked code/Lean repository or rerun reported jobs.
  • The experiments cover selected bitstring black-box problem classes, include uncharged offline computation in the query model, and report no empirical comparison of the resulting algorithms.

Why it matters

If independently reproduced and extended, a process that jointly designs algorithms and machine-checks runtime bounds could expand AI-assisted algorithm discovery. The current evidence is a single author-reported preprint with constrained benchmarks and no empirical comparison of algorithm performance.
