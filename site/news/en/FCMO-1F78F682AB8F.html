Embodied First Exam brings coding-agent evaluation to diverse simulated robots

A Nov. 9 arXiv preprint introduces the Embodied First Exam (EFE), a framework that lets coding agents choose direct control, code-generated control, or policy training across 1,156 tasks in 35 suites and 47 simulated robots. It separates sensor-only Standard and Realtime settings from a Privileged setting with full simulator state and unlimited resets. On 391 core tasks with paired Privileged and Standard tests, the authors report GPT-6 Astra in Codex solved 77% in Privileged and 30% in Standard; adding resets to the Standard interface raised success to 53%, while adding both resets and exact object state raised it to 68%. These are preprint-reported simulation results, not independently reproduced physical-robot capability measurements.

What changed

A Nov. 9 arXiv preprint introduces the Embodied First Exam (EFE), a framework that lets coding agents choose direct control, code-generated control, or policy training across 1,156 tasks in 35 suites and 47 simulated robots. It separates sensor-only Standard and Realtime settings from a Privileged setting with full simulator state and unlimited resets. On 391 core tasks with paired Privileged and Standard tests, the authors report GPT-6 Astra in Codex solved 77% in Privileged and 30% in Standard; adding resets to the Standard interface raised success to 53%, while adding both resets and exact object state raised it to 68%. These are preprint-reported simulation results, not independently reproduced physical-robot capability measurements.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce EFE with 1,156 tasks in 35 suites spanning 47 robots, including 9 new suites, and define Privileged, Standard and Realtime evaluation settings.
  • Claimed — Across 391 paired core tasks, the paper reports GPT-6 Astra in Codex success of 77% in Privileged and 30% in Standard; controlled additions of resets and exact state raise Standard success to 53% and 68%, respectively.
  • Demonstrated — The paper was posted to arXiv on November 9, 2026.
  • Not established — The inspected sources do not establish independent reproduction, repeated-attempt reliability, or robust performance on physical robots; all scored results in the paper are simulated.

Strongest baseline

Within-task paired comparisons contrast the same agent on Privileged and Standard settings. The paper reports GPT-6 Astra in Codex at 77% versus 30% on 391 paired core tasks; resetting and exact-state ablations partially close the gap.

Limitations

  • The benchmark paper and all scores are author-reported and were not independently reproduced here.
  • The core set was selected partly using GPT-6 Luna screening, which may bias comparisons involving the GPT-6 family.
  • Single attempts per agent-test pair do not establish reliability under repeated runs.
  • Simulation results and a small excluded physical demonstration do not establish general physical-robot capability.

What we still don't know

  • The task suite, exact harness/model versions and leaderboard results were not independently replayed in this activation.
  • The authors note the hard core set excluded tasks initially solved by GPT-6 Luna, a model in the GPT-6 family. The effect of this filter on cross-agent comparisons is unmeasured.
  • Aggregate results are simulation-only; the small real-robot demonstration is excluded from reported scores.

Why it matters

EFE makes state access, reset availability and real-time pressure explicit evaluation variables for general-purpose coding agents controlling robots. Its paired results suggest that success under privileged simulation can substantially overstate performance under sensor-limited, less reversible conditions. The framework may help separate planning and control failures across robot types, while its selected core set and simulation-only evidence limit what the scores establish.
