Turn-level action selection improves reported agent success with substantial token overhead

A 4 November arXiv preprint evaluates turn-level best-of-N (Turn-BoN): at each step, a generator proposes 4 or 8 candidate tool actions and a separate language-model selector chooses one before it is executed. On 89 Terminal-Bench v2.1 tasks across four trials, the authors report higher pass rates for Qwen3.6-35B and K2-Horizon-375B generators when paired with Kimi-K3 or DeepSeek V4.1 Flash selectors. Reported gains include K2-Horizon 73.3% to 77.9% at N=4 and Qwen3.6 47.5% to 52.8% at N=4 or 55.2% at N=8. The authors also report 40–50% fewer turns and reduced looping, while output tokens rise to about 3.6–8.2 times baseline. These preliminary paper-reported results use two generator models and stronger selectors; they were not independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors submitted arXiv:2611.06868v1 on 4 November 2026, describing turn-level best-of-N action selection for language-model agents.
  • Claimed — On 89 Terminal-Bench v2.1 tasks over four trials, the authors report K2-Horizon-375B pass@1 rising from 73.3% to 77.9% with N=4, and Qwen3.6-35B rising from 47.5% to 52.8% with N=4 or 55.2% with N=8 when paired with Kimi-K3 or DeepSeek V4.1 Flash selectors.
  • Claimed — The authors report that the tested Turn-BoN configurations used approximately 3.6–8.2 times the baseline output tokens per attempt; they also report about 40–50% fewer turns on average.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, generalization beyond the tested Terminal-Bench setup, benefits with equal or weaker selectors, or a net cost/performance advantage across real deployments.

Strongest baseline

The same generator model in the mini-SWE-agent/Terminal-Bench setup without a selector; authors report four trials and 89 tasks.

Limitations

  • All reported task and compute results are author claims from a preprint; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The study tests two generator model families on 89 Terminal-Bench v2.1 tasks with four trials and stronger selector models; it does not establish how gains behave with weaker or equal-capability selectors.
  • The reported 3.6–8.2x total output-token overhead is not a billed-cost or latency comparison across deployed systems.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the pass-rate, consistency, behavior or compute findings was reviewed.
  • The reviewed arXiv page did not identify an inspected code or task-trace release.
  • The reported selector models are more capable than the generators in the chosen setup; equal or weaker selectors were not tested.

Why it matters

Turn-BoN offers a simple way to steer interactive agents by selecting among candidate actions at every step, and the paper reports gains in task success and fewer looping behaviors. Its measured token overhead is substantial, and the small setup with stronger selector models does not establish a general capability or cost-effective deployment advantage.
