A systematic review maps specification, verification, and enforcement gaps in LLM agent safety

A June 2026 preprint surveys safety methods for LLM agents that take multi-step actions. The authors report a PRISMA 2020 review of 38 studies and organize the field around specification, verification, and enforcement. Their synthesis identifies errors in translating natural-language requirements into formal rules as a major bottleneck and notes that action-level blocking does not necessarily yield safe task completion. The strongest verifier-tax result comes from one study of tau-bench Airline/Retail tasks: in that study, per-proposal interception reached 94.1% in one setting while safe-success rates were below 5% in most tested configurations; the authors also report 2.0–2.8× token inflation for their Triad architectures. The study used two open-weight models, different 15/30-turn caps, and an unseeded stochastic user simulator, and has no independent reproduction identified. The survey itself was unregistered and conducted by a single reviewer without an inter-rater check, so it is useful field orientation rather than a settled comparative assessment.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report a PRISMA 2020 rapid review of 38 English-language studies published from 2022 to 2026, searched across six databases with eight primary search strings and supplementary searches covering 22 named systems. They introduce a taxonomy spanning specification, verification, and enforcement.
  • Claimed — Within that review, the authors characterize natural-language-to-formal specification as a major bottleneck and report semantic correctness of 24% to 35% in the included translation work. They describe runtime monitoring as the most mature enforcement cluster, while emphasizing that it does not provide complete task-level safety guarantees.
  • Claimed — The cited verifier-tax preprint reports, on tau-bench Airline/Retail tasks with GPT-OSS-20B and GLM-4-9B, that per-proposal interception reached 94.1% in one GPT-OSS-20B Retail setting while safe-success rates were below 5% in most model/architecture/domain settings (one GLM-4-9B Airline/Triad cell was 5.3%). It reports 2.0–2.8× agent-token inflation for its Triad architectures. These are author-reported, benchmark-specific results from 2,970 trajectories under model-dependent 15/30-turn caps and an unseeded stochastic Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct user simulator; there is no independent reproduction identified.
  • Demonstrated — The authors disclose that the review was not preregistered and was conducted by one reviewer without an inter-rater reliability check; they identify selection and extraction bias as threats to validity.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares a fragmented literature with its own three-stage taxonomy; it does not present a matched experimental baseline or a new agent-system benchmark.

Limitations

  • This is an author-reported literature synthesis, not an independent evaluation of safety systems; its quantitative summaries inherit the settings and methods of the included papers.
  • The verifier-tax result comes from one study, has Low GRADE certainty in the review, and has no independent replication identified. Its 94.1% interception result is per proposed action in one setting, not a rate of tasks made safe; do not generalize it or the below-5% safe-success figures beyond the tested tau-bench tasks and models.
  • The review uses a single reviewer, has no inter-rater reliability measure, was not preregistered, and includes English-language full-text studies only.
  • The paper's June 2026 search window means it cannot establish the state of work published later.
  • The underlying verifier-tax experiment uses model-dependent 15/30-turn caps and an unseeded stochastic user simulator; the arXiv record links no code or data repository. Its token overhead is specific to the reported Triad architecture and tokenizer accounting.

What we still don't know

  • The review's inclusion and extraction decisions have no second-reviewer reliability check; the protocol was not preregistered, and the authors acknowledge possible selection and extraction bias.
  • The verifier-tax result is drawn from one study, rated Low certainty by the survey authors, and has no independent replication identified. Its underlying experiment used two models, Airline/Retail tau-bench tasks, unequal 15/30-turn caps, an unseeded stochastic user simulator, and no code/data repository linked from the arXiv record.
  • The review was submitted on June 22, 2026; it does not account for work that appeared after its search cutoff or establish that its taxonomy remains exhaustive.

Why it matters

The review puts specification quality and task-level outcomes alongside verifier strength when assessing agent safety. This helps prevent action-block rates from being treated as a substitute for safe task completion, while its own evidence and review-method limits remain material.
