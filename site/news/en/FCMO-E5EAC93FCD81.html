A preprint reports measurable handshake overhead across three AI agent protocols

An arXiv preprint reports a phase-decomposed benchmark of MCP, A2A and ACP agent-startup overhead using their official Python SDKs. For MCP revision 2026-07-28, the official specification removed the initialize/initialized exchange and protocol sessions; the paper uses “handshake” operationally for discovery through task readiness, including server/discover. In its stated setup, the authors report 50 runs per cell and, under a userspace-emulated 50 ms application-layer RTT, median cold-start times of 230 ms for legacy MCP, 122 ms for modern MCP, 61.0 ms for A2A and 62.1 ms for ACP. The paper also reports capability-metadata costs of 24–107 tokens per capability. These are author-reported benchmark results from minimal echo agents, not independent reproduction or measured production workloads. The paper's HTML says experiments ran on 10 Aug 2026, while the linked repository README says the paper's measurements were taken on 30 Sep; the arXiv v1 submission history says 12 Aug even though arXiv's cs.AI new-submission list showed the record on 23 Oct. This chronology and raw-artifact binding remain unresolved.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The preprint reports a benchmark of MCP (legacy and modern), A2A and ACP using official Python SDKs, with N=50 measured runs per protocol/scenario/network cell and minimal echo counterparts.
  • Claimed — Under the paper's userspace-emulated 50 ms application-layer RTT, the authors report median cold-start times of 230 ms for legacy MCP, 122 ms for modern MCP, 61.0 ms for A2A and 62.1 ms for ACP. The paper says TCP and TLS setup were excluded; its relay does not model packet-level network emulation.
  • Claimed — The authors report 24–107 tokens per advertised capability when a language-model-driven initiator reads counterpart metadata, and estimate that 200 capabilities consume 11% of a 200,000-token context window under their tokenizer accounting.
  • Disputed — The experiment chronology is inconsistent across the primary-linked materials: arXiv HTML v1 dates experiments to 10 Aug 2026 and arXiv submission history says 12 Aug, while the linked repository README says measurements in the paper were taken 30 Sep 2026. The cs.AI recent-submission page listed the same arXiv identifier on 23 Oct 2026.
  • Not established — Independent reproduction, production-scale latency or cost impact, and performance over real networks including TCP/TLS setup are not established by this review.
  • Inferred — For MCP revision 2026-07-28, the official specification removes the initialize/initialized exchange and protocol sessions, adding server/discover and cacheable list/read results. The paper-defined modern-MCP cold-start measure therefore covers discovery and capability listing through task readiness, rather than the retired session handshake.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper's reported 50 ms application-layer RTT condition, legacy MCP is compared with modern MCP, A2A and ACP cold-start medians. The comparison is author-reported, uses a non-packet-level relay and excludes TCP/TLS setup; no independent or production baseline is established.

Limitations

  • Results are claims made in one preprint; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The benchmark uses minimal echo counterparts and selected SDK/specification versions rather than representative deployed agents.
  • The 50 ms condition is userspace application-layer delay emulation; TCP/TLS setup is excluded, and packetization is not faithfully modeled.
  • Token estimates depend on the selected tokenizer and assumed model pricing.
  • Experiment and artifact chronology conflicts across arXiv metadata and the author-linked repository README.
  • The paper uses “handshake” as a task-readiness measure; the official MCP 2026-07-28 specification removes the initialize/initialized exchange and protocol sessions, so its modern-MCP result concerns capability discovery/listing, not that retired exchange.
  • arXiv HTML v1 says experiments ran on 10 Aug 2026; the linked repository README says measurements in the paper were taken 30 Sep 2026. The arXiv v1 history says submitted 12 Aug, while the cs.AI new-submission page showed arXiv:2610.30151 in its 23 Oct 2026 batch. These dates do not establish which artifact version was measured.

What we still don't know

  • The paper's experiment date, arXiv v1 submission date, 23 Oct new-submission listing and linked README's 30 Sep measurement date do not reconcile. GitHub raw metadata and a pinned commit were inaccessible in this activation.
  • The 50 ms condition delays application-layer chunks in a userspace relay and excludes TCP/TLS setup; the benchmark uses minimal echo counterparts and specific SDK versions.
  • No independent reproduction of the paper's reported measurements was found or performed.

Why it matters

Handshake round trips and capability metadata may be material in short-lived or remote agent interactions, where setup can consume latency and context before task execution. The paper makes a bounded measurement claim worth tracking, but its minimal agents, userspace delay emulation, excluded TCP/TLS setup and unresolved experiment chronology limit transfer to real deployments.
