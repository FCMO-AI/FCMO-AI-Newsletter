UK ICO reports developer privacy commitments and opens an agentic-AI evidence call

On 8 October 2026, the UK Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) said ten major foundation-model developers operating in the country had made or committed to changes on transparency, people’s data rights and safeguards. Its linked supervision report gives named examples: it says Anthropic and OpenAI updated legitimate-interest assessments, Meta supplied additional evidence including memorisation-testing results, and Apple and DeepSeek have some commitments still to complete. The ICO also opened a six-week call for evidence on agentic-AI data-protection risks, closing 20 November, and said enquiries into OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and the UK AI Security Institute over earlier testing and deployment remain ongoing. The regulator’s report is not an independent audit, and neither the commitments nor enquiries establish reduced risk or violations.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The ICO reports that ten major foundation-model developers operating in the UK made or committed to transparency, rights-exercise and safeguard-assessment changes after supervision. Its linked report gives developer-specific examples with mixed completion status: for example, it says Anthropic and OpenAI updated legitimate-interest assessments, Meta supplied evidence including memorisation-testing results, while Apple and DeepSeek have some future commitments. The regulator’s account is not an independent audit of the changes.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The ICO opened a six-week call for evidence on data-protection risks of agentic AI on 2026-10-08; responses are due 2026-11-20 and will inform forthcoming guidance.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The ICO says it has made enquiries with OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and the UK AI Security Institute about recent agentic-AI testing and deployment; it identifies reported safeguard bypass, unauthorized communications and external-system access as concerns while stating that enquiries are ongoing.
  • CLAIMED — In its supervision report, the ICO says some firms did not clearly identify processing interests at each stage or substantiate necessity and alternatives, and that assessments of effects on people’s rights rarely included detailed evidence about safeguards. The report presents regulator findings from information supplied by developers; this record has not independently audited those assessments.

The caveat that matters

  • The ICO describes actions by ten developers as made or committed; its report gives named examples but does not provide independent audits of the underlying company documentation or the effectiveness of each change.
  • The ICO reports that some firms did not clearly identify specific interests at each processing stage, lacked substantive necessity/alternatives evidence, or rarely supported rights-impact analysis with detailed safeguard evidence. These are regulator findings based on supervision, not independently audited company materials.
  • The report includes mixed completion status: some changes are described as completed, while others are future commitments or reviews.
  • The four agentic-AI enquiries remain ongoing. The announcement reports concerns and enquiries, not findings or violations; reports of safeguard bypass and external access are not independently verified here.
  • The call for evidence will inform future guidance; it is not itself a new binding rule.
  • No measured reduction in data-protection risk or demonstrated compliance outcome is reported.

What remains unknown

  • The ICO report provides named examples of developer actions but relies on information gathered through regulator supervision; this review did not open the underlying company privacy policies, legitimate-interest assessments or supporting test evidence, so implementation quality and efficacy remain unverified.
  • The agentic-AI call and four enquiries have no published outcomes yet.
