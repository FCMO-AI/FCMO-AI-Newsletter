Memento 3 uses a replay-checked world model to learn across unfamiliar environments

An Oct 8 arXiv preprint presents Memento 3, which stores an agent’s current environment hypotheses in a natural-language rulebook and compiles them into executable code for planning. A fixed Claude Opus 5 agent revises the rulebook and program from prediction errors; a new program must satisfy an LLM fidelity check and exactly replay all observed transitions before it is used. The authors report that this setup cleared every level in all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games, with mean RHAE 100 and 7,518 actions (44% of the human total), compared with their cited strongest system at RHAE 99 and 8,347 actions. This is an author-reported result, not an independent reproduction or evidence of general recursive self-improvement.

What actually changed

An Oct 8 arXiv preprint presents Memento 3, which stores an agent’s current environment hypotheses in a natural-language rulebook and compiles them into executable code for planning. A fixed Claude Opus 5 agent revises the rulebook and program from prediction errors; a new program must satisfy an LLM fidelity check and exactly replay all observed transitions before it is used. The authors report that this setup cleared every level in all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games, with mean RHAE 100 and 7,518 actions (44% of the human total), compared with their cited strongest system at RHAE 99 and 8,347 actions. This is an author-reported result, not an independent reproduction or evidence of general recursive self-improvement.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — arXiv lists Memento 3 as submitted on October 8, 2026, with a full preprint available.
  • CLAIMED — The authors describe a fixed-LLM agent that maintains a natural-language rulebook and executable world model, revises them from interaction errors, and checks proposed code for rulebook fidelity and exact replay of recorded transitions before planning with it.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report clearing every level of all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games with RHAE 100.0 and 7,518 total actions, or 44% of the human total; they report the closest compared system at RHAE 99.0 and 8,347 actions.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper says exact replay cannot distinguish among multiple world models that fit the same observed history, and cautions that public-set completion alone cannot isolate an architecture’s contribution.

Strongest baseline

For ARC-AGI-3, the authors' closest cited comparison is baseline1, RHAE 99.0 and 8,347 actions versus Memento 3 at 100.0 and 7,518 actions. They state that baseline scores come from official ARC Prize scorecards. This is a small margin on a saturated public set, not a 59.3-point gain over the strongest available public system; that larger difference is against Claude Opus 5 with the Standard harness.

What remains unknown

  • The paper reports results for itself and takes comparator scores from official ARC Prize scorecards; the research desk has not opened or independently reconstructed the scorecards or run logs.
  • The public comparison does not isolate the contribution of executable world models from the model, harness, prompts or inference budget; the strongest cited score is already 99.0 RHAE.
  • The paper does not provide an independently checked end-to-end token, wall-clock or monetary cost comparison for the public-game results.

FCMO Lens

A persistent world model that can be revised from interaction and checked against observed transitions is a concrete design for agents facing unfamiliar environments. The paper reports completion and action-efficiency gains over existing public systems, but the closest system is already near the benchmark ceiling. The result matters as an agent architecture and evaluation lead; it does not establish general autonomous learning beyond these settings.
