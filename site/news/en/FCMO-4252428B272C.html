Together AI reports a continual agent-driven system for optimizing AI inference

Together AI Research's 10 November 2026 preprint describes Together Compiler (TKCC), a continual agent-driven system for optimizing inference deployments as models and traffic change. It combines a persistent engine that hot-reloads candidate forward passes, fast and higher-fidelity measurement tiers, and a cost model for selecting parallelism configurations. The authors report up to 2.6× higher throughput than leading open configurations on selected SemiAnalysis InferenceX model/hardware settings, within 24 hours and at most two nodes per model; they also report 252× more agent experiments than a restart-per-measurement approach. These are first-party results, not independently reproduced here, and the paper's production-fleet volume statement is likewise unverified.

  • Claimed — The authors report that TKCC achieved up to 2.6× higher tokens per second than leading open configurations on the SemiAnalysis InferenceX benchmark across selected frontier models and hardware platforms, in under 24 hours using at most two nodes per model.   • Claimed — Together AI states that TKCC continually compiles its production fleet, which serves trillions of tokens per day.

Strongest baseline

For its headline frontier-model claim, the paper compares against top published open InferenceX engine configurations available as of 27 October 2026, using their parallelism strategy as the initial configuration. It reports different model/hardware/concurrency regimes; up-to-2.6× is the maximum, not the typical result.

Limitations

  • All system results and production-fleet claims reviewed here come from the Together AI authors; independent reproduction was not found.
  • The headline 2.6× is a maximum across selected model/hardware/concurrency settings and should not be read as a typical or universal speedup.
  • The preprint describes a production system but did not expose a public TKCC implementation in the reviewed source.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark results and 252× experiment-throughput comparison are reported by the system's authors; the review found no independent reproduction.
  • The reviewed preprint does not provide a public TKCC source implementation, preventing direct code and end-to-end protocol audit in this activation.
  • The paper's claim that TKCC serves trillions of tokens daily is a first-party production statement without independent operational measurement.

Why it matters

Inference throughput and optimization labor are major determinants of AI serving cost and deployment capacity. If the reported gains generalize beyond Together AI's selected models, hardware and workloads, a continual optimization loop could change how fast serving stacks adapt to model and traffic shifts. The current evidence is a detailed but first-party preprint; it does not establish independent performance or general deployment impact.
