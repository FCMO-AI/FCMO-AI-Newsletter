Google announces Gemini 4 Carbon with vendor-reported cybersecurity and long-horizon results

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Oct 28, 2026, with initial access for a subset of cyber defenders and broader access described as forthcoming. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Oct 28, 2026, saying initial access would go to a subset of cyber defenders and broader developer, business and consumer access would follow later. The announcement lists introductory API prices of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with $4 and $20 prices after the introductory period. The current Gemini API pricing page lists those dated prices for Argon but did not show a Carbon SKU during this review; Carbon access and billing were not verified.
  • Claimed — Google reports that Gemini 4 Carbon scored 59 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8% on ExploitGym and 71.0% on CWE-bench. At this review, Artificial Analysis independently lists Carbon (high) at Index score 59, and CWE-bench lists 71% deterministic pass@1 and 81% pass@4 (71%/83% on its three-judge panel). SEC-bench Pro’s visible leaderboard does not list Carbon; ExploitGym’s linked repository was inaccessible in this review. No raw Carbon outputs were audited or benchmarks rerun.
  • Claimed — Google reports internal improvements over Argon in secure-code generation and alignment-related measures, including more than 2x uplift on an internal secure-code benchmark, nearly 80% fewer high-severity monitoring alerts in internal live coding, about 2x fewer unsupported claims, and 26% lower overreach with 29% lower under-service. These remain Google-reported internal measurements, not independently validated here.
  • Not established — The reviewed sources establish public leaderboard entries for Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and CWE-bench, but do not establish raw outputs, full evaluation reproducibility, matched conditions across all claimed benchmarks, broader customer outcomes, deployment safety or general reliability. SEC-bench Pro does not currently list Carbon; ExploitGym protocol access failed.

Strongest baseline

CWE-bench lists Carbon at 71% deterministic pass@1 versus Gemini 4 Argon 68% under its high-reasoning Antigravity condition; Carbon’s cost is not reported, while Argon is listed at $6.63 per rollout. The public methodology specifies four one-hour rollouts per each of 120 held-out tasks; the task set is private. This is a benchmark-owner comparison, not a matched evaluation independently rerun by the research desk. SEC-bench Pro’s visible leaderboard does not list Carbon.

Why it matters

A model that combines long-running coding capability with strong vulnerability discovery, validation and repair could affect cyber-defense workflows and frontier-model evaluation. The announcement makes the result worth tracking, while its vendor-reported metrics, restricted initial access and lack of independent reproduction limit what can be concluded about actual capability or deployment impact.
