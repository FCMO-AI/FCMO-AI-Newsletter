Brazil ties data-center tax relief to clean power, local capacity and R&D commitments

What actually changed

Brazil's Lei 15.504/2026 creates Redata, a five-year tax-incentive regime for qualifying data-center installation, modernization and expansion, including AI training and inference. Applicants must make at least 10% of the effective processing, storage and data-treatment supply installed with Redata benefits available to Brazil's domestic market; the law calculates that commitment using domestic gross revenue as a share of total gross revenue from the benefited services, with implementation details left to regulation. Applicants must also meet renewable or low-emission electricity and sustainability requirements, keep cooling WUE at or below 0.05 L/kWh, and invest 2% of incentivized ICT-product purchases in local digital-economy R&D. The law suspends specified taxes on eligible ICT equipment; PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions currently run through 2026-12-31 under the tax-transition clause.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Lei 15.504/2026 establishes Redata for projects installing, modernizing or expanding data-center services in Brazil; the statutory definition expressly includes cloud, high-performance computing and AI model training and inference.
  • DEMONSTRATED — To qualify, applicants commit to make at least 10% of the effective processing, storage and data-treatment supply installed with Redata benefits available to the domestic market. The statute measures domestic allocation using annual gross revenue from domestic-market services divided by total gross revenue from the benefited services; free transfers to eligible ICTs or public bodies may count using a regulatory multiplier, and the 10% availability commitment may instead be replaced by an additional 10% investment in eligible R&D. Applicants must also meet sustainability rules; supply all contracted electricity from renewable or low-emission generation via contracts or self-production; report annual cooling WUE no higher than 0.05 L/kWh; and invest 2% of benefited ICT-product purchases in eligible Brazilian digital-economy R&D. Certain local-service and R&D commitments are reduced by 20% for facilities in the North, Northeast and Center-West.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The law suspends PIS/Pasep, Cofins, IPI and import duty on specified qualifying ICT equipment for data-center fixed assets. Import duty relief is limited to equipment without an equivalent domestic product; the regime lasts five years, while PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions are stated to produce effects through 2026-12-31 under the tax-transition clause.
  • DEMONSTRATED — For installations in the North, Northeast and Center-West, the statutory domestic-service and baseline R&D commitments are reduced by 20%; at least 40% of the specified R&D-program resources must be applied to those regions.
  • CLAIMED — The Ministry of Finance says roughly 60% of Brazilian data are stored outside Brazil and frames Redata as a response to digital-service dependence; this is a ministry-reported rationale, not an independently validated estimate in this record.

Strongest baseline

The new statute formalizes a dedicated Redata regime rather than generic data-center incentives; its commitments and qualifying equipment lists depend on regulation.

The caveat that matters

  • The government’s 60% foreign-hosted-data figure is retained only as a first-party rationale, not independently verified prevalence evidence.
  • Statutory eligibility does not show which companies will receive authorization or whether projects will be built.
  • Several commitments, equipment lists and measuring rules await executive regulation; do not treat announced benefits as realized savings.
  • The five-year regime is subject to the transition clause: PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions currently run through 2026-12-31.

What remains unknown

  • Executive regulations and equipment lists, authorized projects, actual tax expenditure, investment, domestic capacity delivered, energy and water compliance, and regional distribution are not yet verified.

FCMO Lens

The regime trades conditional import and equipment-tax relief for domestic compute availability, clean-energy sourcing, water efficiency and local innovation spending. These terms can affect data-center location and project economics, but actual tax expenditure, approvals, construction and compliance outcomes remain unmeasured.
