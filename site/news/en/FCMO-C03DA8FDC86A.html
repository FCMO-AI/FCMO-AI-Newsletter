Google releases PARDIG global precipitation datasets with author-reported multi-network evaluation

Google DeepMind and Google Research released PARDIG, an experimental global precipitation-estimation dataset trained on sparse NASA/JAXA GPM spaceborne radar retrievals with satellite imagery and atmospheric-field inputs. What changed

Google DeepMind and Google Research released PARDIG, an experimental global precipitation-estimation dataset trained on sparse NASA/JAXA GPM spaceborne radar retrievals with satellite imagery and atmospheric-field inputs. It provides 0.1° grids every 15 minutes: a near-real-time stream for 2016 onward with roughly 2–8 hours effective latency, and a historical variant from 1999–2023. The paper reports better precipitation-detection scores than several global products across three regional radar networks and 36,678 quality-controlled stations, but these results have not been independently reproduced here. The product is openly licensed under CC BY 4.0 and explicitly is not an official warning product.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Google DeepMind and Google Research announced and documented two publicly accessible experimental PARDIG precipitation datasets: PARDIG-NRT for 2016 onward and PARDIG-LR for 1999–2023, at 0.1° spatial and 15-minute temporal resolution. Google documents CC BY 4.0 licensing and describes the near-real-time stream as updating in six-hour batches with approximately 2–8 hours effective latency.
  • Claimed — The paper authors report that PARDIG-NRT scores above IMERG V07, MSWEP V3, ERA5 and HRES across analyzed precipitation thresholds and regions, using radar composites over the contiguous United States, Europe and Japan and 36,678 quality-controlled surface stations. The authors report their largest relative gains at heavier precipitation rates; the research desk has not independently recomputed these comparisons.
  • Claimed — The authors report that a WeatherNext 3 precipitation head trained on an earlier but closely similar PARDIG version improved forecast scores against independent radar observations relative to the model’s IMERG-trained head; at a six-hour example, reported CRPS was approximately 15% lower. This is a paper-reported comparison, not an independently reproduced forecast result.
  • Demonstrated — Google labels PARDIG experimental and says it is not intended, validated or approved for real-world warnings. The paper also reports weaker orographic performance at some high-elevation sites, an Alpine seasonal bias, and unconstrained extrapolation at polar latitudes.
  • Not established — The sources do not establish independent performance across all regions and regimes, including polar precipitation, complex mountain terrain, or operational warning use. The 1999–2007 historical variant also has a reported distribution shift associated with sparser early satellite observations.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares against NASA IMERG Early/Final, MSWEP V3 gauge-corrected, ERA5 and ECMWF HRES using three regional radar products and station gauges. For the reported six-hour WeatherNext 3 example, the comparison is between model heads trained on PARDIG and IMERG targets. the research desk did not independently audit or reproduce these baselines.

Limitations

  • All headline model-performance and downstream forecast comparisons are paper-author-reported; the research desk did not independently recompute them.
  • Ground-based validation covers three regional radar networks and a filtered station set; it does not establish uniform skill at every global location.
  • The paper reports occasional station-level deficits at high elevations and underestimation of wintertime versus summertime precipitation in the European Alps.
  • Polar estimates rely on unconstrained extrapolation because GPM coverage ends near 68° north/south; use there requires particular caution.
  • The historical PARDIG-LR record has a distributional shift before 2008 associated with sparser early satellite observations.
  • The official catalog calls PARDIG experimental and not intended, validated or approved for real-world warnings.

What we still don't know

  • No independent team has reproduced the paper’s cross-product comparisons using the released PARDIG data and evaluation protocol in this activation.
  • Transfer to polar latitudes, complex mountain terrain, sparsely gauged regions and consequential extreme events is not established by the reported evaluation.
  • The paper reports a distribution shift in PARDIG-LR prior to 2008; continuity with the later NRT record is not established for that early period.

Why it matters

A licensed global precipitation record at 15-minute cadence and roughly 10 km grid spacing can support weather, hydrology and climate research, especially where ground measurements are sparse. If the reported comparisons hold, its spaceborne-radar training and dense outputs may improve precipitation analysis and downstream forecasting. The evidence remains a first-party paper and data release, with regional coverage limits and important polar, mountain and early-record caveats.
