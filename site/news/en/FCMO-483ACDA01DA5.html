What changed

On 2026-10-30, OpenAI, Google and Microsoft announced plans or support for a shared robots.txt signal for website operators to express preferences about using content for generative-AI training. Anthropic draft author Kevin Kelley separately reported that Anthropic had started implementing the signal, while saying rollout across services would take time. The mechanism is described in an individual IETF Internet-Draft that is not endorsed by IETF and has no formal standing in its standards process. No provider conformance, publisher outcome, or resulting change in content collection or training data has been observed.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — On 30 October 2026, OpenAI and Google announced plans to honor a shared robots.txt AI-training preference; Microsoft also announced support and says its preference will begin taking effect as it crawls from 1 February 2027.
  • Demonstrated — The cited individual draft defines the __usage_ai-training policy token in ordinary robots.txt groups, allowing path-specific preferences about use for generative-AI training while leaving existing crawler rules unchanged.
  • Demonstrated — The IETF Datatracker identifies the document as an active individual Internet-Draft and says it is not endorsed by IETF and has no formal standing in the IETF standards process; Google and Microsoft both describe standardization work as continuing.
  • Not established — The inspected sources do not establish live implementation or conformance across providers, legal enforceability, publisher outcomes, or changes to content collection or training data.
  • Claimed — The individual draft author, writing to the IETF AI Control working group, reports that Anthropic has started implementing support for the signal; he says rollout across Anthropic services will take time. This activation did not independently observe that implementation or test crawler behavior.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI and Google describe separate provider-specific rules as the prior publisher burden. Microsoft adds that its own page-level grounding choices remain distinct. No measured baseline for publisher effort, provider conformance, or outcomes was inspected.

Limitations

  • The mechanism is specified in an individual Internet-Draft; the IETF Datatracker says it is not endorsed by IETF and has no formal standing in the standards process.
  • OpenAI says implementation is planned in the coming months; Google targets support in 2027; Microsoft says the preference begins taking effect as it crawls from 1 February 2027. These announcements are not observed implementations.
  • Microsoft separately announced page-level training and grounding controls; these product-specific controls are not the same as the shared robots.txt preference.
  • The policy token communicates a usage preference, not a crawling directive. No legal enforceability, provider conformance, changed data use, or publisher outcome was established.
  • Anthropic implementation is reported by the individual draft author, who is affiliated with Anthropic; no public service-level rollout detail, implementation artifact or crawler behavior was independently inspected.

What we still don't know

  • OpenAI, Google and Microsoft announced future support with different timelines; Anthropic draft author Kevin Kelley reports that implementation has started but rollout across its services will take time. No provider crawler behavior or cross-provider conformance test has been independently observed.
  • The cited token definition remains an individual Internet-Draft without IETF endorsement or formal standards-process standing; Google and Microsoft say working-group standardization continues.
  • No evidence links these commitments to changes in crawling, content inclusion, training datasets, grounding use, publisher traffic, or other publisher outcomes.

Why it matters

If implemented as announced, a shared preference could reduce the need for website operators to maintain separate training-use rules for each participating provider. Microsoft says training and grounding choices are separate; it warns that restricting grounding reduces visibility in AI-generated experiences, while neither choice guarantees inclusion, traffic, or payment. This exposes a potential discoverability trade-off for publishers, but no measured traffic or revenue effect is available. Microsoft has stated a 2027-02-01 start as pages are crawled, while OpenAI and Google give broader future timelines. These are provider commitments, not evidence of present compliance, legal enforceability, or changed data practices.
