OpenAI, Google and Microsoft announce support for a shared robots.txt AI-training preference

On 30 October 2026, OpenAI and Google announced plans to honor a shared robots.txt signal for website operators to express preferences about using content for generative-AI training. What changed

On 30 October 2026, OpenAI and Google announced plans to honor a shared robots.txt signal for website operators to express preferences about using content for generative-AI training. Microsoft also announced support and says its shared training preference will begin taking effect as it crawls from 1 February 2027. The mechanism is described in an individual IETF Internet-Draft, which is not endorsed by IETF and has no formal standing in its standards process. No live implementation, cross-provider conformance, or change in content collection or training data has been observed.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI and Google describe separate provider-specific rules as the prior publisher burden. Microsoft adds that its own page-level grounding choices remain distinct. No measured baseline for publisher effort, provider conformance, or outcomes was inspected.

Limitations

  • The mechanism is specified in an individual Internet-Draft; the IETF Datatracker says it is not endorsed by IETF and has no formal standing in the standards process.
  • OpenAI says implementation is planned in the coming months; Google targets support in 2027; Microsoft says the preference begins taking effect as it crawls from 1 February 2027. These announcements are not observed implementations.
  • Microsoft separately announced page-level training and grounding controls; these product-specific controls are not the same as the shared robots.txt preference.
  • The policy token communicates a usage preference, not a crawling directive. No legal enforceability, provider conformance, changed data use, or publisher outcome was established.

What we still don't know

  • OpenAI, Google and Microsoft announced future support with different timelines; no live crawler behavior or conformance test has been observed. Microsoft says it will honor settings as it crawls from 1 February 2027.
  • The cited token definition remains an individual Internet-Draft without IETF endorsement or formal standards-process standing; Google and Microsoft say working-group standardization continues.
  • No evidence links these commitments to changes in crawling, content inclusion, training datasets, grounding use, publisher traffic, or other publisher outcomes.

Why it matters

If implemented as announced, a shared preference could reduce the need for website operators to maintain separate training-use rules for each participating provider. Microsoft says training and grounding choices are separate; it warns that restricting grounding reduces visibility in AI-generated experiences, while neither choice guarantees inclusion, traffic, or payment. This exposes a potential discoverability trade-off for publishers, but no measured traffic or revenue effect is available. Microsoft has stated a 1 February 2027 start as pages are crawled, while OpenAI and Google give broader future timelines. These are provider commitments, not evidence of present compliance, legal enforceability, or changed data practices.
