OpenAI says it will honor a shared robots.txt preference signal for AI training

OpenAI announced on 30 October 2026 that it will honor a shared robots.txt signal for website operators to express preferences about using their content for generative-AI training, with implementation planned in the coming months. The referenced IETF document is an individual Internet-Draft, not an IETF-endorsed standard.   • Demonstrated — The referenced draft defines the __usage_ai-training policy token in ordinary robots.txt groups, allowing path-specific preferences about use for generative-AI training while leaving existing crawler rules unchanged.
  • Demonstrated — The IETF Datatracker classifies the document as an active individual Internet-Draft and states that it is not endorsed by the IETF and has no formal standing in the IETF standards process.

Limitations

  • The IETF Datatracker explicitly identifies the document as an individual Internet-Draft with no IETF endorsement or formal standing in the standards process.
  • OpenAI says implementation is planned in the coming months; this review found no deployed implementation or conformance test.
  • The token communicates a usage preference. The draft states that it is not a crawling directive; it does not itself establish legal enforceability or guarantee provider compliance.
  • No evidence was inspected showing adoption by other providers, changes to training-data collection, or effects on publishers or model training.

What we still don't know

  • The only inspected implementation commitment is OpenAI's statement that it plans to implement the signal in coming months; no deployed behavior or support by other providers was found.
  • The referenced text is an individual draft with no IETF endorsement or formal standards-process standing; its future working-group status is unknown.
  • No evidence links the announcement or draft to changes in crawling, content inclusion, training datasets, or publisher outcomes.

Why it matters

A common signal could reduce the need for website operators to maintain separate training-preference rules for each provider and make a content-use preference easier to communicate across services. The current evidence establishes an OpenAI commitment and a draft mechanism, not implementation, broad provider uptake, legal force, or changed data practices.
