Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that sequential post-training of Qwen3-8B-Base across math, search, e-commerce and instruction following produced substantial task-dependent forgetting: after e-commerce training, AIME26 fell from 23.33 to 6.04 and Natural Questions from 45.2 to 14.6; the final checkpoint only partly recovered prior task peaks. Baseline sequential results are reported over three independent runs.
  • Claimed — The paper proposes Mixture of Low-Rank Experts (MLE): replay-filtered supervised fine-tuning over specialist trajectories followed by separate RL-trained LoRA experts routed by observable environment context. In the paper’s Qwen3-8B-Base setup, MLE reports stronger combined in-domain and out-of-domain scores than the shared-checkpoint MMOPD, SDFT and model-merging alternatives on several evaluated tasks; it does not dominate every task-specific expert.
  • Not established — The paper says the experiments mainly use Qwen3-8B-Base and a fixed four-stage curriculum; expert routing under ambiguous or changing environment context and within-episode combinations is untested.

Strongest baseline

The final sequential Qwen3-8B-Base checkpoint and the paper’s matched MMOPD, SDFT and model-merging alternatives, with independently trained per-task experts as specialization references.

Limitations

  • The results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • The experiments mainly use Qwen3-8B-Base and one fixed four-stage curriculum; transfer across model families, tasks and stage orders is unestablished.
  • The reported sequential forgetting patterns are task-dependent; consolidation approaches recover different capabilities and introduce tradeoffs rather than uniformly improving all domains.
  • MLE routes based on observable environment context; ambiguous or changing contexts and combining specializations within one episode remain untested.
  • The public paper reports benchmark outcomes, not a production deployment; adapter memory, routing latency and serving economics are not established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or public artifact was inspected for the sequential forgetting analysis or MLE comparisons.
  • The study uses one fixed four-stage order and one base model family; generalization across model families and curricula is unknown.
  • MLE routing assumes observable environment/tool context; ambiguous context, context changes and combined specializations within one episode were not tested, and inference latency/memory tradeoffs remain unmeasured.

Why it matters

If replicated across models and curricula, this work could shift multi-stage agent training from assuming capabilities accumulate to explicitly measuring and protecting skills that later stages can overwrite. Its key design tradeoff is retaining task-specific adapters with context-based routing; the evidence does not yet establish transfer, robust routing or deployment cost.
