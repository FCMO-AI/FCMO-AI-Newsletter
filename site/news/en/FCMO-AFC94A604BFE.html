Evidence-gated authority in language-model review trades fewer corruptions of correct proposals for missed welfare-only repairs

In a September 2026 paper, Fuad Ali compares broad reviewer override with a rule that lets a reviewer replace an upstream proposal only after it supplies a mechanically valid certificate of a binding-constraint violation and a compliant repair. The paper reports paired experiments with Qwen3-8B and Mistral-Small-24B on 96 synthetic spatial-policy tasks and a 96-task portfolio-transfer benchmark. The gate prevents changes to correct proposals by construction, but also bars welfare improvements to compliant, suboptimal proposals. What changed

In a September 2026 paper, Fuad Ali compares broad reviewer override with a rule that lets a reviewer replace an upstream proposal only after it supplies a mechanically valid certificate of a binding-constraint violation and a compliant repair. The paper reports paired experiments with Qwen3-8B and Mistral-Small-24B on 96 synthetic spatial-policy tasks and a 96-task portfolio-transfer benchmark. The gate prevents changes to correct proposals by construction, but also bars welfare improvements to compliant, suboptimal proposals. A prospective retain/replace/escalate interface protects correct proposals for Qwen, which then declines welfare-only repairs; Mistral still replaces and corrupts all 48 correct proposals in that test. These are author-reported results from quantized local models and structured synthetic tasks, not evidence of deployment prevalence or a model-independent safety guarantee.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The paper compares broad reviewer override with evidence-gated authority that permits replacement only after a mechanically checked binding-constraint violation and compliant repair.
  • Claimed — On 96 synthetic spatial-policy tasks, the paper reports that broad review changed 85 of 96 correct proposals to non-optimal alternatives for each model; the evidence gate retained all correct proposals and gave an 88.5 percentage-point exact-optimality effect in that condition, while forgoing some repairs to compliant but suboptimal proposals.
  • Claimed — On a 96-task portfolio-transfer benchmark, the paper reports that the evidence gate protected correct proposals but substantially reduced exact-welfare repairs of compliant suboptimal proposals; the reported magnitudes differed between Qwen3-8B and Mistral-Small-24B.
  • Claimed — In a prospective retain/replace/escalate interface test on 48 tasks per proposal state, Qwen retained all 48 correct and all 48 compliant-suboptimal proposals, while Mistral replaced and corrupted all 48 correct proposals; therefore the interface was not a model-independent safeguard in this test.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish real-world deployment prevalence, performance with frontier APIs or human reviewers, or that evidence-gated review is universally safer or welfare-optimal.

Strongest baseline

Paired broad-override condition with the same fixed reviewer output is the primary comparator. The action-menu comparison changes the prompt and response and is interpreted separately.

Limitations

  • All numeric outcomes are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced in this record.
  • Only two quantized open-weight models were tested; results do not establish behavior for frontier APIs, human reviewers or heterogeneous teams.
  • Tasks use structured seven-option synthetic tables with mechanically known objectives; transfer to open-ended or contested real decisions is not established.
  • Natural proposal-state frequencies come from one prompt family and benchmark, not deployment prevalence.
  • The action-menu test changes the prompt and reviewer output; it does not isolate authorization while holding reviewer behavior fixed.

What we still don't know

  • No independent recomputation or reproduction of the reported task outcomes and confidence intervals was performed in this activation.
  • The experiments do not measure deployment prevalence of correct, violating and compliant-suboptimal upstream proposals.
  • The study does not establish effects with frontier APIs, human reviewers, open-ended actions or contested constraints.

Why it matters

The study makes reviewer authority a measurable system design choice: the same review output can repair violations or corrupt correct actions depending on which changes are allowed to bind. Its reported results expose a correction–corruption tradeoff that depends on model behavior, interface and upstream proposal states. The paper does not establish which rule is best in deployed systems; evidence-gate checks, task distributions and human escalation require separate evaluation.
