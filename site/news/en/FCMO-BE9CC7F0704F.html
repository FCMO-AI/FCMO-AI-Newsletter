UK government accepts all 44 recommendations on AI healthcare regulation and opens AI Airlock Phase 3

On 6 October 2026, the UK government published its response to the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare and accepted all 44 recommendations. It sets a one-year delivery focus on proportionate lifecycle regulation, post-market assurance, system readiness, transparency and predictable regulatory pathways. The MHRA says it intends to issue draft guidance on Predetermined Change Control Plans by December 2026 and launch a consultation on device qualification and classification by Spring 2027; most other timelines are also due by Spring 2027. The MHRA opened applications for AI Airlock Phase 3, focused on post-market surveillance and lifecycle regulation, funded through April 2029. The programme charges no application or participation fee, but selected candidates are expected to pay for their own studies, testing work and relevant data access; each bespoke engagement is generally scoped for 6–12 months. This is a government commitment and sandbox launch, not enactment of new statutory requirements or evidence of improved patient outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The September 10, 2026 National Commission report was an independent, non-statutory advisory set of recommendations. The October 6 government response changes their status to accepted government commitments while leaving most implementation details and any legislative reform to future work.

The caveat that matters

  • Acceptance of recommendations is not the same as enactment or implementation; most delivery detail is scheduled for later guidance, consultation or a Spring 2027 roadmap.
  • The response contains commitments and policy intentions, not measured evidence of improved safety, clinical outcomes, AI adoption, innovation or economic growth.
  • The three-year additional AI Airlock funding is stated without an amount in the inspected policy response.
  • The response is UK-specific and applies to healthcare and medical-device regulation, not general-purpose AI across sectors.
  • Phase 3 has no application or participation fee, but participating candidates are expected to fund their own studies, testing delivery and relevant data access; the notice does not establish which candidates can afford those costs or the programme’s distributional effect.

What remains unknown

  • Implementation detail and legal status of the accepted recommendations are not yet available; most items are assigned future guidance or consultation milestones.
  • The application notice specifies Phase 3 funding through April 2029 and that candidates finance their own testing/data access, but gives no amount of programme funding or results of the post-market surveillance work.
  • No measured clinical, safety, adoption or economic effect is supplied for the policy package.

FCMO Lens

If implemented, the response could materially change evidence, version-control, post-market monitoring and market-entry expectations for AI-enabled medical devices across the UK, including products built on general-purpose models. The near-term consequence is a clearer implementation direction and a testing channel funded through April 2029. Participation is free, but applicants bear study, testing and data-access costs, so the programme does not itself establish that smaller developers face lower deployment costs. Most requirements remain future work, and economic or safety effects are not yet demonstrated.
