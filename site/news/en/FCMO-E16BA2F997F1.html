COMPASS reports decentralized language-model control for 1,024-agent flocking rollouts

A robotics preprint presents COMPASS, which couples per-agent language-model reasoning states to a learned multi-agent spatial transformer (MAST) that exchanges local neighbor messages and generates both low-level actions and a feedback token. The frozen Qwen3-1.7B configuration is trained on 32- and 64-agent expert rollouts, then evaluated in 30-second flocking rollouts up to 1,024 agents. The authors report mean heading error decreasing from 2.79° at N=8 to 0.34° at N=1,024, with comparisons against a centralized Claude Opus 4.8 controller and a language-only communication ablation. This is an author-reported result on a narrow modeled flocking task; the paper does not report physical robot trials, and independent replication or artifact execution was not performed.

Evidence

  • Claimed — COMPASS couples local language-model reasoning states to a learned multi-agent spatial transformer (MAST), which exchanges multi-hop neighbor information and emits low-level actions plus a feedback token.
  • Claimed — With a frozen Qwen3-1.7B reasoning engine trained on N=32 and N=64 expert rollouts, the authors evaluate 30-second flocking rollouts through N=1,024 agents and report heading error decreasing from 2.79° at N=8 to 0.34° at N=1,024.
  • Claimed — The authors report comparisons with a centralized Claude Opus 4.8 controller and a language-only communication ablation; they report fragmentation and slower movement for the language-only method at N=64 and reduced coordination for the centralized controller as team size grows.
  • Not established — The paper reports modeled flocking rollouts and held-out prompt trials, but does not report physical robot execution, deployment reliability, or transfer beyond the tested flocking and direction-command setup.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares with a centralized Claude Opus 4.8 controller using hand-engineered feedback and a language-only communication ablation; it also reports an expert-oracle comparison. The bounded scale results are author-run modeled rollouts, not physical matched trials.

Limitations

  • The paper reports modeled rollouts and does not describe a physical robot trial or hardware deployment.
  • Evidence is limited to flocking and direction-following; it does not establish transfer to manipulation, obstacle-rich navigation or heterogeneous fleets.
  • The 1,024-agent scale result is an author-reported rollout; no independent reproduction or artifact execution was performed.
  • The learned controller is trained on expert rollouts at N=32 and 64; transfer from those modeled training sizes to physical fleet behavior remains untested.

What we still don't know

  • The scale, heading-error and coordination results have not been independently reproduced, and no author artifact was located or executed in this review.
  • Physical robot execution, communication delay/loss, energy cost, safety and failure recovery are not established by the reported modeled flocking rollouts.

Why it matters

If reproduced, the separation of semantic instruction interpretation from local learned coordination may offer a useful architecture for scaling language-guided multi-agent control beyond centralized action generation. The paper reports a large modeled team size, but the evidence is a narrow flocking rollout and does not establish deployment on physical robots or general task transfer.
