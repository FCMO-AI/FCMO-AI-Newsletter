What changed

An arXiv preprint published on 2026-11-05 compares multiple-choice and open-ended versions of WMDP questions on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B. The authors report that models often answer forced-choice hazardous-knowledge questions even when they refuse matched open-ended questions, and present tuned-lens, refusal-direction and attention-head interventions as a mechanistic account. The causal head-ablation result is robust on Llama-3.1-8B but not Qwen3-8B. These are author-reported preprint results; no independent replication was inspected.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv:2611.08639v1 on 2026-11-05, reporting an analysis of multiple-choice versus open-ended WMDP prompts on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B.
  • Claimed — The authors report that forced-choice prompts are almost never refused even on items often refused in open-ended form, with similar MCQ correctness across open-ended refusal groups. They report a mid-layer answer-letter commitment, lower alignment with a refusal direction, and format-dependent suppression across biosecurity, chemistry and cybersecurity items.
  • Claimed — The authors report that ablating selected answer-letter attention heads partially restores refusal on Llama-3.1-8B while layer-matched random-head controls do not; the corresponding causal result did not hold robustly on Qwen3-8B.
  • Not established — This single preprint does not establish that the measured mechanism generalizes across model families, current frontier systems or safety-evaluation formats beyond the tested models and WMDP prompts; independent reproduction was not inspected.

Strongest baseline

Matched open-ended questions and layer-matched random-head ablations; the wording rewrite affects a stated subset of prompts.

Limitations

  • The central empirical results are author-reported in one preprint and have not been independently reproduced in this activation.
  • Open-ended prompts rewrite option-dependent stems for 394/1,273 biosecurity, 176/408 chemistry and 260/1,987 cybersecurity items, so format and wording are not perfectly isolated.
  • The causal head-ablation result is robust on Llama-3.1-8B; Qwen3-8B evidence is weaker and the refused-item samples are small.
  • The paper studies two instruction-tuned models and the WMDP dataset; it does not establish current frontier-model or cross-benchmark generalization.

What we still don't know

  • The WMDP format and mechanism findings are author-reported results from two instruction-tuned models; no independent matched reproduction was inspected.
  • The study does not test current frontier models or establish whether the measured activation mechanism transfers to other architectures and evaluation settings.

Why it matters

If it generalizes, a safety score from multiple-choice hazardous-knowledge questions can reflect knowledge retrieval without measuring whether a model activates refusal in open-ended use. This could change how such evaluations are designed and interpreted; two models, rewritten prompt variants and author-run analyses do not establish a general effect.
