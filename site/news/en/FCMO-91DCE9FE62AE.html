LongChang tests free exploration for evidence-supported discovery in simulated physics

The LongChang preprint reports a benchmark for whether language models can notice unexpected behavior in simulated physical worlds, choose questions and test altered physical laws without assigned research tasks or hints. Across 12 models and 15 fixed-start MuJoCo scenes, the authors report a 16.7% discovery rate for GPT-6-Astra and 0% for the other 11 models; discovery required both correct law identification and evidence support. In the ordinary-physics control, 36.1% of reports were rejected for false anomalies or unsupported explanations. GPT-6.1-Sol judged reports and GPT-6-Astra reviewed apparatus and generated/reviewed evolved scenes. These are author-reported results in a bounded simulation, not evidence of general scientific autonomy or real-world discovery.

What changed

The LongChang preprint reports a benchmark for whether language models can notice unexpected behavior in simulated physical worlds, choose questions and test altered physical laws without assigned research tasks or hints. Across 12 models and 15 fixed-start MuJoCo scenes, the authors report a 16.7% discovery rate for GPT-6-Astra and 0% for the other 11 models; discovery required both correct law identification and evidence support. In the ordinary-physics control, 36.1% of reports were rejected for false anomalies or unsupported explanations. GPT-6.1-Sol judged reports and GPT-6-Astra reviewed apparatus and generated/reviewed evolved scenes. These are author-reported results in a bounded simulation, not evidence of general scientific autonomy or real-world discovery.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted version 1 of the LongChang preprint to arXiv on October 27, 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report evaluating 12 language models in 15 fixed-start MuJoCo scenes, with three runs per model per scene and up to 100 turns per run, without a research task or hints about altered laws. GPT-6-Astra achieved a 16.7% discovery rate; the other 11 evaluated models scored 0%. Discovery required both correct identification of an altered law and experimental support in the exploration record.
  • Claimed — On the ordinary-physics control scene, the authors report that 36.1% of model reports were rejected for falsely claiming altered laws or giving unsupported explanations. The project page says each model was run three times per scene.
  • Claimed — GPT-6.1-Sol judged claim correctness and evidence support, while GPT-6-Astra reviewed apparatus and generated and reviewed evolved scenes. The paper therefore does not provide an evaluator-independent comparison of the evaluated models.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish autonomous discovery in physical laboratories or open-ended real science, performance beyond its simulated altered-physics tasks, or independent reproduction of its reported model comparisons.

Strongest baseline

The benchmark compares 12 models under shared scene and turn conditions, with three runs per model per scene; it includes a normal-physics false-discovery control and reports controlled conditions for task guidance, turn budget and apparatus access. The paper uses GPT-6.1-Sol as judge and GPT-6-Astra for apparatus and scene-generation/review roles. The linked repository does not currently expose executable source code, and there is no independent reproduction.

Limitations

  • All model-comparison results are author-reported preprint findings and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • The main comparison uses a normal-physics control plus 14 altered-law scenes in fixed-start MuJoCo simulations, with at most 100 turns; the findings do not establish transfer to laboratory experiments or open-ended science.
  • GPT-6.1-Sol judges correctness and evidence support, and GPT-6-Astra reviews apparatus and participates in scene generation/review, creating a model-role dependence in the evaluation pipeline.
  • GPT-6-Astra’s reported 16.7% discovery rate is distinct from its 97.6% evidence-support rate and 19.0% law-correctness rate; supported partial observations do not by themselves count as discovery.
  • The linked public repository README states that source code is not included; its benchmark run examples require an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, an API key, and configured explorer and judge models. No executable code or pinned model/API configuration was available in this inspection.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of LongChang was inspected. The linked public repository currently contains a README and .github assets but states that source code is not included; its example benchmark commands require a configured OpenAI-compatible endpoint, API key, and model names.
  • The paper evaluates altered laws in MuJoCo simulations with explicit ground truth; it does not test open scientific domains whose correct explanations are not pre-specified.

Why it matters

LongChang turns free-form scientific inquiry into a testable benchmark: a system must notice an anomaly, choose a question, devise experiments, and support its conclusion from an exploration record. The reported low discovery rate and normal-control false-discovery rejections show both the promise and difficulty of this narrow task. The simulation and evaluator/model-role limits mean the result does not establish autonomous discovery in physical laboratories or open-ended research.
