What actually changed

Li et al. describe a recursive-in-recursive process that edits an execution harness and trains a task model over three cycles. On their task-ID-held-out scientific-task benchmark, the authors report single-attempt accuracy rising from 42.2% to 73.3%, with 33.3% of cases changing from incorrect to correct and 2.2% from correct to incorrect. The measurements are author-reported benchmark outcomes, not verified scientific discoveries. The linked repository’s maintained preview uses a different task inventory and experiment configuration from the paper case study, so it does not independently validate those values.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The inspected paper describes a three-cycle recursive-in-recursive procedure that edits an execution harness and trains a task model, with a fixed GPT-6 Astra editor helper in the reported case study.
  • CLAIMED — On the paper’s task-ID-held-out benchmark, authors report single-attempt scientific-task accuracy increasing from 42.2% to 73.3%; 33.3% of cases change from incorrect to correct and 2.2% from correct to incorrect, across four task families.
  • CLAIMED — For a standalone harness adaptation experiment with a fixed model, authors report validation accuracy increasing from 31.1% to 51.1% after 24 batches comprising 288 simulated adaptation conversations; a separate fixed-harness pass@4 coverage measure rises from 48.3% to 67.8%.
  • SPECULATIVE — The results establish that ScienceBuddy makes scientific discoveries or broadly improves scientific productivity in real research settings.

Strongest baseline

The reported headline comparison is the initial task-model/harness system at 42.2% single-attempt accuracy versus 73.3% after three co-evolution cycles; this is an author-reported within-study comparison, not an independent benchmark.

The caveat that matters

  • Quantitative results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced.
  • The reported task benchmark covers four task families and measures benchmark task answers rather than verified scientific discoveries or downstream research impact.
  • The paper’s case study uses a fixed GPT-6 Astra editor helper and a Qwen3.5-4B task model; broader model and editor transfer is unknown.
  • The paper’s 895-task collection and reported adaptation protocol are not bound in this review to the currently maintained repository experiment. The repository preview uses a different 715/90/90 inventory and different cycle, search-step and RL-update settings.
  • Validation selection and material-group overlap across train/test described in the repository weaken interpretation of test independence for the maintained preview; those preview results must not be conflated with paper plots.
  • Improved benchmark task performance does not establish that the system made valid discoveries or improved real research outcomes.
  • The paper evaluates task completion and benchmark answers; its results do not establish scientific discoveries or validate the truth of generated research outcomes.
  • The authors’ currently maintained repository preview uses a different task inventory and experiment configuration than the paper case study. The public code inspected therefore does not independently reproduce the reported paper values.

What remains unknown

  • The current public repository documents a maintained preview that differs from the paper case-study configuration. This review did not inspect an exact paper code/config/data snapshot, raw logs or weights that bind the reported 42.2%→73.3% result to an executable artifact.
  • Benchmark task accuracy measures task completion, not whether a system produced a valid scientific discovery or useful result in a live research workflow.
  • All quantitative outcomes reviewed are author-reported; the research desk did not independently execute the system.

FCMO Lens

If replicated on independently authored scientific tasks with outcome checks, co-evolving a research execution harness and its task model could improve task completion. The current evidence establishes a bounded author-run benchmark result; it does not show that the system made scientific discoveries or that the currently maintained repository reproduces the paper case study.
