A controlled agent-population study finds indirect convention shifts can lower tipping thresholds

A September 21 preprint studies how committed minorities can redirect conventions in homogeneous, fully connected naming-game populations of four open-weight LLMs. The authors report that fewer than 10% of single-intermediate paths reduce total committed effort versus a direct transition. In evaluated triplets, allowing a third convention during a single stage produces cost-effective transitions in 15–40% of cases across model populations, compared with 12–23% for isolated two-stage routes. These are results from a controlled model, not observed attacks or evidence about deployed heterogeneous agent networks.

A September 21 preprint studies how committed minorities can redirect conventions in homogeneous, fully connected naming-game populations of four open-weight LLMs. The authors report that fewer than 10% of single-intermediate paths reduce total committed effort versus a direct transition. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The preprint defines and evaluates a repeated naming-game model with homogeneous, fully connected populations instantiated from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-4, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B.
  • Claimed — The authors report that fewer than 10% of single-intermediate paths reduce additive committed effort below the direct-transition requirement; this fraction declines for longer paths.
  • Claimed — Across evaluated model populations, an integrated single-stage approach with a third convention available yields cost-effective transitions in 15–40% of cases, versus 12–23% for isolated two-stage routes over the same triplets.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The results concern a controlled naming-game model with homogeneous populations; the paper does not demonstrate attacks on deployed heterogeneous agent networks or validate real-world intervention success.

Strongest baseline

Direct pairwise convention transition; the comparison also includes isolated multi-stage paths over the same evaluated triplets.

Limitations

  • This is a preprint and the findings are author-reported; no independent reproduction was run here.
  • The model uses homogeneous, fully connected populations and a naming-game coordination task, which narrows transfer to real agent networks.
  • The authors state that fewer than 10% of single-intermediate routes are sub-additive; advantageous routes are the exception in the modeled landscape.
  • No deployed attack, field measurement or validated defense is shown.

What we still don't know

  • The reported model outcomes were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • No evidence in the paper establishes that these convention dynamics occur in heterogeneous, deployed agent populations.

Why it matters

The paper suggests that pairwise tests of an agent population’s resistance may miss paths through intermediate conventions, while also finding those cost-reducing paths are uncommon in its model. If such structure transfers to deployed systems, safety evaluations may need to consider the available convention set and sequences of interventions. No real-world attack or mitigation effectiveness is established.
