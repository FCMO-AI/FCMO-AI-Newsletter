A preprint reports smaller validation-loss gaps for two 4-bit AdamW state schemes, with reproduction still open

An October 8 arXiv preprint introduces ZIP-SR, which chooses stochastic-rounding probabilities in AdamW preconditioner space, and ZE-EDEN, which calibrates a zero-excluding second-moment codebook. The authors report 1.0625 bytes per parameter for persistent optimizer moments versus 8 bytes for FP32 moments, and smaller mean validation-loss gaps to 32-bit AdamW than TorchAO 4-bit AdamW across evaluated GPT- and Llama-style pretraining runs from 130M to 2.7B parameters. What actually changed

An October 8 arXiv preprint introduces ZIP-SR, which chooses stochastic-rounding probabilities in AdamW preconditioner space, and ZE-EDEN, which calibrates a zero-excluding second-moment codebook. The authors report 1.0625 bytes per parameter for persistent optimizer moments versus 8 bytes for FP32 moments, and smaller mean validation-loss gaps to 32-bit AdamW than TorchAO 4-bit AdamW across evaluated GPT- and Llama-style pretraining runs from 130M to 2.7B parameters. At 1.4B parameters, the paper reports the largest gap reduction, 70.1%; at 2.7B the TorchAO run diverged late, so the paper does not give a percentage comparison there. In full-parameter SFT, the two methods report lower validation loss than TorchAO and results close to 32-bit on downstream tasks, without a consistent downstream advantage. These are author-reported preprint results, not independently reproduced.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The arXiv paper defines ZIP-SR, which retains zero in the second-moment codebook and selects stochastic-rounding probabilities in preconditioner space, and ZE-EDEN, which uses a zero-excluding codebook with EDEN block calibration. It links PyTorch implementations and example scripts.
  • CLAIMED — Across the paper’s GPT- and Llama-style pretraining experiments from 130M to 2.7B parameters, the authors report both methods reduce TorchAO 4-bit AdamW’s mean validation-loss gap to 32-bit AdamW at each evaluated size. The largest stated reduction is 70.1% at 1.4B; the 2.7B TorchAO run diverged late and is not assigned a percentage reduction.
  • CLAIMED — For full-parameter supervised fine-tuning, the paper reports lower validation loss than TorchAO 4-bit AdamW and results close to 32-bit AdamW on downstream tasks, without a consistent downstream-task advantage.
  • CLAIMED — The paper calculates persistent moment storage of 1.0625 bytes per parameter for its two 4-bit configurations, versus 8 bytes per parameter for two FP32 moment buffers; this is optimizer-state storage, not total training memory.

Strongest baseline

TorchAO 4-bit AdamW at the same persistent moment-storage bit budget; 32-bit AdamW is the reference for validation-loss gap.

The caveat that matters

  • Results come from one arXiv preprint and were not independently reproduced.
  • Pretraining comparisons use three paired seeds; supervised fine-tuning uses five. Uncertainty and robustness beyond these runs remain open.
  • At 2.7B the TorchAO reference diverged late; the paper reports no percentage gap reduction for that comparison.
  • The 1.0625-versus-8 bytes per parameter calculation covers persistent optimizer moment storage, not parameters, activations, temporary buffers, allocator overhead or total peak job memory.
  • Lower SFT validation loss does not yield a consistent downstream-task advantage in the reported results.
  • No evidence here establishes faster training, lower cost, broad model-family transfer or production adoption.

What remains unknown

  • The reported validation-loss comparisons and storage behavior have not been independently reproduced.
  • Paper storage accounting covers persistent moments, but total peak memory and runtime impact are not established by the reported per-parameter storage ratio.

FCMO Lens

Optimizer moments consume substantial persistent memory during training. If these methods reproduce under matched hardware and training budgets, they could free memory for other training needs at the same optimizer-state bit budget. The paper compares only its evaluated settings; it does not establish that total job memory falls by the same ratio, that training is faster, or that downstream quality improves consistently.
