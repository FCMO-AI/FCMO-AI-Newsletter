What actually changed

The SSD-LLaMA preprint describes an SSD-to-RAM-to-VRAM expert-delivery system with a three-tier cache and CPU–GPU execution placement for MoE inference. On a stated consumer testbed with one RTX 5090, 16 GB host DRAM and a 4 TB NVMe SSD, the authors report running a 2.8T-parameter Kimi-K3 at 1.217 prefill tokens/s and 0.465 decode tokens/s. Section 5.4 reports baseline-specific throughput gains, while the abstract and conclusion give different aggregate ranges; the exact mapping is unresolved. These are paper-reported measurements, not an independent reproduction.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that SSD-LLaMA runs a 2.8T-parameter Kimi-K3 on a consumer testbed with one RTX 5090, 32 GB VRAM, 16 GB host DRAM and a 4 TB NVMe SSD, reaching 1.217 prefill tokens/s and 0.465 decode tokens/s.
  • CLAIMED — For batch-size-1 tests across three evaluated MoE models, Section 5.4 reports prefill throughput gains of 1.90×–3.61× over llama.cpp and 1.06×–2.92× over KTransformers, and decode gains of 1.35×–3.44× over llama.cpp and 1.57×–15.61× over KTransformers; comparisons to Colibri are limited to GLM-5.2.
  • CLAIMED — The paper's abstract and conclusion instead summarize prefill gains as 1.52×–4.19× and decode gains as 2.10×–15.58×. The source does not reconcile these aggregate ranges with the baseline-specific Section 5.4 values, so no single aggregate range is treated as settled here.

Strongest baseline

Section 5.4 compares against llama.cpp and KTransformers across the three stated model families, and Colibri only on GLM-5.2. The paper reports baseline-specific gains but its abstract/conclusion aggregate ranges do not reconcile with those section values.

The caveat that matters

  • All performance results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced; the paper's executable artifact and exact checkpoint revisions were not inspected.
  • The paper reports a 0.465 tokens/s decode rate for 2.8T Kimi-K3; the abstract's greater-than-one-token-per-second phrase should not be read as a decode-rate result.
  • The abstract/conclusion prefill and decode ranges (1.52×–4.19× and 2.10×–15.58×) do not match the baseline-specific ranges reported in Section 5.4; the source does not explain the aggregate mapping.
  • The testbed is one configuration with batch size 1 and two prompt workloads; performance on other SSDs, CPUs, GPU memory configurations, concurrency levels and serving workloads is unknown.
  • The source's cost discussion is an estimate; this review did not verify hardware prices, energy, utilization, service cost or field deployment.
  • Within this same paper, the abstract and conclusion report prefill 1.52×–4.19× and decode 2.10×–15.58×, while Section 5.4 reports different baseline-specific ranges (including 1.06×–3.61× prefill and 1.35×–15.61× decode across its named comparisons). The difference may reflect a different aggregation or comparison set, but the paper does not resolve it.

What remains unknown

  • The Kimi-K3 rates and baseline comparisons have not been independently reproduced, and implementation/checkpoint correspondence has not been audited.
  • The abstract/conclusion aggregate speedup ranges differ from the baseline-specific ranges in Section 5.4; the comparison set and aggregation are not reconciled.

FCMO Lens

SSD-backed expert delivery may lower the hardware capacity needed to load large MoE models locally, which could change where inference is feasible. The reported Kimi-K3 decode rate is below one token per second, and the paper's speedup ranges conflict across sections, so it does not establish a general consumer-PC performance or cost frontier.
