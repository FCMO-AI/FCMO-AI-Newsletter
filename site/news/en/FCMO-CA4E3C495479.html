A preprint reports GPT-6 Astra leading a simulated robot benchmark, with sharp control limits

A September 21, 2026 arXiv preprint reports an evaluation of GPT-6 Astra acting directly as a robot manipulation policy, without task-specific fine-tuning or a learned motor policy in the control path. Across 42 RoboDojo simulation tasks and 2,100 trials, the authors report 22.48% average success and a score of 28.97, above the 40 public policies in their September 10 leaderboard snapshot. The result is uneven: Astra trails the top policy on precision, long-horizon and memory axes, while GPT-5.5 and DeepSeek-Flash perform much worse in the same reported interface. The authors say they stopped real-robot trials after unsafe actions damaged equipment; the paper does not establish real-world reliability or safe control. These are preprint-reported results without independent reproduction, and the public-policy runs did not receive the wiki-derived task text supplied to the LLMs.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v1 preprint exists and describes an evaluation of a frozen GPT-6 Astra model selecting robot end-effector targets directly, with non-learned target validation/path conversion and no learned policy in the control path.
  • Claimed — The authors report that GPT-6 Astra achieved 22.48% micro average success and 28.97 score over 2,100 RoboDojo simulation trials across 42 tasks, ranking above the 40 public policies in their September 10 leaderboard snapshot. The result is uneven, and public-policy comparisons did not receive the wiki-derived task text supplied to the LLMs.
  • Claimed — The authors report that they halted real-robot testing after physically unreasonable or unsafe actions damaged hardware; they report no person was injured. These are statements in the preprint, not independently verified incident findings.
  • Claimed — On selected perturbation trials, the authors report continued task success under some altered visual/action conditions and describe traces consistent with within-episode correction; the small probes do not isolate the feedback mechanism or establish general adaptation.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares against a Sep 10 public RoboDojo leaderboard snapshot and tests GPT-5.5 and DeepSeek-Flash under the same reported action interface; wiki-derived task text was not supplied to public policy runs.

Limitations

  • Single unreproduced preprint; reported outcomes are author claims, not independently demonstrated results.
  • RoboDojo is a simulation benchmark; the authors say hardware testing was halted after unsafe actions and equipment damage. Safe or reliable real-world robot control is not established.
  • The model has low absolute success and sharply uneven task/axis performance; a benchmark aggregate lead does not imply broad manipulation competence.
  • The preprint notes that public baseline policies did not receive the wiki-derived task text supplied to the LLMs, limiting interpretation of leaderboard rank as a fully matched comparison.
  • The perturbation study uses selected small probes and traces; it does not isolate the cause of apparent corrections or establish general within-episode adaptation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the 42-task simulation results or audit of episode-level records was recovered.
  • Compared public policies and the LLMs were not given identical task information: the paper says LLMs received wiki-derived task recipes while public-policy runs did not.
  • The paper says real-robot trials stopped after unsafe actions damaged equipment; the event and any real-world reliability claims were not independently corroborated.

Why it matters

If independently replicated under matched inputs, direct language-model action selection could change how semantic reasoning and low-level robot control are divided. The reported benchmark lead is limited by low absolute success, uneven capability, an input-information mismatch with public baselines and the authors' report of unsafe hardware actions.
