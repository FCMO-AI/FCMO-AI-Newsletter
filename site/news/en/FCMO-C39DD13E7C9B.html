JRC reports uneven AI adoption across EU strategic sectors, with integration still shallow in many industries

On 14 October 2026, the European Commission Joint Research Centre published an analysis mapping Eurostat ICT enterprise-survey data onto ten sectors in the EU Apply AI Strategy. The JRC reports that firm use of at least one AI technology in those strategic sectors rose from 7% in 2021 to 18% in 2025, versus 8% to 20% across broader EU economic areas. It reports higher uptake in media, energy and electronic communications, lower uptake in mobility, tourism and agri-food, and says much of the increase reflects accessible general-purpose tools used for text/audio and marketing or sales work. Skills shortages and regulatory uncertainty are reported as barriers. The account is based on JRC’s summary and report metadata; the research desk did not inspect the full report or recompute the sector estimates, and use rates do not establish productivity or production integration.

Evidence

  • Not established — The European Commission Joint Research Centre published a report on 14 October 2026 mapping AI adoption across the ten sectors covered by its Apply AI Strategy, using Eurostat ICT enterprise-survey data from 2021–2025.
  • Claimed — The JRC reports that the share of firms using at least one AI technology in the ten strategic sectors increased from 7% in 2021 to 18% in 2025, compared with 8% to 20% across the broader EU economic areas.
  • Claimed — The JRC reports uneven sector uptake: media, energy and electronic communications led at 38%, 34% and 30%, while mobility, tourism and agri-food were among the lowest. It attributes much of the increase to accessible general-purpose AI used for text and audio analysis or generation, often in marketing and sales.
  • Claimed — The JRC analysis reports that specialized skills and regulatory uncertainty, including privacy and data-protection concerns, are leading barriers. It argues that adoption measures should be tailored by sector and target integration into core processes.
  • Not established — The reviewed JRC release does not establish causal productivity gains or show that reported AI use is deeply integrated into production; it explicitly says use often remains concentrated in accessible marketing and sales tasks.

Strongest baseline

The JRC compares its ten-sector strategic grouping (7% in 2021 to 18% in 2025) with broader EU economic areas (8% to 20%). Because groupings and denominators differ, this is not a matched sector-level control.

Limitations

  • This account reports a JRC analysis; the research desk did not independently recompute the estimates or inspect the full report tables.
  • Firm-level use of an AI technology is not equivalent to intensive deployment, production integration or measured productivity gains.
  • The JRC article attributes growth mainly to accessible general-purpose AI, but the reviewed material does not establish that LLM access caused the observed increase.
  • The sector-specific grouping uses a JRC mapping of Eurostat NACE activity data; the detailed classification and weighting remain to be audited.

What we still don't know

  • The report landing record identifies the work as public, but its full report file was not retrievable in this interface; the accessible JRC release summarizes the headline data and methods. Detailed NACE-to-strategic-sector mapping, weighting and uncertainty are therefore not audited here.
  • The JRC release reports firm-level use of at least one AI technology, but the reviewed materials do not quantify usage intensity, deployment scale, or productivity effects.

Why it matters

The analysis offers a new sector-level baseline for AI diffusion in areas the EU identifies as strategically important, and it distinguishes broad tool uptake from integration into production and core workflows. That can sharpen adoption policy and economic tracking. Its headline percentages remain JRC-reported survey analysis, not measures of productivity gains or causal impact.
