RSI-Prime reports decision-level supervision gains for autonomous model-training agents

A 17 November preprint introduces RSI-Prime, a dataset reported to contain 350 complete model-training trajectories across seven task categories, and decision-level quality annotations for selecting training supervision. On PostTrainBench v1.2, the authors report that full-trajectory SFT raises a GLM-5.3 training agent’s weighted score from 36.56 to 45.61, while decision-level SFT reaches 48.24 and retains 75.48% of supervised tokens. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors submitted arXiv:2611.23138v1 on 17 November 2026, describing a dataset and training method for improving agents’ model-training capability.
  • Claimed — The paper reports RSI-Prime as 350 complete experimental trajectories spanning seven task categories, with decision-level identification and quality annotation used to mask supervision on low-quality behavior while retaining full experimental context.
  • Claimed — On PostTrainBench v1.2, the authors report that full-trajectory fine-tuning raises the GLM-5.3 training agent’s weighted score from 36.56 to 45.61, while decision-level SFT reaches 48.24 (31.95% relative gain over the original agent). Decision SFT retains 75.48% of supervised tokens and scores 2.63 points above full SFT.
  • Claimed — The authors report a human audit of 400 randomly sampled decision records, reviewed independently by two annotators with third-reviewer adjudication; post-adjudication acceptance was 98.75% for decision identification, 99.50% for action classification, and 97.50% for quality assessment. The sample does not estimate omitted-decision recall.
  • Not established — The inspected preprint does not establish independent replication, transfer across training agents or harnesses, or sustained recursive self-improvement beyond the reported PostTrainBench setup.

Strongest baseline

The original GLM-5.3 agent scores 36.56 on the paper’s weighted aggregate; full-trajectory SFT scores 45.61 and decision-level SFT scores 48.24. The paper reports three valid independent reference runs per task for original GLM-5.3; this does not establish the same repetition count for all tuned agents.

Limitations

  • The reported performance results are author claims from one preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The experiment uses a GLM-5.3 agent in the DeepSeek Harness with one NVIDIA H100 and a cumulative 10-hour execution budget; transfer across models, harnesses, or longer horizons is not established.
  • The authors disclose training–evaluation content overlap for HumanEval, one of six benchmark tasks.
  • The 400-record human audit samples already identified records and does not estimate recall of omitted decisions.
  • The original GLM-5.3 reference row averages three runs per benchmark; the paper’s summary does not establish equivalent independent repetitions for every fine-tuned comparison.

What we still don't know

  • No independent matched replication of full SFT versus decision-level SFT on RSI-Prime was inspected.
  • The paper reports one training-agent/harness regime and does not establish transfer to other training agents, harnesses, or sustained iterative improvement.
  • The paper notes training/evaluation content overlap for HumanEval, one of six weighted tasks in the PostTrainBench aggregate.

Why it matters

The work studies whether agents can learn better model-training decisions from full experimental histories instead of imitating every recorded action or selecting only by step/tool outcome. If its bounded PostTrainBench result replicates, decision-quality supervision could improve research-agent post-training; current evidence does not show autonomous recursive improvement, cross-harness transfer, or real-world research productivity.
