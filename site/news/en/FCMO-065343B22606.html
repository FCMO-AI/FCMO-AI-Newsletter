Evidence

  • Claimed — VideoXAgent is an online harness that decomposes long-video questions and invokes specialized tools to gather multimodal evidence as needed.
  • Claimed — The authors report 85.1% on Video-MME-Long, 76.5% on LVBench and 65.7% on MINERVA with about 50k agent-context tokens per sample; on MINERVA they report performance in the range of frontier systems at about 15% of a 1,024-frame dense-packing baseline context footprint.
  • Inferred — These results do not establish lower total token or compute cost, independent reproduction, or performance outside the reported benchmark and evaluation regime.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares with prior multimodal models and video agents on the evaluated benchmarks. Its MINERVA context comparison uses a 1,024-frame dense-packing baseline; context footprint is not total compute or token cost.

Limitations

  • All benchmark results are author-reported; the research desk did not run the code or independently reproduce the measurements.
  • The paper defines efficiency through retrieved evidence and maximum agent-model context, not total token cost; total compute economics are not established.
  • The capability taxonomy is mined from MINERVA expert reasoning traces and may reflect dataset-specific distributions.

What we still don't know

  • Reported benchmark and context-footprint results have not been independently reproduced.
  • The paper’s efficiency metric omits total token cost and does not provide a full lifecycle comparison across tool calls and models.

Why it matters

If the reported results generalize, query-conditioned tool use could reduce the context needed to answer questions about long videos while retaining benchmark performance. The paper makes a useful design case for progressive evidence acquisition, but its comparison does not establish lower total compute cost, generality beyond the evaluated datasets, or independently replicated performance.
