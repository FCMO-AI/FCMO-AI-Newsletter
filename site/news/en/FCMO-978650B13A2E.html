Singapore publishes quantum-safe migration milestones and pilot grant

Singapore’s Cyber Security Agency published quantum-safe migration milestones and launched a pilot grant on 14 October. Non-CII organizations exposed to quantum risks are advised to prepare a migration plan by the end of 2027 and aim to complete migration by 2035. CII owners have a 31 March 2027 plan-submission deadline, a 31 December 2031 migration target, and quantum-safe procurement considerations from 2028. The Quantum-Safe Kickstarter Grant is open to Singapore-incorporated non-government organizations for Singapore-based work, prioritizes CII owners, and covers up to 70% of eligible costs with 30% cash co-funding; funded pilots may last up to six months during the 1 November 2026–31 October 2027 grant period. No award, completed pilot or migration outcome is established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Singapore’s Cyber Security Agency published a quantum-safe migration advisory on 14 October 2026. It advises non-CII organizations exposed to quantum risks to establish a migration plan by the end of 2027 and aim to complete migration by 2035; the advisory is broad guidance, not evidence that organizations have acted.
  • Demonstrated — The official guidance sets separate CII milestones: submit migration plans by 31 March 2027, complete migration of CII systems by 31 December 2031, and account for quantum-safe considerations in new CII system procurement from 2028.
  • Demonstrated — CSA launched the Quantum-Safe Kickstarter Grant. Applications are open; the grant period runs from 1 November 2026 through 31 October 2027, with pilots lasting up to six months. It covers up to 70% of eligible project costs and requires a 30% cash contribution. Eligible applicants are non-government organizations registered and incorporated in Singapore undertaking work in Singapore; CII owners receive priority.
  • Not established — The published documents describe milestones, grant terms and required pilot reporting, but this review found no awards, completed pilots, organization-level migration progress or measured reduction in cryptographic risk.

Strongest baseline

CSA’s earlier June 2026 guidance for CII owners set a 2031 migration target. The 14 October advisory extends planning guidance to other organizations with a 2035 target and end-2027 plan milestone; the grant adds partial cash support for bounded migration pilots.

Limitations

  • The advisory recommends milestones; this review did not establish that the non-CII timelines are legally mandatory.
  • Grant terms establish funding rules and applicant eligibility, not award volume, recipient selection or successful migration.
  • No organization-level progress, completed pilots, measured performance, interoperability results or cryptographic risk reduction is reported.
  • Migration progress depends on cryptographic inventories, vendor readiness, legacy-system constraints and organizational implementation; these are not measured here.

What we still don't know

  • The advisory sets milestones but no organization-level adoption, migration-plan submissions or CII procurement compliance data were found.
  • The grant is open, but no awards or completed pilot reports were available in the reviewed materials. The page says reports should document technical implementation, testing methods, results, limitations and resourcing.
  • No evidence yet shows that guidance or funded pilots reduce cryptographic exposure or improve migration readiness at ecosystem scale.

Why it matters

The guidance turns a broad quantum-readiness goal into dated planning, procurement and migration milestones, while the grant can help organizations test discovery and migration on real systems before a cryptographically relevant quantum threat exists. The rules create observable implementation checkpoints, but their effect depends on organizational uptake and pilot quality; no migration or risk-reduction result is yet established.
