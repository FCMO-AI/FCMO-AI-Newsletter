What actually changed

The September 2026 v2 arXiv paper introduces GraphSkillAA, which represents external skills as a graph of addressable applicability, execution and exclusion fields plus typed relations. A teacher compares successful and failed executions to propose local edits; an affected-case Local Gate and a whole-update-pool Big Gate accept or roll back changes. The authors report three-seed held-out mean hard accuracies of 81.5% on SearchQA, 66.7% on LiveMath and 91.2% on DocVQA with GPT-5.6-sol, and highest reported means across nine model–benchmark comparisons in a separate SkillOpt-protocol table. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced evidence; the paper limits its conclusions to the tested splits, moderate graphs and primarily single-turn tasks.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — GraphSkillAA represents external skills as addressable graph fields and typed relations, routes proposed repairs using contrasted successful and failed cases, and uses local and whole-update-pool gates to accept or roll back changes.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report GPT-5.6-sol three-seed held-out mean hard accuracies of 81.5% on SearchQA, 66.7% on LiveMath and 91.2% on DocVQA under the paper’s main protocol.
  • CLAIMED — In a separate SkillOpt direct-chat comparison, the authors report GraphSkillAA has the highest mean in all nine model–benchmark combinations.
  • INFERRED — The reported findings are bounded to the paper’s benchmark splits, model/API setup and primarily single-turn tasks; they do not establish real-world deployment value or general agent self-improvement.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that, relative to the preceding G0 + Trace/Usage setting, GraphSkillAA improves GPT-5.6-sol mean hard accuracy by 5.3, 15.4 and 3.0 percentage points on SearchQA, LiveMath and DocVQA; one-factor ablations report average drops of 8.3 points without structured attribution, 4.9 without the Local Gate and 1.3 without correct-case protection.
  • DEMONSTRATED — At the inspected repository revision, the authors’ release-contract tests check fixed model/seeds, command construction and update/test manifest separation; these checks do not execute model requests or independently verify reported scores.

Strongest baseline

For the main protocol, the paper compares no-skill, flat-skill, structured-graph and graph-plus-trace/usage settings. A separate direct-chat protocol compares Trace2Skill, TextGrad, GEPA and SkillOpt using released test IDs, original inputs, inference budgets and scorers; this is not the same experimental protocol as the main table.

The caveat that matters

  • The authors explicitly limit conclusions to fixed released test IDs and their method-specific procedures; three-seed half-ranges are not confidence intervals, and strict-match metrics can reject aliases or partial answers.
  • The main evaluation uses moderate graphs that can largely be rendered in full and primarily single-turn tasks; thousands-skill libraries and long-horizon delayed credit remain untested.
  • The public repository README says raw benchmark files are not redistributed and model requests require API access; model behavior can change over time.
  • No independent reproduction, independent baseline implementation audit, or deployment evaluation was performed in this review.
  • The Big Gate optimizes aggregate hard accuracy on the update pool; this does not establish safety or subgroup-risk behavior for consequential deployments.
  • The paper’s separate baseline comparison follows a direct-chat protocol distinct from its main graph-optimization protocol; claims across the two should not be merged.
  • The author repository’s release-contract tests check fixed configuration, command construction and update/test manifest separation, but do not execute model requests or verify benchmark outcomes.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of the three benchmark results or direct-chat baseline comparisons was inspected.
  • The inspected public README says raw benchmark files are not redistributed, and the paper reports latency and monetary cost separately at run level without aggregate costs in the inspected result tables.
  • The paper’s tested graphs are moderate in size and tasks are primarily single-turn; longer-horizon and deployment utility are not established.

FCMO Lens

If independently reproduced, the method offers a concrete way to update external agent procedures at specific graph objects while checking regressions and rolling back failed edits. This could influence how persistent skill libraries are optimized. The current evidence covers three benchmark suites and author-run model APIs, so it does not establish improved long-horizon agents, deployment utility or general self-improvement.
