A post-training delivery benchmark separates model gains from successful service delivery

An arXiv paper proposes a governed benchmark for agents delivering post-trained models, where stage-level decisions are scored from platform-recorded evidence and a held-out acceptance gate checks the delivered model. Across five certified SFT scenarios and 38 real-GPU episodes, four current agent arms produced no train-but-does-not-learn deliveries and bypassed no deploy gate. Yet 31 of 38 metric-passing submissions were rejected by delivery-contract checks; supplying an oracle-correct training configuration still left 22 of 24 deliveries rejected. The study reports a constructed delivery plane and small scenario set, not field prevalence or general deployment reliability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Across five certified SFT delivery scenarios and 38 real-GPU episodes, the authors report zero train-but-does-not-learn deliveries and zero deploy-gate bypasses by four current agent arms. The preference scenario failed its positive control and was excluded before scoring.
  • Claimed — The authors report that 31/38 metric-passing submissions were rejected by delivery-contract checks. Under a pinned oracle-correct configuration, all 24/24 deliveries passed held-out acceptance but 22/24 were still rejected by contract requirements. Three flagship arms staged human approval on 360/360 deliverable pressure-test trials; cheaper first-generation arms showed reduced compliance.
  • Not established — The results do not establish how often real post-training services or deployed agents fail delivery contracts; they come from one constructed governed plane and a small set of certified SFT scenarios.

Strongest baseline

A scripted reference pair certifies scenario controls before agent scoring. The same oracle scores four current agent arms and two human ML-engineer-plus-assistant arms. A pinned-configuration intervention tests whether correct training settings alone repair the delivery failures.

Limitations

  • All measurements are author-reported; no independent artifact review or reproduction was performed.
  • The certified campaign covers one constructed delivery plane, five SFT scenarios and 38 episodes; it does not establish field prevalence or broad deployment reliability.
  • The preference scenario failed its positive control and was excluded; Glaive-FC and the clear Bitext scenario were not included in the certified campaign denominators.
  • Current flagship pressure-test results cover three models and the stated ticket suite; first-generation cheaper-tier arms behave differently, so findings are capability-tier and harness dependent.
  • The benchmark’s task selection, platform facts, acceptance margin and delivery contract are researcher-defined; transfer to production PTaaS operators is unmeasured.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reconstruction of the benchmark or reproduction of the reported gate outcomes was conducted.
  • The result comes from one constructed delivery plane and a limited certified scenario set; transfer to other platforms, providers and production service contracts is unknown.
  • The preference scenario did not pass positive-control certification and was excluded, limiting coverage of preference-optimization delivery.

Why it matters

A training run can lower loss and pass routine dashboard checks while failing the customer’s actual objective or delivery contract. This paper tests that gap with held-out acceptance and platform-recorded gates; its results suggest that evaluating agentic post-training only by metric improvement can miss schema, cost, registry and approval failures. The evidence is a bounded author-run benchmark, not a measure of how often deployed services fail.
