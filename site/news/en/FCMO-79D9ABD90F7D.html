CodeMidas builds coding RL tasks from source code and reports gains on five external benchmarks

The authors introduce CodeMidas, a pipeline that derives executable reinforcement-learning tasks from implemented functionality in open-source codebases, constructs execution-grounded tests, and filters environments using execution checks and solution rollouts. They report 5,545 training tasks from 3,185 repositories across 23 languages and 15 technical domains. Training MiMo-V2.5 with GRPO on this dataset reportedly improves all five listed external coding benchmarks relative to the same initial policy and evaluation settings. Examples include DeepSWE pass rate rising from 10.0% to 21.7%, Terminal-Bench v2.1 from 63.7% to 72.2%, and ProgramBench Almost Solved from 4.5 to 21.5. The authors report task-set disjointness from CodeMidas Val and the five external benchmark sets. These are author-reported results for one model/training setup, not an independent reproduction or general scaling law.

Evidence

  • Documented publication event — The authors posted arXiv preprint 2609.22068v1 on 2026-09-18.
  • Claimed — The authors report constructing 5,545 coding reinforcement-learning training tasks from 3,185 open-source codebases spanning 23 programming languages and 15 technical domains, using source-derived task statements, execution-grounded tests and post-rollout filtering.
  • Claimed — Training MiMo-V2.5 with GRPO on CodeMidas reportedly improves all five listed external benchmark scores against the same initial policy and identical evaluation settings; examples include DeepSWE pass rate 10.0% to 21.7%, Terminal-Bench v2.1 63.7% to 72.2%, and ProgramBench Almost Solved 4.5 to 21.5.
  • Limitation reported by the authors — The reported training and evaluation results concern one MiMo-V2.5 setup and remain author-reported; the authors state task-set disjointness, but no independent reproduction or data-leakage audit was performed in this review.

Strongest baseline

The same MiMo-V2.5 initial policy evaluated on the same official task sets and identical evaluation settings; the paper also compares filtered 5,545/1k/3k task pools with an approximately 8,000-task unfiltered pool under the same training configuration.

Limitations

  • All results are reported by the paper authors; no code, dataset, checkpoint, independent reproduction or leakage audit was performed in this review.
  • The reported training effect is for MiMo-V2.5 under one GRPO setup with binary execution reward, batch size 32 and 32 rollouts per task. Transfer to other model families, scales, training algorithms or resource budgets is unknown.
  • External results use the authors’ benchmark task sets and compare against the same initial policy; ProgramBench Almost Solved is the share of tasks passing at least 95% of tests, while the other listed metrics are pass rates.
  • The paper reports its training-task set is disjoint from validation and external benchmark task sets, but independent semantic/near-duplicate contamination analysis was not performed here.
  • Environment construction, verifier quality, filtering cost, RL compute and cost per usable task are not independently measured in this review.

What we still don't know

  • The linked code, dataset, checkpoints and evaluation scripts were not inspected or independently reproduced; reported gains and claimed task-set disjointness remain unverified here.
  • Generalization beyond one model/checkpoint family and one GRPO configuration has not been established.
  • Verifier false-positive rates, semantic benchmark contamination and task-generation/compute costs were not independently measured.

Why it matters

Coding-agent RL depends on task diversity and executable reward signals. CodeMidas reports a way to source both from existing software functionality, then filter candidate environments for execution consistency and reward reliability. Its reported benchmark gains make this a meaningful training-data and environment-construction result, while the evidence remains limited to one model/checkpoint family and author-reported filtering/evaluation.
