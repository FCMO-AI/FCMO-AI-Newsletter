PASTABench reports weak optimal-timing performance in proactive agent-safety monitoring

The authors introduce PASTABench, a paper-reported benchmark of 1,139 synthesized multi-turn agent trajectories across five risk categories and thirteen subcategories, with human-checked Earliest-Signal and Trigger turns defining an Optimal Intervention Window. In their evaluation of 16 language models, the paper reports that GPT-4o reached 40.74% Perfect timing and 68.22% On-Time interventions. The authors also report sensitivity to explicit hazard vocabulary in open-weight models. These are benchmark results on constructed trajectories, not evidence of deployed-agent safety or real-world harm reduction; independent reproduction remains open.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published PASTABench, a benchmark of 1,139 synthesized multi-turn trajectories in five risk categories and thirteen subcategories.
  • Claimed — In the paper's evaluation of 16 language models, GPT-4o achieved 40.74% Perfect and 68.22% On-Time intervention timing under the authors' Optimal Intervention Window metric.
  • Claimed — The authors report that neutralizing explicit hazard vocabulary substantially weakens proactive-safety results for some open-weight models in their benchmark.
  • Not established — The evaluated trajectories are synthesized and annotated benchmark scenarios; these results do not establish safety performance in live deployed agent workflows.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares 16 models under a common constructed benchmark and distinguishes Perfect from broader On-Time timing. It is not a deployment or incident-rate comparison.

Limitations

  • The benchmark trajectories are synthesized with an LLM and span a bounded taxonomy; results do not measure live deployment safety.
  • Model rankings and timing metrics are authors' reported results and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • Evidence of lexical sensitivity in this benchmark does not establish that all open-weight models or safety systems fail similarly.

What we still don't know

  • Independently reproduce the paper-reported metrics on the published benchmark and verify prompts, inference settings, sampling variability and artifact availability.
  • Test whether synthetic-trajectory results transfer to diverse live agent workflows and measured safety outcomes.

Why it matters

If the reported benchmark behavior generalizes, agent monitors often fail to intervene at the useful point in a multi-step workflow, and surface hazard keywords may overstate some models' safety understanding. That would affect how agent safety is evaluated and deployed. The paper does not establish field performance, and its synthetic benchmark and model set bound the claim.
