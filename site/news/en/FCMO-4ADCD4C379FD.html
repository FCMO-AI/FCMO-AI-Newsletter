Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published a preprint defining state-conditioned minimal sufficient evidence recovery and releasing the SERBench task specification and evaluation materials.
  • Claimed — On the authors’ 500-state Test500 evaluation, MSS-Complement recovered a complete evidence set in 73.0% of states at five source items versus 61.4% for Qwen3-Embedding-8B plus Qwen3-Reranker-8B; a matched three-call similarity-only control reached 66.6%.
  • Claimed — The authors report a 5.0-point advantage on a gold-blind repository-discovery test and downstream improvements on AMA-Bench and repair localization under the stated harnesses.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The main 500-state comparison uses released candidate pools that isolate set selection from full evidence discovery; benchmark certificates are kept in a separate organizer evaluator. No independent replication is established by this record.

Strongest baseline

Qwen3-Embedding-8B plus Qwen3-Reranker-8B on the same released Test500 states and pools; a matched three-call similarity-only ablation controls for controller-call budget.

Limitations

  • All comparative results are from the authors’ evaluation; no independent reproduction was performed here.
  • The main Test500 candidate-pool track isolates set recovery from full-repository candidate discovery.
  • SERBench certificates are held in a separate organizer evaluator, so the published method code alone does not reproduce the complete scoring path.
  • The paper reports supplementary transfer and repair-localization results, but these do not establish general performance across live production agents.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the Test500 and downstream results, including artifact/version binding and complete protocol, has not been established.
  • The main candidate-pool test isolates set recovery and does not establish gains in prospective, end-to-end coding-agent decisions across repositories or controller families.

Why it matters

If the result generalizes beyond the authors’ coding-agent harness, selecting complementary evidence against a recorded decision state could improve context use and repair decisions where ordinary relevance ranking repeats known information or leaves a required support gap.
