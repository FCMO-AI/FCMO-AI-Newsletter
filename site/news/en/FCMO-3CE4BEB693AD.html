SkelWAM reports cross-embodiment manipulation transfer from one source robot

The authors introduce SkelWAM, which maps whole-body geometry, tool pose and gripper state into a shared 25-dimensional action representation. In the author-reported LIBERO-Cross10 simulation benchmark, it reached 43.3% success across 1,000 paired episodes on ten tasks and ten target embodiments (95% Wilson interval 40.3–46.4%), compared with 7.1% for the strongest evaluated baseline, RoVi-Aug with a Fast-WAM backbone and target-specific end-effector inverse kinematics. Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that SkelWAM uses a shared 25-dimensional representation of arm centerline geometry, tool-center-point pose and parallel-jaw commands, with embodiment-specific decoders, and does not use target-task demonstrations or target-policy updates.
  • Claimed — On LIBERO-Cross10, the authors report 43.3% success over 1,000 paired episodes (95% Wilson interval 40.3–46.4%), compared with 7.1% for RoVi-Aug with a Fast-WAM backbone and target-specific end-effector inverse kinematics, the strongest evaluated baseline. Each method is evaluated on the same ten target embodiments, ten tasks and ten paired trial indices per cell; target-independent calibration is allowed.
  • Claimed — The authors report a deployment of a JAKA mini2-trained policy on a Feagine A03 continuum robot for three tabletop manipulation tasks.
  • Not established — The inspected source does not establish independent reproduction, performance across broader task distributions, or the amount of physical validation beyond the three reported tabletop tasks.
  • Claimed — The authors report higher average success on Cross-Spatial and Cross-Object (61.3% and 61.0%) than on Cross-Goal and Cross-Long (15.0% and 18.0%); the physical Feagine A03 deployment is presented qualitatively without a reported trial count or success rate.

Strongest baseline

RoVi-Aug adapted to a common Fast-WAM backbone, with target-specific end-effector inverse kinematics, reports 7.1% mean success against SkelWAM’s 43.3%. The target/task trial matrix is shared, but pretraining and source-adaptation budgets differ, and the baselines do not receive SkelWAM’s skeleton observations or body-tracking objective.

Limitations

  • All performance numbers are reported by the paper authors; this review did not execute code or independently reproduce them.
  • The benchmark comprises ten selected LIBERO tasks and ten target embodiments, with Franka Panda as the sole source robot; source data include 467 successful demonstrations, and policy selection uses held-out source episodes.
  • The 43.3% aggregate hides substantial task-family variation: 61.3%/61.0% on Cross-Spatial/Cross-Object versus 15.0%/18.0% on Cross-Goal/Cross-Long.
  • Each method uses the same target/task trial indices, but VLA and WAM baselines use released checkpoints, adaptation budgets differ, and no baseline receives SkelWAM’s skeleton representation or body-tracking objective.
  • Evaluation permits task-independent target calibration, so “zero-shot” means no target-task demonstrations or target policy updates, not no target setup or calibration.
  • The physical JAKA mini2-to-Feagine A03 study covers three tabletop tasks and is qualitative in the inspected report; physical trial counts and success rates are not reported there.
  • The linked project site could not be retrieved during this review; code execution and independent artifact verification remain open.

What we still don't know

  • The paper tables and protocol were inspected, but the linked project site could not be retrieved and no independent reproduction or code execution was performed.
  • The physical deployment section describes three tasks but gives no physical trial counts, task success rate or uncertainty estimate.

Why it matters

If it transfers to other tasks and robots, a shared geometric action representation could reduce robot-specific data collection and integration. The paper reports a large simulation advantage under a matched target/task trial matrix, but baseline pretraining budgets differ, target calibration is allowed, performance varies sharply by task family, and the physical demonstration has no quantified success rate.
