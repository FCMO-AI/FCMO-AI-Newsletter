COMED selectively escalates cross-model collaboration to trade rescue gains against collaboration harms

The Sep. 22, 2026 COMED paper studies when a multi-model system should trust its initial answer, check it with a peer, or invoke fuller collaboration. The controller uses anchor self-consistency, router ambiguity and a lightweight peer probe to direct additional model calls toward uncertain or persistently disagreeing cases. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published COMED, a post-anchor controller that chooses to accept an anchor answer, verify it with a peer, or escalate to cross-model collaboration.
  • Claimed — Across 16 open-weight settings spanning medical, scientific and general reasoning benchmarks, the paper reports that COMED improves its corresponding fixed or routed anchor in every setting, averaging +4.6 percentage points, with at most two models per query and about 33% fewer decoded tokens than dense collaboration.
  • Claimed — In a Frontier-3/Humanity's Last Exam stress test, the paper reports GPT-5.5 accuracy increasing from 23.13% to 28.13%, with collaboration used on 70% of examples.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The reported results are author-run benchmark results in specified model pools and evaluation settings; this review found no independent reproduction or public code artifact and they do not establish general deployment gains.

Strongest baseline

Corresponding fixed-anchor or learned-router systems without post-anchor control, plus dense Always-Collaborate and majority-vote baselines under the paper's evaluation protocol.

Limitations

  • All performance results are author-reported; no independent reproduction was found in this review.
  • The evaluation uses specific benchmark datasets, model pools and thresholds; it does not establish generalization to production traffic, other providers, or different tasks.
  • Decoded tokens and model-call counts are proxies for inference cost; latency, monetary cost, throughput and serving hardware effects are not established.
  • The Frontier-3/HLE experiment is a bounded stress test and does not establish broad frontier-model gains.
  • The controller depends on sampled self-consistency and a learned router; sensitivity to distribution shift, prompt changes and calibration error remains relevant.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the main 16 open-weight settings and the Frontier-3/HLE result with pinned model versions, splits, decoding budgets and scoring.
  • Measure actual latency, throughput, monetary cost and hardware utilization rather than decoded-token and model-call proxies.
  • Test controller calibration and rescue-harm behavior under prompt, task-distribution and model-pool shifts.

Why it matters

The work makes cross-model collaboration a selective inference-time decision and explicitly accounts for peers correcting wrong answers as well as corrupting correct ones. If independently reproduced, the controller could improve accuracy-cost tradeoffs in multi-model serving. The current evidence is bounded to author-run benchmarks and does not establish production impact.
