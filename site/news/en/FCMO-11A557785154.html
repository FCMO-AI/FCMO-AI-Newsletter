OpenAI and Upwork report task rebundling in AI-related freelance work

A Nov 10 OpenAI/Upwork report compares sampled ChatGPT activity with Upwork job postings and examines how AI-related projects combine tasks. Across 63 matched work categories, the report describes a non-uniform relationship between ChatGPT-use intensity and changes in marketplace share. It reports that task diversity in AI-related postings rose from an effective 14.6 to 21.2 clusters between early 2023 and late 2025; adjusted fixed-price budgets were about 52% higher and first-90-day hours about 41% higher for hired hourly contracts, while adjusted hourly rates were about 1.5% higher. These are descriptive, first-party marketplace associations, not causal effects or evidence of aggregate employment change.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI and Upwork published a November 2026 report comparing sampled ChatGPT conversations with Upwork postings and analyzing task bundles in AI-related freelance work.
  • Claimed — The authors report that 43 of 63 matched categories followed a pattern in which higher ChatGPT-use intensity aligned with a falling Upwork share or lower intensity aligned with a rising share; the remaining categories did not follow that pattern.
  • Claimed — The authors report that task diversity in AI-related postings rose from an effective 14.6 to 21.2 task clusters from early 2023 to late 2025; adjusted fixed-price budgets were about 52% higher and first-90-day hours about 41% higher, while adjusted hourly rates were about 1.5% higher.
  • Not established — The report does not establish that AI caused the observed marketplace changes or determine net employment, earnings, or labor-market-wide effects.

Strongest baseline

Within-category and posting-month adjusted comparisons of AI-related versus other Upwork posts; no causal control group is established.

Limitations

  • The analysis covers one freelance marketplace and sampled consumer ChatGPT activity, not the whole labor market.
  • The authors explicitly describe comparisons as non-causal.
  • Some conversation and job-post tasks are classified or inferred; raw data/code were not independently audited here.

What we still don't know

  • The reported estimates have not been independently recomputed or replicated on another marketplace.
  • The report is descriptive and does not identify causal effects of AI-related classification or changes in ChatGPT use.
  • Upwork postings and sampled ChatGPT conversations do not represent the full labor market; task groups are inferred.

Why it matters

The report adds a task-bundle and project-scope view to labor evidence about AI: it suggests that within one online marketplace, AI-related work is associated with less concentrated task mixes and larger projects. Its relevance is bounded by platform selection, inferred task classifications, and the absence of causal identification or aggregate employment outcomes.
