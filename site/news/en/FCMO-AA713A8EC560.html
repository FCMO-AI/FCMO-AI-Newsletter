Canadian privacy regulators call for stronger governance of AI in health care and research

Canada's federal, provincial and territorial privacy commissioners and ombuds passed a resolution urging responsible AI development, procurement and use in health care and health research, according to an October 29 release from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. The release summarizes six principles and calls for legally required governance and accountability frameworks in health AI, updated research-ethics guidance and stronger ethics-board capacity. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Canada's federal, provincial and territorial privacy commissioners and ombuds passed a resolution on responsible AI development, procurement and use in health care and health research, as reported by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada on October 29, 2026.
  • Claimed — The official release says the resolution urges governments to consider legislation or regulation requiring AI systems used in health care to meet six principles—privacy protective, valid and reliable, safe, human-rights affirming, transparent and accountable—and to require effective governance and accountability frameworks for developers, procurers and users.
  • Claimed — The release says the regulators call for updated research-ethics guidance for AI-enabled studies involving human participants, stronger ethics-board capacity, and continued regulator advocacy and coordination on enforcement, guidance and public education.
  • Not established — The resolution and its official summary do not establish that any government has enacted the proposed rules, that health providers have adopted the recommended frameworks, or that patient, research or health outcomes have changed.

Strongest baseline
