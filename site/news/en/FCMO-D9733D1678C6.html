A controlled paper study reports answer-only RLVR did not improve citation faithfulness

The paper tests whether reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) for answer correctness also improves whether citations actually support model claims. Using a shared Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base and three open-weight RLVR agents across four question-answering datasets, the authors report that the baseline produces roughly one unfaithful citation in seven questions on Wikipedia-based datasets. In fixed-context evaluation, Search-R1 and ReSearch track the base within a few percentage points, while R-Search is slightly worse; the authors state their sample could detect shifts of about seven percentage points, ruling out a large gain in this setup. A no-planting control improves on the prior measurement method. However, the evaluation is single-turn and differs from the agents' multi-turn training format; the planting-based scorer may classify obedience to altered passages as unfaithfulness, and flagged cases were not manually adjudicated. These are author-reported, bounded findings, not a general claim that RLVR cannot improve citation faithfulness.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On Wikipedia-based question datasets, the authors report that a shared Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct baseline produces roughly one unfaithful citation in seven under their planted-passage measurement.
  • Claimed — Under fixed-context evaluation across four question-answering datasets, three RLVR agents trained from the shared base model do not show a large citation-faithfulness improvement: Search-R1 and ReSearch are within a few percentage points of baseline, while R-Search is slightly worse. The authors state the sample could detect shifts of about seven percentage points.
  • Not established — The study does not establish that answer-only RLVR never improves citation faithfulness in interactive retrieval, other languages, prompts or model families; its planting-based score may conflate post-rationalization with citing an altered passage as instructed.

Strongest baseline

The instruction-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base model under the same fixed-context citation evaluation; the three RLVR agents are compared against this shared base.

Limitations

  • All results are author-reported; no independent reproduction or manual adjudication of flagged examples was conducted.
  • The comparison uses one shared Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base, three RLVR agents, four datasets, English text, one prompt and one decoding configuration.
  • Agents trained for multi-turn search were evaluated in a fixed-context single-turn citation format, which may affect abstention and citation behavior.
  • The scorer is deterministic string matching over planted spans; a flagged citation could represent obedience to an altered passage rather than post-rationalization.
  • Higher abstention and format-failure rates filter which answers are scored; sample sizes differ by model.

What we still don't know

  • The reported citation rates and RLVR comparison have not been independently reproduced.
  • Flagged citations were not manually adjudicated; citing a planted altered passage may reflect obedience to the evaluation prompt rather than post-rationalization.
  • The fixed-context single-turn evaluation differs from the multi-turn search format used to train the RLVR agents.

Why it matters

A retrieval-augmented answer can be factually correct yet cite a passage that does not support the claim. If reward training checks only answer correctness, citation quality may remain a separate failure mode. This study reports no large improvement under its fixed-context protocol, but its evaluation format and planted-passage interpretation limit how broadly the result can be applied.
