What changed

The authors introduce EpiSEM-Bench, with 300 matched unsafe/safe task pairs over six tool-use domains, fact-level omission of safety evidence and an optional recovery tool. In one reported evaluation of ten models, access to recovery reduced unsafe execution on matched conditions for every model; nine also improved permissible-task completion, while one declined slightly. These are author-reported synthetic-benchmark findings, not independent or live-system results.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v1 paper defines EpiSEM-Bench with 300 matched prohibited/permissible task pairs across six synthetic tool-use domains, fact-level suppression of safety-relevant evidence and an optional tool that returns omitted facts.
  • Claimed — Across ten evaluated serving configurations, the authors report that evidence-recovery access reduced unsafe-commit rates for every model on matched critical-omission conditions; nine models also improved permissible-task completion, while MiMo-V2.5-Pro declined by 0.6 percentage points.
  • Claimed — The authors report large variation in the joint paired useful-recovery metric: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash scored 58.4% and GLM-4.7-Flash 0.0% under the paper's matched synthetic task protocol.
  • Not established — The evaluated environments are synthetic tool interfaces, with temperature set to zero and one rollout per condition. The source does not establish transfer to live systems, independently authored task environments or real-world safety outcomes; no independent reproduction was located in this review.

Strongest baseline

Matched suppressed-evidence versus recoverable-evidence conditions on the same task, world and omission mask; each model also has full-evidence safe/unsafe baselines.

Limitations

  • The paper establishes a benchmark design and reports one evaluation, not real-world incident rates or operational safety.
  • The results are author-reported and lack an independent reproduction in this review.
  • One rollout per condition at temperature zero does not quantify stochastic variability.
  • The deterministic recovery tool supplies all suppressed facts on a valid call, unlike many real retrieval and authorization systems.
  • No monetary cost, latency or causal deployment impact is established.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark aggregates and model outcomes have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The six domains are synthetic and author-constructed; behavior on live or independently authored tools is unknown.
  • One rollout per condition at temperature zero does not estimate within-configuration variation or stochastic policy behavior.

Why it matters

Tool agents can receive only true but incomplete information and still take an action that is prohibited by the full environment state. EpiSEM-Bench pairs otherwise identical permitted and prohibited tasks, then measures both unsafe execution and successful completion after evidence recovery. If independently validated and extended beyond these synthetic environments, this could improve evaluation of whether agents verify authorization before consequential actions without simply refusing everything.
