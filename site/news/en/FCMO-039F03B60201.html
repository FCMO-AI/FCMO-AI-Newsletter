BeNice! reports near-lossless KV sharing and lower simulated serving costs for looped language models

A new arXiv paper proposes BeNice!, a post-training method intended to make KV-cache sharing reliable in looped language models. The authors report near-matching shared and unshared validation quality on tested Parcae checkpoints, a 66.2% global HBM reduction for a 1.3B model at recurrence eight, and throughput/TTFT gains in simulated replay using proxy models. These are paper-reported results; independent reproduction and transfer to live deployments or other architectures remain open.

What changed

A new arXiv paper proposes BeNice!, a post-training method intended to make KV-cache sharing reliable in looped language models. The authors report near-matching shared and unshared validation quality on tested Parcae checkpoints, a 66.2% global HBM reduction for a 1.3B model at recurrence eight, and throughput/TTFT gains in simulated replay using proxy models. These are paper-reported results; independent reproduction and transfer to live deployments or other architectures remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that BeNice! post-training on tested Parcae checkpoints brings shared-cache validation perplexity close to unshared results while matching standard finetuning quality; Table 5 reports three-seed finetuning.
  • Claimed — For a 1.3B Parcae model at recurrence 8, the authors report global HBM use falling from 63.82 GB to 21.60 GB (66.2%) on Core-Extended with batch size 64 and context length 2048.
  • Claimed — In simulated replay of production-derived requests using proxy looped models, the authors report up to 23.6% higher throughput and p90 time-to-first-token falling from 139.2 seconds to 23.2 seconds at 2 requests per second.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper reports results for specific trained Parcae models, benchmarks and simulated serving settings; it does not establish independent replication or gains in live deployments and other architectures/workloads.

Strongest baseline

Standard finetuning without the auxiliary BeNice! loss and the same model with unshared KV caches; serving comparisons are against unshared looping under the paper’s simulated workload settings.

Limitations

  • All performance figures are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
  • Serving results use simulated replay of production-derived requests and proxy models; live deployment transfer is unknown.
  • The evaluated approach concerns trained looped Parcae models and does not establish gains for general transformer deployments.
  • The paper reports that released Parcae models can lose quality under naive KV sharing; the method requires training and tested results do not imply that arbitrary looped models are shareable.
  • The serving gains are simulated with production-derived request traces and proxy models, not a live production deployment.

What we still don't know

  • The reported quality and memory/serving results have not been independently reproduced.
  • The paper’s serving evaluation uses simulated trace replay and proxy models; live deployment results and transfer to other architectures/workloads are not established.

Why it matters

KV-cache memory grows with recurrence in looped models. If these reported results generalize, trained cache sharing could increase usable context or concurrency and improve serving latency under memory pressure. The paper’s results are bounded to particular models and simulated workloads, so the deployment impact is not yet established.
