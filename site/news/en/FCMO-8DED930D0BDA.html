Preprint reports that incomplete tool-output wording can cause agent overclaims and verifier errors

An arXiv paper reports a paired study of four ways tool-output formatters can misrepresent search scope, result completeness, uncertainty or identifier provenance in one production RAG archive system. Across three model families and 32 holdout cases, the authors report that repaired wording reduced unsupported agent claims for the first three defect families; in a fixed-answer verifier test, false answers were accepted 30/30 times under warped wording and 0/30 under clear wording for scope, completeness and uncertainty. Results are author-reported and limited to the studied cases, models and prompts; identifier-provenance wording had a much smaller effect. The paper also links an offline artifact and two upstream formatter/tool fixes: the cited OpenClaw fix is merged, while the Hermes truncation fix remains an open pull request. These code changes do not independently reproduce the paper results or estimate prevalence.

What changed

An arXiv paper reports a paired study of four ways tool-output formatters can misrepresent search scope, result completeness, uncertainty or identifier provenance in one production RAG archive system. Across three model families and 32 holdout cases, the authors report that repaired wording reduced unsupported agent claims for the first three defect families; in a fixed-answer verifier test, false answers were accepted 30/30 times under warped wording and 0/30 under clear wording for scope, completeness and uncertainty. Results are author-reported and limited to the studied cases, models and prompts; identifier-provenance wording had a much smaller effect. The paper also links an offline artifact and two upstream formatter/tool fixes: the cited OpenClaw fix is merged, while the Hermes truncation fix remains an open pull request. These code changes do not independently reproduce the paper results or estimate prevalence.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In a paired study in one production RAG archive system, the authors report that clear formatter wording reduced unsupported agent claims for scope, completeness and uncertainty defects across three model families and 32 holdout cases; the reported improvement was 62–92 percentage points, depending on defect family.
  • Claimed — For fixed false answers, the authors report that three verifiers accepted 30/30 answers under warped wording and 0/30 under clear wording in each of the scope, completeness and uncertainty families; the tested provenance wording changed acceptance from 18/30 to 14/30.
  • Not established — The selected cases do not establish prevalence across production systems or generalization to unrelated tools, models, tasks or verifiers; the paper's exploratory audit-prompt exercise is not an independent prevalence estimate.
  • Demonstrated — At the time of this activation, the author-linked OpenClaw attachment-lookup fix pull request was merged, while the author-linked Hermes search-line truncation disclosure pull request remained open. These repository states document two specific downstream code changes, not independent validation of the paper study or a prevalence estimate.

Strongest baseline

For the fixed-answer comparison, the paired baseline is the same false answer with only the formatter wording changed. For agent claims, the paired conditions keep the case and prompt aligned while changing warped versus clear formatter text.

Limitations

  • The paper studies four defect families in one production RAG archive system; the 32 holdout cases are selected stress cases, not a prevalence sample.
  • The reported 62–92 percentage-point reduction belongs to full repaired wording, which combines factual disclosures and behavioral instructions in some conditions; disclosure alone is not isolated.
  • The fixed-answer verifier comparison measures the effect of formatter wording on the same false answers under the tested prompts and cases; it does not establish general verifier competence.
  • Identifier-provenance wording had a much smaller reported effect than the other three families, so clearer wording alone was not a universal repair.
  • The exploratory audit-prompt demonstration was designed with prior knowledge of defects in the authors' own system and does not establish cross-system prevalence.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or evaluation on a second unrelated production system was run in this activation. The linked artifact advertises an offline replay, but the full F3/F4 B database inputs were not saved, so those corrected renderings remain frozen evidence rather than reconstructable output.
  • The author-reported holdout cases are selected from four known defect families in one system and do not measure how prevalent formatter failures are across production agents.

Why it matters

Agent evaluations often treat tool output as the evidence available to a model. This paper shows a bounded case in which the output formatter itself changed what agents claimed and what verifiers accepted, making faithful reporting of scope, completeness, uncertainty and identifiers a concrete reliability concern. The results do not estimate how common these failures are across production systems.
