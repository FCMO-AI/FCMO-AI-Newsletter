Google reports controlled evaluation gains from Earth AI geospatial reasoning agent and expands experimental access

Google Research’s Oct 14 announcement expands experimental access to its Geospatial Reasoning agent and links a technical report that compares four configurations around Gemini 3.1 Pro and Gemini 3.8 Flash. In the paper’s 76 held-out open-ended GeoExpertQueries, GRA with Gemini 3.8 Flash scores 62.7/100, compared with 52.2 for the same model plus GRA’s geospatial tools without full planning and execution guidance, and 3.3 with basic tools. The full orchestration configuration does not improve the same test with Gemini 3.1 Pro (47.6 versus 50.8), making the added benefit model-dependent. The paper reports the largest gains from the specialized geospatial tool suite, introduces two benchmarks and releases their development splits. A material count mismatch remains: the paper reports 220 held-out GeoKTools queries, while the official v1.0.0 repository lists 210. These are author-reported results from one provider’s Gemini stack, not independent replications or field-deployment outcomes.

In the paper’s 76 held-out open-ended GeoExpertQueries, GRA with Gemini 3.8 Flash scores 62.7/100, compared with 52.2 for the same model plus GRA’s geospatial tools without full planning and execution guidance, and 3.3 with basic tools. A material count mismatch remains: the paper reports 220 held-out GeoKTools queries, while the official v1.0.0 repository lists 210. Evidence

  • Claimed — Google Research reports that its Geospatial Reasoning agent combines Gemini with modular geospatial skills, data and tools, begins with an explicit plan, and executes multi-step analytical workflows; the Oct 14 post expands experimental access through Ask Google Earth and an agent-to-agent API.
  • Claimed — The paper compares four configurations around the same Gemini 3.1 Pro and Gemini 3.8 Flash models on four benchmarks, with three runs per test-set condition. On 76 open-ended GeoExpertQueries test cases, GRA with Gemini 3.8 Flash scores 62.7 versus 52.2 for the same model with GRA tools but without the full planning/execution guidance, and 3.3 with only basic tools. With Gemini 3.1 Pro, full GRA scores 47.6 versus 50.8 for GRA tools without that guidance, so the orchestration gain is model-dependent.
  • Claimed — The authors report that specialized geospatial skills and tools account for the largest measured gains: with Gemini 3.8 Flash, GeoExpertQueries rises from 3.3 with basic tools to 52.2 with GRA tools before full orchestration; GRA scores 85.9 on GeoKTools and 86.4 on the modified 44-question CBGB evaluation. UnivEARTH is saturated in this setup: even a standalone model without Earth Engine access scores 86.9–93.6, and the authors say systems can answer from source text without doing the intended geospatial analysis.
  • Claimed — The paper reports GeoExpertQueries with 101 authored prompts (76 held out) and GeoKTools with 305 queries (220 held out), and says it releases the development splits and evaluation materials. The official benchmark repository at commit c21a6d9 identifies itself as v1.0.0 and reports 25/76 for GeoExpertQueries but 85/210 for GeoKTools (295 total), leaving a 10-query discrepancy with the paper’s 85/220 split.
  • Demonstrated limitation — This review did not independently reproduce the author-reported results. The paper evaluates Gemini models only; excludes tasks that require unavailable/proprietary data; relies on an LLM autorater for open-ended tasks; modifies CBGB and finds UnivEARTH saturated; and states that its benchmark/tool design favors configurations using GRA tools. Reported inference costs exclude external data and Earth Engine services.
  • Disputed — The paper’s GeoKTools test split (220) conflicts with the official v1.0.0 repository README and provenance document (210); the repository’s 85 development plus 210 held-out cases total 295, not the paper’s 305. The cause is unresolved. GeoExpertQueries counts agree at 25 development plus 76 held out.

Strongest baseline

For the added value of full orchestration, the closest matched baseline is the same Gemini model with the GRA geospatial skills/tools but without upfront planning and execution guidance. For the contribution of domain tooling, compare full GRA against the same-backbone basic-tools configuration; that comparison bundles access to unique Google models/data with tool use and is not a general-model-only effect.

Limitations

  • All benchmark outcomes are reported by the paper’s authors; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • Only two Gemini model versions are evaluated; transfer to other model families is unknown.
  • Upfront planning/execution guidance is model-dependent: the reported GeoExpertQueries gain is 10.5 points for Gemini 3.8 Flash, while full GRA is 3.2 points below the GRA-tools configuration for Gemini 3.1 Pro.
  • GeoExpertQueries uses an LLM autorater and expert-crafted workflows; absolute scores depend on workflow and judge validity.
  • GeoKTools is designed around tool operations GRA supports and therefore favors tool-using configurations.
  • The modified 44-question CBGB protocol excludes one infeasible item and updates four data-sensitive reference answers; scores are not directly comparable to the original benchmark.
  • UnivEARTH is saturated in this setup: a standalone model scores highly without performing the intended analysis.
  • Estimated token inference costs exclude external data and compute services such as Earth Engine; partner testing does not quantify deployment outcomes.
  • A material count discrepancy remains between the paper and its official benchmark release: GeoKTools is 85 development + 220 held-out (305 total) in the paper, but 85 + 210 (295 total) in the v1.0.0 repository README/provenance. Held-out queries are private, so this review could not resolve which test split produced the reported numbers.
  • The paper reports 305 GeoKTools queries, including a 220-query test split (arXiv:2610.15750v1, §4.2/Table 2 and §5.3/Table 6); the official v1.0.0 repository README and provenance at commit c21a6d9 state 85 development plus 210 held-out cases (295 total). The cause of this 10-query difference is unresolved; the current official repository is not silently treated as correcting the paper.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the four-configuration results on the released benchmark development materials and an independently held-out geospatial task set.
  • Whether the agent’s measured benchmark performance transfers to real partner workflows and produces useful, decision-relevant outcomes at known data and service costs.
  • How much of the reported advantage comes from Google-exclusive models and data versus reusable geospatial tooling and orchestration, and whether the model-dependent orchestration effect persists outside this setup.
  • The official GeoKTools v1.0.0 repository lists 210 held-out test queries while the arXiv report lists 220; the reported results’ exact test split cannot be reconstructed from the public repository.

Why it matters

The report tests an important agent-design question with matched backbone models: whether a domain-specific tool layer and a planning/execution harness add more than a general model and basic tools. Its results point to specialized data/tools as the largest contribution, while showing that added orchestration can help one model and slightly hurt another. The benchmarks also expose saturation in a public geospatial test. The released GeoKTools test count also conflicts with the report by 10 queries, limiting exact reconstruction of that result. These findings are relevant to applied agent evaluation, but do not yet establish transfer beyond Google’s geospatial stack or real-world partner workflows.
