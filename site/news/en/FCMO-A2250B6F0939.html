Anthropic reports Claude-designed binders for four scorpion neurotoxins, without neutralization evidence

Anthropic’s Oct 20 technical report describes an internal Claude Science campaign against five medically relevant scorpion neurotoxins. From an initial prompt and four follow-ups, the system generated 318,484 designs and selected 180; the team added 31 designs for laboratory testing. The authors report that 17 of 211 designs met their binding criteria across four toxins, including two Cn2 binders with single-digit nanomolar affinity or tighter in the reported assays. None of 151 designs targeting multiple toxins met the criteria against more than one. The report and sequence/binding dataset are public. These are author-reported binding results: no neutralization, animal efficacy, matched expert/pipeline baseline, or therapeutic outcome is established.

What changed

Anthropic’s Oct 20 technical report describes an internal Claude Science campaign against five medically relevant scorpion neurotoxins. From an initial prompt and four follow-ups, the system generated 318,484 designs and selected 180; the team added 31 designs for laboratory testing. The authors report that 17 of 211 designs met their binding criteria across four toxins, including two Cn2 binders with single-digit nanomolar affinity or tighter in the reported assays. None of 151 designs targeting multiple toxins met the criteria against more than one. The report and sequence/binding dataset are public. These are author-reported binding results: no neutralization, animal efficacy, matched expert/pipeline baseline, or therapeutic outcome is established.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that 17 of 211 tested designs met their stated binding criteria for one of four scorpion toxins; none met criteria against Ts1.
  • Claimed — The authors report single-digit-nanomolar-or-tighter affinity estimates for Cn2-B1 in assays at both CROs and competition with the LR antibody.
  • Claimed — None of the 151 designs made against two or more toxins met the authors' binding criteria against more than one toxin.
  • Claimed — The study did not test any binder for cellular neutralization or in-vivo efficacy and did not establish an antivenom.

Strongest baseline

No expert-designed or fixed-pipeline experimental baseline was run in this campaign. The report therefore cannot separate Claude's contribution from the design tools or quantify time/cost savings against a conventional campaign.

Limitations

  • Binding was measured against recombinant toxins of undocumented activity; no design was tested for cellular neutralization or in-vivo efficacy, and no antivenom was produced.
  • The study reports 17 binders across four toxins, but LqhIII was tested at one lab only; affinity estimates rely on limited concentration-series/replicate conditions and some surface/fit limitations are reported.
  • Two Cn2 designs have reported low-nanomolar affinity and competition with LR, but competition is not a functional neutralization assay; the complexes are predicted and no design structure was solved.
  • None of 151 multi-toxin designs met the criteria against more than one toxin; the result may reflect model capability, prompting, tools or biological feasibility.
  • No expert or fixed-pipeline experimental baseline was run, so the report does not quantify model contribution or comparative time/cost savings.
  • The campaign used about 10,000 H100 GPU-hours and two CROs, limiting accessibility; claimed efficiency gains from separate work rely on in-silico scores and do not validate this experiment.
  • Safety, immunogenicity, serum half-life, stability, manufacturing cost, distribution and patient outcomes were not measured.

What we still don't know

  • No candidate was tested for neutralization of channel activity in vitro or for protection in animals; binding/antibody competition does not establish function.
  • Reported binder classifications and affinities have not been independently reanalyzed from released raw data or reproduced by an independent laboratory.
  • No matched expert or fixed-pipeline baseline was experimentally run; the reported compute/time cannot quantify comparative advantage.
  • No multi-toxin design met binding criteria against more than one target; mechanism of failure is unresolved.

Why it matters

The result is a notable step in AI-assisted molecular design because two contract research organizations tested a large model-generated design set against medically relevant toxins. It shows reported binding activity across several targets, while also exposing a sharp boundary: every qualifying design was toxin-specific, and the multi-toxin campaign did not meet its stated objective. The work is not an antivenom result and does not establish that Claude’s contribution outperforms existing design pipelines.
