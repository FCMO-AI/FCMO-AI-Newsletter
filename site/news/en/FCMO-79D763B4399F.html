What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces BATON, which combines Bayesian Feedback Attribution within trajectories with equal-mass aggregation across trajectories. On Qwen2.5-Instruct models, the authors report gains over matched GRPO and GiGPO variants on ALFWorld, WebShop and seven-dataset search-augmented QA; a 2×2 ablation reports gains from each component separately. For example, ALFWorld success with Qwen2.5-1.5B rises from 72.9% to 85.6% for GRPO and from 86.8% to 94.1% for GiGPO. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced. The authors explicitly say the feedback likelihood measures model-based compatibility rather than causal effects of counterfactual actions.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper defines BATON as Bayesian Feedback Attribution (BFA), which reweights action contributions within a trajectory using model likelihoods of observed environment feedback, combined with Trajectory Mass Normalization (TMN), which gives each complete trajectory equal outer objective mass. The authors state that BFA measures model-based feedback compatibility, not causal effects of counterfactual actions.
  • Claimed — With Qwen2.5-Instruct, the paper reports improvements over matched GRPO and GiGPO variants on ALFWorld, WebShop and seven search-augmented QA datasets. For ALFWorld at 1.5B, GRPO success is reported as 72.9% baseline and 85.6% with BATON; GiGPO is 86.8% and 94.1%. The setup says matched variants share checkpoint, rollout budget, reward/advantage construction, optimization settings and evaluation protocol.
  • Claimed — A reported 2×2 ablation separates BFA and TMN. On ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-1.5B, GRPO is reported at 72.9% baseline, 84.1% with BFA, 82.4% with TMN and 85.6% with both; GiGPO is 86.8%, 93.4%, 90.7% and 94.1%, respectively.
  • Claimed — For ALFWorld evaluation with Qwen2.5-1.5B, the paper reports lower mean actions and generated tokens at update 150: GRPO changes from 21.51 actions/1,734 tokens to 16.39/1,301; GiGPO changes from 13.51/1,431 to 10.82/746. These are author-reported evaluation measurements.

Strongest baseline

Matched host-learner GRPO and GiGPO variants sharing initial checkpoint, rollout budget, reward/advantage construction, optimization settings and evaluation protocol.

Limitations

  • All measured outcomes are paper-reported; this activation did not reproduce them.
  • BFA uses model likelihood compatibility for observed feedback; the paper explicitly says this does not measure causal effects of counterfactual actions.
  • The evaluated backbones and tasks are limited to Qwen2.5-Instruct and the reported benchmark settings; no deployment benefit is established.

What we still don't know

  • The reported gains, ablation and trajectory-cost results come from one author preprint; no independent reproduction was located or run.
  • The inspected experiments use Qwen2.5-Instruct at 1.5B/3B/7B and ALFWorld, WebShop and search-augmented QA; evidence for other model families, environments and deployment outcomes is absent.

Why it matters

The paper separates two training choices for multi-turn agents: how environment responses weight actions within a trajectory, and how long versus short trajectories contribute across a batch. Its reported matched comparisons and component ablation make this a concrete optimization result to track. The evidence is limited to one preprint's benchmarks and Qwen2.5 regimes; it does not establish generalization to other model families or deployment settings.
