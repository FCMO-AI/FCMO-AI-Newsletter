AlphaGenome 2 reports higher cell-type-specific variant recall in genomic benchmarks

Google DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2, a genomic sequence model that conditions predictions on a target tissue, cell type or cell state. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Google DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2 on 2026-11-11 and linked a full manuscript describing the model and its evaluations.
  • Claimed — At 90% precision on the TenK10K single-cell eQTL causality task, the authors report recall of 30% for AlphaGenome 2 versus 16% for AlphaGenome 1 fine-tuned on single-cell data (AG1-sc); reported overall AUPRC is 0.76 versus 0.72 for AG1-sc and 0.69 for Scooby.
  • Claimed — At the same benchmark, the authors report TenK10K single-cell eQTL sign-prediction recall of 43% for AlphaGenome 2 versus 23% for AG1-sc at 90% accuracy; overall AUC is 0.85 versus 0.79 and 0.77, respectively.
  • Claimed — The paper describes a model that conditions 1-Mb human or mouse DNA sequence on a target tissue, cell type or cell state. It reports training on bulk and single-cell datasets, including 107 million human single-cell RNA-sequencing profiles, and says the API is available for non-commercial research; model source code and weights are planned for final publication.
  • Not established — The results were not independently reproduced in this activation. The paper reports no new wet-lab experiments, says high-precision recall remains a minority for fine-mapped eQTLs and putative GWAS variants, and states that AlphaGenome 2 has not been validated or approved for clinical or diagnostic use.

Strongest baseline

For the quoted TenK10K recall comparison, AG1-sc is the closest within-family comparator: AlphaGenome 1 fine-tuned on single-cell data. The paper also compares against Scooby, a specialized sequence-to-function model. These are author-run manuscript results, not independently replicated here.

Limitations

  • The reported performance figures are first-party manuscript results and were not independently reproduced by the research desk.
  • The study reports no new wet-lab experiments; its cell-level predictions are computational analyses on previously collected datasets.
  • The authors state that high-precision recall recovers only a minority of fine-mapped eQTLs and putative causal GWAS variants; results can be weaker outside accessible chromatin and for post-transcriptional mechanisms.
  • Transient, stimulated and disease states are underrepresented; observational condition embeddings encode correlations, not causal relationships.
  • The API is offered for non-commercial research. The model source code and weights are planned for final publication. The authors state that the model is not validated or approved for clinical or diagnostic use.

What we still don't know

  • The TenK10K metrics and other reported evaluations were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The linked manuscript says model source code and weights will be released upon final publication; this activation did not test the non-commercial API.
  • The results use computational benchmarks on existing datasets and do not demonstrate causal biological effects, clinical validity or patient outcomes.
  • The authors report that high-precision recall remains low for fine-mapped eQTLs and putative GWAS variants and that transient, stimulated and disease cell states are underrepresented.

Why it matters

Regulatory variants can act differently across cell types, while large single-cell maps rarely include enough donors for direct variant association tests. If the reported gains hold up, conditioning on cell state could help researchers prioritize candidate variants and where to study them. The paper’s computational results do not establish causal biology, clinical utility or improved patient outcomes.
