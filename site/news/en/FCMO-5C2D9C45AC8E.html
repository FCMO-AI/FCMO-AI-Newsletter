GittinsEval reports near-zero model-selection regret at a fraction of exhaustive benchmark cost

The arXiv v2 paper introduces GittinsEval, a Bayesian cost-aware policy for choosing which model configuration to evaluate next and when to stop. It combines Gittins-index allocation with an anytime recommendation rule for partially evaluated configurations. Replaying fixed response matrices from GSM8K, PIQA, AlpacaEval and MMLU, the authors report near-zero simple regret often using 1%–2% of exhaustive evaluation cost, with adaptive stopping commonly at 1%–10%. These are author-reported results on precomputed benchmark responses, not a live deployment study or independent reproduction. The claimed optimality is conditional on a Gaussian surrogate, independent configurations, conditionally i.i.d. item scores and predetermined costs; the paper explicitly does not provide a distribution-free finite-sample guarantee.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v2 preprint formulates LLM configuration selection as cost-aware Bayesian Markov-chain selection and proposes GittinsEval with index-based allocation, adaptive stopping and an anytime recommendation for partially evaluated configurations.
  • Claimed — On fixed response matrices from GSM8K, PIQA, AlpacaEval and MMLU, the authors report near-zero simple regret often at 1%–2% of exhaustive-evaluation cost and adaptive stopping typically at 1%–10%. This is author-reported offline evaluation, not independent replication or a live deployment result.
  • Demonstrated — The paper’s exact batch-level optimality proposition is conditional on a Gaussian surrogate, independent arms, conditionally i.i.d. scores and predetermined batch costs; the authors state it is not a distribution-free finite-sample identification guarantee for the realized response matrix.
  • Not established — Reported cost is modeled using published API prices and per-configuration cost assumptions on precomputed response matrices; end-to-end savings in live evaluation pipelines, including operational overhead and changing prices, were not established.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares to configuration-level Bayesian optimization and cost-aware/cost-unaware bandit and racing baselines; advantages are strongest in large-example and large-candidate regimes.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported on fixed response matrices from four benchmarks; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The policy’s Bayesian optimality is conditional on the stated Gaussian surrogate, independent arms, conditional i.i.d. scores, and predetermined positive costs; numerical gridded indices and the anytime recommendation add further approximations.
  • The experiments do not establish performance in a live evaluation workflow or include complete operational costs beyond the modeled evaluation cost.
  • Simple regret and empirical benchmark recommendation do not establish statistical validity of benchmark claims, robustness to benchmark contamination, or broad model quality.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported regret and evaluation-cost curves was performed.
  • Experiments replay precomputed response matrices and do not establish savings in live evaluations where configurations, prices, prompts or systems can change.
  • The formal guarantee depends on independent arms, a Gaussian model and predetermined costs; robustness under correlated configurations, nonstationary costs and misspecified score distributions is not established.

Why it matters

If it transfers beyond the tested response matrices, adaptive cost-aware evaluation could reduce the expense of comparing many model, prompt and decoding configurations, especially when inference costs differ. The reported savings do not establish general evaluation accuracy or live workflow savings; results rely on fixed response matrices and assumptions behind the policy.
