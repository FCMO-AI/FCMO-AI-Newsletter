PhysRSI-eval reports five-axis measurements of iterative robot-manipulation improvement

Galbot researchers introduce PhysRSI-eval, a five-axis profile separating first-attempt performance, timing of gains, attained scores, physical interaction budget and recovery from early failure. In the main simulation evaluation, the paper reports six model-plus-runtime systems on 18 manipulation tasks: GPT-6 Astra solved 15, GPT-6.1 Sol 12, GPT-6 Sol 7, GPT-5.6 Sol 1, Claude Opus 5.5 13, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 11. The paper reports that final score, early improvement and budget efficiency can diverge, and exploratory associations between public capability scores and the five outcome axes. It also reports Astra workspace interventions and held-out layout tests. All results remain author-reported. The main comparison has one improvement chain per task; the capability analysis covers six systems without independent validation; Astra's ablation/frozen tests cover six tasks, and transfer evidence comprises three cases.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Galbot researchers published PhysRSI-eval, a five-axis evaluation profile for iterative robot-manipulation improvement, on 19 October 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report evaluation of six model-plus-runtime systems over 18 RoboDojo manipulation tasks, with task counts solved by K17 of 15, 12, 7, 1, 13 and 11 respectively for GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 5.5.
  • Claimed — Across the six evaluated systems, the authors report stronger associations of spatial scores with first-attempt performance and gain timing, and of perception scores with attained performance and later recovery; coding scores had weaker associations in this sample.
  • Claimed — In Astra's six-task, two-chain-per-task ablation, the paper reports 15/36 late-round successes with the full workspace and feedback condition versus 3/36 with no history across rounds K4–K6.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent multi-chain replication, repeated physical execution reliability, generalization beyond the tested simulation tasks, or predictive validity for deployment outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The study compares six model-plus-runtime configurations in its main simulation protocol and uses within-Astra workspace ablations; the primary model comparison has one improvement chain per task.

Limitations

  • The main model comparison uses one improvement chain per task; repeated K0 runs do not estimate variability across complete chains.
  • The capability association analysis covers six model-plus-configuration systems, uses public benchmark settings that differ from the robot runs, and has no independent validation; the smallest adjusted q-value reported is 0.11.
  • The workspace ablation and frozen-layout tests cover Astra and six tasks. Cross-task transfer is reported for three cases, not a broad transfer evaluation.
  • The results come from simulation episodes with native grading; the paper does not establish repeated real-robot execution reliability or deployment transfer.
  • The authors report a separate Gemini 4 partial run over ten tasks; it is excluded from main 18-task association analyses because its task set differs.

What we still don't know

  • No independent multi-chain reproduction of the PhysRSI-eval comparison or the reported workspace interventions was inspected.
  • Whether the proposed axes predict real deployment reliability or resource costs has not been established.

Why it matters

Embodied-agent evaluations that report only eventual task success can hide when improvements arrive, how many physical interactions they consume and which early failures are recovered. The paper proposes measurements that expose those differences and reports task-level results under a bounded simulated manipulation protocol. The evidence supports an author-reported evaluation design and results, not a general conclusion that physical recursive self-improvement is reliable or transfers to deployed robots.
