Nature reports Paper2Agent, a framework for turning research papers into tested, callable research tools

A peer-reviewed Nature paper introduces Paper2Agent, which turns research papers and associated code into structured MCP resources, workflow prompts and tested executable tools. Across 100 computational-biology papers, the authors report 74 successful conversions and 593/599 proposed tools passing automated validation; 26 papers could not be agentified, often because of missing artifacts or environment failures. On 300 tutorial-derived questions, the system reports 91.2% accuracy and lower query cost and latency than a direct-repository baseline using the same model. Across 42 execution tasks from 10 non-biology papers, it reports 98.1% accuracy. Results are author-run and use Claude Sonnet 4; paper agents support execution and reuse, while open-ended scientific reasoning remains human-in-the-loop.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The peer-reviewed paper reports that Paper2Agent converts papers and associated code into MCP resources, prompts and tested executable tools. Across 100 computational-biology papers, it successfully agentified 74, proposing 599 tools, of which 593 passed automated validation. The 26 failed conversions were attributed mainly to missing code, data or model artifacts, environment/dependency failures, or non-generalizable scripts.
  • Claimed — On 300 tutorial-derived questions, Paper2Agent using Claude Sonnet 4 reports 91.2 ± 1.6% accuracy, versus 80.3 ± 2.3% for Claude Code with direct repository access using Sonnet 4 and 86.3 ± 1.1% using Sonnet 4.6. The paper reports per-query cost and latency of US $0.20 and 1.6 minutes, versus US $0.38 and 4.3 minutes for the Sonnet 4 direct paper/repository baseline.
  • Claimed — Across 42 execution-based tasks from 10 non-biology computational papers, the authors report 98.1 ± 0.8% accuracy across 5 independent runs. They also describe a multi-paper psoriasis case that prioritized GPR137 using computational predictions and independent perturbation datasets; statistical support was condition-dependent, and a human researcher selected the validation analysis.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper states that open-ended hypothesis generation and mechanistic interpretation remain human-in-the-loop, and that agreement with a single reference on open-ended questions primarily measures faithful execution rather than analytical validity. It also identifies security, intellectual-property, attribution and maintenance challenges for executable research agents.

Strongest baseline

For 300 tutorial-derived questions, Sonnet 4 Paper2Agent reports 91.2 ± 1.6% versus direct-repository Claude Code at 80.3 ± 2.3% with Sonnet 4 and 86.3 ± 1.1% with Sonnet 4.6; reported per-query cost/latency were US $0.20/1.6 minutes versus US $0.38/4.3 minutes for the Sonnet 4 baseline. All values are author-reported in the stated setup.

Limitations

  • The experiments are author-run and this activation did not install, execute or independently reproduce the public implementation.
  • Only 74 of 100 computational-biology papers were successfully agentified; missing code, data/model artifacts and environment or dependency failures bound applicability.
  • The benchmark corpora and tasks do not establish performance on arbitrary, less curated scientific repositories or all disciplines.
  • The evaluated system uses Claude Sonnet 4; cross-model transfer is not established by these results.
  • Reference-output fidelity does not establish scientific validity. The authors state that open-ended hypothesis generation and mechanistic interpretation remain human-in-the-loop.
  • Executable research agents raise security, intellectual-property, attribution and ongoing maintenance issues; no general hostile-repository safety guarantee is established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the paper's conversion-rate, benchmark, cost/latency or cross-domain execution results was conducted in this activation.
  • The 100-paper biology corpus and other task sets do not establish performance on arbitrary scientific repositories, incomplete artifacts or less curated workflows; 26/100 biology conversions failed.
  • The reported evaluations use Claude Sonnet 4 and do not establish transfer to other base models, orchestrators or agent hosts.
  • Reference-output tests do not verify scientific validity, robustness to distribution shift, containment of malicious repositories or durable operation under upstream dependency changes.

Why it matters

Research papers often contain methods that are hard to reuse without locating code, rebuilding environments and reconstructing workflows. Paper2Agent reports a way to package those materials as tested, callable interfaces and gives evidence of lower query cost and latency in its evaluated tasks. If the conversion and validation process transfers to less curated work, it could change how computational methods are disseminated and composed. Its failed conversions and human-oversight requirements bound that claim.
