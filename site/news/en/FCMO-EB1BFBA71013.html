Evidence

  • Claimed — Hacktron reports that its three researchers used Claude Opus 4.8 and Opus 5 to analyze and develop a working exploit for a Discourse HEIF image-upload path, then used the resulting chain with an OpenAI single-sign-on flaw to reach employee ChatGPT/Codex accounts; a human-directed Codex session opened a harmless internal-repository pull request before testing stopped.
  • Demonstrated — Discourse's July 28 security advisory states that an upstream libheif vulnerability permits remote code execution through malformed HEIF uploads, lists patched Discourse versions, and credits Hacktron as reporter.
  • Claimed — Hacktron reports that OpenAI confirmed a fix and paid $6,500 for the OpenAI-side identity finding; the award did not cover testing against the Discourse-hosted forum.
  • Not established — The sources inspected do not establish autonomous hacking, access to repository contents, a generalizable exploit success rate, or a measured causal reduction in human time from model use.

Strongest baseline

No matched human-only or alternative-model comparison is supplied for the complete task. Hacktron gives a within-project chronology involving Opus 4.8 and Opus 5, but does not provide a controlled comparison that isolates model contribution.

Limitations

  • The OpenAI identity flaw and employee account/repository-access account are reported by Hacktron; the internal pull request is redacted and no attributable OpenAI public confirmation was located in this review.
  • The Discourse advisory independently confirms the HEIF upload RCE and reporter credit, not the OpenAI SSO or account-access portion.
  • The model-assistance description is first-party and unreplicated; Hacktron says skilled human guidance remained important.
  • The report does not establish autonomous hacking, source-code reading, broad transfer, a general exploit success rate or measured human-time savings.

What we still don't know

  • Hacktron's account of model contribution, OpenAI SSO exploitation and employee account/internal-repository access has not been independently reproduced; the Discourse advisory corroborates only the HEIF-upload RCE.
  • The report's claims about labor time, adaptation speed and increased small-team throughput lack a matched human-only comparison and repeated observations suitable for estimating a general effect.

Why it matters

This is a bounded, source-reported example of model assistance in a real, disclosed security chain involving a frontier lab's account and repository boundary. If independently corroborated, it would matter for how organizations assess image-processing, identity federation and connected coding-agent permissions. The record does not show autonomous hacking, access to source-code contents, a general exploit success rate, or measured human-time reduction.
