Aikido reports a 328 GB pruned GLM-5.3 security model with near-parent CVE recall

Aikido released Altar-1, a 328 GB W4A16/REAP-pruned derivative of GLM-5.3 intended for local defensive-security workloads. Aikido reports that pruning reduces its already-quantized parent from 488.2 GB to 328.0 GB while retaining 60.4% average recall per run, versus 61.5% for the parent, on an internal benchmark of 32 known vulnerabilities across 30 repositories with three runs per case. Both versions rediscovered the same 23 vulnerabilities at least once. The vendor says the model is configured for vLLM on four H200 GPUs. These are company-reported results from targeted known-CVE rediscovery inside a larger pipeline; they do not demonstrate blind vulnerability discovery, exploit validation, patch quality or independent performance.

What changed

Aikido released Altar-1, a 328 GB W4A16/REAP-pruned derivative of GLM-5.3 intended for local defensive-security workloads. Aikido reports that pruning reduces its already-quantized parent from 488.2 GB to 328.0 GB while retaining 60.4% average recall per run, versus 61.5% for the parent, on an internal benchmark of 32 known vulnerabilities across 30 repositories with three runs per case. Both versions rediscovered the same 23 vulnerabilities at least once. The vendor says the model is configured for vLLM on four H200 GPUs. These are company-reported results from targeted known-CVE rediscovery inside a larger pipeline; they do not demonstrate blind vulnerability discovery, exploit validation, patch quality or independent performance.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Aikido reports that Altar-1 starts from GLM-5.3 AWQ INT4 and uses REAP expert pruning to reduce stored weights from 488.2 GB to 328.0 GB, retaining 168 of 256 routed experts per layer. Relative to the unpruned 1,506.7 GB BF16 parent, the reported storage reduction is 78.2%.
  • Claimed — On Aikido’s internal benchmark of 32 known vulnerabilities across 30 repositories, with three runs per case, Altar-1 averaged 60.4% recall per run and rediscovered 23/32 vulnerabilities at least once across three runs. The GLM-5.3 AWQ INT4 parent averaged 61.5% recall and covered the same 23/32; the full-precision parent averaged 65.6% and covered 25/32.
  • Demonstrated limitation — Aikido defines this as targeted CVE rediscovery within a pipeline that uses other models for surrounding stages. It does not measure blind discovery across a codebase, exploit validation, or fix proposals. The published evaluation is an internal vendor benchmark and has not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • Demonstrated — Aikido publishes Altar-1 model weights and documents a vLLM serving configuration targeting four NVIDIA H200 GPUs; its model card identifies the model as a 504B-parameter, W4A16 pruned GLM-5.3 derivative.

Strongest baseline

The closest compression baseline is the same GLM-5.3 checkpoint quantized with AWQ INT4 before REAP pruning: Aikido reports 488.2 GB, 61.5% mean recall per run and 23/32 vulnerabilities covered at least once, compared with Altar-1 at 328.0 GB, 60.4% and 23/32. The benchmark is internal and the evaluation has not been independently replicated.

Limitations

  • All model-performance figures in the release are Aikido-reported; no independent replication was located or performed.
  • The evaluation covers 32 known vulnerabilities in 30 repositories with three runs per case and is not a blind discovery test.
  • The benchmark runs within Aikido’s larger pipeline, which uses other models for surrounding stages; it isolates neither the security model’s complete product contribution nor human review effects.
  • The report gives recall and coverage but no precision, false-positive rates, exploit-validation outcomes, patch quality, runtime, throughput or serving cost.
  • A four-H200 serving target documents a feasible configuration claimed by the model provider; it is not a measured minimum hardware requirement or independently verified production profile.

What we still don't know

  • The recall and coverage comparison comes from Aikido’s internal 32-CVE benchmark and has not been independently reproduced or adjudicated.
  • The release does not report false-positive rates, serving latency/throughput, resource cost, or quality on unknown-vulnerability discovery and remediation tasks.

Why it matters

The result gives a concrete example of trading model weight storage against task-specific security recall: Aikido reports another 32.8% reduction against an already-quantized parent with about a 1.1 percentage-point lower mean recall on its internal benchmark. If independently confirmed, this could lower the hardware threshold for local defensive-security inference. The test does not establish that the model finds unknown vulnerabilities or performs a complete penetration test.
