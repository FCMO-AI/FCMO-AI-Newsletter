A robotics paper shows hidden cross-module error dependence can break downstream uncertainty calibration

A September 20, 2026 arXiv paper analyzes how uncertainty estimates from separately calibrated robotic modules can fail when combined if their errors are correlated. It studies a two-module moving-obstacle example in which position and velocity errors share fixed Gaussian marginals while their correlation varies. The authors report that individual 95% coverage stayed near nominal, but downstream coverage under an independence assumption ranged from 99.98% at correlation -0.9 to 86.46% at +0.9. In a simulated crossing controller, positive dependence increased collisions to 39.7 per 10,000 trials under the independence interface versus 8.7 with oracle joint covariance; joint-covariance estimation restored coverage near 95%, while robust bounds reduced risk at a conservatism cost. This is a controlled simulation result, not evidence of failure rates in physical robots or deployed stacks.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper analytically shows that marginal distributions do not identify the uncertainty of a composed quantity when module errors may be dependent. In its controlled simulated position/velocity pipeline, component coverage remained within 0.06 percentage points of nominal while independence-assumed downstream 95% coverage ranged from 99.98% at correlation -0.9 to 86.46% at +0.9. At +0.9, the simulated crossing planner recorded 39.7 collisions per 10,000 trials under independence versus 8.7 under the oracle joint covariance. These are author-reported results from a Gaussian simulation, not measured rates in deployed robots.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the marginal-only independence interface against oracle joint covariance, empirical joint covariance estimated from calibration samples, and a worst-case dependence-robust bound. At positive correlation +0.9, independence gave 86.46% coverage and 39.7 collisions per 10,000 trials; oracle covariance gave about 95% coverage and 8.7 collisions per 10,000.

Limitations

  • The study analytically isolates a two-module position/velocity composition and uses Gaussian errors with fixed marginals; it does not establish prevalence in deployed robot stacks.
  • The crossing-controller results are simulated with fixed assumptions and random seed; no physical robot or real perception system is evaluated.
  • The paper itself says the controlled Gaussian study establishes mechanism, not empirical behavior in nonlinear systems.
  • No independent reproduction was performed in this record.
  • Dependence-robust bounds improve safety in the tested positive-correlation regime but can be overly conservative for negative dependence; they are not a universally superior interface.

What we still don't know

  • Only a two-module Gaussian model and simulated crossing controller were tested; nonlinear perception, multimodal or context-dependent errors, distribution shift and physical robot outcomes are not established.
  • No independent reproduction of Table I or closed-loop threshold-sensitivity results was inspected.
  • The finite-calibration experiment evaluates a specific Gaussian parameterization and pre-specified two-percentage-point coverage tolerance; sample requirements under other regimes are unknown.

Why it matters

A downstream planner cannot reconstruct joint uncertainty from separately calibrated module marginals when their errors are dependent. If modular robotics systems omit this dependence, risk estimates can be too confident or overly cautious, changing decisions. The paper identifies a concrete interface and evaluation concern, but tests only a deliberately controlled Gaussian simulation.
