SQD disaggregates subquadratic-attention inference around heterogeneous memory and compute regimes

SQD (SubQuadratic Disaggregation) argues that serving systems designed around dense-attention assumptions leave efficiency on the table for sparse, linear and sliding-window attention models. It splits decode according to which subquadratic components require full-KV indexing versus static-memory compute, then maps those components onto heterogeneous DRAM/SRAM resources. In the authors' adjusted 8xB200 system proxy, reported average tokens/J gains over their strongest GPU-only baselines are 53% on GLM 5.2, 31% on Nemotron 3 Ultra and 56% on Gemma 4 31B. An analytical Rubin+LPX fixed-power model reports 1.2x-1.5x tighter achievable latency and up to 3.6x throughput versus attention-FFN disaggregation.

What actually changed

SQD (SubQuadratic Disaggregation) argues that serving systems designed around dense-attention assumptions leave efficiency on the table for sparse, linear and sliding-window attention models. It splits decode according to which subquadratic components require full-KV indexing versus static-memory compute, then maps those components onto heterogeneous DRAM/SRAM resources. In the authors' adjusted 8xB200 system proxy, reported average tokens/J gains over their strongest GPU-only baselines are 53% on GLM 5.2, 31% on Nemotron 3 Ultra and 56% on Gemma 4 31B. An analytical Rubin+LPX fixed-power model reports 1.2x-1.5x tighter achievable latency and up to 3.6x throughput versus attention-FFN disaggregation.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — In the authors' adjusted 8xB200 heterogeneous-system proxy, SQD improves average tokens/J by 53% on GLM 5.2, 31% on Nemotron 3 Ultra and 56% on Gemma 4 31B versus their strongest GPU-only baselines.
  • DEMONSTRATED_IN_MODEL — In the authors' analytical fixed-power Rubin+LPX model, SQD yields 1.2x-1.5x tighter achievable latency and up to 3.6x throughput versus attention-FFN disaggregation.
  • INFERRED — Comparable gains will persist on shipping heterogeneous production systems under real network contention and scheduler overhead.

Strongest baseline

The paper describes strongest GPU-only baselines for the adjusted B200 proxy and attention-FFN disaggregation for the Rubin+LPX analytical comparison.

The caveat that matters

  • The adjusted 8xB200 result is a system proxy rather than a clean unaffiliated production-cluster reproduction.
  • The Rubin+LPX headline comparison is analytical and therefore depends on assumed memory, interconnect, power and scheduling parameters.
  • Reported gains can change substantially with batch size, sequence length, KV footprint, sparsity pattern, interconnect contention and accelerator utilization.
  • The evidence establishes a promising serving design in the studied regimes, not a universal advantage for every subquadratic model or workload.
  • No independent reproduction was recovered in this activation.

What remains unknown

  • No unaffiliated hardware-real reproduction was recovered.
  • Sensitivity of the Rubin+LPX result to memory bandwidth/capacity, link bandwidth, queueing and workload mix remains a key transfer question.

FCMO Lens

If these gains survive hardware-real reproduction, subquadratic model architecture changes not only FLOP complexity but the optimal serving topology: memory-heavy selection and compute-heavy attention/FFN can be provisioned separately. That could materially change inference-cluster design as hybrid and sparse attention become common.
