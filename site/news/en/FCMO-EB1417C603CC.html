Limitations

  • The upper and lower bounds hold under assumptions and a constructed distribution family; they do not show that typical language distributions exhibit the same separation.
  • The score-oracle error regime may differ from errors produced by trained diffusion language models; this transfer was not measured.
  • The pass-complexity result is not a wall-clock, throughput, monetary-cost or hardware benchmark.
  • No independent review of the long proof or lower-bound construction was performed in this activation.

What we still don't know

  • Independent verification of the key upper bounds, critical-window analysis and masked-diffusion lower-bound construction.
  • Determine whether the paper's score-oracle error assumptions and constructed family resemble trained masked, uniform and Gaussian dLLMs.

Why it matters

Parallel generation is a key proposed advantage of diffusion language models. By comparing sampling complexity across three paradigms and isolating a case where their pass requirements separate, this work gives theory for when that advantage may depend on the diffusion process and score-error regime. The practical effect on trained language models remains unknown.
