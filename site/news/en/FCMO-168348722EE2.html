SpecOpt reports residue-aware optimization of predicted molecular selectivity

Evidence

  • Claimed — On a 915-compound ChEMBL-derived benchmark, the authors report an improved target-minus-off-target Vina-score gap for 776 compounds (84.8%); the mean changes from −0.718 to +0.472 kcal/mol, with mean Tanimoto similarity 0.72.
  • Claimed — In a residue-identity ablation, the paper reports no improvement for 29 of 29 compounds; in a five-off-target check, all five tested off-target scores weakened for 5 of 20 compounds.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares optimized compounds with starting compounds under its own docking and contact-difference objective; no experimental affinity or selectivity measurement is reported.

Limitations

  • Results rely on AutoDock Vina docking scores and do not establish experimental binding affinity, selectivity or biological activity.
  • The main benchmark considers up to three off-targets; the five-off-target check covers only 20 compounds and weakens all five tested scores for 5 compounds.
  • The residue-identity ablation reports no improvement across 29 compounds, indicating sensitivity to residue representation.
  • The linked code was not publicly accessible in the reviewed source check; no implementation run or independent reproduction was conducted.

What we still don't know

  • No independent execution or reproduction of the docking benchmark was conducted; the linked anonymous code page did not expose usable source in this review.
  • Predicted docking-score gaps have not been validated against measured binding affinity or experimental selectivity.

Why it matters

If validated experimentally, residue-aware optimization could help prioritize molecules that favor a target while limiting predicted binding to selected off-targets. The current evidence supports a computational screening method and benchmark result; it does not establish biochemical selectivity or therapeutic performance.
