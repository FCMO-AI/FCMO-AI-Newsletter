OpenAI reports Iran- and Russia-linked false-front influence campaigns using its models

OpenAI reports that it banned two account clusters it attributes to separate Russian- and Iranian-origin influence operations. The company says one operated a purported Latin American research center and used unwitting local staff, fabricated documents and synthetic media; the other used seven journalist bylines to pitch articles and generated batches of social-media comments. OpenAI says some content appeared in real media outlets, and estimates the Russian-linked operation at category 5 and the Iranian-linked operation at category 4 on the Brookings Breakout Scale. These are company assessments based partly on its account data and open-source checks, not an independent audit of attribution or reach. Spanish-language reporting confirms that related false content circulated in the region, but the reviewed examples do not establish linkage to these account clusters; one superficially similar Ecuador recruitment fake was reported as circulating in 2025.

What actually changed

OpenAI reports that it banned two account clusters it attributes to separate Russian- and Iranian-origin influence operations. The company says one operated a purported Latin American research center and used unwitting local staff, fabricated documents and synthetic media; the other used seven journalist bylines to pitch articles and generated batches of social-media comments. OpenAI says some content appeared in real media outlets, and estimates the Russian-linked operation at category 5 and the Iranian-linked operation at category 4 on the Brookings Breakout Scale. These are company assessments based partly on its account data and open-source checks, not an independent audit of attribution or reach. Spanish-language reporting confirms that related false content circulated in the region, but the reviewed examples do not establish linkage to these account clusters; one superficially similar Ecuador recruitment fake was reported as circulating in 2025.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — OpenAI publicly reported on 2026-10-08 that it banned two account clusters it associates with separate Iran- and Russia-origin influence operations and shared information with relevant authorities.
  • CLAIMED — OpenAI says the Russian-linked operation used a purported Latin American research center and unwitting staff, while the Iranian-linked operation used seven journalist bylines to pitch articles and generated batches of social-media comments.
  • CLAIMED — OpenAI estimates that the Russian-linked operation reached category 5 and the Iranian-linked operation category 4 on the Brookings Breakout Scale; it reports nearly 100 Iranian-linked articles across about a dozen outlets.
  • DEMONSTRATED — A Spanish-language Ecuador fact-check describes a similar fake recruitment video circulating since 2025-03-24 and published its fact-check on 2026-04-03; this establishes prior circulation of similar content, not a link to OpenAI’s reported account clusters.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Ecuadorian reporting in June 2026 describes a separate forged email directing schools to stage a political event and says the education ministry filed a complaint; that report does not attribute the incident to the account clusters OpenAI describes.

Strongest baseline

OpenAI compares the cases with pre-AI influence operations and its prior reporting on 30 covert operations; no matched comparison isolates how much AI changed cost, output volume, success rate or audience reach.

The caveat that matters

  • Attribution of the account clusters to state-linked operators and the operation descriptions rely on OpenAI’s account telemetry and investigation; the underlying case files are not public.
  • The Brookings Breakout Scale categories in the source are OpenAI's assessments; no independent evaluation of the score or comparative audience reach was conducted.
  • Nearly 100 articles are reported as published or syndicated under bylines linked to the Iranian operation; the precise outlet list, audience totals, and causal effect on readers are not fully established here.
  • OpenAI says internal operator reports used deceptive impact calculations and that some claimed fakes were not found in open sources; counts and claimed reach should not be treated as verified output totals.
  • Spanish-language fact checks and reporting document related or superficially similar false content, but the inspected sources do not link these specific incidents to the two account clusters.
  • Lupa Media's page is dated 2026-04-03 while its text says the video circulated from 2025-03-24; the precise timeline and whether the campaign reused or reintroduced the material remain unresolved.
  • Lupa Media reports that a superficially similar Ecuador recruitment fake circulated from 2025-03-24, before the 2026 campaign period described by OpenAI. This does not disprove a later reuse or the cluster attribution, but it means the reviewed public match cannot establish that OpenAI’s reported actors originated that content.
  • OpenAI itself says operators sometimes claimed credit for public events or content they had not caused, and says it could not corroborate some of the reported fakes.

What remains unknown

  • No independent investigator reviewed the underlying account records or confirmed actor/state attribution for the two clusters.
  • The relationship between OpenAI’s reported campaign examples and similar Latin American false content documented by regional fact-checkers is unresolved; some source dates predate the account-cluster period.
  • The report does not isolate how AI use changed total campaign cost, throughput, publication success or audience impact compared with non-AI influence operations.

FCMO Lens

If the attribution and operational account are accurate, the cases show AI being used as one part of influence workflows that also rely on fake organizations, human intermediaries, fabricated documents and placement in real outlets. The source says model use was often internal reporting or editing rather than generating the full campaign, and reports exaggerated self-measured impact; that distinction matters when assessing what AI contributed and how much reach the operations achieved.
