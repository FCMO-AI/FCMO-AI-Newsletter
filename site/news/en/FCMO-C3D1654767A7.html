Learned classical energies report large efficiency gains on structured reasoning puzzles

A Nov 12 preprint introduces Compact Reasoning Associative Memory (CRAM), a learned pairwise-energy model that solves structured constraint puzzles through recurrent energy descent. The authors report three-seed test results on Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Unique, N-Queens and graph coloring; the abstract reports 99.996% Sudoku-Extreme and 100% Maze-Unique accuracy after 42 ms and 132 ms of training on one H200, respectively. The paper reports at least 1,000× faster fitting than the compared looped models on these tasks and at least 282× fewer Sudoku inference FLOPs at matched baseline accuracy. These are author-reported results on structured puzzle families, not evidence of comparable performance on open-ended reasoning.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors posted arXiv:2611.17309v1 on November 12, 2026, describing a learned energy model called Compact Reasoning Associative Memory (CRAM).
  • Claimed — The authors report three-seed Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Unique, N-Queens and graph-coloring experiments; the abstract reports 99.996% Sudoku-Extreme and 100% Maze-Unique test accuracy, with 42 ms and 132 ms training times on one H200, and at least 1,000× faster fitting than compared looped models.
  • Claimed — The authors report at least 282× fewer Sudoku inference FLOPs than looped baselines at matched baseline accuracy; their comparison includes all search chains and failed searches.
  • Not established — The inspected paper does not establish comparable performance on open-ended reasoning or tasks requiring inferred latent quantities; the authors caution that high accuracy on these structured puzzles does not show scalability to harder tasks.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares with seven looped-model implementations, including HRM, TRM, GRAM, EqR, FRM, TRMP and EBR. It reports three-seed means for most baselines and notes published scores where its reproduction was lower. Fit-time process protocol differs between baselines and CRAM.

Limitations

  • All performance numbers are reported by the paper authors and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • The experiments concern structured constraint-puzzle families; the authors state this does not establish scaling to harder open-ended reasoning tasks.
  • Fit-time comparisons use different process protocols: baseline runs in fresh processes, while CRAM is reported as a median of seven runs after a first run in the same process.
  • No code or artifact audit was performed in this activation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent matched reproduction of the reported training, accuracy or inference-FLOP comparisons was inspected.
  • The paper does not establish transfer beyond structured constraint puzzles to open-ended tasks requiring inferred latent quantities.

Why it matters

If the reported comparisons hold in their stated regime, learned pairwise energies offer a much smaller and faster alternative to deep looped models for some constraint problems. The work also warns that near-perfect scores on simple, rule-structured puzzles may say little about scaling to tasks requiring latent-variable inference; both points can affect architecture choices and benchmark design.
