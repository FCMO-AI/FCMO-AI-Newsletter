What changed

HGM-Dual is a harness-search method that summarizes the effects of earlier edits along a lineage and uses observed constraint failures to generate targeted alternatives alongside a general edit. In author-reported DeepPlanning travel and shopping evaluations, it improves case accuracy over a customized HGM baseline at 4,000 plan evaluations; on shopping it exceeds that baseline’s 4,000-evaluation result with 1,000 evaluations. These are results on two constrained tasks with fixed model choices. Appendix API costs cover task-agent evaluations and depend on cache assumptions; they do not establish total optimization cost or general planning capability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — HGM-Dual augments evolutionary harness search with structured records of edits and their outcomes along a parent lineage, and generates both a general child and error-conditioned children when diagnostic-stability gates are met. The paper leaves the underlying language-model weights fixed.
  • Claimed — On DeepPlanning travel and shopping tasks, the authors report that at a 4,000-plan evaluation budget HGM-Dual exceeds the strongest HGM-CF baseline in case accuracy: 64.38% versus 56.25% on travel and 66.67% versus 60.83% on shopping. These are author-reported results on two constrained tasks, not an independent reproduction or general planning-capability result.
  • Claimed — At 1,000 plan evaluations, the authors report a 70.83% shopping case-accuracy result for HGM-Dual, exceeding HGM-CF's 60.83% result at 4,000 evaluations. The paper estimates task-agent API use for 1,000 sample evaluations at $140–$1,010 depending on task and prompt-cache assumptions; these estimates exclude the editor's optimization cost and are not measured total-dollar savings.
  • Not established — The study does not establish that the reported gains transfer beyond the two DeepPlanning tasks, persist across model choices or prospective benchmarks, or reduce total optimization cost in dollars.

Strongest baseline

The primary comparison is HGM-CF, a customized HGM variant given the same deterministic interface for task-agent code and logs. It is the strongest baseline on the four reported 4,000-evaluation metrics. The paper also compares ADAS, GEPA, vanilla HGM and HGM-CA, but evaluates ADAS and GEPA only at the 1,000-evaluation budget.

Limitations

  • The evaluation covers two constrained DeepPlanning tasks, travel and shopping; transfer to other planning domains is unestablished.
  • The main setup uses fixed GPT-5.4 editor and GPT-5.4-mini task-agent models. The additional Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B task-agent test reports higher composite score but little case-accuracy change; it does not establish broad model transfer.
  • Task-agent API cost values are estimates for sample evaluations under stated prompt-cache assumptions, not measured total optimization cost or realized savings.
  • All performance results are reported by the paper's authors; no independent reproduction was performed in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of HGM-Dual's reported results or analysis was inspected.
  • The study evaluates only travel and shopping tasks from DeepPlanning; prospective transfer to other constrained planning environments is unknown.
  • The appendix estimates task-agent API evaluation cost under prompt-cache assumptions but does not give a complete measured dollar cost including editor calls and search overhead.

Why it matters

Long-horizon planning agents can fail when one constraint violation invalidates an otherwise useful plan. If the reported results generalize, retaining edit-outcome history and targeting recurring failures may help harness search spend a limited evaluation budget more effectively. The current evidence comes from two planning tasks and does not establish broader transfer or total monetary savings.
