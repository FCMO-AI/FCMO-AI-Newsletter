What changed

A September 20 preprint introduces pseudo self-distillation (PSD), which uses outputs from a closed-model oracle as prompt context for a Qwen model acting as a privileged teacher, then trains a student to match its output distribution without access to teacher logits or hidden states. In the authors’ LoCoMo test, a PSD-trained Qwen3-4B receives an overall LLM-judge score of 0.8699 versus 0.849 for the original MEMORA pipeline using GPT-4.1-mini; the paper also reports improvements over its own SFT checkpoint at all three tested Qwen sizes. These are author-reported results. The reported score comparison does not establish total training-plus-inference cost savings.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On the full LoCoMo benchmark test split, the authors report an overall LLM-judge score of 0.8699 for PSD-trained Qwen3-4B, compared with 0.849 for MEMORA using GPT-4.1-mini. The paper reports that the PSD stage improves over SFT at all three tested Qwen3 sizes; these are author-run results, not independent reproductions.
  • Claimed — For LongMemEval, the authors evaluate on a category-balanced 100-question subset of the 500-question benchmark. Qwen3-4B PSD scores 0.77 on this subset, while the paper’s Full Context and Nemori references are 0.656 and 0.746 on the full benchmark; the different evaluation denominators limit direct comparison. The within-paper Base/SFT/PSD checkpoints use the same subset.
  • Not established — The reported benchmark results do not establish lower total cost than repeated GPT-4.1-mini memory construction, because oracle-data generation, fine-tuning and student inference costs are not compared under a matched workload.

Strongest baseline

For the reported overall LoCoMo LLM-judge score, MEMORA with GPT-4.1-mini is 0.849 versus 0.8699 for PSD-trained Qwen3-4B. BLEU and F1 still favor some full-context and larger-backbone systems. LongMemEval baselines are evaluated on all 500 questions while the PSD model uses a 100-question subset, so those cross-system values are not matched.

Limitations

  • The numerical results are reported by the authors and were not independently reproduced.
  • The primary outcome is an LLM-judge score using GPT-4o-mini; no human-judged replication or judge-sensitivity analysis was performed here.
  • LongMemEval’s PSD result uses a 100-question subset, whereas the cited Full Context and Nemori values use the full benchmark.
  • The paper does not measure end-to-end economics including oracle-data generation, fine-tuning and student inference under a matched workload.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of LoCoMo or LongMemEval results was located or performed.
  • Total teacher-data generation, fine-tuning and student inference costs are not compared against repeated GPT-4.1-mini memory construction under the same workload.
  • The LongMemEval PSD result is measured on a 100-question subset while cited Full Context and Nemori results use the full benchmark.

Why it matters

If the reported performance generalizes, black-box distillation may transfer structured memory-construction behavior into smaller models where direct prompting performs poorly. That could change deployment choices for persistent agent memory, but the paper does not compare total oracle generation, fine-tuning and student inference cost with repeated calls to the larger model; its LongMemEval comparison also uses a 100-question subset against baselines evaluated on the full set.
