Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Virginia Governor Abigail D. Spanberger signed Executive Directive 6 on 23 October 2026. It directs the Secretary of Public Safety and Homeland Security to establish a Cyber Consortium and integrate AI-related cybersecurity into the Commonwealth’s Unified Readiness Framework.
  • Demonstrated — Building on Executive Order 22’s AI Task Force, the directive calls for a Commonwealth-wide cyber-attack prevention and response plan and an infrastructure-hardening plan addressing AI-related and other identified cyber risks. It directs the Task Force to engage frontier technology companies, researchers, regulators, private-sector leaders, higher-education institutions and other states.
  • Demonstrated — The directive calls for AI-risk briefings at least quarterly and a tabletop exercise at least annually. It also directs an independent privacy and security review team for Virginia’s interoperable data systems.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The directive establishes planning and engagement instructions, not proof that the consortium, plans, briefings or review have been completed, or that cyber incidents, exposure or costs have fallen.
  • Demonstrated — Virginia’s 18 September Executive Order 22 had already directed the AI Task Force to assess cybersecurity risks to state systems and critical infrastructure, coordinate with frontier developers and outside experts, and support proactive system hardening. Executive Directive 6 adds a Cyber Consortium, integration into the Unified Readiness Framework, statewide plans, quarterly briefings, an annual exercise and an independent review of interoperable state data systems.

Limitations

  • The directive records instructions and intended coordination; it does not document completion or compliance.
  • The document provides no budget, procurement commitment or implementation timeline for the plans beyond immediate preparation and recurring briefings.
  • No independent readiness assessment or measured security outcome was found.
  • Executive Order 22 had already established the AI Task Force’s cybersecurity remit; Executive Directive 6 is a follow-on preparedness and coordination step, not Virginia’s first AI cybersecurity action.

What we still don't know

  • The directive requires planning and engagement, but this review found no published statewide plan, consortium membership record or completed briefing.
  • No evidence establishes that the directive improves readiness, hardens infrastructure or reduces AI-related cyber incidents or costs.

Why it matters

Virginia had already directed its AI Task Force to assess cybersecurity risks, harden systems and coordinate with frontier developers. Executive Directive 6 adds a statewide preparedness structure and recurring planning and briefing duties through the Unified Readiness Framework. It is a follow-on governance step; the orders do not establish completed plans, participation or improved security outcomes.
