Authors report unified likelihood-based training with accuracy, coverage and retention tradeoffs

A 67-page preprint frames pretraining, midtraining, supervised fine-tuning and suitable reinforcement-learning updates as likelihood fitting over a time-varying mixture of static data and successful model-generated targets. Its shared objective and trainer allow those sources to overlap; conventional staged training is a special case. The authors report controlled 124M-parameter reasoning-puzzle experiments and heterogeneous-data experiments with a 0.76B-parameter model. They report better accuracy, coverage, retention or continual-learning outcomes in several evaluated settings, alongside task and schedule tradeoffs. The paper explicitly describes the results as small-scale and does not establish predictive scaling laws or a universally preferred data mixture.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In controlled experiments with a 124M-parameter model and reasoning puzzles, the authors report settings where unified training improves accuracy, coverage or generalization under matched training-compute comparisons, while other evaluated settings favor staged training on some metrics.
  • Claimed — In heterogeneous-data experiments with a 0.76B-parameter model, the authors report retention benefits and a stronger starting point for subsequent coding reinforcement learning in tested mixtures, with tradeoffs across accuracy and coverage.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, a predictive scaling law, a universally preferred data mixture, or transfer of its measured effects to frontier-scale models and varied multi-task training regimes.

Strongest baseline

Within the controlled experiment, staged training uses the same pretraining and midtraining sources, MaxRL objective, RL-update budgets and evaluation tasks; authors select the staged initialization by its final RL accuracy. Compute-frontier comparisons allow the best observed checkpoint under each total budget. In the heterogeneous experiment, staged and unified runs start from the described base model and use the same subsequent coding RL configuration; the paper includes an asynchronous-trainer 0% mixing ablation.

Limitations

  • All numerical outcomes in this record are author-reported; no independent reproduction or code artifact was inspected.
  • The controlled study uses a 124M-parameter model and synthetic verifiable reasoning puzzles; the separate heterogeneous-data study uses a 0.76B-parameter model. Neither establishes transfer to frontier-scale models.
  • The paper explicitly describes its empirical scope as small-scale, does not establish predictive scaling laws or a universally preferred mixture, and reports tradeoffs among accuracy, coverage and retention.
  • Most runs optimize a single RL task at a time. The results do not establish joint multi-task training behavior or advantages over long sequences of subsequent tasks.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the paper's controlled or heterogeneous-data results was found in this review.
  • The paper's experiments cover 124M and 0.76B models and mainly one RL task at a time; scaling behavior and joint multi-task transfer remain unestablished.
  • The paper does not identify a generally optimal mixture or schedule, and does not establish a predictive method for selecting mixtures from model/task properties.

Why it matters

If the reported results transfer across tasks and model scales, teams could treat data allocation across pretraining and post-training as one coupled design problem instead of selecting separate training algorithms for each stage. The evidence supports a potentially useful training framework and measured small-scale tradeoffs, not a general replacement recipe for frontier-model training.
