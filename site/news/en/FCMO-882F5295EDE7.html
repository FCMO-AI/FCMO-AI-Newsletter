State-Grounded Conditioning reports lower direction drift in a live game-coaching agent

A double-blind preprint introduces State-Grounded Conditioning (SGC), which moves state-dependent tool and response control into three explicit wrappers for intent/context, live fact slots, and session history. On 200 anonymized production sessions (~1,000 turns) from one in-game coaching system, the authors report that Full SGC reached 96.7% turn-level grounded accuracy and 83.5% session-level grounded accuracy, versus 69.8% and 26.5% for their Plan-and-Execute agent baseline; mean first-token latency was 1.5 s versus 6.1 s. Test labels used two independent human annotators with third-annotator adjudication. This is a bounded, author-reported result in one domain. The baseline had less product-specific prompt tuning, some reliability statistics and confidence intervals are deferred to camera-ready, and no transfer outside gaming or direct user outcome is demonstrated.

What changed

A double-blind preprint introduces State-Grounded Conditioning (SGC), which moves state-dependent tool and response control into three explicit wrappers for intent/context, live fact slots, and session history. On 200 anonymized production sessions (~1,000 turns) from one in-game coaching system, the authors report that Full SGC reached 96.7% turn-level grounded accuracy and 83.5% session-level grounded accuracy, versus 69.8% and 26.5% for their Plan-and-Execute agent baseline; mean first-token latency was 1.5 s versus 6.1 s. Test labels used two independent human annotators with third-annotator adjudication. This is a bounded, author-reported result in one domain. The baseline had less product-specific prompt tuning, some reliability statistics and confidence intervals are deferred to camera-ready, and no transfer outside gaming or direct user outcome is demonstrated.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors propose State-Grounded Conditioning with three wrappers that externalize intent/tool selection, live fact-slot grounding, and cross-turn dialogue control into explicit state-dependent interfaces.
  • Claimed — On 200 anonymized production sessions sampled over approximately four weeks (~1,000 turns) from one in-game coaching system, Full SGC reports 96.7% turn-level grounded accuracy and 83.5% session-level grounded accuracy, versus 69.8% and 26.5% for PE-Agent; mean first-token latency is 1.5 s versus 6.1 s.
  • Not established — The preprint reports no experimental validation outside the game-coaching deployment, discloses that PE-Agent did not receive the same product-specific prompt tuning as SGC, and defers full agreement estimates, some confidence intervals, and the complete discordant tables to camera-ready supplementary material.
  • Not established — The reported model-output metrics do not establish improvement in user retention, satisfaction, in-game performance, or other direct user outcomes.

Strongest baseline

PE-Agent shares the same LLM backbone, tool schemas, grounding references, and serving setup, and reports 69.8% turn-level and 26.5% session-level grounding with 6.1 s mean first-token latency. The authors disclose that PE-Agent did not receive the same product-specific prompt tuning during deployment iteration, so the reported gap combines architecture and tuning effects.

Limitations

  • Evidence comes from one in-game coaching deployment. Transfer to the three discussed domains is analytical, not experimentally validated.
  • PE-Agent followed its published specification but did not receive the same iteration of product-specific prompt tuning as SGC; some measured difference may reflect architecture plus tuning.
  • Full 200-session inter-annotator agreement estimates, some 95% confidence intervals, and complete discordant-pair tables are deferred to camera-ready supplementary material; pilot agreement statistics are reported for a 50-session subset.
  • The work reports model-output quality and mean first-token latency, not retention, re-engagement, satisfaction, or in-match performance.
  • Operational applicability requires live user-state constraints, measurable direction-drift cost, and machine-readable state slices; the paper says simpler one-shot or low-stakes tasks may need only a generic harness or retrieval.

What we still don't know

  • The reported accuracy and latency results have not been independently reproduced or tested in a second product/domain.
  • The baseline comparison is not effort-matched for product-specific prompt tuning, and full agreement/CI statistics are deferred to camera-ready material.

Why it matters

If the reported effect holds in similar stateful agents, deterministic wrappers around live intent, facts, and session memory can reduce direction drift while avoiding a serial plan-execute latency path. The result may affect architecture choices for interactive agents where state changes and multi-turn guidance matter; it does not establish general gains across agent domains.
