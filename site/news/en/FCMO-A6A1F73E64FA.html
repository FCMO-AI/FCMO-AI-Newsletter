What changed

A September 2026 paper reports a temporal holdout study of partial evaluation for one production analytics agent: 574 runs of a 519-question benchmark were split evenly between calibration and held-out periods. It compares random sampling, historical caching, fixed subsets and adaptive testing. At 200 executed questions, the paper reports 1.37 percentage points of pass-rate mean absolute error in its results section, while its abstract reports 1.03 points for that budget. Difficulty-stratified fixed subsets were deployed for simplicity and tested on five additional agent families. These are author-reported results from one organization and task; the paper does not establish broad external reliability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report using 574 historical benchmark runs over 52 days, split chronologically into 287 calibration and 287 held-out runs, for one production analytics agent. Its central benchmark has 519 questions and takes about three hours per full run.
  • Disputed — At 200 questions (38.5% of the benchmark), the abstract reports 1.03 percentage points of mean absolute error for multidimensional 2PL adaptive testing, while Section 5.1 reports 1.37 points for adaptive testing. The source does not reconcile these figures.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that difficulty-stratified fixed subsets performed best at smaller budgets, and that the authors deployed this method for operational simplicity. It reports transfer without recalibration to five other agent families; the paper describes 299 pooled transfer runs.
  • Claimed — For the held-out runs, the authors report adaptive testing improving score fidelity at larger question budgets and difficulty-stratified subsets providing strong ranking fidelity at smaller budgets; historical caching showed weaker score fidelity at comparable execution fractions but preserved rankings better than score error alone suggests.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper, random sampling, historical caching, difficulty-stratified fixed subsets and adaptive testing are compared against full-run scores on the same 287 held-out runs. External baselines are not established.

Limitations

  • This is one production analytics agent’s benchmark and a single organization’s historical data; task and organizational transfer are not established broadly.
  • The paper's abstract and results section disagree on the 200-question adaptive-testing MAE (1.03pp versus 1.37pp); no resolution was found in the inspected source.
  • The results are reported by the paper authors; this activation did not inspect code/data artifacts or independently reproduce the comparisons.
  • Cross-agent transfer uses five anonymized agent families and 299 pooled runs; family-specific independence and external replication are not established here.
  • Internal source inconsistency: arXiv HTML abstract (lines 75–77) reports 1.03pp MAE at 200/519 questions for multidimensional 2PL adaptive testing, while Section 5.1 (lines 239–246) reports 1.37pp at the same budget. The discrepancy remains unresolved.

What we still don't know

  • The abstract and Section 5.1 report different 200-question adaptive-testing MAE values (1.03pp and 1.37pp).
  • The paper reports results from one organization’s production analytics agent and anonymized transfer families; independent external validation is absent in this review.
  • Underlying run-level data and implementation artifacts were not inspected in this activation, so analysis cannot be independently audited from the recorded source locators alone.

Why it matters

Repeated full agent benchmarks can be costly; if these results transfer, difficulty-stratified subsets offer one practical way to reduce recurring evaluation work while tracking score and ranking changes. The evidence is bounded to one production analytics benchmark, and the paper gives conflicting MAE figures for the 200-question adaptive-testing result.
