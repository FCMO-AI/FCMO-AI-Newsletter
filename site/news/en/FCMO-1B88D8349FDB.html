Preprint reports a visual-grounding refusal gap across five vision-language models

A 5 October 2026 preprint evaluates five vision-language models on 15,401 matched harmful visual requests, varying whether each request asks for a text answer or a spatial grounding such as a point or box. The authors report that grounding refusal trails text-answer refusal by 31–59 percentage points depending on the domain, and that safety system prompts do not close the gap. A fine-tuning recipe combining grounding-form refusals, capability data and benign examples reportedly improves refusal on two open models while tracking over-refusal and grounding capability. This is a single author-run preprint scored by one LLM judge; 14,578 of 15,401 pairs are from the social-bias dataset, code is not released, and real-world harm incidence is not established.

What actually changed

A 5 October 2026 preprint evaluates five vision-language models on 15,401 matched harmful visual requests, varying whether each request asks for a text answer or a spatial grounding such as a point or box. The authors report that grounding refusal trails text-answer refusal by 31–59 percentage points depending on the domain, and that safety system prompts do not close the gap. A fine-tuning recipe combining grounding-form refusals, capability data and benign examples reportedly improves refusal on two open models while tracking over-refusal and grounding capability. This is a single author-run preprint scored by one LLM judge; 14,578 of 15,401 pairs are from the social-bias dataset, code is not released, and real-world harm incidence is not established.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — Across five evaluated VLMs and three repurposed safety domains, authors report lower refusal when matched harmful visual requests ask for a point or box than when they ask for a text answer.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The 15,401 matched pairs comprise 659 VLSU, 14,578 BBQ-V and 164 Asimov-2.0 items; one Gemini 3.5 Flash LLM judge scores refusal/compliance.
  • CLAIMED — Authors report a fine-tuning recipe improves grounding refusal on Qwen3-VL-8B and VisionReasoner-7B while separately measuring benign over-refusal and PointArena capability.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper does not establish real-world harmful-action incidence or broad deployment prevalence.

Strongest baseline

Matched VQA-mode requests using the same image and harmful intent; for training, each model’s own pre-fine-tuning behavior. The paper also compares prompting and reports it does not close the gap, with some benign over-refusal trade-offs.

The caveat that matters

  • Results are author-reported in one preprint and scored by a single LLM judge; no independent replication was recovered.
  • The dataset mix is highly imbalanced: BBQ-V contributes 14,578 of 15,401 matched pairs, while VLSU contributes 659 and Asimov-2.0 contributes 164.
  • Only five models are evaluated and fine-tuning results cover two open models.
  • Code and checkpoints were not available for this review; authors say code will follow acceptance.
  • No real-world incidence, downstream agent action or robot outcome is measured.
  • Repurposed benchmark prompts and model-native output templates may affect results; the study does not settle transfer across models or deployment settings.

What remains unknown

  • The matched output-mode gap and proposed fine-tuning mitigation have not been independently replicated with human-validated labels and multiple judges.
  • The benchmark results do not measure real downstream harmful actions or general prevalence.

FCMO Lens

When a model’s coordinates are consumed by a GUI agent or robot, a refusal policy that works for text may not carry over to the actionable spatial output. The paper motivates evaluating matched response modes and tracking safety alongside benign over-refusal and grounding capability. Its results do not establish deployment prevalence or real-world harm; the dataset mix, one-judge evaluation and small model sample need independent testing.
