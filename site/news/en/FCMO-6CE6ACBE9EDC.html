What actually changed

A September 18 preprint defines root-scoped authorization quiescence for agents whose work can survive in queues, callbacks, delegated credentials and provider operations. It combines a durable root-epoch cut, sink-side fences, carrier accounting and provider evidence into a bounded certificate. The paper proves conditional safety properties and reports a provider-free 17-case late-effect suite plus a separately implemented checker; the results were not independently reproduced in this review.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors define a root-scoped quiescence certificate that accounts for registered old-root carriers and pre-fence acceptances, excludes later protected acceptance under the retired root, and allows exact rebind to a current independent authorization witness.
  • CLAIMED — Under the paper’s stated mediation, manifest, and channel-accounting assumptions, the authors prove post-cut issuer non-expansion, support-sound projection, compositional soundness, independent-support preservation, merge-order independence, and crash/replay stability.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports 17/17 registered outcomes from a provider-free late-effect suite, agreement by a separately implemented checker on all 17 traces, and rejection of 44/44 consistently rehashed semantic regressions.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The paper does not demonstrate behavior with remote MCP, A2A, OAuth, scheduler, or other provider implementations, nor does it establish global system idleness, rollback, business completion, or effects outside the declared mediation boundary.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares cancellation acknowledgement alone and root cut without sink fencing against the same scheduled unit and local protected-effect oracle under cut-plus-fence. Its cited RISU v0.3 artifact is a complementary fixed-profile evidence verifier, not an active protocol or provider integration; no matched tests against remote provider implementations are reported.

The caveat that matters

  • All formal and evaluation claims are reported by the paper authors; the proof and artifact were not independently audited or reproduced here.
  • The test suite is provider-free and uses a local protected sink; endpoint labels do not establish actual MCP, A2A, OAuth, scheduler or cloud-provider behavior.
  • The guarantee is relative to a retired authorization root and complete bounded manifest; it does not mean global idleness, rollback, business completion or physical cessation.
  • Correctness depends on complete mediation/accounting, sound sink fences and exact cross-provider channel conservation; opaque endpoints may leave the result indeterminate.
  • The paper’s cited RISU Bounded Agent Closure v0.3 specification was inspected and appears complementary in scope: it verifies submitted evidence for a fixed profile but expressly has no actuators, integrations, orchestration or distributed protocol. Neither source establishes a deployed end-to-end provider implementation.

What remains unknown

  • No independent execution of the proof checker or registered late-effect suite was performed.
  • No implementation or test evidence was inspected for actual remote provider adapters or production agent runtimes.
  • The paper cites RISU Bounded Agent Closure v0.3, whose Phase-1 specification verifies submitted closure evidence for a fixed profile and explicitly excludes actuators, integrations, orchestration and distributed protocols; the paper presents its active cut/fencing protocol as complementary. No comparison with a deployed orchestration system or actual provider adapter was performed.

FCMO Lens

If the method holds under real provider adapters, it gives agent systems a more precise shutdown guarantee than cancellation acknowledgement or token revocation alone, while preserving work that has an independent authorization root. The paper explicitly limits its guarantee to registered, completely mediated paths, so it does not establish global idleness or real-world deployment safety.
