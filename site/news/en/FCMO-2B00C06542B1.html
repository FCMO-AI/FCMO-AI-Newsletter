BarrierFormer reports predictive safety control with low simulated inference latency

BarrierFormer trains a causal transformer to predict short state rollouts and enforce control-barrier constraints through a training-time safety teacher, removing online optimization at inference. The authors compare it with four reimplemented baselines across a double integrator, Dubins car and Crazyflie simulator, using 32 environments and three seeds. Their learned-dynamics variant reports 95.83%–96.88% safety on two 2D systems and 96.88% on the 3D system, with per-step batched inference around 4.79–5.12 microseconds on one workstation. This is a simulation result: training still uses a black-box simulator, performance degrades as complexity rises, and the authors warn of a potentially large simulation-to-real gap.

What changed

BarrierFormer trains a causal transformer to predict short state rollouts and enforce control-barrier constraints through a training-time safety teacher, removing online optimization at inference. The authors compare it with four reimplemented baselines across a double integrator, Dubins car and Crazyflie simulator, using 32 environments and three seeds. Their learned-dynamics variant reports 95.83%–96.88% safety on two 2D systems and 96.88% on the 3D system, with per-step batched inference around 4.79–5.12 microseconds on one workstation. This is a simulation result: training still uses a black-box simulator, performance degrades as complexity rises, and the authors warn of a potentially large simulation-to-real gap.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In three simulated systems evaluated over 32 environments and three seeds, the authors report learned-dynamics BarrierFormer safety rates of 95.83% for Double Integrator, 95.83% for Dubins Car and 96.88% for Crazyflie, with success rates of 88.54%, 92.71% and 89.58%, respectively.
  • Claimed — The authors report per-step inference times of 4.79–5.12 microseconds for the learned-dynamics variant on one RTX 4000 Ada workstation, using batched evaluation across 32 environments; model-predictive control takes 2,773–9,280 microseconds in the same reported setup.
  • Not established — The results do not establish safety on physical robots: training depends on a black-box simulator, the paper reports performance decreases with problem complexity, and identifies simulation-to-real transfer as an open risk.

Strongest baseline

Four baselines are reimplemented under common dynamics, observations and obstacle settings: CBF-RL, CoBL-Diffusion, ConBaT and model-predictive control. BarrierFormer's learned-dynamics variant provides the strongest model-free balance of safety and goal-reaching in the reported table; the simulator-trained variant is compared against MPC.

Limitations

  • All safety and latency results are paper-reported simulation outcomes; the research desk did not reproduce them.
  • The learned-dynamics policy is trained using a black-box simulator; the paper does not demonstrate physical-robot safety or sim-to-real transfer.
  • Only three simulated systems are evaluated; authors report performance loss as complexity rises from linear to nonlinear and from 2D to 3D.
  • Latency is measured on one RTX 4000 Ada workstation using batches of 32 environments and averaged across a 256-step rollout; it is not embedded-robot single-environment latency.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the simulation results or latency measurements was performed.
  • The method has no reported physical-robot evaluation and may have a substantial simulation-to-real gap.
  • Performance declines as the simulated problem moves from linear to nonlinear and 2D to 3D; robustness to noise is bounded in the appendix.

Why it matters

The paper tests whether multi-step safety constraints can be learned into a policy to avoid online optimization costs. If the simulation results transfer, predictive safety could be cheaper at runtime for robots with partial observations. Current evidence is limited to three simulated dynamics and one workstation; it does not establish physical-robot safety or real-time behavior on embedded hardware.
