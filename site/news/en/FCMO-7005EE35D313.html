Strongest baseline

Amazon reports AWS net sales increased 39% year over year to $46.0B and AWS operating income was $17.8B versus $11.4B a year earlier. No matched AI-only revenue or cost baseline is disclosed.

Limitations

  • All product usage and customer figures are issuer-reported.
  • AWS revenue and segment operating income aggregate AI and non-AI cloud workloads.
  • The release gives Bedrock growth multiples without starting or ending token-volume and spending baselines.
  • No independent telemetry, earnings-call replay, or Q3 Form 10-Q review was completed in this activation.
  • Capital expenditure attributed to AI does not itself establish productive utilization or positive returns.
  • The approximately $220 billion 2026 cash-capex outlook is company-wide guidance; Amazon does not allocate it to AI or AWS or establish productive utilization or returns.

What we still don't know

  • Amazon’s release provides Bedrock spending and token-volume growth multiples without absolute baselines, cohorts, or a breakdown of AWS revenue and costs attributable to AI services.
  • The reported AgentCore customer and deployment counts do not define active production use, retention, workload intensity or monetization.
  • The company attributes most of the year-over-year increase in net property and equipment purchases to AI investment and expects approximately $220 billion in company-wide cash capital expenditures for 2026, but does not allocate spend across AWS, AI services or capacity already utilized.

Why it matters

If sustained, the disclosure points to fast growth in a major cloud provider’s infrastructure business and use of its managed model and agent services. The reported metrics can inform compute-demand research, but aggregate AWS sales and issuer-defined usage multiples do not quantify AI revenue, costs or returns.
