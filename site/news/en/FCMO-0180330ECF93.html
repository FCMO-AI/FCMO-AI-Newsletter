DataSpace reports large harness-dependent accuracy differences on heterogeneous data-agent tasks

The paper introduces DataSpace, a benchmark for data agents that must combine evidence scattered across heterogeneous task workspaces and return complete, verifiable tables. Its 410 cross-language tasks use 7,439 artifacts totaling 15.01 GB across six file and media types. In author-run comparisons, six backbones reach a best accuracy of 66.34% with DataSpace-Agent fixed; with MiMo-V2.5 fixed, five harnesses range from 30.98% to 46.34%, a 15.36-point spread. Authors also report that multimodal evidence integration and joins reduce accuracy across all tested backbones, and that 76 tasks are missed by all six. Evidence

  • Claimed — DataSpace is a benchmark of 410 cross-language tasks over task-local heterogeneous workspaces, with 7,439 artifacts totaling 15.01 GB across CSV, JSON, SQLite, Markdown, PDF and video; the task output is a complete verifiable table.
  • Claimed — With MiMo-V2.5 fixed, the authors report task accuracy from 30.98% for Smolagents to 46.34% for Grok Build, a 15.36 percentage-point spread across five tested harnesses. With DataSpace-Agent fixed, the best of six tested backbones reaches 66.34%.
  • Claimed — The authors report that multimodal evidence integration and joins reduce accuracy across all six tested backbones; 76 of 410 tasks are missed by every backbone in the reported comparison.
  • Not established — The benchmark does not establish that harness choice generally matters more than model choice, nor that the measured spread transfers to other datasets, model versions, harness configurations or live organizational analytics.

Strongest baseline

For harness comparison, the fixed MiMo-V2.5 backbone with Smolagents reports 30.98% accuracy; Grok Build reports 46.34%. For backbone comparison with DataSpace-Agent fixed, GPT-5.6 Sol reports 64.63% and Grok 4.5 reports 66.34%. These are separate controlled blocks, not a factorial estimate of model-by-harness effects.

Limitations

  • All benchmark construction and model results are reported in one paper; no independent reproduction was located or run.
  • The 15.36-point harness spread is measured on one 410-task benchmark, one fixed MiMo-V2.5 backbone and five specific systems/versions.
  • The separate backbone comparison fixes DataSpace-Agent; the two experiment blocks do not estimate a full model-by-harness interaction.
  • Task content and artifact distribution bound external validity; live organizational analytics may differ in data quality, access and user behavior.
  • Benchmark accuracy measures complete-table correctness and does not measure user utility, safe access control or operational reliability in deployment.

What we still don't know

  • The task benchmark and reported model/harness rankings have not been independently reproduced.
  • The separate fixed-backbone and fixed-harness comparisons do not identify the model-by-harness interaction across all tested combinations.
  • Transfer to live organizational workspaces and user utility is not measured by exact-table benchmark accuracy.

Why it matters

Data-agent evaluations that isolate SQL, retrieval or open-ended answers can miss failures when an agent must locate, reconcile and join evidence across databases, files, documents and media. A benchmark with deterministic complete-table scoring can make such failures easier to compare. The reported harness spread suggests that integrated agent design affects outcomes in this task regime, but does not establish a universal ranking or live-workplace performance.
