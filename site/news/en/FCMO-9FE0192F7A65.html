Paper reports a 5B controllable video world model streaming at 24 fps on one L20

An arXiv technical report describes Astronex-World 1.0, a 5.35B-parameter model built on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B prior with camera and 64-dimensional action conditioning. The authors report that its causal form generates 832×480 video at 24 fps in real time on one NVIDIA L20 48 GB GPU, after a five-stage post-training recipe run on two such GPUs. On the authors' WBench Full 289 evaluation it scores 70.0, compared with 68.9 for YUME 1.5 (also post-trained from the same 5B prior) and 69.7 for 14B Helios; the paper also reports a weaker interaction score and long-horizon drift. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced findings.

What actually changed

An arXiv technical report describes Astronex-World 1.0, a 5.35B-parameter model built on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B prior with camera and 64-dimensional action conditioning. The authors report that its causal form generates 832×480 video at 24 fps in real time on one NVIDIA L20 48 GB GPU, after a five-stage post-training recipe run on two such GPUs. On the authors' WBench Full 289 evaluation it scores 70.0, compared with 68.9 for YUME 1.5 (also post-trained from the same 5B prior) and 69.7 for 14B Helios; the paper also reports a weaker interaction score and long-horizon drift. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced findings.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that Astronex-World-5B, a 5.351B-parameter model based on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, supports text/image-to-video generation with camera intrinsics/extrinsics, 64-D continuous actions and embodiment identifiers; its causal form uses block-causal attention and KV caching.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports 832×480 output at 24 fps and real-time causal streaming on one NVIDIA L20 48 GB GPU; the five-stage post-training recipe ran on two NVIDIA L20 48 GB GPUs.
  • CLAIMED — On WBench Full 289, the authors report an average score of 70.0 (Quality 78.3, Setting 73.8, Interaction 47.6, Consistency 82.4, Physical 68.1). Their table gives 68.9 for 5B YUME 1.5, 69.7 for 14B Helios and 70.9 for 22B LTX-2.3; the authors' comparison is benchmark- and protocol-specific.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports VBench scores on partial runs covering 240/6,220 text-to-video videos and 118/5,590 image-to-video videos, and identifies long-horizon color/structure drift, weak complex interaction and motion suppression after distillation as limitations.

Strongest baseline

On the paper's WBench Full 289 comparison table (leaderboard snapshot dated 2026-09-13), Astronex-World scores 70.0 versus YUME 1.5 at 68.9, LongCat-Video at 69.9, 14B Helios at 69.7 and 22B LTX-2.3 at 70.9. The paper says YUME is post-trained from the same Wan2.2 5B prior; evaluation provenance and hardware differ across systems, so this is not a controlled matched comparison.

The caveat that matters

  • All performance and benchmark results are author-reported in one arXiv v1 technical report; the research desk did not reproduce them or execute the public repository.
  • WBench Full results use the paper's adapter and a Volcengine Doubao VLM judge; baseline scores are drawn from a September 13, 2026 live-leaderboard snapshot, and hardware/training provenance varies by model.
  • VBench coverage is partial: 240 of 6,220 T2V videos and 118 of 5,590 I2V videos had been generated and scored at writing time.
  • The reported full-split Interaction score is 47.6; the paper identifies limited text-switch event control, long-horizon drift, motion suppression and limited physical fidelity.
  • The action-sequence output path is reserved for downstream post-training; the paper does not demonstrate autonomous-driving or robot task success, and visual coherence is not proof of physical accuracy.
  • The project uses the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B prior; reported post-training GPU count does not account for all compute used to create the base prior.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of real-time throughput, WBench scores or the five-stage post-training result was performed.
  • WBench comparison uses author evaluation against a live leaderboard snapshot with different model and hardware provenance; relative advantage is not a fully matched comparison.
  • The paper's VBench results cover only a small fraction of both official prompt suites, and downstream control utility is not measured.

FCMO Lens

If reproduced, the combination of camera/action-conditioned video generation, causal streaming and reported WBench performance from a 5B model post-trained on two L20 GPUs could make interactive world-model experiments more accessible. The comparisons are protocol-bound: WBench Full uses an author-run evaluation and VLM judge, while VBench coverage is only 240 of 6,220 text-to-video and 118 of 5,590 image-to-video prompts. The paper does not demonstrate reliable physical simulation or robot/driving control.
