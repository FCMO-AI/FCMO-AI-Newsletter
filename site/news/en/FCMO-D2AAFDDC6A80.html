Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Cognizant published a report and its author linked a public MIT-licensed repository for a 75M-parameter ARC-AGI-specialized autoregressive transformer.
  • Claimed — Cognizant reports about 76% pass@2 on the ARC-AGI-1 public evaluation set after a little over four hours of training plus evaluation on one NVIDIA H100; it reports about 80% for an eight-model ensemble, approximately 17%/22% on ARC-AGI-2 for one model/ensemble, and about 44% for a faster approximately 17-minute ARC-AGI-1 configuration.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The report says scores are from the public evaluation set, not ARC-AGI’s private leaderboard. The repository says headline scores average multiple seeds, while example pages display best-seed scores among eight. No independent reproduction or code audit was performed.

Strongest baseline

The post compares the ARC-AGI-1 public-set estimate with reported large-model scores, while noting different capabilities and cost accounting. No matched model, prompt, candidate-budget, training-cost and scoring-harness control is established.

Limitations

  • All benchmark scores and compute times are first-party claims; code was not run and no independent reproduction was performed.
  • Evaluation is on ARC-AGI public evaluation tasks, not the private leaderboard; the author describes them as estimates and cautions that comparisons depend on evaluation settings and cost accounting.
  • The report compares score ranges with much larger models, but the tasks, prompting, test-time procedure and training scope are not matched to general frontier systems. This is not evidence of broad capability parity.
  • The repository README says headline results average multiple seeds; linked example pages show best-seed outputs among eight. The exact summary aggregation and variance were not reconstructed here.
  • The benchmark contains evaluation demonstrations in the ARC-related training data while held-out test outputs are excluded according to the report/repository; no independent contamination audit was performed.
  • The 17-minute configuration reaches about 44%, showing a material score-compute tradeoff; 76% should not be presented as the result of the faster recipe.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the reported ARC-AGI-1/2 scores and training times has not been found or performed.
  • Exact score aggregation, uncertainty across seeds and parity of the public evaluation harness with ARC Prize reporting remain unresolved.
  • The public report does not provide a matched frontier-model comparison with identical test-time candidate budget and total training-plus-inference cost.

Why it matters

The report is a concrete case where a task-structured 75M model and four hours of single-GPU work are claimed to reach a high ARC-AGI-1 public-set score. If independently reproduced under a clean split, this would inform how much specialized representation, augmentation, multi-token prediction and candidate selection can substitute for broad pretraining on one structured reasoning benchmark. It does not show general reasoning parity with frontier models: ARC-AGI-2 scores are lower, the evaluation is public-set only, and no matched direct baseline or independent run was established.
