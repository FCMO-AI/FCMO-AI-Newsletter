StoreBench measures long-horizon commerce agents against human and scripted operators

An arXiv paper introduces StoreBench, a replayable live-commerce simulation with 11 apparel-store tasks, per-cell scripted anchors and a matched tool interface for models and human operators. In the authors’ 33 short-horizon task-seed cells, the best evaluated model averaged 0.700 composite versus 0.708 for sampled human experts and 0.764 for a scripted smart-triage policy; the benchmark’s calibrated pass rates were 49% and 39% for model and humans, respectively, versus 97% for smart-triage. A separate single-model GRPO run improved held-out task mean from 0.136 to 0.373 but passed only 3 of 99 episodes and remained below the checklist policy on all tasks. These are paper-reported simulation results; the full environment and evaluation suite are withheld, and the study does not establish real-store performance.

What actually changed

An arXiv paper introduces StoreBench, a replayable live-commerce simulation with 11 apparel-store tasks, per-cell scripted anchors and a matched tool interface for models and human operators. In the authors’ 33 short-horizon task-seed cells, the best evaluated model averaged 0.700 composite versus 0.708 for sampled human experts and 0.764 for a scripted smart-triage policy; the benchmark’s calibrated pass rates were 49% and 39% for model and humans, respectively, versus 97% for smart-triage. A separate single-model GRPO run improved held-out task mean from 0.136 to 0.373 but passed only 3 of 99 episodes and remained below the checklist policy on all tasks. These are paper-reported simulation results; the full environment and evaluation suite are withheld, and the study does not establish real-store performance.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The authors published StoreBench, a deterministic, replayable apparel-store simulation with 11 short-horizon tasks, 29 merchant tools, calibrated scripted anchor policies, and a separately evaluated full-year task.
  • CLAIMED — Across 11 tasks, three reported seeds and three attempts, the paper reports mean composite scores of 0.700 for its best model, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 0.708 for sampled human experts and 0.764 for the scripted smart-triage policy. The corresponding calibrated pass rates are 49%, 39% and 97%.
  • CLAIMED — In a separate GRPO post-training run on Qwen3.5-27B, the paper reports held-out-task mean composite improvement from 0.136 to 0.373 across 99 episodes; the trained checkpoint passed 3/99 episodes and remained below the rule-based anchor on all eleven tasks.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The authors state that they release five training-split tasks, sample trajectories and scoring tools, while withholding the simulation engine and evaluation suite; the paper describes its scope as a parametric direct-to-consumer apparel store.

Strongest baseline

Scripted smart-triage operator mean composite 0.764 and 97% pass rate; human expert sample mean 0.708 and 39% pass. These baselines are all within the paper’s simulated store and scoring design.

The caveat that matters

  • All performance numbers are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • The human comparison uses one selected human outcome per cell versus three model attempts and allows a human retry.
  • The 365-day task uses a different harness and reasoning configuration than the short-horizon suite.
  • The full environment and held-out evaluation suite are withheld; the paper limits scope to a parametric apparel store, without advertising, learned competitors or market price discovery.
  • The post-training result is one hand-adjusted GRPO run on one base model; it improves scores but leaves 96 of 99 episodes below the pass bar.
  • Human experts had one selected outcome per cell while models were averaged over three attempts; participants could retry, so the human-versus-model comparison is not a fully symmetric estimate.
  • The full-year task uses a different harness and reasoning setup from the short-horizon suite; the paper explicitly says their scores cannot isolate harness effects.
  • GRPO results come from one base model and one hand-adjusted training run, and the checkpoint still passed only 3 of 99 episodes.

What remains unknown

  • The complete simulation engine and held-out evaluation suite are not public, limiting independent reproduction of the leaderboard.
  • The simulated direct-to-consumer apparel store omits advertising, learned competitors and market price discovery; real-store transfer is unknown.
  • The reported post-training gain rests on one hand-adjusted run for one model, and the trained policy still rarely meets the benchmark pass threshold.

FCMO Lens

The benchmark puts long-horizon business judgment, service reliability and continued operation into one agent task, and compares model operators with deterministic policies and human experts on the same simulated tools. Its reported results expose a gap between improving a composite score and reliably meeting task thresholds. The tested store is a bounded simulation, so the paper does not show how agents would perform in live commerce.
