Evidence

  • Claimed — ONS reports that 30% of businesses said they used at least one AI technology in September 2026, based on Wave 165 of its Business Insights and Conditions Survey. This is an ONS-reported survey estimate, not a causal or productivity result.
  • Demonstrated — ONS states that Wave 165 ran from 21 September to 4 October 2026, sampled 38,592 businesses and received 9,933 responses (25.7%); the bulletin identifies the statistics as official statistics in development.
  • Not established — The survey estimate does not establish productivity gains, causal economic impact, or AI use across every UK business; the multi-year trend and large-business subgroup are not assessed in this record.

Limitations

  • The 30% figure is self-reported survey usage for September 2026, not measured productivity, economic impact or causal effect.
  • Wave 165 had a 25.7% response rate; responses are voluntary, and ONS classifies these figures as official statistics in development.
  • The bulletin conflicts on the employee threshold for the large-business subgroup; that subgroup is not included.
  • No confidence interval for the composite “at least one AI technology” estimate is present in the inspected Wave 165 aggregate workbook; its individual category intervals cannot be combined because responses may overlap.

What we still don't know

  • The Wave 165 confidence-interval workbook gives intervals for individual AI technology responses, but its All businesses row does not provide an interval for the bulletin’s composite “at least one AI technology” estimate. The composite confidence interval is therefore not reported here.
  • The bulletin gives conflicting employee thresholds for its large-business subgroup; the subgroup is excluded. The reported multi-year trend is also excluded because comparability around question changes was not reconciled.

Why it matters

A nationally reported survey estimate offers a useful indicator of business AI adoption for policy and economic monitoring. Its meaning is limited by voluntary response, developing-statistics status and unresolved uncertainty; adoption is not evidence of productivity or causal economic impact.
