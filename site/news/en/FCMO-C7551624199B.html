MCPGuard paper reports conformance gaps in official Model Context Protocol SDKs

A new arXiv preprint introduces MCPGuard, a method that compares the Model Context Protocol specification with official SDK models and session logic, then checks candidate gaps with executable proof-of-concept tests. On Python SDK 1.26.0 and TypeScript SDK 1.29.0, the authors report 13 and 19 confirmed gaps, including 27 structural findings and 5 behavioral findings. They also report that two malformed URI-format metadata fields were accepted by all 32 public servers that completed the study’s handshake test. The preprint says its findings were privately reported to maintainers. These are paper-reported results, not an independent audit of current releases; the tested versions, remediation status and independent reproduction remain unresolved.

Why it matters

SDKs sit between an agent host and external MCP servers. If protocol constraints are missing from a shared SDK, dependent deployments may inherit the gap unless they validate independently. The paper describes a concrete approach combining deterministic schema matching, LLM-assisted behavioral review and dynamic adjudication; its reported results warrant checking whether current SDKs and servers have been fixed.
