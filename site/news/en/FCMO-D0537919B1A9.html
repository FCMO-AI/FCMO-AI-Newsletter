UK and Google DeepMind report early PlanAssist results as live pilot expands to 15 councils

The UK government says early PlanAssist testing in Barnet, Camden and Dorset cut officer time on key householder-application tasks by 58% and links that result to a reduction in routine processing from eight weeks to four. Google DeepMind reports that more than 70% of generated reports needed no edits. Both are involved-party claims without disclosed pilot denominators or comparison design. The official tender defines the eight-to-four-week target around stages the tool can affect and excludes consultation, site visits, neighbour notification, external-consultee responses and committee scheduling, so the broader end-to-end implication is not established. PlanningLens reports a 7.71-week pre-trial median across 7,663 decisions and a matched 16-council panel frozen before outcomes; its early May–July data are not attributed to the tool, and it says results are too early. The official award notice lists £8.288 million including VAT for a contract to Google Cloud EMEA Limited, not realized spend or proven value. The live test expands to 15 councils for 12 months, with officers retaining review and decision authority.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The UK government announced expansion of the PlanAssist live pilot from Barnet, Camden and Dorset to 12 additional councils for a 12-month testing phase; planning officers remain responsible for reviewing applications and decisions.
  • Claimed — The government says early testing cut officer time on key householder-application assessment tasks by 58% and says this is helping halve processing times for routine householder applications from eight weeks to four. It does not disclose the measurement design or how task time maps to end-to-end processing time; both figures remain government-reported claims.
  • Claimed — Google DeepMind reports that officers approved more than 70% of generated reports without edits; this is a provider-reported acceptance measure, not a measure of decision accuracy or quality.
  • Not established — The inspected sources do not establish an independently measured or generalizable reduction in end-to-end application processing time, improved planning-decision quality, a causal increase in housing delivery, or results from the expanded live phase.
  • Claimed — PlanningLens reports that its analysis of 7,663 householder decisions found a 7.71-week median from validation to decision across Barnet, Camden and Dorset in the 24 months before the pilots. It says a 16-council matched comparison panel was frozen before outcomes existed. PlanningLens is not part of the pilot and says it has no access to PlanAssist; the research desk did not reproduce its dataset, matching or calculations.
  • Claimed — PlanningLens publishes May–July 2026 early decision records but explicitly does not attribute their movement to PlanAssist because many decisions concern applications submitted before the tool arrived and officer-assist effects may take months to appear in the decision record. Its report says it is too early to assess the tool’s effect.
  • Demonstrated — The official tender describes the 8-to-4-week reduction as a target for the process stages the solution can affect and explicitly excludes the statutory 21-day consultation period, site visits, neighbour notifications, external-consultee responses and committee scheduling. This target scope does not establish that the public-facing end-to-end eight-to-four-week result has been measured.
  • Demonstrated — The official contract notice names Google Cloud EMEA Limited and an award value of £6,906,513 excluding VAT (£8,287,815.60 including VAT), with an estimated February 2026–May 2028 term and a possible 12-month extension. This is the contract award value, not evidence of cash already spent, realized savings or cost-effectiveness.

Strongest baseline

PlanningLens reports a 7.71-week pooled pre-trial median across 7,663 decisions from Barnet, Camden and Dorset, measured validation-to-decision without extension allowances, with 16 matched control councils whose panel was frozen before outcomes. It reports May–July 2026 early data but expressly attributes no movement to the tool because many decisions predate use and assisted effects take months to reach decision records. The independent analysis and dataset were not reproduced by the research desk. The official tender states that the eight-to-four-week target excludes process stages outside the solution’s control; the newer public claim links task-level savings to routine processing time but does not disclose an end-to-end evaluation.

Limitations

  • The 58% figure concerns officer time on key assessment tasks in early testing. The Oct 30 government release says this is helping halve routine processing times from eight weeks to four; the June 16 announcement had described that eight-to-four-week reduction as a target. The reviewed pages do not disclose the measurement design or how task-time savings map to end-to-end processing time, so the connection remains a first-party claim rather than an independently established outcome.
  • The sources do not provide sample sizes, council-level denominators, confidence intervals, comparison design or independent evaluation for the early pilot metrics.
  • The more-than-70% no-edit report rate is provider-reported and does not establish correctness, fairness or decision quality.
  • The 12 additional councils are entering a 12-month live-application test; later outcomes and national availability remain conditional on successful testing. Human officers retain review and decision authority.
  • The official contract’s eight-to-four-week target excludes statutory 21-day consultation, site visits, neighbour notifications, external-consultee responses and committee scheduling. The Oct 30 government release links 58% task-time savings to faster overall processing, but the reviewed sources do not establish that the end-to-end eight-to-four-week result has been achieved.
  • PlanningLens reports 7.71 weeks as the pooled pre-trial median for 7,663 decisions across the three pilot councils and a 16-council matched panel frozen before outcomes. Its May–July 2026 early data are not attributed to the tool; the analyst says decisions influenced by officer assistance may take months to appear. the research desk did not reproduce the dataset, matching or calculation.
  • The official award notice’s £8,287,815.60 including VAT (£6,906,513 excluding VAT) is a multiyear contract award value to Google Cloud EMEA Limited, not realized cash expenditure, a PlanAssist-only unit cost or evidence of return on investment.

What we still don't know

  • No independent evaluation or detailed measurement protocol was found for the early 58% task-time reduction, the reported more-than-70% no-edit rate, or the government’s claim that testing is helping halve routine processing time from eight weeks to four.
  • The June announcement framed the eight-to-four-week processing-time reduction as an aim. The October follow-up links early task-time savings to halving processing times but does not disclose whether end-to-end time was measured, modeled or projected, or how the task measure maps to that outcome.
  • The official notice gives an approximately £8.288 million including-VAT contract award value, but the reviewed sources do not provide realized contract spend, usage volume, staff-cost savings, operating costs or a cost-effectiveness comparison.
  • PlanningLens reports a preregistered pre-trial baseline and says its first May–July 2026 decision data are too early and not attributable because many applications predate tool use and assisted decisions take months to reach the public record. No post-pilot estimate or control-panel comparison is yet reported in the inspected scorecard.

Why it matters

If the reported task-time savings persist in live use without degrading planning quality, AI assistance could release capacity in a high-volume public-service workflow. The present evidence supports a bounded pilot result and expansion, not system-wide processing-time reductions or faster housing delivery.
