DeepInstructor reports experience-grounded alignment gains on bounded idea-evaluation benchmarks

The paper proposes DeepInstructor, which retrieves dimension-specific evaluative relations from a graph built from 58,607 ICLR 2024–2025 reviews. On ExpertEval, a set of 147 ideas with expert scores, the authors report that DeepInstructor using ChatGPT-4o as backbone improves mean Hit@1 and Hit@2 agreement over standalone ChatGPT-4o by 24.4% and 29.7%. Their ICLR-derived DeepInstruct set contains 398 ideas and 289 pairs and shows mixed dimension-specific pairwise and level-accuracy changes. These are paper-reported results; the research desk did not reproduce them.

Evidence

  • Claimed — DeepInstructor builds a structured experience graph from peer-review data and uses dimension-aware retrieval and agentic reasoning to assess research ideas.
  • Claimed — On the paper's ExpertEval set of 147 ideas, the authors report mean Hit@1 and Hit@2 improvements of 24.4% and 29.7% over standalone ChatGPT-4o when DeepInstructor uses the same backbone.
  • Not established — The reported results do not establish reliable or generalizable evaluation of research ideas across fields, reviewer populations, or consequential decisions.

Strongest baseline

Standalone ChatGPT-4o with the same backbone on ExpertEval; additional comparisons include passage-level RAG, DeepReview, ChatGPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-V4-Pro. The constructed DeepInstruct set shows mixed pairwise and level-accuracy changes across dimensions.

Limitations

  • All result estimates are reported by the authors; the research desk did not reproduce the system or verify the benchmark annotations.
  • ExpertEval has 147 ideas; DeepInstruct has 398 ideas and 289 pairs, with dataset construction involving LLM extraction and grading plus human validation.
  • The authors report limited overall performance on significance assessment and restrict their experience graph to two years of computer-science open peer reviews.
  • The findings do not establish cross-domain validity or safe use in consequential review decisions.

What we still don't know

  • The evaluator and reported benchmark results have not been independently reproduced.
  • Generalization to independent ideas, reviewer populations and fields beyond computer science is unknown.

Why it matters

Research-idea assessment may become a bottleneck as AI systems generate more candidate work. This paper tests whether retrieved reviewer experience can improve score alignment and concern-level evaluation, while its bounded datasets and weak significance-assessment results leave cross-domain validity unresolved.
