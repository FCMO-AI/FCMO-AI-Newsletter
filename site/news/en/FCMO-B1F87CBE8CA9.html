What changed

A 2026 arXiv paper proposes an Agent-Editing World Model (AEWM) that judges whether an agent’s proposed reasoning-action pair is critical, exploratory or noisy, then revises noisy proposals before executing them against real tools. In the authors’ three-run evaluations, EditAct improves average scores over the strongest baseline by 6.7, 5.2 and 3.2 points for Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B across six search, terminal and software-engineering benchmarks. A held-out 3,000-decision Action Judge benchmark reports 70.5% macro-F1 versus 59.9% for DeepSeek-V4-Pro. These are paper-reported results; independent replication and cost accounting remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On a held-out 3,000-decision benchmark across Search, Terminal and software engineering, the paper reports AEWM at 70.5% macro-F1 versus 59.9% for DeepSeek-V4-Pro, a 10.6 percentage-point difference.
  • Claimed — Across six benchmarks, the paper reports that EditAct improves average score over the strongest baseline by 6.7, 5.2 and 3.2 points for Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, respectively; each benchmark is evaluated in three independent runs.
  • Claimed — For Qwen3.5-35B-A3B fine-tuned on its own trajectories, the paper reports AEWM-RFT exceeds Self-RFT by 2.2-2.6 points across three reported domains without AEWM guidance at inference.

Strongest baseline

DeepSeek-V4-Pro for overall Action Judge macro-F1; the strongest among ReAct, step-level Best@3 and trajectory-level Best@3 for each EditAct benchmark.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported and have not been independently replicated in this activation.
  • EditAct results use Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in three specified scaffolds; generality to other model families and agent stacks is unestablished.
  • DeepSearchQA and SWE-Bench Pro are described as out-of-distribution; the other four benchmarks are in-distribution relative to training.
  • Training uses internal deep-search data, approximately 52B mid-training tokens and 120K SFT examples; the paper does not provide a complete independently auditable compute-cost comparison in the inspected sections.
  • Performance scores do not by themselves establish lower total cost, higher reliability in deployment or improved outcomes on untested tasks.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of AEWM, EditAct or AEWM-RFT results was performed; all reported scores in this record are author-run paper results.
  • The paper reports 52B mid-training tokens, 120K SFT examples and internal deep-search training data, but this review did not establish full data availability, total training compute or inference-time overhead relative to baselines.
  • EditAct benchmark results use three Qwen3.5 inference backbones; transfer to other model families and independently assembled agent scaffolds remains unknown.

Why it matters

If the reported effects hold outside the authors’ setups, changing an agent’s proposed reasoning and next action before execution could reduce persistent task-state errors while improving performance across search, terminal and software tasks. The paper reports a large Action Judge benchmark margin and multi-scale task gains, but does not establish independent reproduction, deployment reliability or lower total cost.
