StateMem reports persistent task memory and adaptive VLA caching gains in simulation and robot tests

StateMem adds a persistent single-token memory to a vision-language-action policy: prediction-error residuals update task state, while an adaptive router reuses cached VLM prefixes and applies a correction branch. The paper reports 97.6% average success on LIBERO, 20.25% fewer prefix refreshes than full refresh, and 26.8% task success / 44.4% cumulative success on RoboMemArena among the compared single-VLA methods. Across six real-world tasks and 20 trials each on one xArm7 platform, it reports 65% average success versus 44% for π0.5 and 53% for a MemoryVLA reimplementation. Efficiency gains depend on workload: LIBERO latency falls from 156.36 to 150.78 ms, while high refresh on RoboMemArena makes routing slower than full refresh. Training used four RTX A6000 GPUs for about five days. These are author-run results, not independent validation; one-token memory and one embodiment limit transfer.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On LIBERO, authors report 97.6% average success, 0.7 percentage points above π0.5, and 20.25% lower VLM prefix refresh versus full refresh. On RoboMemArena, they report 26.8% average task success and 44.4% cumulative success, highest among the compared single-VLA methods; in the Occlusion category, 21.8% TSR and 44.3% CSR.
  • Claimed — Across six real-world manipulation tasks on one xArm7/dexterous-hand platform, the paper reports 65% average success over 20 randomized trials per task, versus 44% for its π0.5 baseline and 53% for a MemoryVLA reimplementation.
  • Claimed — On LIBERO, prefix routing lowered refresh from 100% to 79.75%, latency from 156.36 to 150.78 ms and raised throughput from 6.40 to 6.64 Hz. On RoboMemArena, a 98.9% refresh rate meant routing overhead raised latency to 137.9 ms versus 135.1 ms for full refresh.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The authors identify one memory token, a limited range of embodiments, sensing modalities and task horizons, and cases where high refresh rates erase inference savings as limitations. Results are from one author-run preprint with no independent reproduction in this review.

Strongest baseline

LIBERO average success: π0.5 at 96.9%, MemoryVLA at 96.7%, CronusVLA at 97.0% versus StateMem 97.6%. Real world: π0.5 44%, author MemoryVLA reimplementation 53%, StateMem 65%. Full-refresh latency baseline on LIBERO: 156.36 ms versus 150.78 ms with routing.

Limitations

  • All reported results are from one author-run preprint; no independent reproduction was inspected.
  • Real-world evaluation uses six tasks on one xArm7, one dexterous hand and one wrist camera, with 20 randomized trials per task.
  • Simulation results use the paper’s own training/evaluation setup; LIBERO uses 50 demonstrations per task and 50 randomized trials per task; RoboMemArena uses 100 demonstrations per task and 50 seeded episodes.
  • The MemoryVLA real-world comparator is an author reimplementation, not the original authors’ system.
  • Training requires two stages totaling 60K steps on four RTX A6000 GPUs and about five days.
  • Inference benefit depends on refresh frequency: on RoboMemArena, 98.9% refresh leads routing overhead to exceed full-refresh latency.
  • Authors note one memory token may not retain multiple fine-grained events and that embodiment, sensing and horizon coverage is limited.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or evaluation on additional robot embodiments, sensing modalities or longer task horizons was inspected.
  • The real-world MemoryVLA comparison uses an author reimplementation, and matched training data, tuning and evaluation parity are not independently audited.

Why it matters

Robots acting over long interactions must retain relevant history without repeatedly recomputing large visual-language prefixes. StateMem combines a compact residual memory with adaptive cache reuse and reports gains on standard simulation, memory-focused tasks and a small real-robot suite. Its workload-dependent latency result and limited embodiment make the paper useful evidence about the design tradeoff, not proof of broad robot transfer or universal inference savings.
