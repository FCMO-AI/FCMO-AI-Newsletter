Score distributions improve retrieval abstention on reasoning-heavy tasks

The authors evaluate six zero-cost query-performance predictors across 11 retrieval architectures and 28 datasets spanning semantic, logical and temporal tasks. They report that score-distribution signals such as Score Gap and LSMV outperform raw maximum-score confidence on reasoning-intensive retrieval, with AUROC gains up to 0.16 where magnitude-based confidence is weak. The paper’s end-to-end BRIGHT answer-quality test uses one generator and one judge; no independent reproduction or production-distribution validation was conducted.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Across 11 retrieval architectures and 28 datasets, the authors report that distribution-based predictors outperform raw maximum-score confidence on logical and temporal retrieval; reported AUROC gains reach 0.16 in settings where magnitude is weak.
  • Claimed — The authors report that Score Gap and a variance-based score signal improve downstream answer quality in an end-to-end BRIGHT test using Qwen3.6-27B as generator and GPT-4o as judge.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that these predictors use the existing top-k retrieval-score distribution and require no additional model inference or retraining.

Strongest baseline

The paper’s raw maximum retrieval score (MaxScore) baseline, compared with Score Gap and other distributional metrics using the same ranked retrieval results and benchmark protocol.

Limitations

  • The end-to-end BRIGHT answer-quality evaluation uses one generator (Qwen3.6-27B) and one judge (GPT-4o); transfer to other architectures and evaluation protocols is untested.
  • Experiments use public benchmarks and may not match proprietary corpora, production query distributions or custom-trained retrievers.
  • The paper does not establish an absolute performance ceiling for zero-cost abstention or close the gap to cross-encoder reranking and model-based verification.
  • Results are author-reported; the research desk did not independently execute the paper’s code or reproduce the measurements.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction has tested the paper’s retrieval predictor comparisons or downstream answer-quality result. The end-to-end evaluation uses one generator and one judge.
  • The evaluation uses public BEIR, BRIGHT and TEMPO benchmarks; behavior under proprietary corpora, production query distributions and custom retriever training is unknown.

Why it matters

If the reported pattern transfers, RAG systems can make more reliable retrieval-abstention decisions from existing scores without extra inference, retraining or latency. The evidence is limited to the paper’s public benchmarks and one end-to-end generator/judge, so it does not establish production-wide gains.
