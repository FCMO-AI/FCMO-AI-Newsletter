ForTy releases a global 10 m forest typology map and early annual change series

Google DeepMind, Google Research, the World Resources Institute and IIASA released ForTy, a global 10 m map classifying 2020 land into six forest and land-use types, alongside an early annual series for 2017–2025. The authors describe a cascaded deep-learning pipeline trained using about 1.7 million labeled locations and AlphaEarth satellite embeddings. In the paper’s validation, 8,190 of 9,100 sampled sites were resolved by expert consensus; the authors report 90.2% stratified overall accuracy for six classes. Performance varies by class: planted forest had the lowest reported F1 at 58.2%, while the dominant Other category strongly influences aggregate accuracy. The data are publicly available, but the time series is preliminary and this review did not independently validate the maps.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Google DeepMind, Google Research, WRI and IIASA released ForTy, a global 10 m map for 2020 with six land and forest classes and an early annual series covering 2017–2025.
  • Demonstrated — The paper describes a cascaded deep-learning pipeline using about 1.7 million labeled locations and AlphaEarth satellite embeddings; the map and supporting validation data are publicly available.
  • Claimed — The authors report 90.2% stratified overall accuracy for the six-class map on 8,190 sites resolved by expert consensus from 9,100 sampled locations.
  • Claimed — Performance varies by class: the authors report F1 scores of 82.9% for primary forest, 72.6% for naturally regenerating forest, 66.7% for plantation forest, 58.2% for planted forest and 96.4% for Other; the dominant Other class substantially affects overall accuracy.
  • Not established — The early 2017–2025 time series has not been independently validated in this review, and aggregate benchmark results do not establish reliable local performance for every biome, class or regulatory use.
  • Demonstrated — The paper treats the 40-year rotation threshold as an observable proxy for planted versus plantation forest and subsumes other wooded land into Other; confidence scores are relative scores and may not be calibrated probabilities.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares its classification with existing global forest maps and reports stronger aggregate accuracy, but the indexed article’s detailed matched comparison was not independently reconstructed. Local and per-class errors remain relevant; no external replication was performed.

Limitations

  • Aggregate accuracy is strongly influenced by the dominant Other class; per-class results vary, and planted forest has the weakest reported F1 score.
  • The 2017–2025 time series is described as an early release and was not independently validated in this review.
  • The map subsumes other wooded land into Other and uses a 40-year rotation proxy to distinguish planted from plantation forest; these choices can limit regulatory and regional interpretation.
  • The paper reports confidence scores as relative scores that may not be calibrated probabilities.
  • The direct Nature fetch redirected to an inaccessible identity-provider page; this review used indexed article text and did not download or analyze the rasters or training code.

What we still don't know

  • No raster download or independent validation was conducted; reported aggregate accuracy may conceal regional and class-specific errors.
  • The 2017–2025 annual time series is an early release and was not independently checked for temporal consistency.

Why it matters

ForTy extends global forest mapping beyond tree-cover presence by distinguishing natural, regenerating, planted and plantation forests, tree crops/agroforestry and other land at 10 m resolution. If its class-level results hold across regions, the open map could support land-use research, conservation and supply-chain monitoring. Its lower performance on some classes, the operational planted-versus-plantation proxy and the early status of its time series limit direct regulatory use without local validation.
