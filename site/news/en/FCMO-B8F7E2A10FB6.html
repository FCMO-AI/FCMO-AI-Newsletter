Agima reports a single omni model planning with imagined visual outcomes in four simulated tasks

An Oct. 19 preprint introduces Agima, which trains one 14B-parameter omni model (7B active) to propose actions, generate visual predictions of their outcomes, and plan from those imagined observations before acting. The authors report results across Maze2D, Sokoban, Pacman and PushT. In a four-box Sokoban test with real feedback every five actions, the SFT model solves 40% of puzzles versus 2% for an action-only model fine-tuned from the same backbone. Replacing imagined observations with blank or stale frames sharply reduces success; oracle observations improve it, linking the reported gains to prediction content. Results are author-reported in simulated tasks; the research desk did not reproduce them, and the paper reports no inference FLOPs or latency.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The preprint reports that Agima, trained on two-box Sokoban, solves 40% of four-box puzzles when receiving real observations every five actions, versus 2% for a same-backbone action-only baseline.
  • Claimed — Across four simulated environments, the authors report that replacing imagined observations with blank or stale frames sharply reduces task success, while replacing them with ground-truth observations improves it.
  • Demonstrated — The paper describes a two-stage method: supervised fine-tuning on interleaved action/image traces, followed by GRPO using task-success reward to adapt how the model compares imagined plans.

Strongest baseline

A separately fine-tuned action-only model from the same BAGEL backbone and task data, plus image-content ablations that replace imagined frames with blank, stale or oracle ground-truth observations. The model comparisons and outcomes are author-reported.

Limitations

  • All numerical performance results are reported by the paper authors; the research desk did not independently reproduce them.
  • The evaluation covers four simulated task families and selected held-out configurations, not physical robots or deployed environments.
  • The reported action-count reductions and success rates do not establish inference latency, FLOPs, monetary cost or energy savings.
  • The public repository lists checkpoints and dataset artifacts but excludes result tables and evaluation logs; no artifact was executed.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported planning gains or visual-prediction ablations was completed.
  • Transfer beyond simulated grids and tabletop PushT, and behavior under real sensor/model error, are unknown.
  • The paper reports action counts but not inference latency, FLOPs, energy or wall-clock costs for imagined rollouts.

Why it matters

The work tests a practical planning design for environments where actions are costly or irreversible: one multimodal model predicts what actions will look like, then uses those predictions to choose what to do. If the reported gains generalize, learned visual imagination could help agents act with less frequent real feedback. The evidence remains bounded to four simulated task families and does not establish real-world robotics, production reliability, or compute savings.
