A reward-free teacher repair improves agentic on-policy distillation on three text-agent benchmarks

Zhao and Yu introduce Rollout-to-Scorer Distillation (R2S), which uses selected teacher takeovers from high-disagreement states of an untrained student to fine-tune the teacher’s token-level scorer before ordinary on-policy distillation (OPD). The student still trains against the frozen scorer with the standard sampled reverse-KL objective; task reward is not used to train it. Across ALFWorld, WebShop and ScienceWorld, the authors report that R2S outperformed three recent agentic-OPD baselines in all twelve model/student/environment cells, averaging 12.8 success points above the strongest baseline in each cell; Appendix E reports that R2S exceeds every baseline in mean on all 24 evaluation splits. The study uses two Qwen teacher families and four students. These are preprint-author results, not independently reproduced; the main table uses one training run per cell and the repair requires up to 19,200 teacher takeover episodes.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that R2S exceeds three recent agentic-OPD baselines in all twelve main model/student/environment cells, averaging 12.8 success percentage points above the strongest baseline in each cell.
  • Claimed — The authors report the mechanism: mine high-disagreement states from an untrained student, collect eight teacher takeovers per state, select a passing takeover using consensus or an LLM judge, fine-tune the teacher to score the student’s suffix with takeover-informed token targets, then freeze that teacher for ordinary OPD.
  • Demonstrated — The public repository provides the r2s code tag and documents the training pipeline, environment setup and release of 24 checkpoints; its README identifies an Apache-2.0 license.
  • Not established — The reported gains do not establish independent replication, transfer beyond the evaluated Qwen models and replayable text-agent environments, or a net compute/cost advantage after takeover and teacher-repair work.
  • Claimed — In Appendix E, the authors report that R2S exceeds every baseline in mean on all 24 evaluation splits shown in Table 12; these are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced by the research desk.

Strongest baseline

The authors compare against vanilla OPD, TCOD-F2B, Guided-OPD and FutureBridge under a shared model, prompt, estimator and training protocol. They report averaging 12.8 points over the best of these baselines per main cell; independent matched replication is open.

Limitations

  • All performance figures are preprint-author reports; the research desk did not execute the released code or reproduce results.
  • The main table covers two related Qwen teacher families, four students and three replayable text-agent benchmarks; transfer to other model families, modalities or less replayable environments is unknown.
  • Each main model/student/environment cell uses one 150-step training run; three final decodes do not estimate between-training-seed variation.
  • The repair can require up to 19,200 takeover episodes plus teacher fine-tuning; no net end-to-end compute or cost advantage is established.
  • The code README states that synthesized ScienceWorld ordering changes the paper’s unseen 134-row slice; exact reproduction needs the campaign test.jsonl rather than the synthesized ordering.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the R2S-versus-baseline results was found or performed.
  • The main table uses one student-training run per cell and three final decodes, so the published standard deviations do not measure between-training-run variation.
  • Up to 19,200 teacher takeovers and teacher fine-tuning are incurred before student distillation; net compute or cost efficiency is not measured.
  • The repository warns that synthesized ScienceWorld ordering produces a different unseen 134-row subset from the paper’s campaign task file.
  • Results cover two related Qwen teacher families and replayable text-agent tasks only.

Why it matters

If the result generalizes, a teacher’s successful rollouts can improve the token-level signal it provides to an agent student, addressing a local design choice in agentic post-training without adding task reward to the student objective. The study reports gains across several students and three tasks, but evidence is limited to Qwen models and replayable text-agent benchmarks; repair compute and transfer to other model families remain unmeasured.
