Tev reports long-horizon agent training gains from verified environments and dense progress rewards

A 13 October preprint introduces Tev-Gym, a 1,170-environment synthetic training corpus with hidden latent-world specifications, strict task verifiers and staged progress scores. The authors use progress-potential reward shaping to train Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.8-27B, reporting higher scores than their base checkpoints on Agents’ Last Exam’s computing-and-math subset (ALE-CM). Tev-27B’s reported Pass@5 is 0.28 versus 0.17 for its base and equals the paper’s reported GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 values under the listed harness. The authors also report transfer to selected Terminal-Bench computing-and-math subsets and a larger GLM-5.3-Flash model. These are author-reported results; this review did not independently reproduce them, and the project page lists code and data as forthcoming.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The 13 October preprint describes Tev-Gym, a latent-world-first corpus with 1,170 task environments across seven domains, and progress-potential reward shaping for long-horizon agent RL.
  • Claimed — The authors report Tev-27B reaches ALE-CM Pass@5 0.28 versus 0.17 for Qwen3.8-27B base, equal to the paper’s listed GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 values under its harness.
  • Claimed — The authors report gains on selected Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0 computing-and-math subsets and a 0.794 Pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1; the selected subsets are 22 and 18 tasks and were formed with a model-assisted classification step.
  • Not established — The preprint’s conclusion identifies synthetic terminal environments as a study limitation and says the hardest benchmarks still show a gap to leading frontier agents; the project page listed code, models and data as forthcoming when reviewed.

Strongest baseline

The closest controlled comparison is each trained model against its corresponding base checkpoint under the paper’s training/evaluation setup. On ALE-CM, Tev-27B reports P@5 0.28 versus 0.17 for Qwen3.8-27B base. Frontier-model comparisons use the paper’s harness and are not independent matched-implementation audits.

Limitations

  • All performance values are author-reported; this review did not reproduce training or evaluation.
  • Tev-Gym consists of synthetic terminal environments; the study does not establish performance on live professional workflows or physical environments.
  • The paper evaluates selected computing-and-math subsets of Terminal-Bench 3.0 and 4.0; an LLM-assisted domain-selection step was used, and each subset contains only 22 or 18 tasks.
  • The paper reports a gap between Tev models and leading frontier models on the hardest Terminal-Bench tasks; gains do not establish broad frontier parity.
  • The project page listed code, model weights and data as forthcoming, so environment construction, reward implementation, grader independence and evaluation harness are not yet independently auditable from the linked release.
  • No independent multi-seed reproduction or matched dollar/energy cost comparison was performed.
  • PPRS retains a terminal progress-potential term (lambda = 0.25), unlike standard potential-based reward shaping that zeroes terminal potential to preserve the optimal policy. The authors state that this deliberately changes the objective and analyze conditions where policy preferences can reverse; the method is not policy-invariant by construction.

What we still don't know

  • The linked project page lists code, model weights and data as forthcoming; the environment generators, grading code and evaluation harness could not be audited from an available release.
  • No independent group has reproduced the reported ALE-CM or Terminal-Bench gains; training seed variance and matched compute across baselines remain unclear.
  • PPRS retains terminal potential (lambda = 0.25), deliberately changing the objective; the reported gains do not establish how policy rankings vary with this coefficient or in other task regimes.

Why it matters

If reproducible, combining exact intermediate-state verification with dense progress rewards could make long-horizon agent training more data-scalable and reduce wasted RL rollouts when no sampled trajectory finishes a task. The reported improvements are limited to synthetic terminal environments and selected evaluation subsets, so the paper does not yet establish broad professional-work capability or general transfer.
