Contrastive Discovery reports more resolved questions under matched experimental budgets

The authors introduce Contrastive Discovery, a way for scientific discovery agents to evaluate a question against several competing explanations rather than one focal hypothesis and its unstructured negation. On six scientific and social datasets, they report 161.9 resolved questions at top-K 200 versus 97.2 for pointwise surprisal (+67%) under the same 200 experimental validations. A blinded study with 38 experts reports agreement on 78% of resolved questions. The results are author-reported and use GPT-4o-based discovery and belief-elicitation agents; two higher-cost datasets have one run each, no independent reproduction was performed, and the method may omit the explanation best supported by evidence. The paper does not establish external generality or validated scientific discoveries.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors introduce Contrastive Discovery, which represents a scientific question with a set of competing explanations and scores evidence by how it redistributes belief across those alternatives, rather than testing one hypothesis only against its unstructured complement.
  • Claimed — Across six scientific and social datasets, the authors report 161.9 resolved questions at top-K 200 for Contrastive Discovery versus 97.2 for pointwise surprisal (+67%), with both methods allocated the same 200 experimental validations. The result is averaged across datasets; the two highest-cost datasets each had one run.
  • Claimed — In a blinded comparison, 38 human experts selected the same explanation as Contrastive Discovery for 78% of resolved questions and 82% of questions where the pointwise method rejected its hypothesis and stopped. This agreement test does not establish that the selected explanations are scientifically true.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproduction, performance across other agent backbones or real-world research programs, or that its resolved questions constitute externally validated scientific discoveries.

Strongest baseline

Pointwise surprisal on the same discovery scaffold with the same 200 experimental validations. At top-K 200 the paper reports 97.2 resolved questions on average versus 161.9 for Contrastive Discovery. These are author-reported results; run structure differs by dataset.

Limitations

  • All reported comparisons are from the authors’ evaluation, not an independent reproduction.
  • The discovery and belief-elicitation agents use GPT-4o; transfer to other model families is untested.
  • The two highest-cost datasets have one run each.
  • A contrast set can omit an evidence-supported explanation; the optional abstention safeguard was not used in the main experiments.
  • Expert agreement measures agreement with the selected explanation, not ground truth or downstream scientific validation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported six-dataset comparisons or expert agreement was found or performed in this activation.
  • The evaluation uses a GPT-4o-based discovery backbone and belief elicitor; the two highest-cost datasets have one run each. Generalization to other model families, larger tasks and operational science workflows is unknown.
  • Generated contrast sets may omit the explanation supported by the evidence; the paper discusses an abstention alternative but reports that the main experiments do not include this safeguard.

Why it matters

If reproduced beyond these datasets and this GPT-4o-based scaffold, representing explicit alternatives could help research agents extract more information from each experiment and make negative results more informative. The reported gain remains a paper-level result; it does not show that an agent produces more externally validated science.
