What changed

An October 13 arXiv revision presents EGMemory, a training-free memory framework that preserves message provenance and state histories in evidence chains, verifies proposed updates against source messages and prior state, and navigates evidence iteratively at answer time. The authors report 68.2% accuracy on GroupMemBench and 77.9% on EverMemBench, 22.7 and 21.4 percentage points above the strongest evaluated baselines; on dyadic LoCoMo they report 73.6%, 4.3 points above the best listed baseline. The results are paper-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report 68.2% accuracy on GroupMemBench and 77.9% on EverMemBench, respectively 22.7 and 21.4 percentage points above the strongest evaluated baselines. On LoCoMo, they report 73.6%, 4.3 points above the strongest listed baseline. These are the paper's evaluation results, not an independent reproduction.
  • Claimed — In the GroupMemBench ablation, the authors report accuracy reductions of 4.7 points without reranking, 5.0 without versioned state, 7.9 with a single-round reader, and 2.3 without evidence verification.
  • Not established — The reported benchmark results do not establish general performance on real deployed agents, causal transfer to other model families or datasets, independent reproducibility, or end-to-end economic advantage.

Strongest baseline

The strongest listed baseline varies by benchmark: 45.5% on GroupMemBench, 56.5% on EverMemBench and 69.3% on LoCoMo. The paper compares against MemoryBank, MemOS, A-MEM and Mem0, alongside dense, BM25 and hybrid one-shot retrieval controls. Results and baselines are from the authors' benchmark evaluation; the research desk did not independently rerun them.

Limitations

  • All numerical results are reported by the paper authors; the research desk did not independently reproduce them or audit the benchmark datasets.
  • The evaluation uses Qwen3.7-max, Kimi-K3, text-embedding-v4 and qwen3-rerank; results may depend on these model and tool choices.
  • The paper reports benchmark accuracy and selected ablations but does not establish deployment-level reliability, latency or total cost against matched alternatives.
  • An anonymous code repository is linked from the paper; its contents and reproducibility were not checked in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of the reported benchmark results was located or performed.
  • Results use a particular set of commercial foundation, judging, embedding and reranking models and benchmark-specific answer protocols.
  • The paper does not provide a matched end-to-end deployment cost and latency comparison for construction plus iterative retrieval against the strongest baselines.

Why it matters

If the reported comparisons hold beyond the tested benchmarks, preserving provenance and revisions while retrieving evidence adaptively could improve memory reliability in multi-actor agents without memory-specific policy training. The paper also reports gains over one-shot retrieval and existing memory systems, but the benchmarks use particular models and protocols and do not establish general deployment performance or lower end-to-end cost.
