What changed

A new preprint presents SimpleLoRIF, a gradient-embedding method for locating benign-looking fine-tuning examples associated with unsafe behavior. The authors report that removing the 20% of Tulu 3 examples most aligned with unsafe responses reduced GCG attack success across Qwen, Llama and Olmo, while largely preserving selected capability scores. These are bounded author-reported results; the paper tests one target set and general safety, and independent reproduction remains open.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors submitted version 1 of “Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data” to arXiv on October 27, 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report that removing the 20% of Tulu 3 examples with the greatest gradient similarity to unsafe SafeRLHF responses reduced GCG attack success from 47.7% to 37.0% for Qwen 3 1.7B, from 38.0% to 30.3% for Llama 3.2 1B, and from 48.3% to 41.0% for Olmo 2 1B. They report three fine-tuning seeds and standard errors; capability results were largely preserved or improved on the selected benchmarks.
  • Claimed — The authors report that gradients from insecure-code fine-tuning examples aligned with harmful behaviors after fine-tuning, with AUC 0.865 for one Mistral model and 0.604 for one Qwen model.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish independent reproduction, effectiveness on other target sets or specific behaviors such as sycophancy, or improved safety in production deployment.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares against baseline training, random removal, LLM-as-a-judge filtering and a prior gradient-based filtering method. Reported GCG success decreases by 10.7, 7.7 and 7.3 percentage points for Qwen, Llama and Olmo respectively.

Limitations

  • All outcome measurements are reported by the paper authors and remain unconfirmed by independent reproduction.
  • Reported filtering results cover three small models, selected attack and capability benchmarks, and one unsafe-response target set.
  • The paper does not test specific safety behaviors such as sycophancy.

What we still don't know

  • No independent group reproduced the gradient-attribution or SafeTulu fine-tuning results in this activation.
  • The authors state they test general safety, not specific behaviors such as sycophancy, and use one unsafe-response target set.
  • Code, dataset and exact scoring artifacts were not audited or executed in this activation.

Why it matters

The work proposes a way to find safety-degrading signals that ordinary text filters may miss, linking model behavior to fine-tuning examples and turning that signal into a data-filtering intervention. If the reported effects reproduce across target sets, model scales and behavior-specific evaluations, this could inform post-training data curation; current results do not establish general safety or production impact.
