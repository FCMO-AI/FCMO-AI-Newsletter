A preprint reports that finite precision breaks exact trajectory matching in Orthrus speculative decoding

The v2 preprint compares Orthrus with its Qwen3-1.7B autoregressive reference across 1,190 prompts in 12 domains. Under BF16, exact token trajectories match in 45% of the released-checkpoint runs and 43% of an independently trained implementation; under FP16 the rate is 87%, and under FP32 it is 100% for the tested prompts. The authors report no systematic downstream-task degradation from divergence in their limited benchmarks. These are paper-reported results from two implementations at one model scale, not an independent reproduction.

What actually changed

The v2 preprint compares Orthrus with its Qwen3-1.7B autoregressive reference across 1,190 prompts in 12 domains. Under BF16, exact token trajectories match in 45% of the released-checkpoint runs and 43% of an independently trained implementation; under FP16 the rate is 87%, and under FP32 it is 100% for the tested prompts. The authors report no systematic downstream-task degradation from divergence in their limited benchmarks. These are paper-reported results from two implementations at one model scale, not an independent reproduction.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — Across 1,190 prompts in 12 domains, the authors report exact trajectory matches for 45% of the released Orthrus checkpoint's BF16 generations and 43% of an independently trained Orthrus implementation; FP16 matching is 87%, and FP32 matching is 100% on this prompt set.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that trajectory divergence does not correspond to systematic degradation on their evaluated downstream benchmarks; point estimates vary with inference precision and are not established as statistically significant improvements or declines.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The v2 preprint specifies exact token-sequence matching as its operational test of losslessness and documents the two Qwen3-1.7B-based implementations, inference precisions and 12-domain prompt set used in its reported comparison.

Strongest baseline

The corresponding frozen autoregressive Qwen3-1.7B model under matched prompts and inference precision; FP32 served as a higher-precision comparison and yielded exact matches on all tested prompts.

The caveat that matters

  • Author-reported preprint results; this review did not execute code/checkpoints or independently reproduce the measurements.
  • The experiments cover two Qwen3-1.7B-based Orthrus implementations, greedy decoding, one hardware/software setup and a curated 1,190-prompt set; behavior at other scales and in other acceleration architectures is unknown.
  • The authors do not identify which computational stage causes the precision-dependent divergence.
  • Downstream evaluation covers GSM8K, HumanEval and IFEval and does not test chain-of-thought propagation or tool selection; trajectory divergence does not establish practical task harm.
  • Exact token trajectory matching is the paper's chosen operational criterion for losslessness; other definitions, including distributional equivalence, are distinct.
  • The paper reports no systematic downstream-task degradation despite frequent BF16 trajectory divergence; exact sequence inequality does not by itself establish lower task quality.
  • FP32 exactly matched all 1,190 prompts in the reported test, showing the observed divergence depends on numerical precision and tested configuration.

What remains unknown

  • The linked implementation and reported comparison were not run in this review, and independent replication was not inspected.
  • The paper studies Qwen3-1.7B-based Orthrus implementations and does not establish transfer to other scales or speculative-decoding architectures.
  • The study does not measure effects of trajectory divergence on long reasoning traces or tool-calling behavior.

FCMO Lens

The study makes the operational meaning of lossless inference concrete: exact output-sequence equivalence can fail under practical low-precision inference even when the decoding algorithm is intended to preserve its reference trajectory. If confirmed across systems, evaluations of accelerated decoding should report precision and exact-match criteria alongside task scores. The results do not show systematic task degradation or establish how often divergence changes consequential reasoning or tool use.
