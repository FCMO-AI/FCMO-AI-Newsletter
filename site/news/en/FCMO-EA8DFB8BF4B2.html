Listen Then Reason weights audio test-time RL by acoustic grounding

Listen Then Reason proposes Perception-Grounded Test-Time RL (PG-TTRL) for audio-language models. It estimates each generated answer’s reliance on audio by comparing token probabilities with full audio access and with audio attention masked; a grounding margin then weights the majority-vote advantage used for unlabeled test-time RL. The authors evaluate Qwen2.5-Omni 3B and 7B on MMAR and MMAU and report higher greedy and Avg@8 scores than standard TTRL in all four model/benchmark pairings, with gains over the base model reaching 4.6 points on MMAR and 4.9 on MMAU. Majority@8 does not improve uniformly. The method adapts LoRA weights on unlabeled prompts from the benchmark test sets; added inference compute and cross-domain transfer are not established, and no independent reproduction was conducted.

Evidence

  • Claimed — PG-TTRL estimates acoustic grounding from the change in token log probability when audio attention is masked, then scales an unlabeled majority-vote TTRL advantage by the grounding margin between majority and dissenting answers.
  • Claimed — On Qwen2.5-Omni-3B and 7B with MMAR and MMAU, the authors report higher greedy and Avg@8 scores than standard TTRL in all four model/benchmark pairings; majority-vote accuracy does not improve uniformly.
  • Claimed — The paper reports improvements over the base model of up to 4.6 points on MMAR and 4.9 points on MMAU, using unlabeled test prompts for one epoch of LoRA adaptation and reserving labels for evaluation.

Strongest baseline

Standard majority-vote TTRL using the same GRPO-style optimization without grounding-aware reliability weighting, plus the unadapted base model.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are author-reported; no independent reproduction was conducted.
  • The method adapts on unlabeled prompts from the evaluation test sets for one epoch. This is a transductive test-time adaptation regime and should not be conflated with frozen-model benchmark performance.
  • Evaluation covers two Qwen2.5-Omni sizes and two multiple-choice audio benchmarks; transfer to open-ended tasks, other model families or held-out audio domains is unknown.
  • Maj@8 results are mixed and do not improve uniformly over standard TTRL.
  • The method uses multiple rollouts and two model forward conditions for grounding; added compute, wall time and deployment latency are not quantified.
  • No public implementation link was found in the inspected arXiv report.
  • The reported majority-vote accuracy is not uniformly improved: PG-TTRL trails standard TTRL on some Maj@8 comparisons, including the 7B MMAU result.
  • The test-time adaptation procedure adds rollout and forward-pass compute, but the report does not quantify its cost or latency relative to standard TTRL.

What we still don't know

  • No independent implementation or reproduction of PG-TTRL results was performed; no public code link was found in the inspected report.
  • Additional rollout, masking-forward-pass and adaptation costs relative to standard TTRL are not measured.
  • Performance on disjoint or streaming audio domains and non-multiple-choice reasoning tasks is not established.

Why it matters

The work uses an explicit audio-dependence signal to decide when unlabeled test-time self-training should trust a model’s majority answer. If reproduced, this could reduce a failure mode where language priors dominate audio reasoning during adaptation. Its reported gains are benchmark- and adaptation-protocol-specific, with mixed majority-vote results and unmeasured runtime cost.
