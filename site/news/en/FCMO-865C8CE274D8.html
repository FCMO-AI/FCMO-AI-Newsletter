Evidence

  • Claimed — On BBBC047, the authors report an agent-selected predictor with held-out Global PCC 0.3153 that remained invariant to compound replacement, while a control-profile-only predictor scored 0.3142. They identify a blocked compound-query path and report that the invariance persisted after refitting with physically disjoint control wells for inputs and target references.
  • Claimed — Across 48 generated candidates on two linked tasks, the authors report that 47 changed predictions under compound replacement on both held-out folds, but only 20 had target-loss gains with intervals above zero on both folds. Falsification-guided revisions recovered positive mean compound contributions on BBBC047.
  • Claimed — In a matched sci-Plex analysis, audit-enriched feedback had higher mean held-out prediction and larger mean compound and dose contributions across five paired trajectories, but paired intervals spanned zero. Refits on an independently acquired cohort retained dose contribution while support for compound identity did not persist.

Strongest baseline

The control-profile-only predictor is the key BBBC047 reference; the paper also compares matched agent policies, fixed model designs and input-replacement tests across folds and cohorts.

Limitations

  • All reported analyses are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The findings concern selected cellular-response datasets, fitted predictors and registered replacement distributions; a positive input contribution does not establish biological mechanism or causality.
  • The paper reports mixed evidence across measures: the sci-Plex paired intervals span zero, and support for compound identity fails to persist on the independently acquired LKCP cohort even though dose contribution persists.
  • Conclusions apply to the tested fits and checkpoints; evidence for one fit is not inherited by later refits.
  • The complete sci-Plex result evaluates an audit-enriched feedback package and does not isolate the effect of each diagnostic.

What we still don't know

  • The code-level input-path check, replacement tests and reported model-search outcomes have not been independently reproduced.
  • Input-use support depends on registered replacement distributions and is not evidence of biological exchangeability, mechanism or causal effects; compound-identity support did not persist in the reported LKCP transfer.

Why it matters

A model can predict held-out cellular responses without using the intervention variable that its scientific claim says matters. Separating code-path use, fitted dependence and target-relevant contribution can expose that gap and guide revisions. The paper also shows that predictive transfer and input-use claims can diverge across cohorts. Its conclusions depend on registered replacement distributions and fitted model settings; they do not establish biological mechanism or causal effects.
