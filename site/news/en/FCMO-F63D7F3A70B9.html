LayerRoute: action-conditioned intermediate-layer routing for VLA policies

LayerRoute augments vision-language-action policies with an action-conditioned mixture over cached intermediate VLM states plus Action-State Reread. In the authors' matched evaluations it improves two VLA architectures across LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboCasa-GR1, four VLM backbones, and 150 physical-robot trials, with modest parameter growth but a measurable inference-latency penalty.

What actually changed

LayerRoute augments vision-language-action policies with an action-conditioned mixture over cached intermediate VLM states plus Action-State Reread. In the authors' matched evaluations it improves two VLA architectures across LIBERO, SimplerEnv, RoboCasa-GR1, four VLM backbones, and 150 physical-robot trials, with modest parameter growth but a measurable inference-latency penalty.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Under the authors' matched protocol, LayerRoute improves both tested VLA policy families across the principal simulation benchmarks and improves all three reported physical-robot tasks.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The routing analysis suggests that action stages and task types prefer different VLM depths, with earlier action computation tending shallower and later stages tending deeper.
  • INFERRED — Adaptive intermediate-layer access will generalize as a useful control interface across substantially different VLA architectures and real-world regimes.

Strongest baseline

The same StarVLA-pi and pi0.5 backbones under the authors' matched training/evaluation protocol without LayerRoute; component ablations, fixed-depth routing, and action-independent routing provide additional attribution controls.

The caveat that matters

  • The main architecture evidence covers two VLA policy families rather than a broad independent set of production robot stacks.
  • The method is not free: reported batch-1 inference latency increases by roughly 10% for StarVLA-pi and 25% for pi0.5.
  • Physical-robot evidence is limited to three tabletop tasks, one embodiment and 50 trials per task; no independent reproduction or multi-site evaluation was found.
  • Evaluation repetitions do not establish robustness across independent training seeds, data mixtures or substantially different robot embodiments.
  • An authoritative released-code repository/commit was not recovered during this activation despite the paper's code-release statement.

What remains unknown

  • Paper states that code is released, but the authoritative repository and exact evaluated commit were not recovered.
  • No unaffiliated reproduction was found for the simulation or physical-robot gains.

FCMO Lens

If the effect generalizes, VLA systems need not treat the final VLM layer as the only useful control interface: the action state can adaptively select semantic depth during execution. That changes a concrete architecture choice for long-horizon embodied policies, while the current gains remain bounded rather than field-resetting.
