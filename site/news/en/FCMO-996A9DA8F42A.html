CS-MoE reports cross-layer expert sharing with matched-budget perplexity gains through 12B parameters

A preprint proposes CS-MoE, which combines layer-local experts with a shared expert pool available across transformer layers. The authors report lower perplexity than dense and layer-isolated MoE baselines under matched parameter and FLOP budgets in Qwen3-backbone experiments spanning 0.6B–12B parameters. At 8B scale, one reported configuration activates 55% of physical parameters per forward pass and reaches perplexity 9.55 versus 9.75 for the dense baseline; this result is not the same as the paper’s separate 4.5-trillion-token downstream comparison. The largest reported models are 12B; routing stability, distributed-system efficiency, and post-training behavior at larger scales remain unestablished.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors propose CS-MoE, combining layer-specific experts with a shared expert pool accessible across transformer layers.
  • Claimed — Across Qwen3-backbone experiments from 0.6B to 12B parameters, the authors report lower perplexity than dense baselines under matched parameter and FLOP budgets; one 8B configuration activates 55% of physical parameters and reports perplexity 9.55 versus 9.75 for the 8B dense baseline.
  • Claimed — At matched total-parameter and active-FLOP budgets, the paper reports perplexity 9.65 versus 9.70 at 8B scale and 9.19 versus 9.40 at 12B scale against its layer-isolated sparse-MoE comparisons.
  • Demonstrated scope limit — The inspected paper states that its empirical validation stops at 12B parameters and leaves 70B-plus scaling, distributed acceleration, and post-training pipelines for future study.

Strongest baseline

The paper’s within-study dense and layer-isolated sparse-MoE baselines matched on total parameter footprint and active FLOPs.

Limitations

  • All quantitative results are reported by the paper authors and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The reported 55%-active-parameter, 8B perplexity comparison is distinct from the separate 8B downstream benchmark reported after 4.5 trillion training tokens.
  • The paper’s largest reported model is 12B; performance at 70B-plus, distributed-system gains, and post-training effects remain unknown.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the training runs or reported perplexity/downstream results was inspected.
  • The authors validate through 12B parameters; large-scale routing, training stability, and system performance at 70B-plus remain unmeasured.
  • The paper does not establish post-training performance or end-to-end serving-cost advantages for CS-MoE.

Why it matters

Cross-layer parameter reuse is a concrete architecture route for trading stored capacity against active computation. If the reported matched-budget gains reproduce, they could affect the design of sparse language models under memory or compute limits.
