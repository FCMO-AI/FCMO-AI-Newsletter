LongZero uses self-generated tasks to train long-context reasoning without an external corpus

LongZero is a preprint method for generating long-context reinforcement-learning tasks without an external document corpus or larger teacher for its reasoning-task synthesis. A target model first writes questions, evidence and answers for fictional worlds, then expands those facts into long multi-document contexts; it also creates retrieval conversations, filters for questions it answers inconsistently, and trains with GRPO. On five benchmarks, the authors report mean-of-two-run average-score gains for Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 (54.68 to 57.42) and Qwen3-8B (44.63 to 45.66). The Qwen3-8B LongBench v2 score does not improve (32.01 versus 32.11 baseline), and 120/200 of its AA-LCR responses contain no answer. The results remain author-reported, limited to two Qwen checkpoints, short training and two runs; independent reproduction is open.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors publish LongZero as a method that has the target model specify questions, supporting facts and answers before expanding those facts into fictional long-document contexts; it also synthesizes retrieval conversations, filters for questions the model answers both correctly and incorrectly, and trains with GRPO. The paper and a public code/data repository are available.
  • Claimed — Across five long-context benchmarks, the paper reports that LongZero raises the unweighted average score for Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 from 54.68 to 57.42 and for Qwen3-8B from 44.63 to 45.66, compared with each base model; results are means of two runs. The Qwen3-8B score on LongBench v2 is 32.01 versus 32.11 for its base model, so this method does not improve that benchmark in that regime.
  • Claimed — The paper reports that 120 of 200 Qwen3-8B responses on AA-LCR contain no answer under its 131K-token evaluation setup; all methods score near zero there. Independent reproduction and evidence of generalization beyond these two Qwen checkpoints are not provided.

Strongest baseline

The unmodified base model and two data-source controls trained under the same RL configuration: difficulty-filtered Enhanced HotpotQA and QA synthesized by Qwen3.5-397B-A17B. The paper says only training data differ between LongZero and those baselines.

Limitations

  • Paper-reported results are bounded to two Qwen checkpoints, five benchmarks, short training runs and two evaluation runs per benchmark.
  • The LongZero reasoning data use model-generated fictional worlds, while retrieval tasks use a specific synthetic conversation template; the result does not establish that self-synthesis replaces external data for factual or broader long-context tasks.
  • Qwen3-8B LongBench v2 is slightly below its base score (32.01 versus 32.11), and AA-LCR performance remains near zero for all methods in the reported setup.
  • No independent reproduction was performed in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the training or benchmark results; the reported scores are means of two runs.
  • Only Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 and Qwen3-8B are evaluated; transfer to other model families and long-context RL settings is unknown.
  • Qwen3-8B does not improve on LongBench v2, and its AA-LCR result is near zero with 120/200 responses containing no answer; context-length and answer-completion constraints limit interpretation.

Why it matters

If the reported gains reproduce, a model could generate and filter useful long-context RL tasks without depending on a large external document corpus or a stronger teacher. That could lower data and teacher-model bottlenecks in post-training. The current evidence is a small two-model study with mixed benchmark-level outcomes, so it does not establish general long-context improvement or a broad replacement for externally grounded training data.
