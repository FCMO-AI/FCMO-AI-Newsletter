Adaptive attacker search makes prompt-injection success rise with compute in two synthetic agent suites

A September 3 preprint models indirect prompt injection as an attacker search problem and evaluates a GLM-5.2 attacker with environment reconnaissance, explicit strategy/history state and victim-feedback probes. In synthetic Workspace and Retail suites, the authors report that attack success rises as the attacker receives more test-time tokens: GPT-5.5 reaches 52.1% ASR at 100,000 tokens in Workspace, while Claude Opus 4.8 reaches 20.6% at 500,000 tokens in Retail. Removing the search harness causes gains to plateau after about 100,000 tokens, and removing explicit strategy management leads to repeated reconnaissance and attack patterns. The study is white-box, uses synthetic tasks and one attacker against two victims; black-box transfer and independent reproduction are not established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Version 1 of the preprint “Rethinking Indirect Prompt Injection as a Test-Time Search Problem” was posted to arXiv on September 3, 2026.
  • Claimed — The authors report that a GLM-5.2 attacker with environment reconnaissance, structured strategy/history tools and adaptive victim feedback finds more indirect prompt-injection successes as attacker token budget increases, in synthetic Workspace and Retail task suites. They report GPT-5.5 ASR of 52.1% at 100,000 attacker tokens on Workspace and Claude Opus 4.8 ASR of 20.6% at 500,000 tokens on Retail. In a Retail ablation, the evaluation-only attacker without the full search harness plateaus after about 100,000 tokens, while removing the Strategy tool produces repeated reconnaissance and attack patterns. These are author-reported results under a white-box threat model, not independent reproduction or production prevalence.
  • Not established — The preprint does not establish that the reported attack-budget curves transfer to black-box attackers, production systems, other attacker or victim models, or deterministic authorization and containment layers; independent reproduction and real-world prevalence are also not established.

Strongest baseline

The paper includes full-harness, no-harness and no-Strategy ablations; these are author-run comparisons in the same synthetic suites.

Limitations

  • The findings are author-reported in one arXiv preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The main experiments use a white-box attacker with environment visibility, one GLM-5.2 attacker, and GPT-5.5/Claude Opus 4.8 victims in synthetic Workspace and Retail task suites.
  • The results do not establish black-box transfer, production prevalence, or how deterministic authorization and containment defenses change attacker success.
  • Victim queries, attacker tokens, compute, cost and wall-clock time are not interchangeable; the reported token curves should not be pooled with studies that use other budgets or benchmarks.
  • Attack success can increase while joint success on the benign task falls as tool breadth grows; ASR alone does not capture this trade-off.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the adaptive attacker or the reported budget-response curves was conducted. The paper reports author-run results and limited independent-run statistics.
  • The attacker sees the environment in the evaluated white-box setting; systematic black-box transfer, deployed-system behavior and deterministic permission/authorization controls were not tested.
  • The paper reports token budgets but does not establish a transferable cost-normalized robustness curve across attacker models, providers or defense protocols.

Why it matters

A static attack score can hide vulnerabilities that an adaptive attacker discovers by inspecting the environment, tracking search history and learning from victim feedback. If the reported pattern generalizes, agent-security evaluations need to state attacker access and compute budgets and measure task utility alongside compromise. The study does not establish black-box transfer, production risk or a universal scaling law.
