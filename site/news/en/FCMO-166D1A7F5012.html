OpenAI releases GPT-6.1 Terra with claimed computer-use gains and lower API prices

OpenAI announced GPT-6.1 Terra on Oct 26 as a model for visual reasoning and computer use, with access through ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. OpenAI reports a 15-point gain over GPT-5.6 Terra on Agents’ Last Exam at medium effort and about one-third the cost, and a 50.3% Terminal-Bench 4.0 solve rate at maximum effort versus the previously published 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra. Performance and factuality figures are first-party claims; this activation did not independently reproduce them or establish matched external cost outcomes.

What changed

OpenAI announced GPT-6.1 Terra on Oct 26 as a model for visual reasoning and computer use, with access through ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. OpenAI reports a 15-point gain over GPT-5.6 Terra on Agents’ Last Exam at medium effort and about one-third the cost, and a 50.3% Terminal-Bench 4.0 solve rate at maximum effort versus the previously published 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra. It also reports lower error rates on an internal factuality benchmark. Standard API prices are listed at $1/M input, $0.05/M cached input and $5/M output tokens. Performance and factuality figures are first-party claims; this activation did not independently reproduce them or establish matched external cost outcomes.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI announced GPT-6.1 Terra on 2026-10-26 and states that it is available in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users and through the API as gpt-6.1-terra.
  • Claimed — At medium reasoning effort, OpenAI reports that GPT-6.1 Terra scores 15 percentage points above GPT-5.6 Terra on Agents’ Last Exam at about one-third the cost. At maximum reasoning effort it reports a 50.3% Terminal-Bench 4.0 solve rate, compared with the published 21.5% GPT-5.6 Terra score.
  • Claimed — On OpenAI’s internal error-inducing-prompt benchmark, the reported factual-error rate falls from 53.6% for GPT-5.6 Terra to 16.7% for GPT-6.1 Terra at low reasoning effort, and is 4.3% at maximum effort.
  • Demonstrated — OpenAI lists standard API rates of $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 per million cached input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, and says these rates are 50% below GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol.
  • Not established — The announcement does not establish independent reproduction, performance under matched external tasks, realized end-to-end cost savings, or generalization of its internal factuality result beyond the described benchmark.

Strongest baseline

GPT-5.6 Terra is the primary prior-model comparator in OpenAI’s announcement; some reported comparisons use its previously published score rather than a demonstrated matched external evaluation.

Limitations

  • Performance and factuality metrics on the release page are first-party reported claims, not independent results.
  • The GPT-5.6 Terra Terminal-Bench comparator is described as its published score; the release does not establish that all benchmark measurements were run as a matched contemporaneous comparison.
  • The factuality result uses OpenAI’s internal error-inducing-prompt benchmark; external validity is not established.
  • Posted token rates do not establish actual task-level savings, which depend on output volume, caching, retries and workload success.

What we still don't know

  • No independent matched evaluation of the reported Agents’ Last Exam, OSWorld 2.1, Terminal-Bench 4.0, or internal factuality results was inspected in this activation.
  • The release post does not provide enough protocol detail to independently assess all comparison settings, especially the internal factuality benchmark and its error-inducing prompts.

Why it matters

The release presents a potentially meaningful price-capability tradeoff for computer-use and coding workloads: if its benchmark results transfer to comparable tasks, teams could consider using a lower-priced model for a broader set of routine agent work. Its reported performance, factuality and cost comparisons remain vendor claims, so they should guide evaluation priorities rather than be treated as independent evidence.
