Evidence

  • Demonstrated — SpaceX's May 20, 2026 SEC Form S-1 states that it entered cloud-services agreements with Anthropic in May 2026 for compute access across Colossus and Colossus II.
  • Claimed — SpaceX states in its SEC filing that Anthropic agreed to pay $1.25 billion per month through May 2029, with May and June 2026 capacity ramping at a reduced fee; either party may terminate the agreements on 90 days' notice. These are disclosed contract terms, not evidence that each fee has been paid or that the agreement will run through May 2029.
  • Claimed — In its SEC response, SpaceX says the agreements are not contracts on which its business is substantially dependent and says it believes capacity and revenue could be redeployed if the agreements end; this is SpaceX's characterization, not an independent measurement of redeployment or the economics to Anthropic.
  • Not established — The reviewed disclosures do not establish monthly payments made, exact capacity reserved or delivered, compute utilization, the contract's full conditions and remedies, or the comparative cost and performance of the supplied compute.

Strongest baseline

The filing provides no matched supplier or workload baseline. SpaceX's own statement that capacity and revenue could be redeployed is a counterparty representation, not an external comparison.

Limitations

  • The $1.25B monthly fee is an issuer-disclosed agreement term, not evidence of cash paid, full-period realization or a guaranteed three-year contract. May and June 2026 were described as reduced-fee ramp-up months.
  • Either party may terminate on 90 days' notice. The reviewed excerpts do not specify minimum take-or-pay obligations, detailed service levels, remedies, accelerator-hours or exact capacity allocation.
  • SpaceX's statements that compute capacity and revenue could be redeployed and that it is not substantially dependent on the agreements are issuer representations, not independent utilization or counterparty-dependence measurements.
  • No matched workload-level comparison establishes the delivered compute's cost, performance, energy use or reliability relative to other providers.

What we still don't know

  • No public record inspected establishes payments made, monthly fee realization, capacity delivery or accelerator-hours used under the Anthropic agreements.
  • The disclosed contract does not quantify Anthropic's delivered compute share, utilization or comparative unit economics across providers.
  • The public summary does not disclose full minimum-use, service-level, shortfall, or termination-remedy provisions.

Why it matters

A large frontier-compute contract provides a rare public view into the scale and structure of AI infrastructure demand. Its monthly fee, ramp schedule and short termination window together matter for financing and capacity planning; the record does not show realized spend, used capacity or Anthropic's dependence on SpaceX.
