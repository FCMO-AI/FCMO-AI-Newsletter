What changed

A September 21, 2026 arXiv paper introduces InfoPPO, which measures temporal progression in RLVR by accumulated information density rather than raw token count and uses that density to adapt policy clipping. In author-reported Mean@16 results across five competition-style mathematics benchmarks, average accuracy for Qwen3-4B/8B/14B is 34.9/41.2/49.4, compared with DAPO at 32.2/36.3/45.3. The paper reports response lengths broadly comparable to DAPO/DAPO-FT and shorter than PPO in its table, and adds one Llama-3.2-3B appendix comparison. These are CLAIMED paper results, not independently reproduced; no seed-level uncertainty is reported in the main aggregate table, and task/model transfer remains open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that InfoPPO replaces token-count time with accumulated state-wise information density for discounting and adaptive policy clipping, and gives higher average Mean@16 accuracy than DAPO across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks for Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3-14B base models.
  • Claimed — In the paper’s Table 1, Qwen3-4B average accuracy is 34.9 for InfoPPO versus 32.2 for DAPO; Qwen3-8B is 41.2 versus 36.3; and Qwen3-14B is 49.4 versus 45.3. Authors report lengths broadly comparable to DAPO/DAPO-FT and shorter than PPO in the listed settings.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproducibility, broad transfer beyond the tested mathematical reasoning tasks, or seed-level uncertainty for the reported aggregate comparisons.

Strongest baseline

DAPO is the main strongest matched practical baseline in Table 1 for the reported aggregate comparison; InfoPPO’s averages are higher on all three Qwen3 scales, but DAPO-FT leads InfoPPO on some individual benchmark cells and the study lacks seed-level uncertainty.

Limitations

  • All reported empirical results are from the authors’ paper; no independent reproduction was found in this review.
  • Table 1 uses Mean@16 with 16 independently sampled responses per prompt, but does not report seed-level uncertainty in the aggregate comparison.
  • Main experiments use Qwen3-4B/8B/14B base models trained on DAPO-Math-17K and five competition-style math benchmarks; generalization beyond this regime is unknown.
  • Appendix C reports one additional Llama-3.2-3B Instruct comparison, which is not broad cross-family validation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported InfoPPO comparisons has been inspected.
  • The main comparison reports Mean@16 per prompt but does not provide seed-level uncertainty in the reviewed table, so training-run variability and uncertainty of aggregate differences are not established.
  • The central empirical suite focuses on competition-style mathematical reasoning and three Qwen3 base-model scales; transfer to other model families, tasks, and reward regimes is limited.

Why it matters

If reproduced, information-conditioned temporal credit and policy updates could offer a useful way to preserve horizon control in long reasoning trajectories without simply lengthening outputs. The reported gains are specific to author-run math RLVR comparisons and are not yet evidence of general post-training improvement.
