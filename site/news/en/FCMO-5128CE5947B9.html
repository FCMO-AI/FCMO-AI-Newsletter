Financial LLM trading-agent review reports disclosed gaps in shock resilience and security controls

The arXiv v1 paper introduces FARSIGHT, a codebook-based method for reviewing published financial LLM trading-agent schemes across market-shock robustness and security controls. It is a scheme-level expert-review framework, not an automated benchmark suite or a live test of deployed agents. Applying it to 15 selected academic schemes, the authors report that 80% lacked evidence for at least one core robustness metric and 100% had at least one security weakness under their codebook. In a five-agent comparison, the LLM-based evaluator disagreed with human ratings on 24/45 metric cells (53.3%) and performed poorly on some newly defined threats. The ratings reflect what papers document, not confirmed absence from implementations; no proprietary production agents or live market behavior were evaluated.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors introduce FARSIGHT, a codebook-based scheme-level method—not an automated benchmark suite—to review published financial LLM trading-agent designs for four market-shock robustness dimensions and three groups of security threats.
  • CLAIMED — Applying the rubric to 15 selected academic trading-agent schemes, the authors report that 80% lacked evidence for at least one core robustness metric and 100% had at least one security weakness under their codebook. Ratings describe controls evidenced in the papers, not confirmed absence from code or running systems.
  • CLAIMED — In a five-agent, 45-metric-cell comparison, the authors report that an LLM-based assessment disagreed with human expert ratings on 24/45 cells (53.3%). The LLM performed worse on newly introduced threat types, with errors up to 100% in some cases.
  • CLAIMED — The paper’s limitations state that the review covers academic schemes rather than inaccessible proprietary systems, uses literature-grounded threat analysis rather than live adversarial tests, and cannot establish whether controls omitted from papers exist in code or later releases.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares human expert codebook ratings with a ChatGPT Deep Research evaluation on a five-agent subsample. Human paper review is the reference for that comparison, but independent inter-team recoding was not conducted.

The caveat that matters

  • Single arXiv v1 systematization paper; ratings are author-applied and were not independently recoded.
  • The final set contains 15 selected academic schemes identified from a literature search ending March 2026; it does not represent proprietary financial agents or establish deployment prevalence.
  • FARSIGHT is a paper-evidence codebook and expert-review method, not a fixed benchmark suite or live penetration test. A low score indicates that a paper did not evidence a control; the control may exist in code or later versions.
  • An LLM-based evaluator disagreed with human ratings on 24/45 cells (53.3%) in a five-agent subsample and had errors up to 100% on some new threat types; automated rubric scoring is not a substitute for human review.
  • Attack scenarios and systemic-risk implications are literature-grounded or simulated; no live market incident, actual financial loss or adversarial broker-in-the-loop exploit was demonstrated.

What remains unknown

  • No independent recoding of the 15 papers or reproduction of the rubric ratings was conducted.
  • The sample consists of 15 selected academic schemes, not proprietary production agents; a low rating means the paper did not evidence a control and does not prove code-level absence.
  • The LLM evaluator disagreed with human ratings on 24/45 cells in the five-agent sample and performed poorly on newly defined threats, limiting automation of fine-grained review.

FCMO Lens

Financial agents can combine external data ingestion, model reasoning and trade execution, so failures in input provenance or deterministic risk controls may affect real capital and interact through markets. The paper offers a structured way to inspect those risks, while its results apply to disclosed academic schemes and do not establish conditions in proprietary deployments.
