Recursive video retrieval reports higher frozen-VLA success on perturbed simulation tasks

A 5 October 2026 preprint reports Recursive Video In-Context Learning (RV-ICL), which indexes one task demonstration into four read-only levels—keyframes, phases, moments and short clips—and lets a planner retrieve detail for its current subgoal. The authors report HarnessVLA success rising from 92.6% to 96.5% on LIBERO-PRO and 86.7% to 95.8% on LIBERO-Plus. These are author-reported simulation results: on LIBERO-PRO the RV-ICL arm used 50 episodes while prior-work baselines used 100, and the paper’s reported significance statistic does not yet have a recovered matched-data audit. On LIBERO-Plus the methods were compared on a stratified 120 of 10,030 variants. The paper reports 1.8x wall-clock time and 1.4x input tokens on that comparison; physical-robot transfer is untested.

What actually changed

A 5 October 2026 preprint reports Recursive Video In-Context Learning (RV-ICL), which indexes one task demonstration into four read-only levels—keyframes, phases, moments and short clips—and lets a planner retrieve detail for its current subgoal. The authors report HarnessVLA success rising from 92.6% to 96.5% on LIBERO-PRO and 86.7% to 95.8% on LIBERO-Plus. These are author-reported simulation results: on LIBERO-PRO the RV-ICL arm used 50 episodes while prior-work baselines used 100, and the paper’s reported significance statistic does not yet have a recovered matched-data audit. On LIBERO-Plus the methods were compared on a stratified 120 of 10,030 variants. The paper reports 1.8x wall-clock time and 1.4x input tokens on that comparison; physical-robot transfer is untested.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors propose a four-level, read-only hierarchy over one demonstration per base task, with clips retrieved as the planner reaches relevant subgoals.
  • CLAIMED — In author-reported simulation results, HarnessVLA success rises from 92.6% to 96.5% on LIBERO-PRO and from 86.7% to 95.8% on LIBERO-Plus.
  • DEMONSTRATED — LIBERO-PRO compares 50 RV-ICL episodes per cell with 100 prior-work baseline episodes; LIBERO-Plus evaluates a stratified 120 of 10,030 variants, with 17–18 cases per perturbation dimension.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The paper reports 1.8x wall-clock time and 1.4x input tokens versus HarnessVLA on the LIBERO-Plus subset; all experiments are in simulation and use one robot and one VLA.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The reported improvement does not establish transfer to physical robots or broad task distributions, and the main LIBERO-PRO baseline comparison is not matched episode by episode.

Strongest baseline

HarnessVLA with the same frozen pi0.5 primitive. On LIBERO-PRO, the baseline values are from prior work and use twice as many episodes per cell; on the paper’s stratified LIBERO-Plus subset, the authors run both methods in the same time window.

The caveat that matters

  • The main LIBERO-PRO baseline values are reported from prior work with 100 episodes per cell; RV-ICL uses 50, so the comparison is not same-run or episode-matched.
  • Authors report z=2.97, p=0.003 for the aggregate LIBERO-PRO comparison, but exact per-task/seed outcomes, seed overlap and test code were not recovered; aggregate-count recomputation and dependence handling remain open.
  • LIBERO-Plus uses a stratified 120 of 10,030 variants, with only 17–18 examples per perturbation dimension.
  • The reported LIBERO-Plus success change costs 1.8x wall-clock time and 1.4x input tokens relative to HarnessVLA.
  • All experiments are simulated, use one robot and one VLA, and do not establish performance on physical robots or other embodiments.
  • Demonstrations are assumed to expose task sub-events; tasks lacking gripper/fixture event signals need other segmentation methods.

What remains unknown

  • No independent matched-seed replication or recovered per-episode RV-ICL results and exact seed list; the paper’s aggregate significance statistic is not independently auditable.
  • Physical-robot transfer and performance across other VLA models, robots and task families remain unknown.

FCMO Lens

The hierarchy makes demonstration video an on-demand memory source: an agent sees task structure before planning and retrieves short clips near a grasp, release or other subgoal. If the reported gains survive matched testing, this could improve long-horizon manipulation without retraining the VLA. Current evidence is limited to one author-run simulation study with unequal LIBERO-PRO sample counts, small perturbation cells and a measurable latency/token cost.
