What changed

An October 23 arXiv preprint argues that AI productivity comparisons should report end-to-end time alongside defects escaping independent review. It defines model-free time-saved and excess-defect quantities plus a break-even downstream defect cost. It reanalyzes public data from METR’s 246-issue developer study, reporting slowdown estimates of 19% or 24% depending on the estimand (34% without adjustment for a chance imbalance), and bounds the share of tasks helped at 3–82%. In a prespecified experiment on AI agents reviewing AI-written briefs from SEC filings, one Claude reviewer made fewer checks when told the drafter’s error rate was ten times lower than it was; the paper reports 22.5% of briefs then passed with a wrong figure versus none in the comparison arm. These are paper-reported results, not independent findings.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The author reports that reanalysis of the same 246 issues in METR’s developer study supports slowdown estimates of 19% or 24% depending on the estimand, or 34% without adjustment for a chance imbalance; the paper bounds the share of tasks helped between 3% and 82%.
  • Claimed — In the paper’s prespecified AI-reviewer experiment, a Claude reviewer told that a draft had a 3% error rate when it was actually 30% made about seven fewer of nine possible checks and let a wrong figure through in 22.5% of briefs versus none in the truthful-information comparison.
  • Claimed — The paper proposes reporting end-to-end time and excess escaped defects together, with a break-even downstream defect cost, rather than treating elapsed time alone as productivity.
  • Demonstrated — The arXiv record identifies version 1 as submitted on 2026-10-23; its appearance in the October 28 cs.AI new-listing page does not make it a new October 28 submission.

Strongest baseline

For the reviewer experiment, same-task AI drafts reviewed under a truthful stated error rate; for the METR reanalysis, published reported summaries of the original randomized study.

Limitations

  • This is an unreviewed single-author preprint; its methodological framing and reported reanalysis have not been independently checked here.
  • The paper reports no new human-subjects data. The prespecified experiment evaluates Claude Sonnet 5.5 reviewing Claude Haiku 5.5 drafts, on one kind of brief with seeded errors and a fixed stated cost model.
  • The author says the experiment was not lodged with an outside preregistration registry; a protocol and freeze record are reported in the linked repository.
  • The paper states its predictions P2 and P3 remain untested; its reviewer experiment tests only selected predictions on AI reviewers, not people.
  • The repository landing page README still describes the superseded April framework. The v2 README was inaccessible in this check; linked artifacts and claimed test counts were not independently run.
  • The paper does not measure downstream defect cost; adopters must supply context-specific costs. Its model uses stylized assumptions about defects and checking.
  • The author identifies and replaces errors in the earlier April TSB proposal, including double-counting repair time and inaccurate imported GDPval and BCG headline figures; the earlier framework should not be treated as current.
  • The METR reanalysis produces different slowdown estimates under different estimands and a wide 3–82% bound on tasks helped, underscoring that the paper does not establish a single universal productivity effect.

What we still don't know

  • The METR reanalysis and reviewer experiment were not independently reproduced in this activation; the GitHub v2 artifact page was not fully accessible.
  • The controlled experiment uses AI reviewers on one financial-document task and does not show human or deployed-workflow effects.

Why it matters

Productivity claims can change adoption and evaluation decisions while omitting the time spent checking and the cost of errors that reach downstream users. The paper offers a framework that keeps time, quality and local defect costs visible rather than collapsing them into a universal score. Its controlled reviewer experiment is limited to AI reviewers and one financial-document task, so it does not establish how people or deployed workflows behave.
