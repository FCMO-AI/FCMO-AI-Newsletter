What actually changed

Nam et al. describe ScientistTwo, a multi-agent workflow that takes research questions and accepted-paper baselines through idea generation, experiments, ablations and manuscript drafting. On 107 benchmark specifications derived from accepted ICLR 2026, NeurIPS 2025 and ICML 2026 papers, the authors report 86 successful executions (80.4%). They report that generated manuscripts scored above accepted-paper averages on ScholarPeer and Stanford Agentic Reviewer for ICLR 2026 and NeurIPS 2025. These ratings come from two automated reviewers, not an independent human expert panel; they do not establish novelty, expert-level discovery or real conference acceptance. The paper also states that its AutoSOTA comparison is not head-to-head because hardware and metrics differ.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that ScientistTwo completed 86 of 107 research tasks (80.4%) based on accepted conference papers and their codebases.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report above-average manuscript ratings versus accepted ICLR 2026 and NeurIPS 2025 comparison papers under ScholarPeer and Stanford Agentic Reviewer; these are automated-review scores, not human acceptance outcomes.
  • CLAIMED — The paper’s AutoSOTA comparison is explicitly not head-to-head on a common metric because systems use different hardware and report against their own reproduced baselines.

Strongest baseline

For task completion, accepted papers’ problem specifications and codebases serve as benchmark tasks. The paper also reports AutoSOTA comparisons, but explicitly says the systems use different hardware and metrics and the delta columns are not a head-to-head common-metric comparison.

The caveat that matters

  • Results are author-reported; no independent code execution or benchmark reproduction was performed.
  • Manuscript quality is evaluated by two automated reviewers; independent blinded domain-expert assessment and novelty adjudication are absent.
  • Successful execution does not establish scientific novelty, correctness or durable gains over a faithfully reproduced baseline.
  • The paper states AutoSOTA comparisons are not head-to-head because hardware and metrics differ.
  • The paper itself reports cases where automated critics or reruns reject purported improvements; these illustrate evaluation failure modes rather than validating overall performance.
  • In an appendix audit, the authors report that an AutoSOTA-selected SAE candidate did not survive its own rerun (0.7557 versus a 0.7586 baseline); they also state that cross-system improvement deltas are not directly comparable.

What remains unknown

  • Task execution counts and generated outputs have not been independently reproduced.
  • Novelty, methodological rigor and result validity rely on automated reviewers and author-selected examples rather than independent blinded experts.

FCMO Lens

If independently validated, an autonomous workflow that can formulate and test modifications to accepted research baselines could change the cost and throughput of computational research. The current evidence establishes that the authors report a substantial benchmark run and automated-review scores; evaluator dependence, novelty adjudication, baseline fidelity and independent reproduction remain decisive gaps.
