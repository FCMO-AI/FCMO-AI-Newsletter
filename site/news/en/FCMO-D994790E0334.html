What changed

The Octopus preprint describes a GPU-resident key-value (KV) state directed acyclic graph for branching search, attention-only mounting of sibling branches and a packed DAG-GRPO training method. In experiments with Qwen3.5-4B, the authors report 49.6% on BrowseComp-Plus and 51.2% on OpenResearcher, alongside KV-mount retention and task-hijacking measurements, throughput and peak-memory comparisons. These are paper-reported results from a single-backbone setup, not independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The paper proposes Octopus, a GPU-resident key-value state DAG for branching multi-agent search. Its described operations include forking, thread reads/waits, rewind and commit/done, with zero-copy radix cursors and selective attention-KV mounting across branches.
  • Claimed — Using Qwen3.5-4B with sparse BM25 retrieval, the authors report 49.6% on BrowseComp-Plus, 51.2% on OpenResearcher and a 17.5 percentage-point gain over their single-agent GRPO comparison. The reported regime uses 830 BrowseComp-Plus test queries and a 14.9-million-document OpenResearch corpus; these outcomes have not been independently reproduced.
  • Claimed — The authors report that attention-only KV mounting retained 92.2% of answer information and produced 2.7% task hijacking, compared with 57.9% answer-information retention in their full-text condition; they also report 3.3× throughput and 2.8× peak KV-memory figures. These are paper-reported results, not independently verified measurements.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproduction, heterogeneous-backbone coordination, persistent memory, multi-node scaling to millions of agents, or generality beyond its reported single-backbone evaluation.

Strongest baseline

The paper’s stated single-agent GRPO Qwen3.5-4B comparison is the closest matched baseline for BrowseComp-Plus; the attention-only versus full-text mount comparison is the internal control for the context-transfer result. No independent matched baseline evaluation was performed.

Limitations

  • The reported results come from one Qwen3.5-4B backbone with sparse BM25 retrieval and bounded benchmark datasets.
  • No independent implementation audit or benchmark reproduction was performed in this activation.
  • The paper does not demonstrate heterogeneous multi-model operation, persistent memory, or multi-node scaling to millions of agents.
  • ArXiv abstract and full HTML list different author counts; attribution remains unresolved.

What we still don't know

  • No independent matched reproduction of the BrowseComp-Plus, OpenResearcher, retention, hijacking, throughput or memory results was found or performed in this activation.
  • The paper links a public implementation repository, but this activation did not pin an immutable commit or inspect serving, KV-mount, trainer, evaluation code or tests.
  • The author metadata differs between the arXiv abstract page and full HTML author block.

Why it matters

A shared latent KV substrate may let search branches reuse computation and context instead of duplicating full prompts. If the paper’s author-reported results survive matched independent evaluation, this could improve multi-step agent search efficiency; current evidence is limited to one model family and paper-reported benchmarks.
