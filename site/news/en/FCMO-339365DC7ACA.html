Japan Digital Agency survey reports self-reported time savings and uneven uptake for government AI Gennai

Japan’s Digital Agency published the first usage survey for Gennai, its government generative-AI environment. The questionnaire received 6,439 responses from government employees across ministries and related bodies; the satisfaction and task-time results reported here use a 3,078-person subset. The Agency reports 71.7% satisfaction in that subset and estimates about 1.83 hours saved per user per week from self-reported task-time bands. Its materials differ slightly on the share reporting at least one hour saved: 54.4% on the release page and detailed PDF page, versus 54.5% on the PDF overview slide. The estimate uses band midpoints, caps “10+ hours saved” at 10, and treats unknown/no-change responses as zero. About 31.2% of all 6,439 respondents said they rarely or never used Gennai. These are first-party survey responses and an assumption-based estimate, not independently measured time savings or causal evidence.

What changed

Japan’s Digital Agency published the first usage survey for Gennai, its government generative-AI environment. The questionnaire received 6,439 responses from government employees across ministries and related bodies; the satisfaction and task-time results reported here use a 3,078-person subset. The Agency reports 71.7% satisfaction in that subset and estimates about 1.83 hours saved per user per week from self-reported task-time bands. Its materials differ slightly on the share reporting at least one hour saved: 54.4% on the release page and detailed PDF page, versus 54.5% on the PDF overview slide. The estimate uses band midpoints, caps “10+ hours saved” at 10, and treats unknown/no-change responses as zero. About 31.2% of all 6,439 respondents said they rarely or never used Gennai. These are first-party survey responses and an assumption-based estimate, not independently measured time savings or causal evidence.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Japan’s Digital Agency published the first Gennai usage survey, conducted by web questionnaire from August 21 to September 18, 2026; it reports 6,439 government-employee responses, including central and regional offices and the House of Representatives and Supreme Court secretariats.
  • Claimed — Among the 3,078 respondents represented in the satisfaction result, 71.7% reported being satisfied with Gennai. This is first-party survey data, not an independently measured service-quality result.
  • Claimed — The Digital Agency estimates about 1.83 hours (about 110 minutes) saved per user per week from self-reported task-time bands. It assigns band midpoints, caps “10+ hours saved” at 10, and treats unknown or near-zero-change responses as zero. The release page and detailed PDF report 54.4% of the task-time subset saving at least one hour per week, while the PDF overview slide reports 54.5%; this small source-internal discrepancy is unresolved.
  • Claimed — The survey reports that 31.2% of all 6,439 respondents rarely or never used Gennai; the task-time and satisfaction results cover a smaller 3,078-person subset.
  • Not established — The survey does not establish representative take-up across the full eligible workforce, independently measured time savings, net productivity after review or rework, service-quality gains, or a causal effect of Gennai.

Strongest baseline

No control group or pre/post instrumented baseline is reported in the inspected first-survey summary. Comparisons are respondents’ recollections of time before versus after using Gennai for selected tasks.

Limitations

  • The Digital Agency is the program operator and the source of the survey and time-saving estimate; no independent measurement was performed in this activation.
  • The 6,439 responses came from a web questionnaire for government employees; the public summary does not establish representativeness or provide a causal comparison.
  • The satisfaction and task-time questions use a 3,078-person subset, not all 6,439 responses or the entire roughly 180,000-person trial workforce.
  • The 1.83-hour weekly estimate imputes values from self-reported ranges; the agency uses midpoints, caps the open-ended savings band at 10 hours, and treats unknown/near-no-change answers as zero.
  • The Agency’s materials report both 54.4% and 54.5% for at-least-one-hour savings; no explanation for the small discrepancy was found.
  • The Japan Digital Agency release page reports 54.4% of the task-time respondents saving at least one hour per week. Its PDF overview slide reports 54.5%, while the detailed PDF results page reports 54.4% (n=3,078). The raw categories and estimates are close, but the agency materials do not explain the rounding difference; retain the discrepancy rather than selecting one figure.

What we still don't know

  • Time savings, satisfaction and adoption frequency are self-reported by respondents; the 1.83-hour estimate is derived from category midpoints rather than direct time measurement.
  • The public summary does not establish that respondents are representative of all eligible staff or provide a counterfactual; satisfaction/time-change results use a 3,078-person subset of 6,439 responses.

Why it matters

This is unusually concrete public-sector adoption evidence: a government agency has reported user feedback, a quantified time-saving estimate and substantial nonuse while its Gennai program targets a much larger workforce. The results can inform deployment choices, but the survey is self-reported, outcome items use 3,078 rather than all 6,439 respondents, and the estimate imputes values from response bands. It does not establish independently measured productivity, net time after review/rework, service quality, or causal impact.
