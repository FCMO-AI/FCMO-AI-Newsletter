MMSafe reports multilingual fine-tuning filter that lowers measured safety regressions

A preprint proposes MMSafe, which scores fine-tuning examples using both a language-shared safety-sensitive layer and language-specific layers. Across four Llama and Qwen model variants, two fine-tuning datasets and three multilingual safety benchmarks, the authors report harmful-response ratios averaging about 39% of random-filtering baselines; the method beats a prior single-layer filter on three of four model variants. They report that general and multilingual utility scores remain broadly similar in the tested settings, but results vary by dataset, model and language-specific weighting. Findings are limited to the paper’s LoRA fine-tuning and benchmark settings. The authors report releasing code, but this activation did not run it or independently reproduce the results.

Evidence

  • Claimed — MMSafe combines a language-shared safety-sensitive layer with language-specific layers to rank candidate fine-tuning examples by estimated safety-degrading potential.
  • Claimed — Across four Llama/Qwen variants, two fine-tuning datasets and six dataset-benchmark combinations, authors report harmful-response ratios of 31.8%–46.5% relative to random filtering; the method beats single-layer LARF on three of four variants.
  • Claimed — The paper reports broadly similar general and multilingual utility in its tested settings, while sensitivity to language-specific weighting varies across models and datasets.
  • Demonstrated scope limit — The results use LoRA fine-tuning and benchmark evaluation; they do not establish deployment safety or eliminate language-specific regressions.

Strongest baseline

Random selection under the same model, dataset and benchmark; LARF is the single-layer comparison.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported; this activation did not execute code or reproduce the study.
  • Safety is primarily evaluated with LlamaGuard 3, with additional automatic and human judges reported in an appendix.
  • Experiments use four model variants, two datasets, LoRA and selected multilingual benchmarks; deployment safety is not established.
  • Weighting trades off differently across models and datasets, with small regressions in some languages.

What we still don't know

  • No independent execution of released implementation or reproduction of aggregate results was performed.
  • Evaluation covers four model variants, two fine-tuning corpora, LoRA and selected safety benchmarks; performance outside this regime is unknown.
  • Preferred language-specific weighting varies by model and dataset; some language-level regressions are reported.

Why it matters

Fine-tuning data can weaken safety behavior even when it is not overtly harmful. The paper tests whether multilingual safety signals span different model layers and reports a data-filtering approach that may help preserve safety during adaptation.
