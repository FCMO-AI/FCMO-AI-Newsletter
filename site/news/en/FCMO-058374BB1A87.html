A paper shows policy shifts can invalidate one-step world-model coverage, then tests ratio-weighted recalibration

The Aug 20, 2026 preprint studies uncertainty calibration when a fixed robot world model is evaluated under a changed action-selection policy. Under a shared conditional transition mechanism, it derives a density-ratio correction for transporting one-step marginal conformal coverage and a bound linking estimated-ratio error to coverage loss. In an author-run experiment using 192 physical Franka transitions from 15 episodes, the predictor remains fixed while the evaluation pool is reweighted by an outcome-blind action-magnitude statistic. At nominal 90% coverage and the strongest controlled shift, ordinary source calibration covers 55.0%; oracle and learned ratio weighting cover 91.0% and 90.9%. The study is paper-reported and does not test a new live policy rollout: the target distribution is a controlled reweighting of the same finite pool. Its guarantees require shared conditional dynamics and are one-step and marginal, not joint trajectory guarantees.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors derive a one-step marginal weighted-conformal coverage result for a fixed predictor when source and target share the conditional outcome mechanism and the target query distribution is absolutely continuous with respect to the source; for policy-controlled dynamics the density ratio factors into state-occupancy and action-propensity terms.
  • Claimed — On 192 one-step physical Franka transitions from 15 episodes, under the strongest controlled reweighting of the same pool, nominal 90% coverage is 55.0% with ordinary source calibration, 91.0% with oracle ratio weighting and 90.9% with learned ratio weighting; the predictor is held fixed.
  • Not established — The reported experiment does not establish calibration during a live deployment-policy change, under changed transition mechanisms, or for joint multi-step trajectory coverage; it uses controlled distribution tilts on one fixed finite pool and reports no independent reproduction.

Strongest baseline

Ordinary conformal calibration on the source distribution, compared with oracle and learned-ratio weighting while holding the predictor and transition pool fixed.

Limitations

  • Author-reported results; no independent reproduction was performed in this review.
  • The physical evaluation uses 192 one-step transitions from 15 Franka episodes covering folding, picking and pushing; the target law is a controlled reweighting of this fixed pool, not a live policy rollout.
  • The guarantee is one-step and marginal and assumes shared conditional dynamics, target support contained in source support and pre-outcome weights.
  • Mechanism shift, including generated dynamics, and joint trajectory coverage are explicitly left for additional methods or data.
  • At the strongest shift, learned weighting restores reported coverage but effective sample size falls from 500 to 73 and the conformal radius widens; clipping at five raises effective sample size to 125 but lowers coverage to 83.8% by changing the target surrogate.

What we still don't know

  • The reported formal and empirical findings have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The physical experiment reweights one fixed pool; it does not evaluate observations collected after a planner actually changes its policy or state occupancy.
  • The guarantee is one-step and marginal under shared conditional dynamics; mechanism shift and joint trajectory coverage are outside the paper’s stated scope.

Why it matters

A world model can keep the same predictor while a planner changes which state-action inputs it queries. If the query distribution shifts, coverage calibrated on the data-collection policy may cease to apply. This paper gives a concrete correction and a controlled physical-data example; the bounded result motivates policy-aware uncertainty checks but does not establish them for live deployment or changing dynamics.
