White-box probes report transfer from broad falsehood training data to agentic deception monitoring

A new FAR.AI-affiliated preprint reports that activation probes trained on a broad dataset of falsehood examples can flag agent side-task behavior and some hidden-goal falsehoods in controlled evaluations. On 699 scored Qwen3.5-397B-A17B episodes across 14 SHADE-Arena scenarios, the authors report probe AUROC of 0.979–0.984 versus 0.960 for an Opus 5.5 text monitor. What actually changed

A new FAR.AI-affiliated preprint reports that activation probes trained on a broad dataset of falsehood examples can flag agent side-task behavior and some hidden-goal falsehoods in controlled evaluations. On 699 scored Qwen3.5-397B-A17B episodes across 14 SHADE-Arena scenarios, the authors report probe AUROC of 0.979–0.984 versus 0.960 for an Opus 5.5 text monitor. The results are author-reported; the study defines its target as knowing falsehood rather than deceptive intent, uses an LLM judge for action labels, and does not establish reliable deployment monitoring.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors introduce FIBS, a dataset of 163,016 transcripts across 28 families, and four activation-probe architectures trained to detect knowing falsehoods from model hidden states.
  • CLAIMED — On Qwen3.5-397B-A17B in SHADE-Arena, the authors report probe AUROC values of 0.979–0.984 versus 0.960 for an Opus 5.5 text-monitor baseline, over 699 scored episodes in 14 scenarios; labels are derived using an LLM judge.
  • CLAIMED — Across several introspective evaluations, the authors report that probes distinguish some falsehoods whose truth depends on the model's elicited belief or hidden goal; performance varies, including 0.63 AUC on one hidden-goal organism without a behavior trigger.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The authors made an inference package and trained probe weights publicly accessible; this activation inspected the repository documentation but did not run the code or reproduce the reported results.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares probes with an Opus 5.5 text monitor, self-report logits and other probe architectures on the same reported benchmark episodes; this is not an independent reproduction or a deployment baseline.

The caveat that matters

  • The quantitative findings are reported in a new arXiv preprint; no independent replication or external peer review was inspected in this activation.
  • The authors define the target as a model statement it believes to be false. This does not establish deceptive intent, all forms of misalignment, or future harmful action.
  • FIBS contains 163,016 rows across 28 families, but the authors say 76% are synthetic/follow-up-derived, 18% on-policy, 6% bespoke, only 5.5% include reasoning, and the set is largely artificial and off-policy for the probed models.
  • SHADE-Arena action-monitor results cover 14 of 25 scenarios after 11 were excluded because a single step could not be reliably labeled; per-step labels are derived from a Claude Haiku 4.5 judge.
  • The hidden-goal organism results are not uniform: one organism is classified at 0.63 AUC without a behavior trigger, and performance rises to 0.93 when an example of its behavior is present in context. The authors link limitations to model belief and persona activation.
  • The linked repository documents inference code and weights, but this activation did not execute code, audit the artifacts or independently reproduce results. The paper's claim that probes cost less is not independently measured here.

What remains unknown

  • No independent group reproduced the probe-versus-text-monitor results or audited the full dataset construction and label pipeline.
  • Transfer to different task distributions, current deployed models, languages and live monitoring decisions is unknown; false-positive and false-negative intervention costs were not measured.
  • FIBS is mostly synthetic and off-policy, while the authors note that probing can fail when a relevant behavior is activated only by a different persona or the subject model does not represent the relevant belief.

FCMO Lens

If independently reproduced across models and tasks, white-box probes that detect behavior missed by text-only monitors could add a useful signal to frontier-agent evaluations, particularly when visible transcripts do not reveal a model's beliefs. The current evidence is limited to the preprint's synthetic/off-policy data and constructed benchmarks.
