GEM reports that geometric coverage can miss task evidence in agent-history compression

A September 2026 preprint introduces GEM, a training-free compressor that protects selected task and execution evidence before adding history blocks by geometric residual. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The preprint describes GEM, a training-free compressor that protects selected task and execution evidence, then adds history blocks by geometric residual; retained original blocks are forwarded in chronological order.
  • Claimed — In a matched-retained-count analysis over 650 checkpoints from 32 trajectories, authors report next-action Top-3 history-block retention of 0.69 with evidence constraints versus 0.31 with geometry-only selection, while pooled centroid similarity rounds to 0.98 in both conditions. Authors treat trajectories as independent units and say the pooled means do not establish statistical equivalence.
  • Claimed — Across 260 WorkBuddyBench tasks, authors report mean combined token use falling from 2.69M to 2.11M per task and mean reward changing from 69.87 to 70.18; domain outcomes vary, including only a small token reduction and lower reward on Code tasks.
  • Not established — The offline Top-3 metric measures retention of blocks most similar to a recorded next action, not task success. The separate 20-task online ablation is small and mixed by domain; the fixed-40 baseline comparison uses a separate, unpaired panel.

Strongest baseline

Geometry-only selection is the matched-budget comparator for the 650-checkpoint evidence-retention result. Online reward/token comparisons include the base agent; the fixed-40 reference panel compares several memory and compression methods but is a separate unpaired task panel.

Limitations

  • All performance measurements are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The matched-budget comparison pools checkpoint means descriptively; the 32 trajectories, not 650 checkpoints, are the independent units, and reported centroid similarity does not establish statistical equivalence.
  • Next-action Top-3 retention is an offline representation metric, not agent task success.
  • The primary online result is one 260-task benchmark and hides domain variation; Code has little token reduction and lower mean reward.
  • The 20-task online ablation is small; the fixed-40 reference is a separate, unpaired panel.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported offline retention analyses or online benchmark outcomes has been completed.
  • Trajectory-level uncertainty estimates for the pooled matched-budget means and replication on another benchmark are needed to assess generality.

Why it matters

History compressors can preserve a global representation while discarding records that carry distinct execution evidence. GEM provides a concrete test of that distinction and reports lower aggregate token use on one online benchmark. Its offline evidence-retention measure must not be read as task success, and the author-run results need independent replication.
