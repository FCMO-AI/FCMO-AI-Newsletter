What changed

The authors introduce Autonomous Science Bench v0.1, an open set of six budgeted experimental campaigns grounded in published biology, chemistry and materials-science data. Its two gates separately score whether an agent discovers promising measured designs and whether its submitted model ranks unseen designs. In a pilot of eight agent-model configurations across 144 trials, the paper reports 31 full passes; Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Code leads at 12 of 18, but no configuration is reliable across all campaigns. Five campaigns replay published measurements and one uses a digital twin; no physical laboratory result is reported. This is a benchmark release and author-reported pilot, not evidence that agents autonomously perform reliable real-world science.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors released version 0.1 of an open benchmark with six budgeted design-build-test-learn campaigns across biology, chemistry and materials science, and a lab service that separates experimental selection from scoring.
  • Demonstrated — The benchmark separately scores discovery among measured designs and learning by ranking held-out designs; a campaign passes only when both gates pass.
  • Claimed — In a pilot with eight agent-model configurations, three trials per configuration per campaign and 144 total trials, the authors report 31 passes; Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code passes 12 of 18 campaign trials, while results vary by campaign and configurations miss different gates.
  • Not established — Version 0.1 uses five replay backends and one digital twin; no physical-lab evaluation is reported. The sample covers six campaigns and eight configurations, with three trials per campaign/configuration. Reported comparisons are between agent-model configurations using different scaffolds, not isolated models.
  • Demonstrated — Because campaigns are based on published experiments, model memorization or retrieval may provide shortcuts; the authors audit known shortcuts but state that unpublished live experiments are needed to fully address this limitation.

Strongest baseline

Calibration includes campaign-specific reference solutions, static selection strategies, adaptive methods and zero-measurement predictors. The pilot compares agent-model configurations, but different coding-agent scaffolds and three trials per cell limit model-level ranking.

Limitations

  • This is an author-reported pilot with 144 trials and three attempts per agent-model configuration on each of six campaigns; independent reproduction is not available in this review.
  • Five campaigns return replayed published measurements and one uses a digital twin; the live physical-lab backend is only a defined interface contract.
  • The campaigns use published data that may be present in model training or reachable through retrieval; known shortcuts are audited, but the paper says unpublished live results are needed to address this fully.
  • Scaffolds and prompts differ between proprietary coding agents and open-weight model agents, so the measured unit is an agent-model configuration, not a model-only comparison.
  • Version 0.1 covers only six campaigns in three scientific fields and eight evaluated configurations.
  • In the chemistry propylene campaign, the repository says the twin returns the source study’s 96 measured values for those designs but surrogate outputs for the other 884 of the 980-design space; that surrogate was trained on all 300 study measurements and returns noiseless means. This campaign therefore tests search within a source-study-derived twin, not prospective chemical discovery or transfer beyond the surrogate’s training coverage.

What we still don't know

  • Independent reproduction of the 144-trial pilot, including trial-level outputs, seed sensitivity and exact cost accounting.
  • Evaluate a live physical-lab backend using unpublished outcomes and prospective experiments to test whether replay/twin rankings transfer to real laboratory workflows.
  • Compare benchmark coverage and scoring against ScienceAgentBench, BoxingGym, Terminal-Bench-Science and other active-experimentation evaluations; determine whether the two-gate distinction changes decisions beyond this initial suite.

Why it matters

The benchmark makes two separable scientific-agent objectives explicit: choose experiments that find worthwhile designs, and learn a ranking that generalizes to unmeasured candidates. The pilot shows these can fail independently, so one aggregate success score can conceal whether an agent struggles with experimental discovery or generalization. The open v0.1 benchmark could inform evaluations, but its small campaign/configuration set, mostly replayed measurements and published-data exposure limit claims about live laboratory science.
