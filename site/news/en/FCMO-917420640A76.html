What actually changed

Song et al. introduce EmbodiedRSI, a harness around a frozen robot foundation model that selects physical experiments by value of information, co-evolves code and skills, and learns which memories improve later adaptation. The 7 October 2026 preprint reports 77.0% overall success on RoboCasa365 and 71.3% on Composite-Unseen versus 40.1% for Harness VLA; on LIBERO-Pro it reports 86.8% versus 72.1%. On a physical SO-101 arm, the authors report 71.3% success across five tasks versus 46.0% for SmolVLA alone, with 30 trials per condition per task. These are author-reported results, not independently reproduced. The physical evaluation is limited to five tasks; the harness lowers success on the glasses-grasp task, and the foundation model is not updated.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — Song et al. introduce a harness that selects robot experiments using a hypothesis graph and value-of-information score, co-evolves executable code and skills, and trains memory actions from downstream reward while keeping the robot foundation model frozen during trials.
  • CLAIMED — On RoboCasa365, the authors report 77.0% overall success and 71.3% on Composite-Unseen, versus 40.1% for Harness VLA on Composite-Unseen; on LIBERO-Pro they report 86.8% versus 72.1% for Harness VLA.
  • CLAIMED — On a physical SO-101 arm, the authors report that EmbodiedRSI's simulation-evolved harness raises success from 46.0% to 71.3% overall across five tasks, with 30 trials per condition per task; success falls on Glasses Bridge Grasp from 60.0% to 56.7%.
  • SPECULATIVE — The reported results establish broad recursive self-improvement of robot foundation models.

Strongest baseline

The paper reports Harness VLA as the strongest RoboCasa365 Composite-Unseen baseline (40.1%) and LIBERO-Pro baseline (72.1%). For physical tasks it compares the same SmolVLA backbone alone with SmolVLA under EmbodiedRSI's harness.

The caveat that matters

  • All performance numbers are author-reported; the research desk did not independently execute code or reproduce simulation or physical trials.
  • The physical evaluation uses one SO-101 arm, one SmolVLA backbone and five selected tasks. Its pooled 71.3% gain is not a general robot-transfer rate.
  • The harness reduces success on Glasses Bridge Grasp from 60.0% to 56.7%; outcomes are not uniformly improved.
  • The underlying robot model is fine-tuned on 50 teleoperated demonstrations per physical task for 20,000 steps using four H200 GPUs before trials; only the harness transfers zero-shot and the backbone remains frozen during trials.
  • The paper reports simulation seeds and trial counts but does not establish independent replication, broad hardware transfer or deployment cost per successful task.
  • The model parameters are not updated; the evidence concerns harness-level adaptation, not recursive self-improvement of the foundation model.
  • In the physical task table, glasses-bridge-grasp success falls from 60.0% with SmolVLA alone to 56.7% with the EmbodiedRSI harness, despite an aggregate gain across five tasks.
  • The paper's zero-shot sim-to-real framing refers to transferring the harness; the backbone was fine-tuned using 50 teleoperated demonstrations for each task before physical testing.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction of the simulation or five-task physical results was conducted, and a public implementation/data release was not established.
  • Physical evidence is limited to one SO-101 arm, one fine-tuned SmolVLA backbone and five tasks; transfer across robots, backbones, labs and task distributions is unknown.

FCMO Lens

If the reported gains hold in broader independent trials, choosing informative physical experiments and jointly updating executable skills and code could make robot adaptation more sample-efficient without retraining the foundation model. The reported real-robot evidence is promising but narrow, and does not establish general recursive self-improvement or broad transfer.
