BackTrend reports that current research agents recover few validated scientific weak signals in a retrospective benchmark

BackTrend is a retrospective benchmark built from 25 mature AI/ML topics and 66 human-validated early problem- and solution-space signals, with systems restricted to evidence available by the end of 2023. Across seven evaluated LLM, RAG and agentic systems, the authors report maximum semantic-judge F1 of 10.1% and Coverage@10 of 18.5%; deeper retrieval helps some systems, while unlimited search can reduce performance. Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors construct BackTrend from 25 mature AI/ML topics and 66 human-validated problem- and solution-space weak signals, using 2019–2024 publication-frequency trajectories and an end-of-2023 evidence cutoff.
  • Claimed — Across seven evaluated LLM, retrieval-augmented and agentic research systems, the paper reports best semantic-judge F1 of 10.1% and maximum Coverage@10 of 18.5%; added retrieval/search helps up to a moderate budget but does not close the measured gap.
  • Claimed — In a stratified sample of 100 unmatched predictions, two annotators judged 49% and 57% plausible as weak signals absent from the reference set, indicating reference-based F1 may understate defensible predictions.
  • Not established — The retrospective benchmark does not establish prospective scientific-foresight performance, generality beyond its AI/ML topic set, or an independent validation of all labels and system scores.

Strongest baseline

The best reported system achieves 10.1% semantic-judge F1 and 18.5% Coverage@10; the paper reports no high-performing system that closes the reference-signal gap.

Limitations

  • The benchmark is retrospective; it does not test real-time prospective discovery before outcomes mature.
  • Only 25 AI/ML topics are included, limiting domain and topic generality.
  • The reference set may be incomplete: two annotators judged 49% and 57% of a sample of unmatched predictions plausible.
  • Scores and label construction are paper-reported and were not independently reproduced here.

What we still don't know

  • The evaluation and construction of the 66 weak-signal labels were not independently reproduced or audited in this activation.
  • A sampled unmatched-prediction audit suggests reference incompleteness may depress reference-based F1; prospective and out-of-domain validity are unknown.

Why it matters

Research agents can produce plausible narratives about emerging science, but this benchmark tests whether they recover concrete precursors that later grew in measured prominence. Its reported low recovery and error modes expose a gap between plausible synthesis and evidence-aligned signal identification. The benchmark is retrospective and its incomplete-reference issue limits direct interpretation as prospective capability.
