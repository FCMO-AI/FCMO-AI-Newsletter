Frontis-MA1 and OpenMLE report gains on machine-learning engineering benchmarks under a 12-hour budget

A July 2026 preprint introduces OpenMLE, an execution-grounded machine-learning engineering environment and search framework, and Frontis-MA1 (35B), a post-trained model for program evolution. On MLE-Bench Lite, the authors report Medal Average rising from 39.39% for the base model to 60.61% with OpenMLE-Evo and 71.21% with OpenMLE-Evo-Max, under a 12-hour per-task budget on one RTX 4090 limited to 12 GB VRAM. On held-out NatureBench Lite, they report Match-SOTA rising from 50% to 70% when substituting the trained model under a fixed framework, and from 20% to 50% when substituting OpenMLE-Evo under a fixed model. These are preprint-reported results in defined benchmark regimes, not independent reproductions or evidence of general recursive self-improvement.

What actually changed

On MLE-Bench Lite, the authors report Medal Average rising from 39.39% for the base model to 60.61% with OpenMLE-Evo and 71.21% with OpenMLE-Evo-Max, under a 12-hour per-task budget on one RTX 4090 limited to 12 GB VRAM. Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The preprint reports Frontis-MA1 (35B) Medal Average of 60.61% with OpenMLE-Evo versus 39.39% for its base model and 71.21% with OpenMLE-Evo-Max on MLE-Bench Lite, under a 12-hour per-task budget on one RTX 4090 capped at 12 GB VRAM.
  • CLAIMED — On held-out NatureBench Lite, the authors report Match-SOTA rising from 50% to 70% when substituting the trained model with the framework fixed, and from 20% to 50% when substituting OpenMLE-Evo with the model fixed.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares the base model with OpenMLE-Evo and OpenMLE-Evo-Max on MLE-Bench Lite and performs separate model-swap and framework-swap comparisons on held-out NatureBench Lite.

The caveat that matters

  • Quantitative results are author-reported preprint findings and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • MLE-Bench Lite and NatureBench Lite task construction, scoring and task-quality controls were not independently audited.
  • The stated 12-hour per-task and 12 GB VRAM regime does not determine full compute, energy, monetary or human-supervision cost.
  • Performance on bounded ML engineering benchmarks does not establish recursive self-improvement of frontier model development or broad transfer.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction or artifact-level audit of the reported MLE-Bench Lite and NatureBench Lite results was performed.
  • The paper's benchmark gains do not establish an open-ended recursive improvement loop or transfer to broader frontier-model development.

FCMO Lens

If reproduced, the paper suggests that execution-grounded training of reusable code-evolution operators and long-horizon search can improve measured performance on machine-learning engineering tasks, with separate model and framework contributions reported on a second benchmark. This is relevant to AI-for-AI research systems, but benchmark gains under long task budgets do not establish autonomous improvement of frontier model development or recursive self-improvement in a broader sense.
