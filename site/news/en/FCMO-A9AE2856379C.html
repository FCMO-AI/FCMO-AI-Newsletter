Limitations

  • The source release artifacts and test badges are project-authored; they establish publication and reported check status, not independent validation.
  • The release notes say not all planned issue #2619 work was accomplished, without enumerating every incomplete goal in the release summary.
  • The flow-control e2e script scopes its proof to priority ordering; realistic weighted fairness and service-level behavior at scale are outside that check.
  • Selected release matrix lanes passing do not establish all cloud/accelerator combinations, all guides, or field reliability under production faults and traffic.
  • Large throughput, latency, cache-hit and cost examples remain provider/customer reports without full matched controls or the research desk reproduction.

What we still don't know

  • The release notes state that not all tracked issue #2619 goals were accomplished but do not identify the incomplete items in their summary.
  • Release badges report selected first-party guide lanes passing, but their runs do not establish realistic weighted multi-tenant fairness or SLO behavior at scale.
  • The release matrix does not independently establish router failure recovery, no-request-loss rollouts, cache continuity or session-affinity behavior under realistic agent traffic.
  • Reported speed, cache and cost figures lack a fully matched account of model, hardware, workload, concurrency, quality target, SLO, utilization and infrastructure cost.

Why it matters

A stable, cross-component release with explicit traffic prioritization, coordinated disaggregated upgrades and agent-session routing could make heterogeneous inference fleets easier to operate and affect serving reliability and cost. The primary release artifacts establish that v1.0.0 was published and that some release-scoped guide lanes are marked passing. Those checks do not show independent service outcomes: the flow-control test documents a narrower priority-ordering scope, and the 5–10x or 11.5x cost and throughput claims still need matched, independent accounting.
