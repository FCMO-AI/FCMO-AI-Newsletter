BlockLock paper reports reproducible batched LLM inference with fixed request geometry

A 4 November 2026 preprint introduces BlockLock, which fixes the execution geometry of one protected request while retaining shared batched prefill for companion requests. Across five dense models and three GPU types, the authors report bitwise-identical target outputs across companion changes under a fixed deployment contract. The headline performance comparison is narrower: at B=4 and 64 output tokens per request, BlockLock-Rows reduced latency by 53.5–55.0% and increased throughput by 115.2–122.2% versus vLLM Batch-Invariant for Qwen3-8B/14B across eight datasets; reported overhead was 5.4–6.9% versus standard vLLM and 0.5–0.8% versus padded PyTorch. These are author-reported fixed-workload results; no independent reproduction, MoE evaluation or queueing/tail-latency study is established.

What changed

A 4 November 2026 preprint introduces BlockLock, which fixes the execution geometry of one protected request while retaining shared batched prefill for companion requests. Across five dense models and three GPU types, the authors report bitwise-identical target outputs across companion changes under a fixed deployment contract. The headline performance comparison is narrower: at B=4 and 64 output tokens per request, BlockLock-Rows reduced latency by 53.5–55.0% and increased throughput by 115.2–122.2% versus vLLM Batch-Invariant for Qwen3-8B/14B across eight datasets; reported overhead was 5.4–6.9% versus standard vLLM and 0.5–0.8% versus padded PyTorch. These are author-reported fixed-workload results; no independent reproduction, MoE evaluation or queueing/tail-latency study is established.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that BlockLock-Rows preserves the protected request’s generated tokens and full-vocabulary logits bitwise across changes in companion requests, under a fixed deployment configuration with repeat-stable kernels and collectives.
  • Claimed — The authors report an equal-delivered-work BlockLock-Rows comparison at B=4 and 64 output tokens per request: for Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B across eight datasets, latency was 53.5–55.0% lower and throughput 115.2–122.2% higher than vLLM Batch-Invariant. Reported geometric-mean latency overhead versus standard vLLM was 5.4–6.9%, and versus padded PyTorch 0.5–0.8%. The broader five-model, three-GPU evaluation supports the reported determinism results; these performance figures are not established across that full matrix.
  • Not established — Independent reproduction, transfer across deployment configurations, benefits under real arrival queues or tail-latency workloads, and performance on mixture-of-experts models are not established by the paper.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares BlockLock-Rows with standard vLLM, vLLM Batch-Invariant, padded PyTorch, and a CoRun-style isolated-prefill baseline. Equal-delivered-work comparisons use the same number of generated tokens for full-batch methods; BlockLock-Pack is target-only and not a same-work comparison.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported in a preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The determinism guarantee is conditional on a fixed hardware/software/precision/backend/batch-geometry configuration and repeat-stable kernels and collectives; changing deployments is outside the claim.
  • Only five dense models and three single-GPU deployment types are evaluated; MoE models are left to future work.
  • The benchmark comparison is controlled and fixed-group; queueing, arrival processes, TTFT/TPOT and tail latency are not measured.
  • At high companion-length variance, padding waste increases; the authors identify this as a limitation.

What we still don't know

  • The paper-reported determinism and timing results have not been independently reproduced; no implementation link was visible in the checked arXiv record.
  • The formal determinism contract is per fixed deployment and assumes repeat-stable kernels and collectives; deployment changes and operational arrival queues/tail latency are outside the demonstrated scope.
  • The paper evaluates dense models only; mixture-of-experts token routing is left for future work.

Why it matters

Reliable reproduction matters for model evaluation, RL rollouts and tool-using systems. If these results generalize, fixed geometry for only the requests requiring reproducibility could reduce the latency cost of deterministic inference while retaining shared prefill batching. The paper does not establish production queueing or cost benefits.
