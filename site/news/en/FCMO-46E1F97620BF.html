OpenVLHarness reports broad gains from visual evidence tools and persistent multimodal memory

An Oct 19 arXiv preprint introduces OpenVLHarness, a training-free multimodal agent stack with nine task-level capabilities, outputs rendered as inspectable evidence, and per-query memory that keeps images, structured results and web references reusable across turns. Across 22 benchmarks and eight open/proprietary backbones, the authors report 9.7-12.6 point average gains over matched no-tool baselines. In their GPT-6 Luna comparison, medium-effort OpenVLHarness averages 56.5 versus 48.0 for the base model at maximum effort, 48.6 for Codex at maximum and 48.8 for Visual Sketchpad at maximum. These are author-reported benchmark results: Qwen3-VL results average three runs, while proprietary-model results are single runs; no independent reproduction was conducted. Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that OpenVLHarness, a training-free multimodal agent harness combining task-level perception/search/computation tools, model-facing evidence rendering and per-query reusable multimodal artifacts, improved average scores by 9.7-12.6 points over matched no-tool baselines across 22 benchmarks and eight open/proprietary backbones.
  • Claimed — On GPT-6 Luna, the authors report a 56.5 average at medium effort for OpenVLHarness versus 48.0 for the base model at maximum effort, 48.6 for Codex at maximum effort and 48.8 for Visual Sketchpad at maximum effort across their 22 benchmarks.
  • Not established — The paper and repository do not establish independent reproduction, generalization beyond the evaluated benchmarks/models, or lower end-to-end cost after tool execution, infrastructure and judging are included.

Strongest baseline

Matched no-tool baseline and training-free harnesses Visual Sketchpad, Codex and OctoTools on shared Qwen3-VL settings; a separate Luna comparison evaluates five effort settings and 22,000+ questions per setting.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are from one author team and have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • Only Qwen3-VL results use three independent runs; proprietary GPT-5, GPT-6 and Kimi results are single runs because of API cost.
  • Benchmarks cover selected visual perception, search, VQA and spatial tasks; they do not establish general agent capability or broad production performance.
  • Reported token costs omit tool execution, GPU services, external search and judging costs, so they are not end-to-end economic results.
  • The paper's abstract and §3.3 prose claim at most 37% of competitor token cost for Luna medium versus competitors at max, but Table 2 implies 46% of Codex max cost; cause is unresolved.
  • Internal paper arithmetic discrepancy (arXiv HTML abstract and §3.3 versus Table 2): the prose says Luna medium uses at most 37% of compared methods' model-token cost at max. Displayed costs are $1.15/1k for Luna-medium OpenVLHarness and $2.52/1k for maximum-effort Codex, a 46% ratio; the other ratios are 36% of Base and 22% of Visual Sketchpad. This review uses the exact displayed table values and does not infer a reason for the mismatch.

What we still don't know

  • The broad score improvements have not been independently reproduced; run-to-run uncertainty is reported for Qwen3-VL but not quantified for single-run proprietary model outcomes.
  • Token cost excludes external tool execution, GPU services, search API and judging, and the Luna cost summary conflicts with Table 2 for Codex.
  • Live web retrieval changes between runs, and results from proprietary backbones are single-run API evaluations.

Why it matters

If the results generalize, a training-free evidence-and-memory harness can improve multimodal task performance more than increasing model reasoning effort alone in the tested regimes, changing how agent teams allocate inference compute and tool engineering. The result is bounded by selected benchmarks/models, first-party evaluation, single-run proprietary scores and incomplete end-to-end cost accounting.
