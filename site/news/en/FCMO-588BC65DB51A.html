Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The plan says operators should offset actual operational electricity use through investment in new renewable generation and storage, and cover connection and network augmentation costs directly caused by their projects. Detailed compliance and verification rules are to be finalized alongside national standards under development.
  • Demonstrated — The plan sets proposed water expectations including transition away from potable water, potable-water offsetting, a data-centre water-usage effectiveness threshold, public reporting and full cost recovery for water infrastructure. It reports 19 applications seeking 19,714 megalitres per year; applications are not approvals, and the plan cites cancellations among AEMO’s project pipeline.

Strongest baseline

Existing Victorian water law and New Customer Contributions rules already allow water corporations to refuse or condition supply and require proponents to fund dedicated infrastructure. The plan adds proposed planning, energy and water expectations; its final compliance rules were not yet complete.

Limitations

  • The action plan describes policy expectations and intended planning/referral controls; it does not establish that every measure is already enacted or enforceable.
  • Detailed compliance and verification requirements and consistent Commonwealth mandatory standards remained under development in the inspected document.
  • The 19 applications seeking 19,714 megalitres per year are applications, not approvals or operational consumption; projected projects may be cancelled.
  • No independent analysis of likely electricity, water, investment or consumer-price effects was conducted for this record.

What we still don't know

  • Final planning amendments, referral triggers and compliance or verification rules are not established by the action plan alone.
  • The plan reports applications and projected demand, but approvals, actual construction, electricity use and water consumption are not measured here.
  • The plan anticipates alignment with Commonwealth mandatory standards that were still under development.

Why it matters

The plan links data-centre growth to electricity supply, grid augmentation and water infrastructure costs, giving developers concrete resource conditions to consider when assessing Victorian projects. The reported application volume indicates potential resource pressure, but does not establish approvals, construction or actual consumption. The plan’s proposed measures and unfinished compliance rules should be distinguished from enacted requirements.
