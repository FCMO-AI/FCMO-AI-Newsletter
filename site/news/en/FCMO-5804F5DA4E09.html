DART reports improved easy-to-hard accuracy for recurrent reasoning models

An arXiv preprint introduces Distributional Adversarial Recurrent Training (DART), which trains recurrent reasoning models with a locally perturbed target distribution and an input-conditioned adversarial critic. The authors report improved easy-to-hard results for Deep Thinking Systems on mazes and chess puzzles and for Tiny Recursive Models on masked Sudoku. For 31×31 mazes, reported accuracy is 99.91±0.13% with DART versus 73.92±26.01% with cross-entropy; at 50% Sudoku masking and depth 56, reported accuracy is 38.0±0.1% versus 32.4±1.7%. Chess still shows late-iteration degradation with DART alone; combining it with progressive training is reported to improve final accuracy. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced here.

What changed

An arXiv preprint introduces Distributional Adversarial Recurrent Training (DART), which trains recurrent reasoning models with a locally perturbed target distribution and an input-conditioned adversarial critic. The authors report improved easy-to-hard results for Deep Thinking Systems on mazes and chess puzzles and for Tiny Recursive Models on masked Sudoku. For 31×31 mazes, reported accuracy is 99.91±0.13% with DART versus 73.92±26.01% with cross-entropy; at 50% Sudoku masking and depth 56, reported accuracy is 38.0±0.1% versus 32.4±1.7%. Chess still shows late-iteration degradation with DART alone; combining it with progressive training is reported to improve final accuracy. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced here.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that DART improved a Deep Thinking System’s 31×31 maze accuracy to 99.91±0.13% versus 73.92±26.01% with cross-entropy, and improved a Tiny Recursive Model’s 50%-masked Sudoku accuracy at depth 56 to 38.0±0.1% versus 32.4±1.7%. These are results in the paper’s evaluated settings.
  • Claimed — On the paper’s chess-puzzle evaluation, DART alone reached a reported peak accuracy of 84.83±0.03% but fell to 11.13±4.64% by iteration 50; progressive DART reached 83.52±0.16% final accuracy, compared with 83.46±0.02% for progressive cross-entropy.
  • Not established — The paper reports evaluation on generated mazes, Lichess-derived chess puzzles and masked Sudoku using Deep Thinking Systems and Tiny Recursive Models; these results do not establish transfer to other model families, tasks or deployment settings. DART alone also did not eliminate late-iteration chess degradation.

Strongest baseline

Cross-entropy and progressive-training variants reported by the paper; the target-smoothing ablation is also relevant.

Limitations

  • All performance numbers are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • Evidence covers generated mazes, Lichess-derived chess puzzles and masked Sudoku using Deep Thinking Systems and Tiny Recursive Models; broader transfer is unknown.
  • DART alone still showed late-iteration degradation on chess; progressive training changed the outcome.
  • No independent artifact audit or compute-cost comparison was performed.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or inspection of linked artifacts was performed; reported means and variance are accepted as author-reported evidence only.
  • The experiments cover two recurrent model families and three structured task settings; transfer beyond these settings is untested in this review.
  • The paper does not establish training or inference cost-effectiveness relative to the compared objectives.

Why it matters

If reproduced, distributional adversarial supervision could help compact recurrent models maintain valid structured solutions as test problems become harder. The evidence is limited to the paper’s selected maze, chess and Sudoku settings; chess also shows that DART alone does not prevent late-iteration collapse.
