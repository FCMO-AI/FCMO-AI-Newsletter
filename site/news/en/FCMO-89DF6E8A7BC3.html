EDGEGEN reports gains from specification-derived edge-case tasks for tool-calling agents

EDGEGEN extracts compliance rules from an agent specification, combines structured rule-violation scenarios with executable database states, and uses the generated tasks for fine-tuning or harness optimization. The authors report improvement over the base model in all 18 model–benchmark comparisons across three benchmarks; on tau2-bench Airline they report relative mean-progress gains ranging from 2% to 42%, while one harness-optimization result for Gemma-4-e4b is 10% above human-curated and 30% above the base harness. Main evaluations use only 10–15 scenarios per benchmark. In a small audit, human reviewers confirmed only 6 of 10 Airline and 4 of 10 ToolSandbox runs labeled successful. These are preprint-reported results, not independently reproduced evidence of general agent reliability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that fine-tuning on EDGEGEN-generated tasks improves mean progress over the base model in all 18 model–benchmark pairs spanning tau2-bench Airline, Retail and ToolSandbox; reported relative gains on Airline range from about 2% to 42%.
  • Claimed — For Gemma-4-e4b on tau2-bench Airline, the authors report harness optimization with EDGEGEN tasks yields mean progress 10% above the human-curated optimized harness and 30% above the base harness.

Strongest baseline

Base model/harness, generic synthetic task generation, human-curated training examples, TaskBench and FuncBenchGen comparisons as reported in the paper.

Limitations

  • The preprint reports small main evaluation sets of 10–15 scenarios per benchmark; supplementary scaling uses 21 Airline scenarios.
  • Human reviewers confirmed only 6/10 sampled Airline and 4/10 ToolSandbox runs that the system marked successful; task assertions and automated judging can be wrong.
  • Rule extraction and database grounding do not guarantee that generated tasks encode the policy correctly; the appendix gives a policy-rule misalignment example.
  • Reported gains do not establish uniform improvement: harness optimization on ToolSandbox was below the human-curated result, and one Gemma harness result slightly degraded.
  • Applicability to coding and web-navigation agents is untested; no independent reproduction was conducted.

What we still don't know

  • Main evaluations use 10–15 scenarios per benchmark. In a small human audit of runs labeled successful, reviewers judged only 6/10 Airline and 4/10 ToolSandbox runs correct; assertion omissions and judge errors were among the failures.
  • The evidence is an author-run preprint study across three benchmark domains; results are not independently reproduced, gains are not uniform for every harness result, and applicability to coding or web-navigation agents is untested.

Why it matters

Policy-conditioned, database-grounded tasks could expose tool-agent failures that generic happy-path prompts miss and could provide training examples for policy compliance. The reported gains are bounded by small evaluation samples, imperfect automated judgments, selected benchmarks and author-run experiments.
