Anthropic reports Claude-designed binders experimentally bound across 14 of 15 interpretable protein targets

Anthropic's August 18, 2026 technical report describes Claude Opus 4.8 and Mythos Preview autonomously planning de novo protein-binder campaigns from a protocol prompt, using open-source design and folding tools, and delivering ranked candidates for testing. Two independent contract research organizations synthesized the designs as delivered and measured binding. Of 16 selected targets, 15 produced interpretable measurements; the mature GDF-8 target aggregated and was excluded. The report says 354 of 1,320 designs bound across the 15 interpretable targets (27%), and Claude designed binders for 14 of those 15 targets. In a multi-target 48-hour setup, Mythos Preview and Opus 4.8 hit rates were 26.7% and 22.6%; Mythos Preview reached 35.1% in separate 24-hour single-target campaigns. Anthropic released prompts, design models and binding data. This is unusually direct external wet-lab testing of model-orchestrated designs, but the report is authored by Anthropic, says further characterization is planned, and does not establish therapeutic efficacy or broad superiority over expert-led campaigns.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Anthropic reports that two independent contract research organizations synthesized Claude's protein designs as delivered and tested binding; 354 of 1,320 designs bound across 15 targets with interpretable measurements, and binders were found for 14 of those 15 targets.
  • Claimed — Anthropic reports hit rates of 26.7% for Mythos Preview and 22.6% for Opus 4.8 in 48-hour multi-target campaigns, and 35.1% for Mythos Preview in separate 24-hour single-target campaigns.
  • Not established — The campaign does not establish drug efficacy, therapeutic safety, general superiority to expert-led design, or that the reported hit rates will transfer to other target sets, laboratories or protocols.

Strongest baseline

The report compares campaign hit rates with a stated 10–15% typical range and gives a specific RBX1 comparison to a public design competition. These are not fully matched expert-versus-agent campaigns across all targets; target, compute and assay regimes differ.

Limitations

  • The report is authored by Anthropic and reports its own campaign; independent CROs tested synthesized designs but this is not an independent replication of the full protocol or analysis.
  • One of 16 selected targets, mature GDF-8, aggregated and had uninterpretable measurements; reported aggregate hit rates exclude its 120 designs.
  • The CROs used different assay formats and labeling procedures. The report describes a harmonization rule and residual plate-specific caveats, including the RBX1 and SpCas9 measurements.
  • Binding is an early molecular-design result, not evidence of drug efficacy, safety, pharmacology or clinical usefulness. Anthropic says additional characterization is planned.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reanalysis or replication of the released campaign data was performed in this activation; seek an outside audit of target-level labels, CRO measurements and hit-rate calculations.
  • Anthropic says it plans more extensive characterization; confirm hit rates and affinity measurements as additional work is released, especially for targets with assay-format or control caveats.
  • The reported campaign regimes differ in target allocation, model, duration and compute; broader comparison with expert-led campaigns requires matched target panels, assay protocols, time and compute budgets.

Why it matters

The result moves AI-assisted protein design beyond in-silico scoring: external laboratories tested designs exactly as delivered, and many bound across diverse targets. If the released dataset and protocol withstand independent reanalysis and replication, autonomous orchestration could lower the expertise and coordination barrier for early discovery campaigns. The result remains bounded to binding assays and the reported compute, prompt, target and campaign regimes; binding is only an early step toward a drug and the report identifies target-specific failures and assay caveats.
