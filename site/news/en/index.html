The arXiv v1 paper describes an infrastructure for packaging scientific codebases with executable checks and generating tasks for agent evaluation, supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. These are author-reported results; independent reproduction and broader external validation remain open.

Xi et al. present Video DeltaNet (VDN), a hybrid attention design instantiated on MiniMax H3 for video diffusion. It keeps exact softmax attention within a local temporal window and at first/last-frame anchors, while bidirectional linear memory handles distant video context; a frame-wise joint delta update couples correlated spatial writes. These are author-reported measurements, not independent reproductions; the final speed combines architecture, distillation and multi-GPU inference.

A paper reports semantic stop-token alignment reduces length inflation in on-policy distillation

A September 17 preprint studies length inflation in sampled-token on-policy distillation (OPD). It argues that when a base student and post-trained teacher encode the same stopping decision with different tokens, the teacher can give a negative signal to the student’s sampled EOS while the teacher-preferred token receives little support. Across Qwen3, Llama 3.2 and Gemma 3, the authors report that aligning total probability over semantically equivalent stop tokens in the distillation signal reduces mismatch-driven length and clipping; merely expanding the decoder stop set does not. An earlier OPD paper already describes EOS canonicalization in Appendix D, so the basic alignment idea is prior art; the newer paper adds cross-family empirical comparisons, a shared-declared-set Gemma test and stage-wise residual analysis. It also reports late-stage K2-Horizon re-inflation after correction. These are author-run, single-turn mathematical-reasoning results; multi-turn/tool semantics and independent replication remain open.

A conditional-independence limit for confidence-ranked parallel diffusion sampling

The authors analyze independent-update samplers that choose parallel token writes using per-position distributions and sample each selected position independently. They claim that matching the training joint distribution at a step requires the written positions to be conditionally independent given fixed tokens, and that per-position marginals alone cannot certify this condition. On their synthetic ScanAndAdd task, they report confidence-ranked writes with perfect sample-level validity metrics but a generated token distribution about 29 times the sampling-noise-floor total variation. The theorem is conditional on the sampler class; the measured distortion is from one synthetic task and is not evidence that deployed diffusion models exhibit the same magnitude of error.
