Perseverance driving data supports paper-reported multimodal traversability learning on an Earth-analog rover

A September 21 arXiv paper introduces a multimodal traversability estimator trained from about 500 sols and 45 km of Mars 2020 Perseverance driving data. On 35 held-out sols, the authors report AUROC 0.874, average precision 0.878 and F1 0.758, above the strongest listed baseline (0.816, 0.802 and 0.602); means are reported across five seeds. They also describe integrating the learned hazard map into modified ENav flight software and testing it on an Earth-analog rover, primarily on flat ground with large rocks. These are paper-reported results. The linked project page says code and dataset are forthcoming; no independent reproduction or Mars deployment is established.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report a multimodal Mars 2020 Perseverance driving dataset spanning about 500 sols and 45 km, with approximately 43,000 images paired with pose and rover proprioception.
  • Claimed — On 35 held-out sols, the paper reports AUROC 0.874, average precision 0.878 and F1 0.758 for its traversability method, compared with AUROC 0.816, AP 0.802 and F1 0.602 for its strongest listed baseline; results are author-reported over five seeds.
  • Claimed — The paper reports integrating the learned traversability score into modified ENav flight software and testing on an Earth-analog rover platform; its hardware section describes primarily flat ground with large rocks, not a Mars field deployment.
  • Not established — Independent reproducibility, public artifact availability at review time, performance across varied terrain or rover platforms, and operational improvement on Mars are not established by the inspected sources.

Strongest baseline

The listed Castro et al. traversability baseline (AUROC 0.816, AP 0.802, F1 0.602) is strongest for the paper’s chosen metrics.

Limitations

  • Metrics are reported by the paper authors and have not been independently reproduced.
  • Code and dataset were marked as forthcoming on the project page at review time; the linked JPL Dataverse landing page did not yield a retrieved artifact in this activation.
  • Hardware experiments are primarily on flat ground with rock obstacles in an Earth-analog test site, using remote GPU inference; this does not establish autonomous operation on Mars.
  • No broad cross-rover, varied-terrain or long-duration closed-loop performance result is reported.

What we still don't know

  • The paper reports five-seed results, but no independent reimplementation or artifact run was performed; code/data were not available from the project page at review time.
  • The physical evaluation is an Earth-analog testbed demonstration, chiefly flat terrain with rocks; operational performance on Mars or varied off-world terrain is not established.

Why it matters

Planetary rovers cannot rely on continuous human correction, and geometric obstacle maps can miss terrain that causes wheel slip. The paper reports a concrete route for using actual Perseverance driving telemetry to add learned traversability signals to ENav. The result is bounded to the authors’ dataset, held-out sols and a limited Earth-analog testbed; it does not establish operational performance on Mars.
