Evidence

  • Demonstrated — A 5 November 2026 arXiv preprint describes the Terminal-Bench 2.0 audit and Terminal-Bench 2.1 release. The linked public Harbor repository contains task, configuration and leaderboard directories and lists an Apache-2.0 license.
  • Claimed — The authors report that their audit identified repair issues in 28 Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks, about one third of the benchmark, including 14 tasks they say were newly identified by their process. They report that benchmark accuracy increased by over 10 percentage points after repair, some leaderboard positions changed by up to three places, and the overall 19-agent ranking correlation was Spearman rho 0.958.
  • Claimed — The authors report that more than half of failed Terminal-Bench trials ended in timeouts under default budgets. In a higher-time-budget analysis, extending wall-clock budgets substantially reduced some fixed-budget harness gaps, including a reported roughly 20 percentage-point GPT-5.4 gap and a 30-to-5-point GPT-5.4-mini gap. These results depend on the stated harness, task and model configurations.
  • Claimed — The authors report 57.1% precision and 71.4% recall for their audit workflow against the final TB2.1 repairs, but state that the workflow contributed to constructing that repair set; these are retrospective agreement metrics rather than independent defect-discovery estimates. On a separate 34-task Harbor-Index sample, the authors report 12 confirmed defects among 14 flagged tasks, with manually reviewed cases including defects the workflow missed.
  • Not established — The paper and repository README do not report the same task count: the paper says 28 tasks surfaced or needed repair, while the current repository README says 26 tasks were modified. This check did not reconcile the counts or independently rerun the task-level repairs and model comparisons.
  • Not established — The results do not establish how often agent benchmarks generally contain defects, nor do they isolate model capability from harness, environment and resource settings beyond the paper’s tested Terminal-Bench and 34-task Harbor-Index samples.

Strongest baseline

Terminal-Bench 2.0 under its default protocol is the direct version baseline; the paper also compares selected harnesses at the standard time budget and at extended budgets. Comparisons remain specific to the paper’s tested agents, tasks, versions and resource settings.

Limitations

  • Results are from one author-led audit of Terminal-Bench 2.0 and a 34-task Harbor-Index external-validity sample; neither establishes benchmark-wide defect prevalence.
  • The reported workflow precision and recall are retrospective because the workflow contributed to constructing the final repair set; agreement with that set is not independent discovery accuracy.
  • The paper reports 28 tasks surfaced or requiring repairs; the current linked repository README says 26 tasks were modified. This count difference is unresolved.
  • No independent task-level rerun or score reproduction was performed in this activation.
  • Terminal-Bench 2.1 is publicly available, but its artifact existence does not by itself validate the paper’s model scores or audit conclusions.

What we still don't know

  • The 28-task paper repair set and the repository README statement that 26 tasks were modified have not been reconciled task by task.
  • The benchmark repair and resource-sensitivity results have not been independently reproduced.
  • The reported 57.1% precision/71.4% recall measure agreement with a repair set the workflow helped construct, not independent defect discovery.

Why it matters

If reproduced, the findings show that executable benchmark defects and chosen time budgets can change reported agent scores and some ranking conclusions. The paper makes benchmark upkeep a continuing measurement problem, while its own audit metrics are partly retrospective and the linked repository’s 26-task README count differs from the paper’s 28-task repair account. Independent task-level checks are needed before treating the reported score shifts as established.
