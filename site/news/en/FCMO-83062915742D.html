What changed

A September 2026 preprint introduces Just-in-Time Memory (JitMem), which stores successful raw agent trajectories and curates a compact, task-specific payload only when a later task is known. Across ALFWorld, WebShop and tau2-bench, the authors report gains over memory baselines, including 16.2 and 16.3 percentage-point success-rate gains over the strongest same-block learned write-time baseline on ALFWorld and WebShop. Gains on tau2-bench vary by domain: the reported increase is largest on Telecom, while Airline and Retail show no improvement beyond run variance. Results are author-reported and use multiple task orderings; they have not been independently reproduced here.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The preprint describes a system that stores successful raw trajectories, retrieves relevant traces for a new task, creates an ephemeral task-adaptive memory payload at read time, and trains a curator using immediate task reward.
  • Claimed — Across ALFWorld and WebShop, authors report that RL-trained JitMem exceeded the strongest same-block learned write-time baseline by 16.2 and 16.3 absolute success-rate points, respectively; these reported means average three task-order runs.
  • Claimed — On tau2-bench, the authors report a 3.9-point macro-average gain over the strongest baseline for a prompted GPT-5.4 curator; the reported benefit is concentrated in Telecom, while Airline and Retail show no gain beyond variance. These means average four task-order runs.
  • Claimed — A curator trained using Qwen3-8B as executor reportedly transfers to GPT-5.4 on ALFWorld, reaching within 1.4 success-rate points of a curator trained with GPT-5.4 as executor.
  • Claimed — The reported evaluation uses three benchmark families and fixed task-specific payload formats; the authors identify BM25 scaling, an extra curator call per task, and extension to turn-level adaptation as limitations.

Strongest baseline

The paper compares with no memory and write-time methods ReasoningBank, MemP and SkillOS, using same- or stronger-curator variants. Its strongest baseline differs by executor, task and domain; the reported gains are not one pooled effect.

Limitations

  • All performance measurements are author-reported; no independent reproduction was performed here.
  • The evaluation covers three benchmark families and a fixed set of executors, memory methods and prompts; broader task and model transfer is unknown.
  • On tau2-bench, gains are heterogeneous by domain; Airline and Retail do not improve beyond variance, despite the Telecom gain.
  • Training uses a fixed bank of base-executor trajectories, while test banks grow with curator-augmented trajectories; the paper characterizes this as a mild train/test shift.
  • The method uses BM25 retrieval, a hand-designed benchmark-specific payload and one extra curator call per task; scale and cost for larger banks remain open.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the task-order runs, environment baselines, or curator-transfer results has been completed.
  • Performance and total cost with larger streaming memory banks and turn-level curation are unmeasured.

Why it matters

Most memory systems decide what to keep when an experience is stored, before its future use is known. JitMem tests the alternative of keeping raw successful traces and shaping a summary around the current task. The paper reports gains across several interactive benchmarks and transfer of a trained curator to stronger executors, while also identifying the extra per-task model call and simple retrieval as costs. These results may inform agent-memory design, but do not establish general gains outside the tested tasks.
