Anthropic says UK AISI declined restricted pre-release access to Mythos 5.1; access later resumed

In UK Parliament evidence on October 13, Anthropic’s Head of EMEA Policy said the company could not provide UK AISI with full, unrestricted pre-release access to Mythos 5.1, but had offered restricted access, which AISI chose not to use; he said AISI had access to the model by the hearing. Asked whether the difference was Anthropic’s decision or driven by the US Government, he said he did not know and had not been privy to those conversations. In an October 22 follow-up letter published October 27, Anthropic repeated that it could not provision unsafeguarded access while a new US framework for pre-deployment testing and access was being considered, and said it later provisioned unsafeguarded access to its latest models. These are Anthropic’s accounts. They establish an offered restricted-access path and later access, but not the reason or the exact scope and duration of the restriction.

Why it matters

Pre-release access affects whether external safety institutes can inspect frontier models before launch and under what conditions.
