WebHarbor releases 153 local web-agent environments and 3,518 tasks; authors report transfer gains for one 4B agent

A 2 November 2026 arXiv preprint and its linked project repository describe WebHarbor, a collection of 153 Dockerized website environments and 3,518 tasks for GUI-agent evaluation and reinforcement-learning training. The paper reports that WebHarbor-4B scored 75.8 on average across WebVoyager and Online-Mind2Web, versus 70.6 for an online-RL OpenWebRL-4B baseline under the authors’ setup. These are author-reported results from one trained 4B model. The environments reconstruct task-relevant parts of real sites; the paper says some interactive features are approximated and that synthetic performance does not guarantee safe or equivalent performance on live websites.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The WebHarbor preprint was posted on arXiv on 2 November 2026, and the linked project repository describes a release of its paper, code, environments, tasks and model checkpoint.
  • Claimed — The paper describes 153 Dockerized website environments and 3,518 tasks; it reports that 31.8% of tasks require authentication, 35.6% modify persistent state, and 53.7% require cross-page reasoning.
  • Claimed — In the authors’ reported evaluation, WebHarbor-4B averages 75.8 across WebVoyager and Online-Mind2Web, compared with 70.6 for OpenWebRL-4B trained with online reinforcement learning. The paper reports results for one trained 4B model and its stated evaluation protocol.
  • Not established — The reported results do not establish independent reproduction, generalization across model scales or families, reliable performance on unmodified live websites, or safe behavior in real transactions.

Strongest baseline

OpenWebRL-4B trained with online reinforcement learning, initialized from the same OpenWebRL-4B-SFT checkpoint according to the paper. The reported comparison remains first-party and method-specific.

Limitations

  • The preprint is a first-party report; benchmark construction, task counts and measured scores have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The paper reports training one 4B model and does not establish how results transfer to other model families, scales, SFT data or RL methods.
  • The paper says WebHarbor reconstructs task-relevant website behavior; some client-heavy features are approximated, and scripted verifiers or model judges may miss valid alternatives or subtle failures.
  • The authors caution that strong synthetic-environment performance does not guarantee equivalent or safe behavior on live websites.
  • The repository README links code, data and checkpoints, but reuse licensing was not established; a root LICENSE path returned 404 in this review.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the reported matched 4B reinforcement-learning comparison was performed or located in this review.
  • The paper trains one 4B model and does not establish transfer across model scales, families, harnesses or RL algorithms.
  • The repository-root license path returned 404; the applicable reuse terms for code, environments, data and checkpoints remain unclear.

Why it matters

GUI agents are difficult to evaluate and train on live websites because content changes, access controls and network failures make results unstable. A broad local suite with authenticated and state-changing tasks could make controlled evaluation and training more practical. Its value depends on task validity, verifier quality and transfer beyond the reported single-model experiment.
