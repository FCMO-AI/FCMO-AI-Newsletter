Weave reports routing-conditioned GPU scheduling gains for MoE inference

The September 18 preprint presents Weave, which uses post-routing information to allocate GPU streaming multiprocessors dynamically between communication and computation for each MoE layer. Its abstract reports a 2.89× geometric-mean speedup for MoE layers and 1.33× end-to-end speedup over five systems across six MoE models on 4×H100 SXM GPUs. These are author-reported results; the full baseline tables and implementation were not independently checked here, and no reproduction or evidence of transfer to other hardware was found.

Limitations

What we still don't know

  • The reported speedups come from one 4×H100 SXM configuration and have not been independently reproduced; transfer to other GPU fabrics, model sizes, traffic mixtures and serving objectives is unknown.

Why it matters

MoE inference spends time coordinating expert computation with dispatch and combine traffic. If Weave's author-reported result holds, adapting the communication/computation split after routing can reduce resource mismatch on each layer and improve end-to-end serving efficiency. The tested 4×H100 result does not establish transfer to other hardware, workloads or deployment objectives.
