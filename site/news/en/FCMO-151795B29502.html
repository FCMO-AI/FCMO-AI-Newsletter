Self-Credit uses cross-rollout turn citations to assign credit in long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning

The arXiv preprint introduces Self-Credit, a long-horizon agentic RL method that has a policy cite earlier turns in its own summaries, then adjusts turn-level credit by comparing cited actions across successful and failed rollouts for each prompt. Across three-seed evaluations, the authors report higher accuracy than the strongest tested agentic-RL baselines on BrowseComp-Plus with Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B, and on GAIA and xBench-DeepSearch with Gemma4-E4B. Evaluation relies on LLM-as-judge scoring; independent reproduction and end-to-end compute comparison are not established.

What changed

The arXiv preprint introduces Self-Credit, a long-horizon agentic RL method that has a policy cite earlier turns in its own summaries, then adjusts turn-level credit by comparing cited actions across successful and failed rollouts for each prompt. Across three-seed evaluations, the authors report higher accuracy than the strongest tested agentic-RL baselines on BrowseComp-Plus with Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B, and on GAIA and xBench-DeepSearch with Gemma4-E4B. Evaluation relies on LLM-as-judge scoring; independent reproduction and end-to-end compute comparison are not established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The v1 preprint describes Self-Credit, which asks the agent to cite earlier turns in its generated summaries and then rescales cited-turn credit across successful and failed rollouts for the same prompt before reweighting turn-level advantages.
  • Claimed — On BrowseComp-Plus, the authors report three-seed Pass@1 of 47.0% for Qwen3-32B and 40.6% for Qwen3-8B, respectively 5.6 and 8.5 percentage points above the strongest reported baseline in each setting. For Gemma4-E4B, they report 39.2% on text-only GAIA and 16.7% on xBench-DeepSearch, 4.9 and 4.7 points above the strongest reported baselines. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced.
  • Claimed — In the paper’s BrowseComp-Plus ablations, removing intra-group rescaling, repeated-failure penalties or self-generated citations lowered the reported accuracy; the size of the drop varied across Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B. This supports component sensitivity within the authors’ setup, not an independently established causal effect.

Strongest baseline

The authors compare against GRPO, GiGPO, SUPO and ECHO. ECHO is the strongest reported baseline for the cited benchmark comparisons. Cross-paper or independently harmonized benchmark parity was not established.

Limitations

  • All performance values are author-reported in one arXiv preprint; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • Accuracy is adjudicated by LLM-as-judge: a separate Qwen3-32B judge on BrowseComp-Plus and rubric-based LLM judging for deep-research tasks. Sensitivity to alternate blinded judges or human assessment is unknown.
  • BrowseComp-Plus training and evaluation use a static corpus and search index; deep-research evaluation uses an open web search API, so conditions differ across datasets.
  • The experiments cover Qwen3-8B/32B and Gemma4-E4B on specified search/deep-research tasks; transfer to other model families, agent tasks or production reliability is not established.
  • Independent artifacts and full end-to-end compute/cost comparisons were not verified.
  • The method assumes that turns contribute independently to outcomes, although the authors note that later actions can depend on earlier retrieved evidence; this approximation may distort turn-level credit.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction was found or performed; benchmark accuracy uses LLM-as-judge.
  • The paper does not establish whether gains persist under matched end-to-end compute or across other model families and task environments.

Why it matters

If the paper’s results generalize, self-generated evidence citations combined with cross-rollout credit rescaling could improve post-training for multi-step search and research agents without adding a separate learned critic. The evidence is one preprint on a bounded benchmark and model set, with judge-based evaluation and no independent reproduction.
