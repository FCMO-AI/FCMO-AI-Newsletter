China proposes protections governing platform work algorithms and consequential worker decisions

On 8 October 2026, China’s Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security published a draft cross-agency measure for public comment through 8 November. The proposal would require platforms to explain labor-management algorithms, prohibit specified forms of discrimination, and require human review before certain consequential decisions such as stopping task assignments or banning accounts, subject to a narrow emergency exception. What changed

On 8 October 2026, China’s Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security published a draft cross-agency measure for public comment through 8 November. The proposal would require platforms to explain labor-management algorithms, prohibit specified forms of discrimination, and require human review before certain consequential decisions such as stopping task assignments or banning accounts, subject to a narrow emergency exception. The measure is a proposal, not adopted law; implementation and effects are not established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — China’s Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security published a draft measure on workers in new forms of employment on 8 October 2026 and opened public comment through 8 November 2026, with six other agencies named as joint drafters.
  • Claimed — The draft defines platform labor algorithms as decision rules and instructions used for automated scheduling, supervision, evaluation and incentives; Articles 27–29 propose worker-facing disclosure and explanation, restrictions on anomalous data in general assessments, anti-discrimination duties and human review before specified material decisions, with an emergency exception and subsequent review.
  • Not established — The proposal has been adopted, enforced, or caused changes in platform systems or worker outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The draft itself and the MHRSS-hosted Nov 2 status report confirm proposal/public-comment status; no independent legal interpretation or implementation evaluation was performed.

Limitations

  • This is a draft under public consultation, not a final rule or evidence of operative legal duties.
  • The draft applies to a defined class of workers using internet platforms under platform rules and instructions; it is not a general regulation of AI systems.
  • No platform implementation, enforcement action, or measured worker outcome was inspected.

What we still don't know

  • The measure remains a draft under public consultation through 2026-11-08; the final text may differ.
  • No implementation, enforcement or worker-outcome evidence is established by the draft or status report.

Why it matters

If adopted and implemented as drafted, the proposal would create explicit process constraints on automated task allocation, evaluation and worker-impact decisions for platform labor. It is relevant to deployment governance for algorithmic management, but does not establish rules for all AI systems or show that platforms have changed their systems.
