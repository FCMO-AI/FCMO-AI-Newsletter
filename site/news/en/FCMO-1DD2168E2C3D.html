ME-VLM reports a unified embodied-agent model and lower M100 prefill latency

The arXiv v2 paper presents ME-VLM, a model intended to combine embodied perception and action with multimodal agent planning and tool use. Its method trains separate agent and embodied experts, then distills them into one student with multi-teacher on-policy distillation. The authors report results on embodied, agent and transfer benchmarks. For M100 edge deployment, they report W4A8 quantization and visual-token reduction from 4,096 to 3,328 alongside prefill latency of 400 ms to 188 ms, with 96.7%/98.2% uncompressed-score retention for the 4B/35B models. These results are author-reported; this review did not reproduce them or establish broader end-to-end latency or power performance.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv v2 preprint describes ME-VLM as a unified model trained by separately optimizing agent and embodied experts and distilling them into a single student through multi-teacher on-policy distillation.
  • Claimed — The authors report results across embodied, agent and task-transfer benchmarks for 4B and 35B-A3B models. Their comparisons are paper-reported; some table entries are identified as not reported in the cited original papers.
  • Claimed — For M100 deployment, the authors report W4A8 quantization and reducing visual tokens from 4,096 to 3,328 alongside prefill latency changing from 400 ms to 188 ms. They report 96.7% and 98.2% retention of uncompressed baseline scores for the 4B and 35B models, respectively. These are bounded paper-reported measurements, not independent reproduction or general end-to-end response latency.

Strongest baseline

Reported benchmark baselines include Hy-Embodied, RynnBrain 1.1, PhysBrain 1.5 and MiMo-Embodied; the edge latency comparison is against the paper’s uncompressed configuration.

Limitations

  • All performance values in this record are author-reported and were not independently reproduced.
  • The 400 ms to 188 ms result is prefill latency under the described combined W4A8 and token-reduction setup, not general end-to-end response latency; power is not reported here.
  • The paper notes some compared benchmark results were not reported in the original papers.
  • The paper’s benchmark and deployment results do not establish broad real-world reliability or performance across other hardware and workloads.

What we still don't know

  • The benchmark and M100 prefill measurements have not been independently reproduced.
  • The reported M100 result covers prefill latency and does not establish end-to-end response latency, power or performance across workloads.

Why it matters

A unified model for physical perception, planning, tool use and execution feedback could simplify embodied-agent systems. If the reported M100 results transfer, quantization and visual-token selection could improve local prefill efficiency while retaining most benchmark score. The paper does not establish independently verified or general end-to-end deployment gains.
