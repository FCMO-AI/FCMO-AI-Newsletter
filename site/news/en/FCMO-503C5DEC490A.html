A paper proposes carbon reporting and a smallest-model selection rule for AI research

The authors introduce carbonbenchmark, which combines CodeCarbon measurements with fitted training curves, and propose a “Smallest Model that Achieves the Job” (SMAJ) selection approach. They report illustrative fits on Fashion-MNIST, GLUE SST-2 and UCI Adult, and an automated review of 5,285 NeurIPS 2025 papers using LLM classification. Their proprietary-inference emissions examples use assumed active parameter counts and generic hardware/grid factors; these are estimates, not measured model-specific emissions. The paper argues for reporting research emissions and treating environmental cost alongside accuracy.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors provide carbonbenchmark, combining CodeCarbon measurements with fitted training curves, and demonstrate it on three small tasks using an RTX 4060 Ti over a one-hour training period.
  • CLAIMED — The authors propose SMAJ as a cost-aware model-selection concept and recommend reporting research emissions; the paper does not establish a generally validated equivalence or noninferiority procedure for selecting the smallest adequate model.
  • CLAIMED — The reported review of 5,285 NeurIPS 2025 papers classifies papers with LLMs after a 200-paper model-agreement study; its corpus estimates are automated classifications, not a human-adjudicated census.

Strongest baseline

The paper discusses accuracy-focused state-of-the-art selection and common learning-curve forms; no validated environmental benchmark baseline is established.

The caveat that matters

  • Training examples cover only three small tasks and one local GPU; they do not establish frontier-scale measurement accuracy.
  • The NeurIPS paper-use estimates rely on automated classification rather than human adjudication.
  • Proprietary inference emissions use assumed active parameter counts and generic hardware/grid factors, not measured per-model energy.
  • SMAJ’s uncertainty threshold is not a demonstrated equivalence design and may require selection-bias control.

What remains unknown

  • No independent validation on frontier-scale training or inference workloads.

FCMO Lens

Environmental measurement choices can affect comparisons of model quality and compute cost. This paper offers methods and a reporting proposal, but its small training examples and assumed frontier-inference inputs do not establish reliable footprint estimates or a validated model-selection standard.
