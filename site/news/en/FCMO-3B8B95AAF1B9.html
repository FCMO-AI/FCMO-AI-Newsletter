Australia’s mandatory government AI policy adds a seven-pattern use classification

Australia’s Digital Transformation Agency says version 2.1 of its mandatory Policy for the responsible use of AI in government was published on 21 October 2026. Its new Appendix D defines seven usage patterns and requires covered agencies to apply them in use-case registers, impact assessments and transparency statements. An independent comparison reports that this replaces a four-pattern classification and adds explicit categories for strategic support, workflow automation and coding while changing other definitions. What changed

Australia’s Digital Transformation Agency says version 2.1 of its mandatory Policy for the responsible use of AI in government was published on 21 October 2026. Its new Appendix D defines seven usage patterns and requires covered agencies to apply them in use-case registers, impact assessments and transparency statements. An independent comparison reports that this replaces a four-pattern classification and adds explicit categories for strategic support, workflow automation and coding while changing other definitions. The DTA describes the changes as minor; the exact v2.0 policy PDF was unavailable for direct comparison, and no agency implementation or outcome evidence is established.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The Digital Transformation Agency states that policy version 2.1 was published on 21 October 2026 as minor updates to version 2.0, which took effect on 15 December 2025.
  • Demonstrated — Version 2.1 Appendix D defines seven AI-use patterns and requires agencies in scope to classify use in their internal use-case registers, when using the impact-assessment tool, and in public transparency statements.
  • Claimed — An independent document comparison reports that the v2.0 classification had four patterns, while v2.1 adds strategic support, workflow automation and coding, narrows workplace productivity, and redefines decision-making around effects on rights or interests. The exact v2.0 policy PDF was not directly available in this review.

Strongest baseline

The accessible DTA v1.1 transparency standard’s Attachment A lists four patterns; the exact v2.0 policy PDF was unavailable. A secondary comparison reports the v2.0-to-v2.1 change.

Limitations

  • The DTA calls the 21 October changes minor; the detailed v2.0-to-v2.1 comparison in this record comes from a secondary source whose saved predecessor PDF was not independently retrieved.
  • No evidence establishes that agencies have updated their registers, assessment workflows or transparency statements to the new classification.
  • The policy classification change does not itself prove new controls, improved oversight or measured public-sector AI outcomes.

What we still don't know

  • The official v2.0 policy PDF used for the reported comparison was not accessible from the DTA link during this review; the current media endpoint returns v2.1 and the old document path returns 404. The precise taxonomy delta is supported by a detailed secondary comparison but not independently checked against the predecessor bytes.
  • The policy text establishes classification requirements but the extent and timing of agency updates to registers, assessments and public statements were not measured.

Why it matters

A shared classification used in government registers, impact assessments and public statements shapes how agencies describe and compare their AI use. If the reported taxonomy changes are adopted consistently, separate categories for coding, workflow automation and strategic support could make government AI inventories easier to interpret. The version update does not itself demonstrate new risk controls or implementation outcomes.
