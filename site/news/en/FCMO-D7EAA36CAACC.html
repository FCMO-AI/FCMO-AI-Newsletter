FoldQuantVLA reports faster low-bit inference for vision-language-action policies with small-task robot results

The authors introduce FoldQuantVLA, which keeps activation coordinate transforms consistent across calibration, quantized weight rounding and TensorRT execution. On GR00T N1.5/N1.6/N1.7 and π₀.₅, they report W4A4 projection speedups of 1.20–1.33× over floating-point TensorRT on Jetson AGX Orin and 1.25–1.52× on a desktop RTX 4070 Ti SUPER. A selective W8A8 setting for two language projections adds 1 ms to the N1.7 Orin path and reports 92.5% pooled success versus 80.0% for uniform W4A4 across 80 physical-robot trials. These are author-reported measurements: selective-precision gains were not resolved in their LIBERO controls, robot trials were small, and latency measurements used one timing run.

What changed

The authors introduce FoldQuantVLA, which keeps activation coordinate transforms consistent across calibration, quantized weight rounding and TensorRT execution. On GR00T N1.5/N1.6/N1.7 and π₀.₅, they report W4A4 projection speedups of 1.20–1.33× over floating-point TensorRT on Jetson AGX Orin and 1.25–1.52× on a desktop RTX 4070 Ti SUPER. A selective W8A8 setting for two language projections adds 1 ms to the N1.7 Orin path and reports 92.5% pooled success versus 80.0% for uniform W4A4 across 80 physical-robot trials. These are author-reported measurements: selective-precision gains were not resolved in their LIBERO controls, robot trials were small, and latency measurements used one timing run.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that FoldQuantVLA's W4A4 projections reduce observation-to-action latency by 1.20–1.33× versus floating-point TensorRT on Jetson AGX Orin and 1.25–1.52× on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER desktop for GR00T and π₀.₅ checkpoints.
  • Claimed — On N1.7 physical-robot tasks, the authors report pooled success of 92.5% for W4A4 with attention-output and feed-forward down projections retained at W8A8, versus 80.0% for uniform W4A4 across 80 trials; on Orin this selective setting adds 1 ms to the reported 119 ms W4A4 path.
  • Claimed — The paper reports N1.6 W4A4 weight storage of 1,011 MB versus 3,802 MB for floating-point TensorRT weights, while complete serialized engine sizes are 2,525 MB versus 5,325 MB.
  • Not established — The results do not establish that low-bit performance generalizes across VLA architectures or that selective INT8 improves closed-loop simulator success: the paper reports significant architecture-dependent W4A4 losses, and preset-matched LIBERO differences were not statistically resolved.

Strongest baseline

The main speed comparison is each checkpoint's own floating-point TensorRT engine. Closed-loop comparisons include BF16 PyTorch, floating-point TensorRT and quantized configurations. Preset-matched LIBERO tests show selective-precision differences of 3–10 episodes on N1.5/N1.6/N1.7/π₀.₅, with p-values 0.17–0.78; they do not resolve simulator gains.

Limitations

  • All results are reported by the paper authors; the research desk did not run the linked code or reproduce measurements.
  • W4A4 results are architecture-dependent: the paper reports large LIBERO losses for SmolVLA and Evo-1 configurations and does not establish recovery from selective INT8 for those architectures.
  • The preset-matched LIBERO tests do not resolve a selective-precision success gain; robot trials used 20 trials per task, and the paper's pooled exploratory test ignores matched-placement and task/session dependence.
  • Latency uses one timing run per checkpoint; measured speedups compare with compiled floating-point TensorRT on specified hardware and batch size 1, not across different hardware or workloads.
  • Only selected projection GEMMs use INT4/INT8; the vision encoder, non-projection operations and action decoder remain floating point. The paper notes H100 execution lowers four-bit operands to INT8 arithmetic.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the paper's latency, memory, simulator or physical-robot results was performed in this activation.
  • Physical-robot trials are 20 per task per arm, and the paper's pooled exploratory Fisher test does not model task or session dependence; the reported difference is observational.
  • Benefit and latency are checkpoint-dependent; W4A4 causes large losses for some tested architectures, and the selective-precision effect is unresolved in preset-matched LIBERO tests.

Why it matters

The paper reports an edge deployment trade-off that couples lower precision with measured observation-to-action latency and physical-task outcomes across several open VLA checkpoints. If the results hold in independent runs, the method could lower latency and memory requirements for robotic policies on constrained hardware. Checkpoint dependence, small physical samples and unresolved simulator comparisons limit the claim.
