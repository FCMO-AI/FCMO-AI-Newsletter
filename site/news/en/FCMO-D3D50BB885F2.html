Google DeepMind preprint extends Boyd-family records across high polynomial degrees

A Google DeepMind preprint presents a five-parameter extension of Boyd's construction for small Mahler-measure polynomials. The authors report verified irreducible examples below Boyd's limit point at all 995 even degrees they evaluate from 12 to 2000, including six improvements over published values. This advances finite-degree records but does not settle Lehmer's conjecture or prove every listed polynomial globally minimal; the reported results have not been independently reproduced in this review.

  • Claimed — Across the evaluated set, the authors report that their construction attains 991 of 995 best-known records and improves six previously published values; this is a finite benchmark result and does not establish global minimality at each degree.
  • Claimed — The paper describes an interactive human–AI workflow combining Gemini, numerical search, distributed compute and exact symbolic verification to identify patterns that informed the generalized construction.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper does not prove or disprove Lehmer's conjecture; the evaluated dataset is finite, and this review did not independently reproduce the construction or reported values.

Limitations

  • This is a single author-reported preprint; no independent mathematical reproduction was performed in this review.
  • The reported 995-degree benchmark is finite and does not prove global minimality for every degree or solve Lehmer's conjecture.
  • The paper's AI workflow attribution and aggregate computational search are described by the authors; independent mechanism-level attribution is not established.
  • The review did not independently recompute the accessible ancillary CSV or rerun supplementary code.

What we still don't know

  • The construction, dataset and six reported improvements were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • The benchmark reports best-known values over evaluated methods and a finite range; it does not prove that every listed polynomial is globally minimal for its degree or resolve Lehmer's conjecture.

Why it matters

Lehmer's problem links number theory with entropy and growth rates in several mathematical fields. Extending explicit constructions across high degrees can improve the finite record map and offers a worked case where computational search and exact symbolic checks feed into a compact analytical family. Independent mathematical review is still needed, and this result is not a solution to the conjecture.
