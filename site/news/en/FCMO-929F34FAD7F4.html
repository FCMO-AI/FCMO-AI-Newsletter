Automated post-training agents show strategy lock despite better execution scaffolds

An empirical study of 1,338 public automated post-training trajectories separates execution-level iteration from strategy-level revision and reports that only 74 of 3,557 adjacent training-experiment pairs changed strategy (~2.1%). Experience scaffolding, initial human guidance and additional inference compute improve execution or initial choice but do not reliably induce spontaneous strategy reopening once training is underway.

What actually changed

An empirical study of 1,338 public automated post-training trajectories separates execution-level iteration from strategy-level revision and reports that only 74 of 3,557 adjacent training-experiment pairs changed strategy (~2.1%). Experience scaffolding, initial human guidance and additional inference compute improve execution or initial choice but do not reliably induce spontaneous strategy reopening once training is underway.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — The study reports only 74 strategy changes among 3,557 adjacent training-experiment pairs (~2.1%).
  • DEMONSTRATED — Experience scaffolding substantially improves execution on easier tasks but does not reliably reopen the high-level strategy during execution.
  • SPECULATIVE — Spontaneous strategy reevaluation is the primary general bottleneck for autonomous AI R&D systems.

Strongest baseline

Autonomous PostTrainBench agent trajectories, with intervention comparisons that add structured experience/evaluation, one-shot human strategic guidance, or greater reasoning-token expenditure.

The caveat that matters

  • Strategy-change labels depend on the study's distinction between local execution changes and high-level strategy changes.
  • Observed lock-in may depend on the tested agent scaffolds, prompts, tool interfaces, benchmark tasks and finite budgets rather than being an intrinsic model limitation.
  • Human guidance is tested as an external intervention; the study does not yet establish the best mechanism for endogenous strategy reopening.
  • Extra inference compute is not equivalent to targeted meta-level training, so failure of token scaling does not show that strategic adaptation cannot be learned.
  • No independent reproduction is present in the canonical record.
  • Human strategic guidance can redirect the agent's initial approach, showing the systems possess at least some capacity to execute alternative strategies; the deficit is narrower than inability to understand or implement strategy.

What remains unknown

  • The reported strategy-change rate and intervention conclusions lack independent trajectory relabeling/reproduction in FCMO AI.
  • The paper diagnoses strategy lock but does not establish whether a simple explicit strategy-reopening controller solves it under matched total compute.

FCMO Lens

Autonomous AI R&D can look highly iterative while remaining trapped inside its first high-level choice. If this failure mode generalizes, research agents need explicit mechanisms and training signals for hypothesis/strategy reopening, not merely longer context, more tools, journals, evaluators or more inference tokens.
