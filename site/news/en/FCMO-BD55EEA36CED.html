SimLife-BP tests long-horizon behavioral-rule inference in synthetic household simulations

Peng et al. present SimLife-BP, a benchmark for inferring symbolic behavioral patterns from long synthetic household timelines. The current v2 paper describes 106 episodes, up to 48.23 hours and 120 in-game days, with 1,439 question-answer pairs; tasks test direct, counterfactual, noisy-counterfactual and inverse reasoning under three hint levels. Across reported model evaluations, performance depends strongly on the evidence pipeline: most vision-plus-language models remain near chance on average question accuracy, while Gemma-4-31B-it reaches 36.2% all-correct and 74.6% question accuracy with full hints over noiseless text-only evidence. The authors also report shortcutting by frequency heuristics and difficulty adapting when patterns change. These are paper-reported results in scripted Sims 4 simulations with deterministic symbolic rules and generated dialogue, not evidence of real human understanding or deployed household-agent performance.

What actually changed

Peng et al. present SimLife-BP, a benchmark for inferring symbolic behavioral patterns from long synthetic household timelines. The current v2 paper describes 106 episodes, up to 48.23 hours and 120 in-game days, with 1,439 question-answer pairs; tasks test direct, counterfactual, noisy-counterfactual and inverse reasoning under three hint levels. Across reported model evaluations, performance depends strongly on the evidence pipeline: most vision-plus-language models remain near chance on average question accuracy, while Gemma-4-31B-it reaches 36.2% all-correct and 74.6% question accuracy with full hints over noiseless text-only evidence. The authors also report shortcutting by frequency heuristics and difficulty adapting when patterns change. These are paper-reported results in scripted Sims 4 simulations with deterministic symbolic rules and generated dialogue, not evidence of real human understanding or deployed household-agent performance.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The current v2 paper describes 106 synthetic episodes up to 48.23 hours/120 in-game days, with 1,439 question-answer pairs and four reasoning question types at three hint levels.
  • CLAIMED — In the paper’s full-hint noiseless text-only condition, Gemma-4-31B-it reaches 36.2% all-correct and 74.6% question accuracy; most vision-plus-language models remain near chance on average accuracy.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report frequency-based shortcuts and difficulty adapting when patterns change, within scripted Sims 4 simulations; no real-human or deployed-agent transfer is established.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper, modality and evidence-pipeline comparisons include vision-plus-language diary summaries, language-only responses over noisier action-log text (L), and language-only responses over noiseless structured text (L-N), with full, partial and no-hint conditions. The comparison reveals input/evidence effects but does not isolate a general reasoning mechanism independent of those pipelines.

The caveat that matters

  • All results are paper-reported; no independent execution or reproduction was performed.
  • The benchmark uses synthetic Sims 4 activity and deterministic symbolic rules; it does not establish inference about real people or actual household-agent performance.
  • Dialogue is generated using model-assisted pipelines and spot-checked; its validity as natural human evidence remains uncertain.
  • Vision-plus-language evaluation uses a two-stage video-to-diary then text-QA pipeline, while noiseless text-only input exposes structured evidence; performance differences therefore mix perception, summarization and reasoning.
  • Hint levels provide varying prior rule information; full-hint/noiseless conditions do not represent the ordinary deployed-agent setting.
  • Transfer to independently collected behavior, other simulators and real-world long-term interaction is unmeasured.

What remains unknown

  • The benchmark results, generated episodes and input pipelines have not been independently audited or reproduced.
  • Transfer from deterministic synthetic household rules and generated dialogue to independently observed human behavior is unknown.

FCMO Lens

The benchmark separates long-context storage from the harder task of inferring and revising a behavioral rule when evidence is distributed across time and modalities. Its large reported gap between raw video-plus-language and structured noiseless text shows that evaluation can be dominated by evidence extraction and organization before rule reasoning is measured. The synthetic deterministic rules and staged diary-summary pipeline make the result useful for evaluation design but limit claims about people or real-world assistants.
