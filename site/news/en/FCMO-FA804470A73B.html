Value-based RL reportedly generalizes robot-policy improvement across simulated tasks

A 4 November arXiv preprint studies online post-training of the pi_0.5 vision-language-action policy across simulated manipulation tasks. The authors report that critic-guided recipes—off-policy generative policy optimization (OGPO) and best-of-N (BoN)—increasingly improve success on held-out scenes and objects as training-task diversity grows, while filtered supervised fine-tuning often forgets on unseen objects. In MolmoSpaces, they report up to +58 percentage points on unseen scenes and +39 points when both scene and object change; one 16-task BoN run used 2.9× fewer gradient steps than 16 single-task runs at matched data-collection budget. These are paper-reported results from three training seeds, not an independent reproduction. The evaluation is simulation-only; on the more challenging LIBERO suite the authors report similar patterns but generalization often gave way to forgetting.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — arXiv:2611.06905v1 was submitted on 4 November 2026 and presents an empirical study of online multi-task improvement for a generalist robot policy.
  • Claimed — Across three seeds in simulated MolmoSpaces, the authors report that critic-guided OGPO and BoN improve success on held-out scenes and objects as training-task diversity increases, with gains over the base policy of up to +58 percentage points on unseen scenes and +39 points when objects also change.
  • Claimed — At a matched data-collection budget, the paper reports that one BoN post-training run across 16 tasks required 2.9× fewer gradient steps than 16 single-task runs for similar multi-task improvement.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproduction or physical-robot transfer. Its evaluation is limited to simulated manipulation using the pi_0.5 policy; the authors report substantially harder generalization on LIBERO, where forgetting remains.

Strongest baseline

Filtered supervised fine-tuning (FSFT) and single-task post-training; authors report critic-guided OGPO/BoN generalize better in MolmoSpaces, while held-out LIBERO remains difficult and often shows forgetting.

Limitations

  • All numerical outcomes are author-reported and were not independently reproduced.
  • The evaluation uses simulated manipulation environments and the pi_0.5 policy; it does not establish physical-robot capability.
  • LIBERO generalization is substantially harder; the authors report that similar methods often show forgetting there.
  • Results average three training seeds with bootstrap confidence intervals; this does not remove task-split or benchmark-specific uncertainty.

What we still don't know

  • The paper reports three-seed simulation results, but no independent rerun was conducted in this activation.
  • Results cover simulated manipulation with the pi_0.5 policy; physical-robot transfer and robustness under deployment variation are untested.

Why it matters

The paper isolates task diversity and critic-guided value-based RL as candidate ingredients for improving a generalist robot policy beyond its post-training tasks. If replicated, this could inform VLA post-training and data-allocation choices. The evidence is bounded to two simulated manipulation suites and one base policy; it does not establish real-robot transfer or robust deployment performance.
