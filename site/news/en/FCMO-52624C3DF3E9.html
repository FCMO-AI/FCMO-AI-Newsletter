DepthGraft reports hierarchical cross-layer KV reconstruction with lower cache and prefill cost

The authors propose hierarchical sharing of key/value (KV) representations across transformer depth, reconstructing deep-layer KV from aligned shallow and middle layers. In experiments on dense and MoE models from 0.5B to 16B parameters, they report a roughly one-third KV-cache reduction. For 9B MLA and 16B GQA models, the paper reports lower time-to-first-token (TTFT) at 8K–128K context and nearly unchanged time-per-output-token (TPOT); at 128K, reported TTFT reductions are 26.8% and 28.8%, respectively. These results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that DepthGraft reduces KV-cache use by roughly one third while retaining prefill early completion.
  • Claimed — At 128K context, the authors report TTFT reductions of 26.8% on 9B MLA and 28.8% on 16B GQA, while TPOT remains within 1% of baseline across tested lengths.
  • Claimed — For a 16B GQA MoE, the paper reports MMLU-Pro increasing from 23.86 to 30.86, an absolute gain of 7.00 points versus its GQA baseline.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish independent reproduction, production deployment, or transfer to arbitrary model families and workloads.

Strongest baseline

Within-paper GQA baselines on the same reported model settings, plus a 9B MLA baseline for long-context latency. The paper also compares adjacent and global-anchor KV-sharing variants in ablations.

Limitations

  • The performance and latency results are reported by the paper authors and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The long-context latency comparison is limited to 9B MLA and 16B GQA configurations, contexts of 8K–128K, and a fixed 2K generation length.
  • The reported 16B MMLU-Pro gain is a within-paper benchmark result; no independent evaluation, error bars or multi-seed reproduction was inspected.
  • The linked project code and detailed evaluation artifacts were not inspected or run; production deployment and serving economics are not established.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of reported quality, KV-cache or latency results was located or performed; linked code was not inspected.
  • The paper does not establish whether the reported benefits transfer to other architectures, production serving stacks or workload distributions.

Why it matters

If reproduced, cross-layer KV reconstruction could reduce inference memory and prefill latency while retaining benchmark performance, affecting long-context model and serving architecture choices. The evidence is a single paper’s experiments and does not establish production deployment, broad model-family transfer or independently verified quality gains.
