Amazon Bedrock adds Kimi K3 with cross-region availability and prompt caching

AWS announced general availability of Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3 on Amazon Bedrock on 18 September 2026. AWS says it is available in all AWS Regions where Bedrock is offered through cross-Region inference, and describes Kimi K3 as the first open-weight model on Bedrock with explicit prompt caching. AWS attributes to Moonshot AI the claims that Kimi K3 has 2.8 trillion parameters, native vision, a 1-million-token context window and about 2.5x better scaling efficiency than Kimi K2. The service launch is documented by AWS; model specifications and comparative efficiency remain vendor-reported, and no independent capability or cost test was conducted here.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — AWS announced Kimi K3 general availability on Amazon Bedrock on 18 September 2026 and says it can be invoked in all AWS Regions where Bedrock is available through cross-Region inference.
  • DEMONSTRATED — AWS describes Kimi K3 as the first open-weight model on Bedrock to support explicit prompt caching; the announcement says caching can reduce latency and input costs when context is reused but provides no quantified measurement.
  • CLAIMED — AWS attributes to Moonshot AI the specifications of 2.8 trillion parameters, native vision, a 1-million-token context window and approximately 2.5x scaling-efficiency improvement over Kimi K2. These model claims were not independently verified in this review.

The caveat that matters

  • The announcement documents service availability, not independent model quality or user outcomes.
  • Parameter count, native vision, context length and scaling-efficiency comparison are attributed to Moonshot AI and were not independently checked here.
  • No pricing, cache-hit rate, latency, throughput or repeated-context cost measurement is reported in the announcement.
  • AWS regional availability through cross-Region inference does not establish that every customer workload or data-residency configuration is supported.

FCMO Lens

Managed availability and prompt caching extend the deployment options for open-weight models inside a major cloud service. Whether that improves practical cost, latency or quality depends on workload and measured service terms; the announcement alone does not establish a capability advantage over other models.
