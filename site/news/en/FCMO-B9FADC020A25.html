Halcyon reports strong text-to-image results without internet-scale language pretraining

An Oct 19 arXiv preprint introduces Halcyon, early-fusion autoregressive text-to-image models trained from scratch on 100M image-caption pairs. On the authors’ GPIC evaluation, Halcyon-0.52B reports 48.0 gFD against 63.3 for a GPIC-retrained LlamaGen-XL/8 baseline, and Halcyon-1.6B reports 35.3 against 41.0 for a retrained JiT-T2I-XXL/8++ baseline. Their controlled 0.52B experiments report no improvement from external text-only pretraining under tested conditions. The study is author-reported, limited to one dataset and 256x256 images, and does not establish a general rule across model families or image-generation regimes.

What changed

An Oct 19 arXiv preprint introduces Halcyon, early-fusion autoregressive text-to-image models trained from scratch on 100M image-caption pairs. On the authors’ GPIC evaluation, Halcyon-0.52B reports 48.0 gFD against 63.3 for a GPIC-retrained LlamaGen-XL/8 baseline, and Halcyon-1.6B reports 35.3 against 41.0 for a retrained JiT-T2I-XXL/8++ baseline. Their controlled 0.52B experiments report no improvement from external text-only pretraining under tested conditions. The study is author-reported, limited to one dataset and 256x256 images, and does not establish a general rule across model families or image-generation regimes.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that scratch-trained early-fusion Halcyon models trained on 100M GPIC image-caption pairs achieve 48.0 gFD at 0.52B parameters and 35.3 at 1.6B on GPIC Test-50K; these compare favorably with their GPIC-retrained LlamaGen-XL/8 (63.3) and JiT-T2I-XXL/8++ (41.0) baselines.
  • Claimed — In controlled 0.52B experiments, the authors report no benefit from external text-only pretraining for their tested T2I setup and report that replacing caption dropout with 10% image-to-text supervision preserves similar generation quality while adding captioning ability.
  • Not established — The results do not establish that internet-scale language pretraining is unnecessary across other datasets, higher resolutions, larger controlled models, or video generation; no independent reproduction was conducted in this review.

Strongest baseline

LlamaGen-XL/8 and JiT-T2I-XXL/8++ retrained on GPIC using their respective architectures and modified image representations; authors acknowledge baseline selection is non-trivial and compare on the same dataset/evaluation protocol.

Limitations

  • All empirical results are from one author team; no independent reproduction was conducted in this review.
  • The main comparison uses one dataset, GPIC, at 256x256 resolution.
  • Controlled data-composition experiments focus on Halcyon-0.52B; the language-initialization comparison uses Qwen3-0.6B.
  • The reported pretraining FLOPs exclude data curation and inference economics.
  • LlamaGen and JiT baselines differ in architecture and image representation from Halcyon, despite being retrained on GPIC.

What we still don't know

  • Results are author-reported in a single preprint; no independent reproduction was conducted in this review.
  • Main results use one GPIC dataset at 256x256; controlled data-composition experiments are limited to Halcyon-0.52B and the language-initialization comparison to Qwen3-0.6B.
  • The reported compute comparison includes pretraining FLOPs but not inference cost, data curation/collection or all deployment economics; baseline systems differ in architecture and tokenization despite being retrained on GPIC.

Why it matters

If replicated across datasets and architectures, training capable text-to-image systems directly from image-caption data could lower the pretraining compute and dependency on large pretrained language encoders. The paper reports favorable GPIC results and controlled findings, but independent replication and broader regime coverage remain open.
