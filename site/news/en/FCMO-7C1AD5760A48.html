A study finds LLM aggregators can treat repeated reports as partly independent evidence

Gao and colleagues study how four language models count repeated reports across controlled sensor logs with an exact Bayesian oracle, web documents, and generated multi-agent transcripts. Their abstract reports that a forwarded copy counts as 0.06–0.42 of an independent reading, and that repeated statements can imply premature commitment on 5%–40% of logs in the tested settings. The authors report that a declaration about copy contribution lowers copy weight to 0.08 or less on controlled logs, while a worker-side rule to refer to readings rather than restate them lowers belief-implied early commitment from 11.2% to 1.1% in their team-log protocol. This is one author-reported preprint, not an operational error rate or independent replication.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED_PUBLICATION_EVENT — The authors published a study of how language-model aggregators interpret repeated evidence across three testbeds and four models.
  • CLAIMED_BY_AUTHORS — Across the tested models, a forwarded copy counted as 0.06–0.42 of a new reading; on 5%–40% of logs with one reading stated three times, reported beliefs implied early commitment that the oracle did not make.
  • CLAIMED_BY_AUTHORS — A worker instruction to refer to readings rather than restate them reduced belief-implied early commitment from 11.2% to 1.1% in the authors’ generated team-log protocol.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The study does not establish deployed multi-agent error rates or independent replication of its results.

Strongest baseline

Matched cases holding the underlying evidence fixed while varying restatement or independent readings; an exact Bayesian oracle is used for controlled logs.

The caveat that matters

  • Evidence is author-reported in one preprint and has not been independently reproduced.
  • Testbeds include controlled synthetic logs, web-document copies and author-generated team logs; they do not establish a deployed-system error rate.
  • Reported early-commitment effects depend on the tested models, prompts, protocol and oracle assumptions.
  • The abstract does not establish transfer to naturally occurring source-provenance graphs or broader agent tasks.

What remains unknown

  • No independent replication or operational multi-agent evaluation was identified in this activation.
  • This activation re-read the abstract, not the current full-text version, public data, or implementation.
  • The abstract reports a worker-side reference rule but does not fully expose how results vary across communication protocols or incomplete relay logs.

FCMO Lens

Multi-agent systems can relay the same observation through summaries, boards and discussion rounds. If an aggregator partly counts each restatement as a new source, confidence may rise without new evidence. The findings support explicit source ancestry and separate tests of worker communication and aggregator reasoning, while leaving real-world rates and cross-domain transfer unresolved.
