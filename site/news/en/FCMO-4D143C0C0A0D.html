OpenAI releases country-level work-activity classifications of consumer ChatGPT use

OpenAI Economic Research released Signals v3.0, a public dataset and methodology describing consumer ChatGPT messages classified into work activities mapped to 165 O*NET Intermediate Work Activities across nearly 100 countries. Quarterly series span Q1 2025–Q3 2026. OpenAI reports that, across 74 countries observed in every quarter, the ten most common activities accounted for an average 51.1% of classified work messages in Q3 2026, down from 56.0% in Q1 2025. The dataset covers individual consumer plans, not enterprise messages or Codex; the work and activity labels are automated model classifications. The release measures message mix, not employment, task completion, productivity or labor-market effects.

What changed

OpenAI Economic Research released Signals v3.0, a public dataset and methodology describing consumer ChatGPT messages classified into work activities mapped to 165 O*NET Intermediate Work Activities across nearly 100 countries. Quarterly series span Q1 2025–Q3 2026. OpenAI reports that, across 74 countries observed in every quarter, the ten most common activities accounted for an average 51.1% of classified work messages in Q3 2026, down from 56.0% in Q1 2025. The dataset covers individual consumer plans, not enterprise messages or Codex; the work and activity labels are automated model classifications. The release measures message mix, not employment, task completion, productivity or labor-market effects.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — OpenAI Economic Research announced and documented Signals v3.0, a public release of aggregated ChatGPT work-use data and its methodology on 26 October 2026.
  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that, among 74 countries observed in every quarter, the ten most common work activities represented an average 51.1% of classified work messages in Q3 2026, compared with 56.0% in Q1 2025. Recalculation from the released CSV reproduces these rounded shares by averaging each country’s top-ten IWA total across the fixed 74-country panel; the released file and underlying classifications remain provider-supplied.
  • Demonstrated — The release documentation describes quarterly country-level work-activity categories mapped to 165 O*NET Intermediate Work Activities; the dataset excludes enterprise messages and Codex, and its work-relatedness and activity labels are inferred by automated classifiers.
  • Not established — The release does not establish changes in employment, job displacement, worker productivity, task completion, or economy-wide AI adoption.

Strongest baseline

The provider compares its own classified activity shares in Q1 2025 against Q3 2026 for the same 74-country panel; this is not an external or employment-weighted baseline.

Limitations

  • All described data and results are from OpenAI, the provider being measured; no independent audit or reproduction was found in this review.
  • The sample covers individual consumer ChatGPT, excludes enterprise messages and Codex, and excludes some account groups; it is not a census of AI use or work.
  • Work-relatedness and O*NET activity categories are automated model inferences from message context, not worker self-reports; classification error and drift may affect comparisons.
  • Message shares do not measure distinct workers, jobs, completed tasks, productivity, employment changes or causal AI effects.
  • Version 3.0 adds July–September 2026 and increases historical samples from 300,000 to 1,000,000 messages/month; earlier periods have been restated across releases and the dictionary documents cumulative privacy budgets.
  • The released CSV arithmetic reproduces the reported fixed-panel statistic, but this is not an independent validation of OpenAI’s source sample, differential-privacy processing or automated work/IWA classifications.

What we still don't know

  • The linked CSV ZIP was downloaded and its 74-country top-ten statistic was reproduced from the released table. This verifies the published aggregation arithmetic only; the provider-generated sample and classifications have not been independently validated.
  • The work-relatedness and O*NET activity labels are inferred by OpenAI classifiers; independent labeling accuracy and cross-country/temporal comparability were not established.
  • The message-share series does not establish worker counts, employment-weighted task prevalence, task completion, productivity or labor-market effects.

Why it matters

A cross-country, task-level time series could help researchers compare consumer AI use with occupational and economic datasets. Its value depends on classifier validity, sampling and interpretation: message shares are not workers or jobs, and the provider-reported concentration trend does not demonstrate productivity or employment change.
