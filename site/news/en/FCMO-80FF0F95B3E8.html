What changed

Keime, Cuperlier and Cottereau report REACT, a fully spiking state-space model that updates from raw event-camera streams without temporal accumulation. On the EvTTC validation split, their v1 preprint reports 9.59±0.74% relative time-to-collision error and 4.6 ms end-to-end latency without a target-box prior. The authors report 4 cm of travel during that latency at the dataset mean approach speed of 9.22 m/s; at 100 km/h the same latency corresponds to 12.8 cm. Their INT8 energy figures are analytical estimates, not hardware measurements. The numerical results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that REACT processes raw events sequentially with continuous-time spiking state-space dynamics, without event accumulation or target-box input.
  • Claimed — On EvTTC, the authors report 9.59±0.74% relative TTC error and 4.6 ms end-to-end latency; 4 cm travel uses dataset mean speed 9.22 m/s, while 12.8 cm uses 100 km/h.
  • Claimed — The authors report 25.7% relative TTC error on one FCWD sequence without fine-tuning; energy figures are analytical estimates based on a 45 nm proxy and await hardware validation.

Strongest baseline

EvTTC comparison in Table II includes Garl-TTC at 9.44% and 113 ms with event+frame input and target box; REACT uses events only and no target prior. The comparison changes inputs and task setup, so it is informative but not a fully matched causal comparison.

Limitations

  • All reported performance values are from the authors’ arXiv v1 preprint and have not been independently reproduced.
  • The 9.59% EvTTC comparison changes input regime relative to target-box baselines; do not present it as a fully matched same-input win.
  • The 4 cm and 12.8 cm distance figures use different vehicle-speed assumptions and should not be compared as conflicting measurements.
  • The INT8 energy estimate is a proxy and should not be described as measured device energy.
  • The authors promise code on publication; the inspected paper does not provide a public implementation.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction of the EvTTC evaluation or reported latency was performed; exact test sequences, preprocessing, runtime hardware and measurement details are not independently checked.
  • Energy results use a 45 nm analytical proxy; no physical neuromorphic hardware or robotic-platform measurement is reported in the inspected source.
  • The test set is limited and zero-shot transfer covers one FCWD sequence; generalization to varied speeds, scenes, sensors and closed-loop robot behavior is not established.

Why it matters

A model that predicts collision timing continuously from raw events without first detecting or tracking a target could shorten the perception-to-action path in fast reactive robotics. The reported accuracy is close to target-box-based learned methods under a different input regime, but this remains a single preprint evaluation on limited driving data; real-robot utility and hardware energy are not established.
