GPT-Live-1.1 system card reports voice-safety regressions on synthetic adversarial prompts

OpenAI's October 15 GPT-Live-1.1 system card reports mixed voice-safety evaluation results. On its voice-native production-prompt set, the company reports no statistically significant regressions versus GPT-Live-1. On its deliberately difficult synthetic-prompt set, it reports statistically significant regressions for GPT-Live-1.1 in sexual, mental-health, emotional-reliance and gore categories; it also reports slight regression on a restrictions-respect evaluation and mixed changes on blocker-disclosure and broken-search tasks. These are first-party results, and neither prompt set estimates safety-event prevalence in production traffic.

What changed

OpenAI's October 15 GPT-Live-1.1 system card reports mixed voice-safety evaluation results. On its voice-native production-prompt set, the company reports no statistically significant regressions versus GPT-Live-1. On its deliberately difficult synthetic-prompt set, it reports statistically significant regressions for GPT-Live-1.1 in sexual, mental-health, emotional-reliance and gore categories; it also reports slight regression on a restrictions-respect evaluation and mixed changes on blocker-disclosure and broken-search tasks. These are first-party results, and neither prompt set estimates safety-event prevalence in production traffic.

Evidence

  • Claimed — OpenAI reports that GPT-Live-1.1 has no statistically significant regressions versus GPT-Live-1 on its voice-native production-prompt evaluation, while its synthetic-prompt evaluation finds statistically significant regressions for GPT-Live-1.1 in sexual, mental-health, emotional-reliance and gore categories.
  • Claimed — The card reports slight regressions for GPT-Live-1.1 on a restrictions-respect evaluation, slightly better performance on blocker-disclosure than GPT-Live-1, and slightly worse performance on a broken-search evaluation; GPT-Live-1.1 Quip changes differ on some comparisons.
  • Not established — The reported scores do not establish safety failure prevalence in deployed traffic, overall real-world harm rates, or the causal contribution of the model update separately from system-level safeguards and evaluation changes.

Strongest baseline

The directly compared predecessors are GPT-Live-1 for GPT-Live-1.1 and GPT-Live-1 mini for GPT-Live-1.1 Quip. OpenAI reports no statistically significant regressions for GPT-Live-1.1 on production prompts but statistically significant regressions in four named categories on synthetic prompts. The benchmark sets target different risk surfaces and are complementary rather than interchangeable.

Limitations

  • All results are first-party system-card reports; the research desk did not independently run the models or reproduce the evaluations.
  • The synthetic voice prompts target deliberately difficult policy-grounded cases and are not prevalence-weighted; their scores do not estimate production incident rates.
  • The production-prompt evaluation uses selected user-shared audio with privacy/eligibility processing and is also not prevalence-weighted.
  • OpenAI says system-level interventions such as Trusted Contact, localized crisis helplines and Parental Controls are not included in the synthetic evaluation; underlying model behavior and full product behavior are distinct.
  • The card says evaluation details evolve and prior system-card scores are generally not directly comparable when policies, graders, data or measurement details differ.

What we still don't know

  • No independent matched test of GPT-Live-1.1 versus GPT-Live-1 on the disclosed voice safety tasks was located or performed.
  • OpenAI states that the production-prompt and synthetic-prompt sets are difficult, non-prevalence-weighted evaluations with different risk surfaces; neither estimates real-traffic prevalence, and the card notes system-level interventions are not included in some measures.

Why it matters

The card exposes a useful deployment distinction: the same voice-model update appears close to parity on a selected production-prompt suite yet has targeted regressions on synthetic adversarial prompts. This makes evaluation-set design, system-level safeguards and category-level change tracking consequential for voice releases. The measurements do not establish real-world harm rates or show how much the deployed safety stack offsets the reported model-only changes.
