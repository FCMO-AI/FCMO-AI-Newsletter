A mechanics benchmark finds models can flag an impossible premise yet return a solved status

Yang and Wang report testing 14 models on 30 pairs of mechanics problems, each with a valid version and a version made impossible by changing an input or assumption. In 12 of 90 replies from three recent models, the system did not reject the flawed problem; in 11 of those replies, raters and numerical checks found that the model described the flaw, answered a corrected problem, and still marked the original as solved. A later retest found that warning/status prompts raised rejection for three of four models but lowered valid-problem solving for three. These are author-reported results on a small mechanics task set, not an operational failure rate.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED_PUBLICATION_EVENT — The authors published a study evaluating 14 models on 30 valid/flawed pairs of mechanics problems.
  • CLAIMED_BY_AUTHORS — Across three recent models, 12 of 90 replies did not reject a flawed problem; 11 of those 12 described the flaw, answered a corrected version, and still reported the original as solved.
  • CLAIMED_BY_AUTHORS — In a retest of four models from one provider, a changed warning/status prompt increased rejection for three models while valid-problem solving fell for three.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The benchmark does not establish an error rate for deployed engineering workflows or generalize to other domains.

Strongest baseline

Paired valid and flawed versions of the same mechanics tasks; answers were checked by two independent solvers per the authors.

The caveat that matters

  • The evidence is one author-reported preprint; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • The benchmark has 30 valid/flawed pairs in mechanics and does not estimate deployment error rates.
  • The 12/90 finding is bounded to replies from three recent models; it is not a population-level rate.
  • Prompt-schema changes can increase rejection while also reducing valid-problem solving, according to the authors.

What remains unknown

  • No independent, domain-diverse replication or deployed-workflow audit was found in this activation.
  • The full task-level outcomes, artifact and uncertainty details were not inspected in this bounded abstract check.
  • The retest suggests response schema can affect both false rejection and acceptance, but task-level tradeoffs were not independently recomputed.

FCMO Lens

Engineering agents and review pipelines often consume structured completion states. If a system recognizes a contradiction in prose yet emits a solved status, recognition alone may not protect downstream decisions. The paper supports measuring diagnosis, final status, repair behavior, and false rejection separately; it does not show this frequency in deployed engineering.
