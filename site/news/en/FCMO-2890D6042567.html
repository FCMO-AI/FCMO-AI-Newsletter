Receipt-backed task state improves agent workflow reliability in bounded tests, with matcher-dependent failures

The 2026-09-22 preprint compares how an LLM agent handles assigned-work state when it is left in the transcript, displayed as a checklist, turned into per-turn directives or enforced by a gate that rejects state-violating actions. The authors report that receipt-backed directives and enforcement raise strict task-lifecycle success in controlled episodes, with gains varying by model and reasoning regime. In one tau-squared-bench airline case, enforcement raises Qwen3-235B pass at one from 0.39 to 0.54; on PM-Bench, showing the record can match or outperform gates, while matcher-based enforcement hurts a smaller model on semantic cue tasks. These are author-reported, bounded results: enforcement depends on correct compiled state and request matching, and the study does not establish general deployed-agent reliability.

What changed

The 2026-09-22 preprint compares how an LLM agent handles assigned-work state when it is left in the transcript, displayed as a checklist, turned into per-turn directives or enforced by a gate that rejects state-violating actions. The authors report that receipt-backed directives and enforcement raise strict task-lifecycle success in controlled episodes, with gains varying by model and reasoning regime. In one tau-squared-bench airline case, enforcement raises Qwen3-235B pass at one from 0.39 to 0.54; on PM-Bench, showing the record can match or outperform gates, while matcher-based enforcement hurts a smaller model on semantic cue tasks. These are author-reported, bounded results: enforcement depends on correct compiled state and request matching, and the study does not establish general deployed-agent reliability.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In the authors’ controlled task-lifecycle testbed, receipt-backed directives and enforcement generally improve strict episode success over a raw transcript or displayed checklist, with results depending on model, reasoning regime, task state and matcher accuracy.
  • Claimed — On the tau-squared-bench airline case, the authors report Qwen3-235B pass at one rising from 0.39 to 0.54 with the enforcement gate; on PM-Bench, showing the record matched or beat gates in some configurations, while matcher-based enforcement reduced Qwen3.6-35B score below raw transcript.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that enforcement improves general deployed-agent reliability; its own results show losses when state or matcher judgments are wrong or when task success depends on semantic cue recognition.

Strongest baseline

The paired raw-transcript and exact-checklist arms under the same task, model and episode conditions; the paper also includes a self-written ledger arm. Results vary across domains: the PM-Bench case does not support a general enforcement advantage.

Limitations

  • Results are author-reported; the paper promises a future artifact release, but no public repository URL was found on the inspected page, so code was not run and experiments were not independently reproduced.
  • Episodes come from one generator in two domains; some closed-model cells have only 32 episodes, and one Qwen3.6-35B thinking row is partial and excluded from claims.
  • The tau-squared-bench case covers one airline domain with an internal user simulator; PM-Bench uses one released week.
  • Tool-harness tools never fail. Budgets, expiry, conflicting obligations and multi-agent or multi-session state are out of scope.
  • Held-out phrasing and ambiguity tests are limited; directives and tool results are plain user-role strings, so spoofing was not tested.
  • The enforcement matcher can confidently misroute a turn, while incorrect compiled state can refuse valid work; no refusal escalation path is evaluated.

What we still don't know

  • The authors say the generator, scorer, implementations, harness and logs will be released, but the inspected paper page provides no public artifact URL; the paired results have not been independently reproduced.
  • External transfer is limited to one airline-policy setting in tau-squared-bench and one released week in PM-Bench; the primary episodes come from one generator in two domains.
  • The gate depends on correctness of compiled state and request-to-step matching, and refusal escalation is not evaluated.

Why it matters

Agents handling multi-step work must distinguish completed, blocked, cancelled and repeatable actions. This paper supplies controlled evidence that moving task state from prose into receipt-backed directives or enforcement can help in some regimes, while making the matcher and state compiler new failure points. The result informs architecture choices, but it is not evidence of a general deployed-agent benefit.
