Study uses failure composition to choose between agent harness changes and weight training

A new arXiv preprint compares self-evolving an LLM agent's runtime harness with training model weights on the harness-produced trajectories. The authors report that on 72 held-out DeepPlanning tasks, harness evolution raised the composite score for Qwen3.5-4B from 0.156 to 0.292 and for Qwen3.5-9B from 0.305 to 0.390, with four-rollout means. In their diagnosis, process failures such as loops and failed delivery fell while poor delivered plans remained; trajectory-trained LoRA adapters reportedly transferred gains to the base harness and reduced content failures for the 9B model. The authors also report a +0.09 gain on 117 unseen WebArena-Lite tasks, where the harness exposed two pages of history rather than one and adapters did not add to that gain. These are paper-reported outcomes from a bounded benchmark study, not independently reproduced results or evidence that the intervention rule generalizes to all agents or tasks.

What actually changed

A new arXiv preprint compares self-evolving an LLM agent's runtime harness with training model weights on the harness-produced trajectories. The authors report that on 72 held-out DeepPlanning tasks, harness evolution raised the composite score for Qwen3.5-4B from 0.156 to 0.292 and for Qwen3.5-9B from 0.305 to 0.390, with four-rollout means. In their diagnosis, process failures such as loops and failed delivery fell while poor delivered plans remained; trajectory-trained LoRA adapters reportedly transferred gains to the base harness and reduced content failures for the 9B model. The authors also report a +0.09 gain on 117 unseen WebArena-Lite tasks, where the harness exposed two pages of history rather than one and adapters did not add to that gain. These are paper-reported outcomes from a bounded benchmark study, not independently reproduced results or evidence that the intervention rule generalizes to all agents or tasks.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — On 72 held-out DeepPlanning tasks, the authors report that self-evolving the harness raised four-rollout composite-score means from 0.156 to 0.292 for Qwen3.5-4B and from 0.305 to 0.390 for Qwen3.5-9B. They report a +0.09 gain on 117 unseen WebArena-Lite tasks. These measurements have not been independently reproduced.
  • CLAIMED — The authors classify trajectories by the first process or content failure signal and report that harness changes chiefly reduced process failures, while trajectory-trained LoRA reduced content failures for Qwen3.5-9B. On WebArena-Lite, the reported gain came from making two pages of history available instead of one; adapters did not add to that gain.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The accessible arXiv v1 paper describes its task split, harness-edit loop, four-rollout measurements, training data and held-out comparisons. This establishes what the paper reports and its stated protocol, not independent correctness of the reported outcomes.

Strongest baseline

The seed harness and frozen base weights are the within-paper baselines; the authors also compare adapters under seed and evolved harnesses and use an answer-shuffled placebo. These are author-run matched comparisons, not independent replications.

The caveat that matters

  • The primary harness and weight comparison uses two Qwen model sizes on the DeepPlanning travel-planning benchmark; WebArena-Lite provides one additional transfer setting.
  • Scores are author-reported four-rollout means. The paper describes measurement noise and cross-night drift; no independent reproduction was performed here.
  • Harness evolution uses an LLM proposer and judge with a restricted harness-edit surface; results do not isolate every possible harness or training design.
  • The rule is empirical in the evaluated settings and does not establish that failure composition always identifies the best intervention.

What remains unknown

  • No independent run has checked the held-out harness and weight-training results or whether the four-rollout noise protocol yields the stated differences.
  • The intervention rule's transfer beyond travel planning and WebArena-Lite remains unestablished.

FCMO Lens

If the reported task-bounded results hold, the study offers a practical way to decide whether agent work should target runtime process failures or model content quality, and reports a case where some harness gains transfer to weights while others remain tied to runtime context. That could improve agent R&D allocation. Evidence remains limited to the study's benchmarks, model sizes and evaluation protocol.
