Preprint reports better test-time scaling from parallel teams of agents

A Nov 6 preprint studies verifier-scored long-horizon tasks using Qwen3.8-27B agents in the Codex harness. The authors report that, at a budget of 100 agents, the best of ten collaborating teams of ten scores higher than the best of 100 independent agents on 10 of 12 tasks, and higher than one team of 100 on all four tasks tested at that size. Averaged across 12 tasks, the paper reports 10% higher scores at a 10-agent budget and 27% at 100 agents. The method combines within-team information sharing through a flat shared forum with best-of-N selection across independent teams. These are author-reported results: large-team runs have only one to five trials per size, the study uses one model family and one harness, and the linked code repository currently returns 404. Independent replication and transfer remain unestablished.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — A version of the paper titled Test-Time Compute Scales Better with Parallel Agent Teams was submitted to arXiv on 2026-11-06.
  • Claimed — On 12 verifier-scored long-horizon tasks, authors report that with Qwen3.8-27B and 100 agents, the best of ten collaborating teams of ten beats the best of 100 independent agents on 10 tasks and one 100-agent team on all four tasks where that comparison was run.
  • Claimed — Across the 12 tasks, authors report average score gains of 10% over independent agents at a 10-agent budget and 27% at a 100-agent budget; the same budget comparison uses fixed 12-hour task runs.
  • Not established — The paper does not establish that parallel teams improve performance across other model families, harnesses, task domains, budgets, or tasks without a reliable verifier; the research desk did not independently reproduce the results.

Strongest baseline

At equal total agent budget, best-of-100 independent Qwen3.8-27B agents and, for four tasks, one team of 100 agents. The paper also compares GPT-5.6-sol at equal dollar cost on eight tasks.

Limitations

  • All performance results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced by the research desk.
  • The paper reports approximately 100 independent runs and 10–11 team runs per task, but only one to five runs per larger team size; trial counts limit precision for those comparisons.
  • The experiments use one model family and one agent harness, on selected tasks with an available verifier, and 12-hour sessions.
  • The linked GitHub code URL returned 404 during this review; code, run logs and full cost accounting were not independently audited.
  • Results do not establish that best-of-N team selection works when a reliable verifier is unavailable.

What we still don't know

  • No independent replication of the reported taskwise or average gains was found or run in this activation; some larger team-size comparisons have only one to five runs.
  • The paper uses Qwen3.8-27B, one Codex harness, 12 selected tasks and fixed 12-hour sessions; transfer across models, harnesses and task families is unknown.
  • Best-of-N selection assumes a verifier can score and rank attempts; performance without a reliable external verifier is not tested.

Why it matters

Inference-time compute is often spent either on many independent attempts or on one large collaborating team. If the reported result generalizes, using several small communicating teams may improve verifier-scored long-horizon search by combining collaboration with between-run variation. The result could change how agent compute is allocated, but the evidence is limited to Qwen3.8-27B, one harness and selected tasks; it does not establish gains where no reliable verifier can rank completed attempts.
