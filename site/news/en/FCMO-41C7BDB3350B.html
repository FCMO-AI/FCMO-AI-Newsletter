Strongest baseline

Shenzhen HRSS says 13 provincial-level administrations shared pilot practices in May 2026 and that its local system had been running since November 2025. The November national report states expanded scope but gives no jurisdiction-level implementation baseline.

Limitations

  • The nationwide expansion is reported by the ministry; the original order or notice was not located in this bounded search.
  • The Shenzhen operational statistics appear in an article hosted by Shenzhen HRSS but credited in its footer to Nanfang+ client; no independent audit was located.
  • The Shenzhen system is one local example and must not be generalized to all pilot jurisdictions.
  • No effect on dispute quality, claimant outcomes, fairness, privacy or labor rights is established.
  • The May 2026 Shenzhen workshop article lists HRSS departments from 13 jurisdictions that shared application-scene experience. It specifically identifies Shenzhen as a pilot area but does not establish that each of the 13 attendee jurisdictions was formally designated a pilot site or had deployed an AI system.

What we still don't know

  • No jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction evidence shows systems deployed or being used under the expanded pilot; no independent outcome, fairness, safety, error-rate or cost evaluation was located.

Why it matters

Labor mediation and arbitration affect employment rights. If implemented, broader AI assistance could change how public agencies process disputes at scale. The current evidence supports a nationwide pilot-scope statement, while leaving actual rollout, human oversight in other jurisdictions and consequences for disputants unmeasured.
