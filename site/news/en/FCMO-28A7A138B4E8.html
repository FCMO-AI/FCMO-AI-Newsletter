Parameterized economic-data web skills improve matched transfer, while approximate retrieval erases gains on uncovered tasks

In arXiv:2609.19523v1, the authors evaluate 50 reusable procedures for retrieving live economic data from authoritative websites. In a controlled study using gpt-5-mini with BrowserGym, they report that a known matched skill raised success from 41.0% to 49.3% across 100 held-out variants repeated with three seeds. In a separate 360-task library-scale study, retrieving five skills was statistically tied with no-skill prompting overall (28.3% versus 28.1%): it scored 48.0% versus 44.0% on directly covered tasks, but 14.3% versus 16.7% on tasks without a direct match. The preprint therefore reports procedural transfer under a known match while exposing coverage-aware selection as the unresolved deployment problem. These are author-reported results from one benchmark/model/harness configuration, not an independent reproduction or evidence of general web-agent improvement.

What actually changed

In arXiv:2609.19523v1, the authors evaluate 50 reusable procedures for retrieving live economic data from authoritative websites. In a controlled study using gpt-5-mini with BrowserGym, they report that a known matched skill raised success from 41.0% to 49.3% across 100 held-out variants repeated with three seeds. In a separate 360-task library-scale study, retrieving five skills was statistically tied with no-skill prompting overall (28.3% versus 28.1%): it scored 48.0% versus 44.0% on directly covered tasks, but 14.3% versus 16.7% on tasks without a direct match. The preprint therefore reports procedural transfer under a known match while exposing coverage-aware selection as the unresolved deployment problem. These are author-reported results from one benchmark/model/harness configuration, not an independent reproduction or evidence of general web-agent improvement.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED_PUBLICATION_EVENT — The authors posted arXiv:2609.19523v1 on 2026-09-17 under the title EconSkills: Studying Skill Transfer and Retrieval for Web Agents on Live Economic Data.
  • CLAIMED — In the authors’ controlled experiment, one directly matched parameterized skill raised success from 41.0% (123/300) to 49.3% (148/300) across 100 held-out variants and three seeds; the paper reports McNemar p=0.0031.
  • CLAIMED — In the separate 360-task library-scale experiment, five retrieved skills scored 28.3% overall versus 28.1% for no-skill prompting, 48.0% versus 44.0% on the 150 directly covered tasks, and 14.3% versus 16.7% on 210 tasks without a direct match.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — Independent reproduction, transfer beyond the tested benchmark and gpt-5-mini/BrowserGym configuration, production reliability, and net economic value are not established by this single preprint.

Strongest baseline

Within the reported study, no-skill prompting is the primary baseline; raw successful-trajectory prompting is included for matched transfer, and random-five, all-50 and optional-hint conditions test library delivery.

The caveat that matters

  • Results are reported by the preprint authors and have not been independently reproduced in this review.
  • The paper reports gpt-5-mini in BrowserGym/AgentLab with a 30-step budget on live EconWebArena economic data tasks; no other model, harness or task domain was tested in the reviewed results.
  • The controlled transfer study uses three seeds per condition; the library-scale conditions use one run per task and provide no across-seed error bars.
  • The library contains 50 skills derived from solved seed trajectories across 37 websites and nine of ten benchmark categories; it does not cover all task families.
  • The overall retrieval condition is statistically tied with baseline, and approximate matches lower success on uncovered tasks; the paper does not establish net production or economic impact.

What remains unknown

  • No independent reproduction or audit of the reported controlled and library-scale episodes was identified in this bounded review.
  • The studies use one model (gpt-5-mini), one browser-agent ecosystem and EconWebArena economic-portal tasks; generalization to other models, websites and task classes is unknown.
  • This review did not inspect the released dataset/skill artifacts, raw trajectories or complete execution logs, so artifact consistency and episode-level scoring could not be checked.

FCMO Lens

If reproduced, explicit, parameterized procedures could make recurring agent work on economic portals more reliable and reduce navigation effort. The results also show why retrieval coverage and semantic checks matter: applying a merely approximate procedure to an uncovered task can erase the benefit and lower success. This is a useful, bounded design result for web-agent memory and economics-data workflows, with generalization beyond the tested benchmark still unknown.
