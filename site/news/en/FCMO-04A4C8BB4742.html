Jiangsu publishes provincial subsidies for AI compute, corpus and model development

A Jiangsu provincial cyberspace office page posted October 9 reproduces a five-agency notice dated September 30 that sets three support schemes for eligible organizations: compute/token vouchers covering up to 50% of qualifying service spending with a CNY 5 million per-entity ceiling; corpus vouchers supporting qualifying data and corpus purchases at up to 50% with a CNY 1 million ceiling, alongside separate support for dataset construction and embodied-robot data collection; and model vouchers covering 30% of qualifying model R&D spending up to CNY 1 million. The official notice page appeared on the provincial cyberspace office site on October 9. These are written eligibility and payment rules, not evidence that funds have been allocated, claimed or paid, or that the measures changed AI activity.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — A notice signed September 30 by five Jiangsu provincial agencies issues implementation schemes for token/compute vouchers, corpus vouchers and model vouchers; the provincial cyberspace office page displays a publication time of October 9.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The token-voucher scheme provides eligible Jiangsu users support for qualifying intelligent-compute purchases of at least CNY 10,000; provincial and city support combined is ordinarily capped at 50% of actual payment and CNY 5 million per entity, subject to local schemes and provincial budget control.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The corpus scheme sets up to 50% support and a CNY 1 million per-entity cap for eligible corpus/data purchases, up to CNY 500,000 for qualifying national high-quality dataset projects or CNY 200,000 for provincial projects, and up to 30% support with a cumulative CNY 2 million cap for embodied-robot data collection transactions through the provincial data exchange.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The model-voucher scheme provides 30% of qualifying model R&D spending, capped at CNY 1 million, and ranks applications using registered-user, API-call and realized-contract indicators.
  • DEMONSTRATED_LIMITATION — The policy text does not establish total appropriation, actual claims, disbursements, uptake or effects on AI activity.

Strongest baseline

A Hangzhou Binjiang notice published September 2, 2026 specifies municipal compute vouchers at 30% (up to 60% for priority computing resources) with a CNY 20 million ceiling, model-token use support up to CNY 10 million, and corpus support up to 30% capped at CNY 2 million per year. This is an announced municipal comparison, not evidence of awards or effects; Jiangsu’s notice is a provincial package and additionally sets out embodied-robot data-collection transaction support.

The caveat that matters

  • The notice is dated September 30, while the official web page displays an October 9 posting timestamp; these dates are kept separate.
  • No aggregate funding budget or city-level rollout plan was included in the opened notice page.
  • No applications, awards, payments, audit outcomes, beneficiary counts or economic effects were inspected.
  • The support schemes are regional and apply only to eligible entities and expenditures; national or province-wide effects cannot be inferred from their issuance.
  • A September 2, 2026 Hangzhou Binjiang policy already specified compute, model-token and corpus support; the Jiangsu notice should be read as a provincial package with distinct written terms, including embodied-robot data-collection support, not as the first local AI voucher program. Neither notice establishes actual uptake or effects.

What remains unknown

  • The notice specifies rules but does not establish city budgets, applications, awards or expenditure under any of the three schemes.
  • No counterfactual or measured change in compute use, corpus transactions, model development, embodied-robot data collection or firm location was reported.

FCMO Lens

The package targets three input costs for AI activity in one major Chinese province: compute, data and model development. If local budgets and uptake are substantial, it could change marginal costs for eligible research institutions, companies and individual business entities and provide a concrete regional policy design to compare with other compute and data subsidies. The notice does not state a total appropriation or show uptake or economic effects.
