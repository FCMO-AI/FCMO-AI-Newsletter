What changed

A September 2026 preprint describes a scientific retrieval system that stores paper-level claims, premises, reasoning chains, conditions and source links, then connects them into question, workflow and evidence views. The authors report nearly twice as many known supporting and contradicting evidence pairs retrieved on SciFact-Open, a 5-point citation-F1 improvement from adding reasoning graphs over the same retrieved papers, and gains on three fixed-model scientific QA benchmarks. The reported evaluations are author-run; omission errors, independent reproduction and direct evaluation of the landscape’s evidence categorization remain open.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors describe extracting papers into source-linked claims, inference factors, ordered reasoning chains, study settings and weak points, then organizing them into question, workflow and evidence views with incremental identity-preserving updates.
  • Claimed — On SciFact-Open at a 50-paper budget, the paper reports 818 known-positive claim-paper pairs retrieved versus 443 for the released retriever, with recall rising from 39.38% to 72.71%. The pooled reference set includes judge- and MultiVerS-admitted candidate positives; original human annotations take precedence.
  • Claimed — On two ScholarQABench subsets, adding reasoning graphs to the same retrieved papers, reranker and writing budget raises citation F1 by 5.3 and 5.1 percentage points, respectively, in the paper’s matched comparison.
  • Claimed — With GPT-5.4 fixed as the answer model, the paper reports LKM retrieval accuracy gains of 9.3, 4.2 and 14.7 points over no retrieval on ChemBench, PubMedQA and SciBench. The paper reports no paired confidence intervals for these point estimates.

Strongest baseline

Released SciFact-Open retriever at 50 papers; same retrieved candidates/reranker/writer budget without reasoning graphs on ScholarQABench; no-retrieval condition with GPT-5.4 fixed on three QA benchmarks.

Limitations

  • The author preprint is under review and its experimental results are not independently reproduced here.
  • The extraction audit samples 100 papers and measures hallucination but not omission; the authors note figure, table and equation evidence may be incompletely captured.
  • Question/workflow/evidence landscape categorization is not directly evaluated in the paper.
  • The reported 1.4-billion-claim production scale and 740-ms median latency are author-reported and vary with a continuously updated service snapshot.
  • The evidence-discovery pooled reference set adds judge- and MultiVerS-admitted pairs; QA point estimates do not include paired confidence intervals.

What we still don't know

  • The paper’s 100-paper extraction audit estimates hallucination but does not measure omitted claims/evidence; no independent audit or reproduction was performed.
  • The paper says the question/workflow/evidence landscape views and Evidence Engine categorization are not directly evaluated; the 1.4-billion-claim scale and service latency are author-reported production statistics.

Why it matters

The work tests a shift from retrieving passages to retrieving structured claims together with how they were supported and under what conditions. If its reported gains hold under independent evaluation, this could improve evidence tracing and reuse in scientific research systems; current results do not establish that the full production-scale knowledge base is complete or self-correcting.
