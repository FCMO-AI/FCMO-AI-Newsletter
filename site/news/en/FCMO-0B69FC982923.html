Google DeepMind and Singapore’s GovTech describe an on-device scam-detection model

Google DeepMind says it and Singapore’s GovTech fine-tuned a Gemma-based scam detector intended to analyze conversations on-device, using a lightweight classifier followed by conversation-level analysis for flagged cases. Singapore’s cybersecurity minister separately confirmed the collaboration is in development. A pre-existing GovTech AntiScam Pango beta is already described in official terms as using on-device AI, including an authority-tuned generative model; the sources do not establish whether the new Gemma work integrates with Pango or improves its performance. Neither announcement supplies measured detection accuracy, device cost or reduced scam losses.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Google DeepMind says it and Singapore’s GovTech fine-tuned a lightweight Gemma model on local scam conversations for on-device scam detection. The stated purpose is to help applications identify manipulative conversation patterns while keeping message processing on the user’s device; deployment within applications is described as phased and future-facing.
  • Claimed — Google describes a two-stage design: an on-device lightweight classifier screens inbound messages, then a fine-tuned Gemma model analyzes a larger conversation snapshot for messages flagged as suspicious. The announced sources provide no quantitative accuracy, latency or energy measurements.
  • Claimed — Google says fine-tuning occurred in GovTech’s managed cloud environment using anonymized datasets derived from scam reports and intelligence feeds, excluding private or personally identifiable information; the model was stress-tested with synthetic dialogues and local corpora. The reviewed announcement provides no dataset size, protocol or independent audit.
  • Demonstrated — Singapore’s Cyber Security Agency published the Minister for Digital Development and Information’s 14 October speech stating that GovTech was collaborating with Google DeepMind to develop real-time AI scam detection on users’ devices while keeping messages on-device. This independently corroborates the collaboration and development status, not effectiveness or deployment.
  • Demonstrated — Singapore GovTech’s AntiScam Pango Terms of Use, dated 9 September 2026, describe a separate experimental/beta mobile service that uses local AI models to assess messages from supported third-party messaging apps. The terms identify Authority-fine-tuned generative model weights quantized for on-device inference and a scam-classification model. They establish an earlier institutional on-device precedent, not effectiveness or a direct comparison with the newly announced Gemma collaboration.
