A survey of a literature-review agent reports stated trust despite mandatory checkpoints

A preprint evaluates CoSLR, a multi-agent system for systematic literature reviews with retrieval grounding and mandatory human checkpoints. In a remote, self-paced survey study, 63 participants completed a simplified workflow in about 15 minutes; recruitment used convenience channels and participants were mainly from software engineering. The authors report that 22/63 (34.9%) rated their trust in generated summaries and reports at 4 or 5 on a five-point scale for acceptance without additional checking after brief interaction. They also report that 27/63 (42.9%) rated usability highly. These are post-interaction self-reports: the study did not observe full reviews or whether participants actually skipped verification.

Evidence

  • Claimed — In a remote 15-minute, survey-based CoSLR evaluation with 63 convenience-recruited participants, 22/63 (34.9%) self-reported trust ratings of 4 or 5 for generated summaries and reports without additional checking after brief interaction. This measures stated willingness, not observed checkpoint use or actual unverified acceptance.
  • Not established — The study does not establish observed checkpoint bypass, actual acceptance of unverified reports, or a causal effect of mandatory checkpoints.

Strongest baseline

No control group or matched manual-review condition. The bounded result is a descriptive self-report within CoSLR’s short system-use evaluation.

Limitations

  • The sample contains 63 voluntary convenience-recruited participants, primarily from software engineering backgrounds.
  • Participants completed a simplified, remote task in about 15 minutes; none completed a full systematic review.
  • The outcome is stated trust on a questionnaire, not observed verification or actual acceptance of unverified synthesis.
  • The paper reports author-run survey results; the research desk did not inspect participant-level data or independently reproduce the study.

What we still don't know

  • The key trust measure is self-reported and the study did not observe complete reviews or actual verification behavior.
  • The 63-participant survey result has not been independently replicated in other disciplines or reviewer populations.

Why it matters

The study suggests that requiring a checkpoint may not ensure that users scrutinize AI-generated synthesis, even when they report the system is usable. Its bounded result is a self-reported attitude in a small convenience sample, not observed behavior or a causal estimate of checkpoint effectiveness.
