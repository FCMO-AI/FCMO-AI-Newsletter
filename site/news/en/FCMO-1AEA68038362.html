GUI-SD-v2 reports higher mobile GUI-agent success after privilege-following and step-level memory distillation

An arXiv preprint introduces GUI-SD-v2, which trains a mobile GUI agent in two stages: first to follow corrective privileged guidance, then to distill step-specific reasoning and task-relevant memory from its own interaction trajectories. With Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B and Kimi-K3-generated supervision, the authors report 67.2%/79.3% Pass@1/Pass@3 on AndroidWorld and 25.6%/31.6% on the GUI-only MobileWorld subset, above the paper’s evaluated baselines under its settings. These are author-reported results on two mobile benchmark settings; code and data were stated as future releases, and neither independent reproduction nor broader transfer was established.

What changed

An arXiv preprint introduces GUI-SD-v2, which trains a mobile GUI agent in two stages: first to follow corrective privileged guidance, then to distill step-specific reasoning and task-relevant memory from its own interaction trajectories. With Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B and Kimi-K3-generated supervision, the authors report 67.2%/79.3% Pass@1/Pass@3 on AndroidWorld and 25.6%/31.6% on the GUI-only MobileWorld subset, above the paper’s evaluated baselines under its settings. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The arXiv preprint presents GUI-SD-v2, a two-stage on-policy self-distillation method for multi-turn GUI agents. Stage one jointly trains rollouts with and without corrective privileged guidance; stage two distills step-specific reasoning and memory signals into action and memory outputs.
  • Claimed — Using Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B, the paper reports 67.2% Pass@1 and 79.3% Pass@3 on AndroidWorld, and 25.6% and 31.6% on the GUI-only MobileWorld subset. These are author-reported benchmark results; no independent reproduction was performed.
  • Claimed — Under fixed training tasks and Kimi-K3-generated supervision, the paper reports GUI-SD-v2 above its evaluated SFT, GRPO, naive OPSD and GUI-SD-v1 baselines on both benchmarks and Pass@1/Pass@3 metrics. This is a within-paper comparison, not evidence of general superiority across models, environments or task distributions.

Strongest baseline

Within the paper: same training-task scale and Kimi-K3 supervision for SFT, GRPO, naive OPSD and GUI-SD-v1; also reported comparisons with selected GUI-agent systems. No independent matched reproduction.

Limitations

  • This is a single unreviewed arXiv preprint; results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced.
  • Experiments use one base model, Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B, and mobile Android environments.
  • MobileWorld results cover its GUI-only subset, not the entire benchmark.
  • Training uses Kimi-K3-generated guidance and selected OpenMobile/MobileForge tasks; transfer beyond this supervision and task mix is unknown.
  • The paper states code and training data will be released; this activation did not verify a release.
  • Benchmark pass rates do not establish broad long-horizon reliability or production behavior.

What we still don't know

  • All reported benchmark results and ablations are from the paper; code and training data are described as future releases and were not available in the inspected paper.
  • Evaluation is limited to Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B on AndroidWorld and the GUI-only MobileWorld subset; transfer to other model sizes, desktop/web interfaces, broader task distributions or production reliability is not established.

Why it matters

The method targets a concrete bottleneck in multi-turn interface agents: a policy may receive useful privileged hints but fail to follow them or retain task state. If the reported gains reproduce, training the same policy to use step-level reasoning and memory guidance could improve mobile-agent post-training. The evidence remains benchmark-bounded and does not establish general agent reliability.
