Anthropic releases Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, reporting broad technical gains

Anthropic announced Claude Fable 5.2 for general use and restricted Claude Mythos 5.2 on Nov 9, reporting broad technical gains and a 30-hour, roughly 1,000-agent Lean formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem. Its newly inspected system card says Mythos 5.2 does not meet Anthropic’s Autonomy-2 threshold for research-staff substitution or dramatic AI R&D acceleration, but records an uncertain AECI slope-change estimate of about 1.88x and weaknesses in open-ended research and coding tasks. Separately, METR’s Nov 5 high-access assessment estimates Anthropic’s lab-level AECI growth at 1.9x since Oct 2025 and judges most current acceleration likely attributable to AI, with substantial uncertainty and major redactions. The lab-level estimate is not a model-specific result or a direct contradiction of Anthropic’s threshold judgment. Benchmark and scientific claims remain mostly first-party and unreplicated.

What changed

Anthropic announced Claude Fable 5.2 for general use and restricted Claude Mythos 5.2 on Nov 9, reporting broad technical gains and a 30-hour, roughly 1,000-agent Lean formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem. Its newly inspected system card says Mythos 5.2 does not meet Anthropic’s Autonomy-2 threshold for research-staff substitution or dramatic AI R&D acceleration, but records an uncertain AECI slope-change estimate of about 1.88x and weaknesses in open-ended research and coding tasks. Separately, METR’s Nov 5 high-access assessment estimates Anthropic’s lab-level AECI growth at 1.9x since Oct 2025 and judges most current acceleration likely attributable to AI, with substantial uncertainty and major redactions. The lab-level estimate is not a model-specific result or a direct contradiction of Anthropic’s threshold judgment. Benchmark and scientific claims remain mostly first-party and unreplicated.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — Anthropic announced Claude Fable 5.2 for general availability and Claude Mythos 5.2 through its Cyber and Life Sciences Verification Programs on 2026-11-09; the release says they share the same underlying model with different safeguards/access.
  • Claimed — Anthropic reports Fable 5.2 results above Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 on most listed evaluations, including 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0, 69.9% on FrontierSWE v2, and 74.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science; Mythos 5.2 reports 75.3% on the latter. The results use lab-selected effort settings and have not been independently reproduced in this activation.
  • Claimed — Anthropic says about 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents completed a Lean formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem in 30 hours with minimal human intervention, compared with 26% completion by an Opus 5.5 team in the same setup; the release describes one run per model and does not establish independent replication or broader mathematical problem-solving performance.
  • Claimed — A Nov 7 preprint contributed by Anthropic reports a first-order Hawking–Page transition at weak coupling and says an internal Claude research model obtained the result after a physicist prompted it and checked known special cases; it also says a separate Claude computation using a different method agreed. Independent specialist review was not established here.
  • Not established — The release and linked preprint do not establish that the reported benchmark gains generalize beyond the stated evaluations, that the scientific results are correct and novel, or that the models reliably deliver the described real-world outcomes; this activation performed no independent reproduction or specialist review.
  • Claimed — Anthropic’s system card says Claude Mythos 5.2 does not meet its Autonomy-2 threshold for full research-staff substitution or dramatic AI R&D acceleration, while describing that assessment as uncertain. Its AECI slope-change fit is about 1.88x (95% CI 1.62x–2.28x), with 32 of 100 refits at or above 2x; Anthropic says AI attribution is also uncertain.
  • Claimed — Anthropic’s system card reports that Mythos 5.2 ended without running an experiment in 63% of medium-effort open-ended research sessions and left requested feasible work undone in 6–13% of replayed coding sessions at medium-to-xhigh effort, versus 4% for Fable 5.1. These are company-reported evaluations, not independent performance estimates.
  • Claimed — METR’s November 5 independent report says Anthropic’s model benchmark scores, measured with AECI, grew at an estimated 1.9x their pre-October-2025 rate from October 2025 to October 2026 (95% confidence interval 1.5x–2.8x); METR judges that most of the current acceleration is likely attributable to AI but emphasizes substantial uncertainty. This is a lab-level estimate, not a causal evaluation of Fable 5.2 or a direct contradiction of Anthropic’s model-specific Autonomy-2 determination.
  • Not established — The reports do not establish a model-specific causal acceleration in AI R&D or a sustained future doubling. METR says Anthropic redacted most evidence it found most informative, and notes limitations from access, task specification, AECI’s lag and the unusually low earlier growth baseline; Anthropic likewise describes uncertainty in its fit and AI attribution.

Strongest baseline

Anthropic compares Fable 5.2 principally with Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5, and with selected GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Carbon scores. For the Fermat case it reports an Opus 5.5 1,000-agent run at 26% completion; both are single-run comparisons.

Limitations

  • Anthropic is the interested first-party source for release, model access, benchmark and safety claims; scores were not independently reproduced here.
  • Effort settings and evaluation regimes differ across benchmark columns; a best-score table does not establish matched general capability or deployment performance.
  • The Fermat formalization comparison is one run per model with approximately 1,000 agents; mathematical correctness, proof checking, novelty, human contribution and cost were not independently assessed in this activation.
  • The linked system card returned 404; detailed safety protocols and full model-card limitations were unavailable in this check.
  • The physics preprint's provenance statement reports physicist checks of known special cases, not independent verification of the new result.
  • The system card’s AECI slope-change fit is an internal benchmark trend, not a direct measurement of AI-caused acceleration; Anthropic says its attribution estimate is uncertain.
  • METR’s Nov 5 estimate concerns Anthropic’s lab-level capability growth from Oct 2025 to Oct 2026, not Fable 5.2 alone. METR had no access to non-public models, says Anthropic redacted most of its most informative evidence, and flags uncertainty about access, benchmark coverage, lag and the earlier baseline.

What we still don't know

  • Benchmark scores, safety evaluations and broad capability comparisons are provider-reported; task-level artifacts and independent matched-harness reruns were not inspected.
  • The Fermat formalization is described as one 1,000-agent run per model; this review did not build the Lean result, inspect the full orchestration trace or obtain specialist review.
  • The official release’s system-card link had returned 404 in the earlier scan; the linked Anthropic PDF was retrieved and inspected in this activation.
  • The model-specific and lab-level acceleration assessments use provider/internal data and remain uncertain. METR’s public report redacts most of the evidence it found most informative, had no access to non-public models and describes limited access; neither report establishes a model-specific causal effect or future trajectory.

Why it matters

If Anthropic’s reported gains hold in their stated regimes, the release could change model choices across coding, science, hardware and cybersecurity. The system card’s below-threshold but uncertain model-level assessment and METR’s separate, redacted estimate of roughly 1.9x lab-level progress show why model capability, total research progress and AI attribution must remain distinct. Neither report establishes a sustained future doubling; benchmark, science and task outcomes remain incompletely independently validated.
