What actually changed

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas on 2026-09-08 as a roughly 1 PB catalogue containing AlphaGenome-derived molecular-effect predictions for more than 9 billion possible single-nucleotide substitutions across the human reference genome, alongside the new AlphaGenome Variant Impact score and interpretable feature contributions. The underlying AlphaGenome model is peer-reviewed and strong across many variant-effect benchmarks, while independent 2026 evaluations show that performance varies materially by cell context and haplotype background. Atlas therefore represents a major scientific-infrastructure change in search economics, not experimental measurement of every variant.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED — AlphaGenome Atlas contains precomputed predictions for more than 9 billion possible human single-nucleotide substitutions and is approximately 1 PB in size.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The underlying AlphaGenome model uses 1 Mb sequence inputs and matched or exceeded strong external models on 25 of 26 reported variant-effect evaluations.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Independent regulatory-variant benchmarking shows AlphaGenome performance can fall sharply in some cellular contexts, so Atlas scores should not be treated as uniformly calibrated biological truth.
  • INFERRED — Precomputation should reduce repeated inference cost for cohort-scale and iterative variant-prioritization workflows, but whether it improves experimentally validated discovery per dollar remains an open systems question.

Strongest baseline

Specialized and general sequence-to-function predictors including SpliceAI/Pangolin, Basenji2, Enformer and other variant-effect models; for Atlas itself, the decisive baseline is repeated on-demand AlphaGenome inference plus conventional variant-prioritization pipelines under the same cohort and wet-lab budget.

The caveat that matters

  • Atlas is a catalogue of model predictions, not experimental measurements of 9 billion variants.
  • The new AVI score does not yet have a broad independent held-out audit in the inspected evidence.
  • Independent MPRA results show large cell-context variation, including much weaker performance in neuronal progenitors.
  • Single-SNV reference-genome scoring does not capture arbitrary multi-variant interactions or all realistic haplotype backgrounds.
  • AlphaGenome's finite sequence context and predicted molecular modalities cannot capture every trans or longer-range biological mechanism.
  • DeepMind explicitly states that AlphaGenome/Atlas are research tools rather than clinically validated diagnostic systems.
  • External MPRA benchmarking reports AlphaGenome Spearman correlations of 0.680 and 0.616 in HEK293T/HepG2 but only roughly 0.147-0.185 across tested architectures in neuronal progenitors, limiting any claim of uniform cross-context accuracy.

What remains unknown

  • Independent Atlas/AVI calibration against held-out functional and clinical assays is missing.
  • Full build-compute, storage, network and repeated-query economics are not disclosed.
  • Atlas-specific collaborator discovery claims need independently inspectable prospective validation artifacts.

FCMO Lens

When an expensive scientific model is repeatedly queried over an enumerable hypothesis space, precomputation can convert repeated inference into a durable searchable substrate. In genomics this could materially lower the cost of rare-variant prioritization and cohort-scale hypothesis generation, but usefulness depends on calibration, biological context and prospective experimental validation rather than catalogue size alone.
