Paper reports pre-action memory signals and selective evidence retrieval for long-horizon agents

The PaMER paper studies whether an agent’s hidden state contains signals that predict when to compress context or retrieve older evidence. It proposes state-guided compression and selective raw-evidence retrieval. What changed

The PaMER paper studies whether an agent’s hidden state contains signals that predict when to compress context or retrieve older evidence. It proposes state-guided compression and selective raw-evidence retrieval. On the authors’ 260-task WorkBuddyBench evaluation with DeepSeek-V4-Flash, PaMER reduced mean total input/output tokens by 73.2% while mean task reward changed from 69.9 to 69.4; PaMER+ reduced tokens by 71.7% and raised mean reward to 71.6. These results are author-reported and specific to the benchmark, harness and model configuration.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report that hidden representations predict compression and recall decisions better than observable length/turn metadata on their decision corpus (mean AUROC 0.831 vs 0.751 for compression and 0.765 vs 0.552 for recall).
  • Claimed — On the 260-task WorkBuddyBench evaluation with DeepSeek-V4-Flash, PaMER reduced mean total input/output tokens by 73.2% with mean reward 69.4 vs 69.9 for the base; PaMER+ reduced tokens by 71.7% and reported mean reward 71.6.
  • Claimed — On a fixed 40-task comparison, the paper reports PaMER and PaMER+ using about 52.4% and 48.1% fewer tokens respectively than the base while achieving slightly higher average reward in that test set.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The token measure includes input and output tokens across the main agent and auxiliary memory calls, but does not establish total latency, storage, external-tool costs or cost under other inference pricing and hardware regimes.

Strongest baseline

The same DeepSeek-V4-Flash base agent and fixed context-management baselines on WorkBuddyBench. Pilot40 is a fixed 40-task subset and should be treated separately from the full 260-task result.

Limitations

  • Results come from one paper-reported benchmark and specific model/harness settings.
  • The full benchmark and fixed Pilot40 results are distinct evaluation sets; token reductions do not alone establish total operating-cost reductions.
  • No independent implementation or reproduction was checked.

What we still don't know

  • Reproduce on WorkBuddyBench using pinned model and harness versions; report total wall time, memory storage, tool calls and cost as well as tokens.

Why it matters

The result isolates memory control as a distinct systems problem: selecting when to compress and which prior evidence to restore may reduce context load while retaining task quality in the tested agent regime.
