DexTacWAM reports higher scores when tactile contact is modeled as part of the robot’s predicted world state

DexTacWAM treats fingertip tactile dynamics as part of a robot’s predicted world state rather than only as an extra policy input. It encodes each finger, compresses five tactile streams into one hand-level representation, and jointly predicts visual and tactile futures for action generation. On six contact-rich real-robot tasks, the authors report mean score 70.6 versus 38.0 for the strongest listed baseline over 20 trials per method/task. A four-task ablation reports a drop from 74.7 to 26.6 when tactile world modeling is removed while keeping tactile features and action expert. The paper also reports four hours of tactile-encoder adaptation, about 100 demonstrations per task, retention of visual prediction within 0.5 dB, and faster training/inference with compression. These are author-run results on one 22-DoF platform; camera access is matched, but the method’s relative end-effector actions differ from baseline action parameterizations, and no independent reproduction was located.

Evidence

  • Claimed — On six contact-rich tasks using a 22-DoF bimanual robot platform, the authors report mean task score 70.6 for DexTacWAM versus 38.0 for the strongest listed baseline, Reactive Diffusion Policy. DexTacWAM scores highest on each task in their table, with 20 real-robot trials per method/task. Camera access is matched within task, but DexTacWAM uses relative end-effector actions while baselines retain their original action parameterization.
  • Claimed — In a four-task ablation with the same tactile features and action expert, removing tactile world modeling reduces mean score from 74.7 to 26.6; this compares predictive tactile-world modeling with direct tactile conditioning in the authors’ setup.
  • Claimed — The authors report adapting the tactile encoder with four hours of interaction data, then using roughly 100 demonstrations per task without tactile midtraining; visual prediction remains within 0.5 dB of vision-only counterparts. The compressor retains 89.4% of pre-fusion contact recall and is reported to enable 2.26× faster training and 1.29× faster inference.

Strongest baseline

Reactive Diffusion Policy mean 38.0 versus DexTacWAM 70.6 in the paper’s six-task table; baselines retain original action parameterization while DexTacWAM uses relative end-effector actions.

Limitations

  • All results are from one author-run preprint; no independent reproduction was inspected.
  • Evaluation covers six tasks on one 22-DoF bimanual platform with 20 trials per method/task. Camera access is matched within each task, but DexTacWAM uses relative end-effector actions and baselines retain their original action parameterization.
  • The ablation is limited to four tasks and one experimental setup; it does not establish that tactile world modeling dominates direct tactile conditioning across platforms or policies.
  • The reported 2.26× training and 1.29× inference speedups depend on the authors’ tactile compression and setup; deployment cost and sensor transfer remain unmeasured externally.
  • Visual prediction within 0.5 dB is reported for the tested tasks and model, not a general guarantee that tactile adaptation preserves visual capability.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction was found; the main baseline comparison changes action parameterization, which may contribute to score differences.
  • The study uses one tactile platform and six tasks; transfer across sensor hardware, hand embodiments, object distributions and longer horizons is unknown.

Why it matters

Dexterous robots need to reason about contact, slip and grasp state that cameras may not reveal. The paper’s ablation supports a specific design hypothesis: predicting tactile contact evolution as part of the world state may add value beyond feeding tactile readings directly to a policy. The reported gains are large but come from one author-evaluated hardware platform and a comparison with differing action parameterizations, so they motivate controlled reproduction rather than establish general transfer.
