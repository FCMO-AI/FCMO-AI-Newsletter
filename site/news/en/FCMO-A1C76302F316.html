A 112-language audit reports language-conditioned geopolitical framing differences across three models

Maxim Chupilkin’s arXiv:2609.20005v1 evaluates 20 curated English statements about the Russia–Ukraine war after translating them into 112 language/script conditions. Three fixed model/API versions produced 67,200 scores. The author reports that the balance between agreement with the paired Russia-leaning and Ukraine-leaning statements varies by language, while responses lean Ukraine-ward in all 112 conditions. Country-level mapped language averages correlate with public Russia favourability (Pearson r=0.569 across 35 countries), UN non-support (r=0.384 across 183) and Ukraine aid allocations relative to GDP (r=-0.707 across 41 tracked donors). The study reports robustness across models, statement-pair omissions and mapping variants. It does not trace political narratives into training data, establish causal effects, measure user belief change or show that model outputs represent national opinion. The topics were not preregistered or randomly sampled; automatic translation checks do not establish semantic equivalence, provider defaults differ, and data/code are described as forthcoming.

Claim vs. evidence

  • DEMONSTRATED_PUBLICATION_EVENT — Maxim Chupilkin posted arXiv:2609.20005v1 on 2026-09-17 under the title Geopolitical Divisions Across Languages in Large Language Models.
  • CLAIMED — The author reports evaluating 20 curated English statements in 112 translated language/script conditions with GPT 5.6 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash, collecting 67,200 scores; the pooled response balance leaned Ukraine-ward in all 112 conditions but varied by language.
  • CLAIMED — Country-level language averages correlated with Russia favourability (Pearson r=0.569, n=35), UN non-support (r=0.384, n=183) and bilateral Ukraine aid relative to GDP (r=-0.707, n=41). These are ecological associations reported by the author, not causal effects.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The audit does not establish semantic equivalence across all translations, a causal effect of information warfare or training data on outputs, representativeness of these statements for geopolitical opinion, national public opinion, or effects on users’ beliefs.

Strongest baseline

Within-study comparison uses the same models and paired statement bank across language/script conditions. There is no neutral-language or semantically human-validated translation control in the reviewed design, and the listed correlations do not constitute causal baselines.

The caveat that matters

  • This is a single-author unreviewed preprint; results were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The study uses 20 curated English statements on the Russia–Ukraine war, translated into 112 language/script conditions. The framing labels describe selected arguments, not their factual status; topics were not a random sample and the study was not preregistered.
  • Translations were automatically checked and frozen across models, but this does not establish independent semantic equivalence. Translation intensity or ambiguity may vary across languages.
  • Three exact model/API versions were queried through provider-pinned OpenRouter routes. Sampling and reasoning parameters remained at provider defaults, which need not be equivalent; results do not represent a population of models or current service behavior generally.
  • Country correlations are ecological and descriptive. They do not show that language-conditioned outputs cause public attitudes, UN votes or aid decisions, or that information warfare changed model training data.
  • The paper says complete prompts/data and analysis code will be provided in a public repository; this activation did not inspect the artifacts. User belief effects were not tested.

What remains unknown

  • The preprint says complete translated prompts and source data will be publicly provided, but this activation did not inspect a released dataset or code repository.
  • The statement bank was curated, was not preregistered or randomly sampled, and translations were automatically checked without independent semantic validation; prompt meaning may differ across language/script conditions.
  • No independent replication or broader conflict/topic battery was identified; only three fixed model/API versions and one geopolitical conflict were studied.

FCMO Lens

If independently reproduced with validated translations and broader, preregistered claims, the result would show that people can receive meaningfully different geopolitical framing when querying the same model family in different languages. It motivates multilingual evaluation rather than English-only political testing. The current evidence is a single computational audit of one conflict and one curated statement bank; its cross-country correlations are descriptive and do not identify why differences arise or how people respond.
