SkillGym reports agent training gains from executable human-workflow environments

A September 2026 preprint introduces SkillGym, a pipeline that turns human-written agent skills into executable tasks with code-based outcome checks and contrastive skill-dependence labels. The authors report 2,756 environments across 12 categories and 8,364 successful trajectories, then fine-tune Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on SkillGym trajectories. They report gains on GDPval-AA v2, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SkillsBench, including performance that persists without inference-time skills. These remain paper-reported results. The paper’s v2 abstract and v2 HTML disagree on the no-skills SkillsBench gain (12.38 versus 14.68 points), and the reported benchmark comparisons depend on specific harnesses; code, dataset and model artifacts were not audited in this review.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The v2 preprint describes SkillGym’s pipeline for converting human-written skills into executable environments with code-based outcome verifiers and reports 2,756 environments across 12 categories and 8,364 successful trajectories.
  • Claimed — The authors report that fine-tuning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B improves GDPval-AA v2 by 199 Elo and Terminal-Bench 2.1 by 19.10 percentage points; the v2 HTML reports SkillsBench v1.1 gains of 28.13 points with skills and 14.68 without skills. These are paper-reported results, not independently reproduced here.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The arXiv v2 abstract reports a 12.38-point SkillsBench v1.1 gain without skills, while the v2 HTML body reports 14.68 points; the difference remains unresolved in this review.
  • Demonstrated limitation — The paper distinguishes skill-dependent tasks, defined under a reference-agent contrastive test, from verifier-passed fallback tasks; it reports 1,081 skill-dependent and 1,675 verifier-passed environments. Across sampled tasks, 8,364 successful trajectories are reported at a 17.4% success rate.

Strongest baseline

The reported base-versus-fine-tuned Qwen3.5-35B-A3B comparison is the most directly relevant within-paper comparison; cross-model leaderboard comparisons are not independent matched implementations.

Limitations

  • Reported training and benchmark results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • Benchmark comparisons depend on the model, harness and skill setting; the paper does not establish performance across arbitrary real-world workflows.
  • The paper reports 8,364 successful trajectories with a 17.4% sampled success rate; the trajectory corpus should not be interpreted as all attempted runs or universal task coverage.
  • Skill-dependent labels establish contrastive outcomes under a reference configuration, not that the skill is necessary for every model or harness.
  • The v2 abstract and v2 HTML disagree on the SkillsBench no-skills gain (12.38 versus 14.68 percentage points); unresolved here.
  • Code, dataset and model artifacts were not inspected during this review.

What we still don't know

  • The paper links code, environment/dataset and model artifacts, but those repositories and artifacts were not inspected in this review; task generators, verifier independence, data composition and training implementation remain unaudited.
  • No independent reproduction of the reported fine-tuning gains or cross-model comparisons was located in this bounded review.
  • The arXiv v2 abstract reports a 12.38-point SkillsBench v1.1 gain without skills while its v2 HTML body reports 14.68 points.

Why it matters

If the paper’s results hold, verifier-backed environments derived from human workflows could provide a reusable route from written procedures to agent training data and help test whether procedural competence transfers beyond prompt-time skill use. The paper reports a large resource and substantial benchmark gains, but its evaluation is author-run, results are not independently reproduced here, and a versioned SkillsBench figure is inconsistent.
