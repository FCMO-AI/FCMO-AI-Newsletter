Terminal-Bench audit shows why an all-fail score does not certify task hardness

A September 2026 paper audits the frozen Terminal-Bench 3 / Frontier-Bench 0.1 production record to ask what an all-fail score actually supports. It covers 1,081 pull requests, 639 scored tasks and 28,801 trials. Of 125 tasks with no honest pass, an ordered screen retains 78 as certified-unsolved candidates; the other 47 are broken-oracle, infrastructure-limited, exploit-only-passable or unresolved cases. The paper defines certified-unsolved narrowly: a reference route passed, infrastructure did not dominate, no strict verifier bypass was observed, and all evaluated agents failed in the frozen setup. Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The authors published an item-level validity audit of a frozen Terminal-Bench 3 / Frontier-Bench 0.1 task-production record.
  • Claimed — In the paper's frozen record, 125 tasks had no honest pass. An ordered validity screen retains 78 as certified-unsolved candidates and classifies the remaining 47 as 14 broken-oracle, 8 infrastructure-limited, 4 exploit-only-passable and 21 with uncertified solvability.
  • Claimed — Among passing tasks, minimum output tokens to first archived pass separated the authors' broader genuine-hard candidates from tractable tasks with AUC 0.750 (95% CI 0.700–0.801); this is observational, not a measure of intrinsic difficulty.
  • Demonstrated limitation — Certified-unsolved means one authored route passed, infrastructure did not dominate, no strict bypass was observed, and evaluated agents failed in the frozen setup; it does not prove intrinsic hardness or failure at the intended capability.

Strongest baseline

Raw all-fail pass-rate classification, compared with a multi-evidence ordered validity screen; task difficulty among passing items is compared using first-pass token costs.

Limitations

  • All task-level adjudication counts are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced here.
  • The study uses one frozen benchmark-production corpus and cannot establish how often the same failure categories occur in other agent benchmarks.
  • Only 25 of the 78 certified-unsolved candidates have at least two recorded oracle runs; a passing reference route does not establish verifier completeness or intrinsic hardness.
  • The paper says the labels do not imply full domain-expert re-review and some failure trajectories remain unresolved.
  • The AUC result for first-pass token cost is observational and mixes reasoning, verbosity, tools, retries, harness policy and model assignment.

What we still don't know

  • Independent re-adjudication of a stratified sample of all-fail tasks with repeated reference runs, infrastructure checks and reward-independent verifier testing.
  • Test whether the five-way item-validity screen and observed defect rates transfer to other agentic benchmarks and task-production pipelines.

Why it matters

A zero pass rate can reflect a capability gap, a broken reference solution, infrastructure failure or a verifier defect. This paper demonstrates an item-level reporting method that keeps those interpretations separate, helping prevent invalid benchmark items from being treated as capability evidence. Its scope is one frozen benchmark corpus and the certification label is deliberately weaker than intrinsic hardness.
