Pelican-Sim pairs numeric robot actions with rendered motion cues and reports downstream gains in RoboTwin

A September 10 technical report from the Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics describes Pelican-Sim 1.0, an action-conditioned video world model that combines a 28-dimensional robot-configuration vector with camera-aligned, URDF-rendered arm-motion cues, sparse mixture-of-experts layers and a distilled four-step rollout model. The authors report improved video-prediction metrics over evaluated baselines on AgiBotWorld Beta, RoboMIND and RoboTwin. In RoboTwin randomized tests, augmenting 10, 30 or 50 demonstrations per task with ten generated trajectories per demonstration raised policy success from 28.5% to 64.5%, 57.0% to 87.0%, and 70.0% to 93.0%, respectively, over five tasks and 200 unseen seeds per task. A world-model-plus-VLM evaluator reportedly ranked five policy checkpoints with Pearson correlation 0.994 after 1,000 task-specific adaptation rollouts; the test replayed the same action trajectories in the world model and simulator. The results are author-reported and primarily simulation-bounded. They do not establish physical-robot policy improvement, independent replication, or reliable closed-loop deployment under model-generated observations.

Evidence

  • Claimed — Pelican-Sim 1.0 combines a 28-dimensional action-value representation with URDF- and camera-rendered whole-arm action videos in a sparse-MoE video diffusion model and reports a four-step causal rollout path.
  • Claimed — On three held-out robot-video datasets, the paper reports PSNR improvements over its strongest evaluated baselines of 4.636 dB on AgiBotWorld Beta, 2.080 dB on RoboMIND and 10.343 dB on RoboTwin.
  • Claimed — In RoboTwin randomized evaluation, adding 100, 300 or 500 generated rollouts per task to 10, 30 or 50 original demonstrations is associated with reported policy success changes of 28.5%→64.5%, 57.0%→87.0% and 70.0%→93.0%, respectively, across five tasks and 200 unseen seeds per task.
  • Claimed — A world-model-plus-VLM estimator reportedly reaches Pearson r=0.994 and perfect rank correlation across five policy checkpoints after 1,000 task-specific adaptation rollouts, using matched action trajectories on 200 held-out conditions per checkpoint; the protocol does not test closed-loop policy execution under generated observations.
  • Inferred — The paper's downstream policy gains and ranking measurements do not establish corresponding gains on physical robots, independent replication, or transfer beyond the reported task and simulator regime.

Strongest baseline

For video metrics, the paper compares against the strongest available evaluated world-model baseline per dataset, including Ctrl-World. The data-generation comparison uses randomized RoboTwin trajectories and policies trained with identical configurations. The action-selection comparison includes single-sample, random-of-10, VLM-direct and Ctrl-World selection under matched candidate pools and a shared VLM evaluator.

Limitations

  • All headline metrics in this record are reported by the paper authors; the research desk did not run the model or independently reproduce them.
  • The strongest synthetic-data policy outcomes are evaluated in RoboTwin randomized simulation, not on physical robot tasks.
  • The policy-ranking experiment replays the same policy action trajectories in the world model and simulator; it does not test closed-loop policy execution under generated observations.
  • Policy ranking uses only five checkpoints from a training run and 200 held-out initial conditions per checkpoint; the reported Pearson correlation does not establish broader calibration or deployment reliability.
  • The action-selection and policy-improvement pipelines depend on a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct evaluator, so world-model and evaluator errors can affect the apparent utility.
  • The generation-time comparison excludes training and upstream preparation, so it does not establish full lifecycle economics or net dollar savings.
  • The reviewed v1 report describes code and checkpoint release as future work; no immutable artifact or independent reproduction was inspected.

What we still don't know

  • The reported video, policy-augmentation and policy-ranking measurements have not been independently reproduced; code/checkpoint availability and immutable artifact identity were not checked in this activation.
  • The downstream utility claims are concentrated in RoboTwin and do not establish policy gains on physical robots or under closed-loop execution from generated observations. The ranking experiment replays fixed action trajectories.
  • The world model's downstream pipeline relies on a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct evaluator for success estimates, candidate ranking and rewards; the reported effects may depend on evaluator calibration and validity filtering.

Why it matters

If the reported effects persist beyond RoboTwin, an action-conditioned video simulator could make robot data collection and early policy evaluation cheaper by using generated trajectories and predicted outcomes before executing every candidate physically. This paper is notable because it evaluates one model across data augmentation, action selection, policy evaluation and simulated policy improvement, while exposing the added dependency on a separately fine-tuned VLM evaluator. The paper does not yet show that the reported gains transfer to physical robot outcomes or remain reliable when policies act on generated observations.
