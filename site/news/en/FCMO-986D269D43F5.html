What actually changed

A paper presents SonoCorpus, a collection of 53 public ultrasound datasets, and SonoBase, an interactive segmentation model adapted from SAM2 with a multi-resolution encoder. The authors report comparative results across eight held-out benchmark datasets and seven fully external datasets, plus selected clinical measurements derived from segmentations. The evaluation is retrospective; the paper calls for prospective validation and does not establish clinical deployment safety.

Claim vs. evidence

  • CLAIMED — The authors introduce SonoCorpus, a resource of 53 public ultrasound datasets with 456,963 images/frames and 1,626,085 masks, and SonoBase, an interactive SAM2-derived segmentation model with a multi-resolution encoder.
  • CLAIMED — Across eight held-out benchmark datasets, authors report 74.4 point-prompt and 78.4 box-prompt mean IoU; across seven fully external datasets they report 64.5 and 79.8, respectively. These are retrospective author-run evaluations.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports selected derived measurement errors within stated inter-observer variability, including ejection-fraction error of 6.63%, fetal head-circumference error of 1.81 mm and gestational-age error of 1.2 days. This does not establish prospective clinical safety or outcomes.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Authors publicly released checkpoints, code, split indices, deduplication hashes and dataset manifests; this activation inspected linked documentation but did not execute or verify results.

Strongest baseline

SAM2, MedSAM2, task-specific specialists and separate MedSAM3 comparisons. Results vary by prompt and evaluation regime; one external abdominal-circumference endpoint favors a baseline before calibration.

The caveat that matters

  • No independent reproduction or external audit was conducted in this activation.
  • All evaluations are retrospective; application-specific prospective validation is needed. SonoBase is described as a research tool, not a medical device.
  • Seven external datasets do not cover every scanner, population, application or acquisition protocol.
  • Demographic metadata are absent from nearly all source datasets, limiting subgroup analysis.
  • A zero-shot box-prompt abdominal-circumference endpoint on ACOUSLIC favors a baseline; the authors report calibration/few-shot adaptation changes this comparison, which needs validation.
  • Dataset licenses are heterogeneous; some restrict non-commercial use, and model weights have a non-commercial restriction.

What remains unknown

  • No independent team reproduced held-out or external results.
  • The 53 constituent licenses, deduplication manifest and all archived per-sample outputs were not audited in this activation.
  • Retrospective benchmarks do not establish prospective safety, fairness or performance at new sites.

FCMO Lens

Ultrasound models can lose accuracy when scanner, operator, anatomy or acquisition conditions change. The paper tests whether ultrasound-specific pretraining over a diverse public corpus improves interactive segmentation across such shifts, including handheld devices and lower-resource settings.
