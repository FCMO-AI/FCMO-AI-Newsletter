Evidence

  • Demonstrated — The UK Pensions Regulator published guidance on 22 October 2026 asking pension trustees to assess direct and indirect AI-related exposure across their total portfolios, use scenario analysis for severe but plausible downturns, and examine whether diversification remains effective if correlations change.
  • Claimed — The guidance says AI-linked companies and financing are changing global market composition and may create less-visible common exposure across technology, infrastructure, energy, real estate and financial assets.
  • Not established — The guidance does not establish measured AI-related exposures in pension portfolios, losses, market mispricing, a bubble, trustee adoption or realized investment outcomes.

Strongest baseline

Existing portfolio and stress-testing practices are discussed as the comparison point, but the guidance does not quantify a prior baseline or report changes after publication.

Limitations

  • This is official guidance about governance and potential risks, not evidence that a market correction or AI-related losses have occurred.
  • The source provides no measured direct or indirect AI exposure for pension schemes and no trustee-adoption or implementation study.
  • The guidance does not quantify how much AI-related concentration changes portfolio correlations or expected returns.

What we still don't know

  • Trustee uptake and any resulting changes to exposure measurement or portfolio decisions have not been established.
  • The guidance gives no measured direct or indirect AI exposure across pension portfolios.

Why it matters

AI-related financing and infrastructure investment can create shared exposures across assets that appear diversified. The guidance makes portfolio-level AI exposure and stress analysis an explicit trustee concern, while leaving actual exposure and implementation effects unmeasured.
