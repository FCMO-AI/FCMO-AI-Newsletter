FRESH reports higher tool-task completion with failure-aware graph memory

A September 2026 preprint proposes FRESH, which stores successful actions, failures, preconditions and repairs in a typed experience graph, then retrieves compact guidance for a frozen language-model executor. Evidence

  • Claimed — On tau-bench, the authors report FRESH has the highest average pass performance among compared memory methods for Gemma-4-26B-A4B, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B, with average relative gains over each no-memory baseline of 10.8%, 38.9% and 53.2%, respectively.
  • Claimed — On AppWorld, the authors report task pass rates of 0.268 on Test-N and 0.146 on Test-C for FRESH, versus 0.250 and 0.115 for the strongest listed baseline. FRESH also reports 9.4 vs 9.9 average steps on Test-N and 10.0 vs 10.4 on Test-C.

Strongest baseline

The no-memory agent is the reference for reported relative gains; AppWorld Test-N/Test-C comparisons use the strongest listed baseline task pass rates of 0.250 and 0.115, respectively.

Limitations

  • All results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this activation.
  • The AppWorld pass rates remain low in absolute terms: 0.268 on Test-N and 0.146 on Test-C, compared with strongest listed baseline rates of 0.250 and 0.115.
  • The reported gains apply to tau-bench and AppWorld benchmark splits with the specified historical trajectories and execution budgets; real-world tool environments are not established.
  • The paper describes ablations, but the inspected tables do not provide uncertainty intervals for the headline gains.
  • The action gate's safety and policy compliance were not independently audited; reported task completion is not proof of safe deployment.

What we still don't know

  • The reported tau-bench/AppWorld results and failure-aware graph pipeline have not been independently run or audited.
  • Benchmark gains do not establish transfer to live APIs, changing policies, or high-consequence state-changing actions.

Why it matters

Tool-using agents can repeat failed calls, miss action preconditions or perform unsafe state changes. FRESH combines structured memory of both successes and failures with a gate for state-changing actions, and reports gains across several small and medium backbones on two tool-use benchmarks. The reported pass rates and relative gains are confined to those benchmark splits; they do not establish reliable real-world operation or the safety of the gate.
