NVIDIA reports Kubernetes validation on two early Vera Rubin NVL72 clusters

NVIDIA describes bringing an NVIDIA AI Cluster Runtime (AICR) Preview recipe online on two early Vera Rubin NVL72 reference clusters. Its public account says the training recipe passed deployment and conformance checks and a cross-node 16 GiB NCCL all-reduce gate at 744.71 GB/s bus bandwidth. The inference variant passed deployment and conformance only; NVIDIA explicitly says inference-serving performance remains deferred. These are company-reported results for a Preview configuration, not independent reproduction or production qualification.

Evidence

  • Demonstrated — NVIDIA published a public Preview recipe family for RKE2 deployments on Vera Rubin NVL72, with separate training and inference variants.
  • Claimed — NVIDIA reports that its training recipe passed deployment and conformance checks on live Vera Rubin reference hardware and passed a cross-node 16 GiB NCCL all-reduce gate at 744.71 GB/s bus bandwidth over multi-node NVLink.
  • Claimed — NVIDIA says the initial bring-up of a previously unvalidated combination of arm64, Ubuntu 26.04, a 64k-page kernel, RKE2 and Vera Rubin was reproduced on a second cluster the following day.
  • Not established — The inspected evidence does not establish independent reproduction, production-supported status, broad hardware availability, RDMA data-path performance or inference-serving performance on Vera Rubin.

Strongest baseline

No matched GB200 or independent platform comparison was reported. The demonstration concerns successful integration on the specified early Vera Rubin configuration.

Limitations

  • All validation outcomes and the second-cluster repeat are reported by NVIDIA; independent replication was not located in this bounded check.
  • The RKE2/VR200 recipe is Preview, not a production-supported or lifecycle-qualified configuration.
  • The 744.71 GB/s result is one cross-node NCCL all-reduce gate on the specified reference configuration, not a general model-training throughput or inference-speed result.
  • The inference variant passed deployment and conformance checks only; inference-serving performance is explicitly deferred.
  • RDMA data-path performance over ConnectX-9 and MIG behavior were not validated.

What we still don't know

  • NVIDIA's linked signed validation artifacts and captured recipe coordinates were not inspectable in detail here, and independent operator reproduction was not located.

Why it matters

A versioned recipe that captures compatible drivers, kernels, operators and scheduler settings can lower the integration cost of bringing up new accelerator clusters. The reported result is limited to NVIDIA's preview configuration and does not establish production readiness or inference performance.
