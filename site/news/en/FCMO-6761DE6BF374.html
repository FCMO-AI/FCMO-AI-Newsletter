SCOPE reports joint capability and safety post-training for computer-use agents

An arXiv preprint presents SCOPE, a post-training recipe for computer-use agents that combines capability demonstrations, safe continuations around environmental hazards, explicit refusals and subsequent capability RL. On Qwen3.5-9B, the authors report 54.17% OSWorld task success and 64.30% OS-BLIND attack avoidance after RL, with a 58.80% harmonic mean in their benchmark setting. Matched-step ablations suggest refusal trajectories contribute most of the measured attack-avoidance gain, while safe-continuation trajectories better preserve task utility. These are author-reported results on one model and benchmark suite; OS-BLIND does not distinguish safe continuation from refusal or incidental task failure, and the aggregate is not a deployment safety guarantee.

Evidence

  • Claimed — The authors report SCOPE joint post-training for Qwen3.5-9B using capability demonstrations, safe continuations and explicit refusals, followed by capability-oriented RL. SCOPE-RL reached 54.17% OSWorld task success and 64.30% OS-BLIND attack avoidance, with a 58.80% harmonic mean in the paper’s evaluation.
  • Claimed — In matched-step SFT ablations, removing 81 explicit-refusal trajectories reduced OS-BLIND attack avoidance from 66.30% to 13.00%; replacing safe-continuation trajectories with additional refusals held attack avoidance at 66.70% but reduced OSWorld task success from 49.72% to 43.21%. The authors interpret refusal and risk-handling trajectories as serving different functions.
  • Not established — OS-BLIND attack avoidance does not distinguish safe continuation from refusal or incidental task failure; the paper states its harmonic-mean aggregate is benchmark-specific and not a deployment safety guarantee.

Strongest baseline

The Qwen3.5-9B base, capability-only SFT and matched-step safety-trajectory ablations in the paper.

Limitations

  • All reported model outcomes are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • The paper evaluates Qwen3.5-9B on OSWorld and OS-BLIND; transfer to other model families, applications and real user environments is unknown.
  • OS-BLIND attack avoidance does not distinguish safe continuation from refusal or incidental task failure.
  • The harmonic-mean capability–safety score is specific to the chosen benchmarks and is not a complete deployment safety guarantee.
  • Public linked artifacts were not inspected for availability, completeness or reproducibility.

What we still don't know

  • No independent reproduction or inspection of the linked data/model artifacts was performed in this review.
  • OS-BLIND attack avoidance can count refusal or incidental failure as avoidance and does not by itself establish safe continuation; the aggregate score is not a deployment safety measure.
  • The reported training and benchmarks use Qwen3.5-9B and a bounded desktop GUI environment; generalization to other model families, applications and real user workflows is unknown.

Why it matters

If reproduced, this work offers a concrete way to teach GUI agents both to avoid hazards while continuing benign work and to refuse harmful goals, then recover task execution through RL. The benchmark measures do not establish safe deployment: OS-BLIND avoidance conflates refusal and failure with safe continuation, and results cover one model family.
