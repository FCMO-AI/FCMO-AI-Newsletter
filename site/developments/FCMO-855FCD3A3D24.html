Groq 3 LPX reaches full-production milestone with ~3.4k tok/s third-party long-context decode measurement

Evidence Astrongly_supportedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

hardware_or_infrastructure · development FCMO-855FCD3A3D24 · verified 2026-08-30T04:02:52Z

Importance: 6/10 — Major. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
Major if the central measured regime generalizes: sustaining several thousand output tokens per second per user at long context would materially change inference architecture, agent latency budgets and the attractiveness of specialized decode racks. It is not scored higher because the demonstrated scope is one 31B model and single-stream decode rather than a broad replacement of general accelerators or a demonstrated shift in total serving economics.

NVIDIA announced Groq 3 LPX in full production and disclosed the first third-party benchmark of the rack-scale LPU system. Artificial Analysis ran Gemma 4 31B on an NVIDIA-hosted private LPX endpoint and measured median output speed of 3,431 OpenAI-standardized tokens/s at 100K input context and 3,382 tokens/s at 10K. NVIDIA positions LPX as the latency-focused complement to Vera Rubin NVL72 for decode-heavy agentic inference, with deterministic compiler-scheduled communication and SRAM-heavy rack-scale execution.

Why it matters: If the measured long-context interactivity transfers to production workloads, specialized decode hardware can remove a major serial latency bottleneck in multi-step agents and makes heterogeneous prefill/decode serving materially more attractive. The evidence does not yet establish public-service economics, energy efficiency, multi-tenant throughput, tail latency or end-to-end agent-task acceleration.

Technical record

Mechanism
Groq 3 LPX uses a rack of 256 LP30 language-processing accelerators with 128 GB aggregate on-chip SRAM. NVIDIA describes a deterministic execution model in which compiler-scheduled compute and chip-to-chip transfers avoid runtime arbitration, with fine-grained compute/communication overlap. LPX can be paired with Vera Rubin NVL72 through prefill-decode disaggregation, attention-FFN disaggregation and external-drafter speculative decoding configurations.
Demonstrated result
Artificial Analysis benchmarked an NVIDIA-hosted private Groq 3 LPX system using Gemma 4 31B and its standardized serving methodology. NVIDIA's technical report reproduces the third-party result: median 3,431 output tokens/s at 100K input context and 3,382 output tokens/s at 10K. Artificial Analysis defines output speed as tokens received per second after the first token, using o200k_base OpenAI-token normalization. The benchmark therefore establishes very high post-first-token single-stream generation speed in this tested regime, not end-to-end response time.
Publisher claim
NVIDIA states Groq 3 LPX is in full production, calls the 100K result roughly 4x the fastest public Gemma 4 endpoint in the comparison, and says Nebius is the first cloud adopter. NVIDIA also reports higher SPEED-Bench numbers and projects large throughput-per-megawatt/economic benefits for heterogeneous Vera Rubin plus LPX configurations; those latter results remain vendor-run or modeled rather than independently validated here.
Strongest baseline
Artificial Analysis's public Gemma 4 31B serving endpoints under the same 10K/100K context methodology provide the strongest directly comparable speed baseline; the NVIDIA technical post shows 870 output tokens/s as the fastest public endpoint at 100K and 1,402 at 10K at the time of testing.
Regime
Gemma 4 31B dense reasoning model on a private NVIDIA-hosted Groq 3 LPX endpoint; 10K and 100K input-context serving tests; sequential single-concurrency requests; output-speed metric excludes time to first token. Hardware is announced as production silicon/racks, while broad public cloud access and production multi-tenant behavior are not yet established.
Implementation
NVIDIA says LPX is in full production and expects volume shipments to early adopters later in the quarter; Nebius is named as first cloud adopter. No generally accessible public LPX endpoint was verified in this activation.
Compute / cost
NVIDIA documents 256 LPUs per rack, 128 GB aggregate SRAM, 12 TB DDR5, 40 PB/s aggregate SRAM bandwidth and 640 TB/s rack scale-up bandwidth. Public price, measured rack power under the Artificial Analysis test, cost per million tokens and matched tokens-per-watt were not established.
Novelty
The salient systems direction is heterogeneous inference: use general accelerators for high-throughput/context-heavy phases and deterministic SRAM-heavy LPU hardware for latency-sensitive decode rather than forcing one accelerator architecture to optimize both regimes.
Reproducibility
The measurement comes from a recognized third-party benchmark operator, but the endpoint was private and NVIDIA-hosted. Independent access to the same hardware, load-testing artifacts and a public endpoint are still missing, so the exact result cannot yet be reproduced by arbitrary researchers.

Evidence claims

  • DEMONSTRATED — Artificial Analysis measured median 3,431 output tokens/s at 100K context and 3,382 output tokens/s at 10K on Gemma 4 31B served by an NVIDIA-hosted Groq 3 LPX system.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The measured output-speed figure describes generation after the first token rather than full end-to-end response latency.
  • CLAIMED — NVIDIA states Groq 3 LPX is in full production and that Nebius is the first cloud adopter, with volume shipments to early adopters expected later in the quarter.
  • CLAIMED — NVIDIA documents a 256-LPU rack with 128 GB aggregate SRAM and deterministic compiler-scheduled execution/communication as core ingredients of LPX's low-latency design.
  • INFERRED — The current evidence is sufficient to establish unusually high single-stream decode speed in the tested regime, but not superior cost, energy efficiency, multi-tenant throughput or complete agent-task wall time.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • Artificial Analysis tested a private endpoint hosted in NVIDIA's data center rather than a generally accessible production service.
  • The disclosed benchmark used sequential single-concurrency requests; multi-tenant throughput, P95/P99 latency and scheduler behavior under load are not established.
  • Output speed is measured after the first token; the headline 3.4k tok/s number is not time-to-first-token or complete end-to-end latency.
  • The directly benchmarked regime is one 31B dense model; transfer to very large MoE models, VLMs and mixed workloads remains unverified.
  • No matched public measurement of rack power, tokens-per-watt, price, cost per million tokens or total cost of ownership was found.
  • NVIDIA's SPEED-Bench results and large throughput-per-megawatt/economic projections are vendor-run or modeled and should not be conflated with Artificial Analysis's third-party speed measurement.
  • NVIDIA says full production, but its earnings call places volume shipments to early adopters later in the quarter; broad public-cloud availability was not verified.
  • No end-to-end agent benchmark demonstrates that the raw decode-speed ratio translates proportionally into task-completion wall time once tool calls, prefill, network latency and external systems are included.

Open evidence gaps

  • open · reproduction_missing — The Artificial Analysis result cannot yet be independently rerun on a public or separately controlled LPX endpoint. — next: Repeat the 10K/100K Gemma 4 benchmark on a public Nebius or other independently accessible LPX service with pinned model and precision.
  • open · other — Multi-tenant throughput, TTFT, P95/P99 latency and scheduler behavior under concurrent load are unknown. — next: Run a stepped-concurrency load test with fixed input/output distributions and report TTFT, output speed, total response time and tail latency.
  • open · other — Energy and economic efficiency are not independently measured in the demonstrated regime. — next: Measure rack power and service pricing under matched workload to derive output tokens/J, requests/W, and cost per million output tokens versus the same model on leading GPU endpoints.
  • open · other — Raw decode speed has not been translated into complete agent-task wall time. — next: Run matched coding/agent trajectories with identical model behavior and tool traces while varying only serving backend; report fraction of wall time attributable to decode and total task acceleration.
