LpWM uses sparse non-negative JEPA latents to reduce world-model predictor complexity

Evidence AsupportedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

paper · development FCMO-47EB61AB3914 · verified 2026-08-29T23:58:00Z

Importance: 5/10 — Notable. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
Notable if the central claim generalizes: changing latent geometry so simpler predictors can model useful control dynamics would affect world-model design and embodied/local-AI efficiency. It is not major or field-shifting yet because evidence is concentrated in small control environments, the advantage shrinks with sufficiently expressive predictors, and there is no independent reproduction or large-scale real-world validation in ARB.

LpWM changes the geometry of JEPA world-model latents rather than merely scaling the predictor. It uses Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization to match encoder features to sparse non-negative Rectified Generalized Gaussian targets. The authors report that on PushT, sparse LpWM requires less predictor capacity than dense LeWM for comparable or better planning, with gains up to 57% at intermediate-capacity predictors; at the highest-capacity AdaLN predictors the gap largely closes. The learned support also tracks discrete dynamical regimes while non-zero magnitudes encode continuous within-regime state.

Why it matters: If the effect generalizes, representation geometry becomes a direct systems lever for world models: make dynamics easier before spending more parameters on the predictor. That could lower control-model complexity and improve interpretability, especially for local or embodied systems where predictor FLOPs matter.

Technical record

Mechanism
An action-conditioned JEPA encoder is regularized with Rectified Distribution Matching Regularization (RDMReg), matching per-timestep features to Rectified Generalized Gaussian targets. With p<=1 and a RepReLU link, the representation becomes sparse and non-negative. The theory motivates this by showing nonlinear Lipschitz dynamics can be approximated arbitrarily well by action-conditioned linear dynamics in sufficiently high-dimensional one-hot latent spaces; distributed sparse codes are used as a practical relaxation.
Demonstrated result
On PushT, the paper reports sparse LpWM beating dense LeWM by 24-57% in planning success for MLP∘LTI predictors, 36-45% for MLP∘LTV predictors, and 11-23% for LTI predictors, while Deep-/Shallow-AdaLN largely close the gap. The sparse representation also beats a dense VICReg variant across several predictor families and exhibits mode-factored structure in controlled environments.
Publisher claim
The authors argue that sparse representations lower the predictor complexity required for successful planning and reveal interpretable dynamical structure.
Strongest baseline
Dense LeWM using the same RDMReg framework matched to isotropic Gaussian targets, plus a dense VICReg representation and a predictor-capacity ladder spanning LTI/LTV/MLP variants through Deep-/Shallow-AdaLN.
Regime
Small-to-medium visual control environments including Wall and PushT for predictor-capacity sweeps, plus Piecewise and OGBench-Cube for mode-factoring analysis; official code uses ViT encoders, CEM/MPC planning, and reproduction scripts for the reported grids.
Implementation
Official MIT-licensed code repository is public with environment setup, training commands, planning entry points and reproduction-grid scripts. ARB has not run an independent reproduction.
Compute / cost
The paper's practical claim concerns reducing predictor complexity rather than reporting a full end-to-end wall-clock or energy advantage. ARB has not yet normalized encoder, predictor and planner FLOPs/latency under matched latent dimensions and planning budgets.
Novelty
Treats sparsity of the latent state as a first-class design choice for making action-conditioned dynamics simpler, rather than using sparsity only for parameter or activation compression.
Reproducibility
Official code exposes train.py, plan.py, predictor configurations, datasets and scripts/reproduce_pusht.sh; this materially lowers reproduction cost, but no independent run is yet canonical.

Evidence claims

  • DEMONSTRATED — Sparse LpWM reduces the predictor capacity needed for strong PushT planning relative to dense LeWM in the reported predictor ladder.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Sparse latent support encodes discrete dynamical regimes while non-zero magnitudes encode continuous state within regimes in the reported controlled analyses.
  • SPECULATIVE — Sparse non-negative latent geometry is generally preferable to dense Gaussian geometry for large-scale world models.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • The strongest gains occur at intermediate predictor capacity and largely disappear with sufficiently expressive AdaLN predictors.
  • Wall saturates near ceiling even with simple predictors, so sparsity does not help in every environment.
  • The reported planning tasks are far smaller than open-world robotics or general video world modeling.
  • No independent reproduction is recorded by ARB despite released code.
  • End-to-end FLOPs, latency, memory and energy are not yet normalized under matched latent dimension and planning budgets.
  • At the highest-capacity Deep-/Shallow-AdaLN predictors, dense and sparse representations become much closer, limiting the claim to predictor-complexity efficiency rather than universal planning superiority.
  • On the simple Wall environment both dense and sparse systems can approach ceiling with very small predictors, showing the representation advantage is task-regime dependent.

Open evidence gaps

  • open · reproduction_missing — ARB has not independently reproduced the predictor-capacity sweep. — next: Run the released PushT reproduction grid for one sparse and one matched dense arm at three predictor capacities.
  • open · measurement_missing — Lower predictor complexity is not yet converted into matched end-to-end FLOPs, latency, memory or energy savings. — next: Profile encoder+predictor+planner under matched latent dimensions and planning budgets.
  • open · external_validity — Evidence does not establish that sparse latent advantages survive large video world models or diverse real robotics. — next: Test sparse-vs-dense latent geometry on a larger stochastic or multi-embodiment benchmark with matched predictor capacity and data.
