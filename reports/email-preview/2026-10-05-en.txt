01. Brazil ties data-center tax relief to clean power, local capacity and R&D commitments
Brazil's Lei 15.504/2026 creates Redata, a five-year tax-incentive regime for qualifying data-center installation, modernization and expansion, including AI training and inference. Applicants must make at least 10% of the effective processing, storage and data-treatment supply installed with Redata benefits available to Brazil's domestic market; the law calculates that commitment using domestic gross revenue as a share of total gross revenue from the benefited services, with implementation details left to regulation. Applicants must also meet renewable or low-emission electricity and sustainability requirements, keep cooling WUE at or below 0.05 L/kWh, and invest 2% of incentivized ICT-product purchases in local digital-economy R&D. The law suspends specified taxes on eligible ICT equipment; PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions currently run through 2026-12-31 under the tax-transition clause.
Why it matters: The regime trades conditional import and equipment-tax relief for domestic compute availability, clean-energy sourcing, water efficiency and local innovation spending. These terms can affect data-center location and project economics, but actual tax expenditure, approvals, construction and compliance outcomes remain unmeasured.

02. An exact site-frequency spectrum for selected alleles extends error-controlled calculations to biobank-scale samples
A preliminary manuscript by Matthew D. Schwartz and Michael M. Desai presents a rational-arithmetic method for evaluating the Sawyer–Hartl Poisson random field site-frequency spectrum. The authors report exact results across classical sample sizes and controlled-error evaluation at biobank scale, compare three common inference packages against the result, and fit the model to gnomAD v4.1 exomes. The reported biological inference remains conditional on the paper’s equilibrium/panmictic modeling and synonymous-calibration assumptions.
Why it matters: Site-frequency-spectrum methods support inference about how natural selection shapes genetic variation. If the reported numerical method and error controls hold, researchers can test likelihood accuracy in sample-size and selection regimes where floating-point implementations are difficult to audit, including large exome cohorts.

03. OpenAI lists GPT-Rosalind research API rates and schedules billing from October 5
OpenAI’s API pricing page lists gpt-rosalind-research at $5 per 1 million input tokens, $0.50 per 1 million cached input tokens and $25 per 1 million output tokens; the page states billing begins October 5, 2026. The API changelog dates the trusted-access release and these standard rates to September 8. This makes a model-specific marginal API price visible for approved internal life-sciences research, but the sources reviewed establish the posted schedule rather than an observed customer charge or a cost per validated scientific result.
Why it matters: A posted model-specific rate is a concrete input to whether qualified research teams can operationalize specialized scientific-model workflows. The listed output rate is five times the uncached input rate and fifty times the cached-input rate, so actual workflow economics depend on token mix, caching, tools and supervision. No benchmark-to-cost or scientific productivity conclusion follows from the rate alone.
