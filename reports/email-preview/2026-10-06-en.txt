01. GPT-6 Astra reaches OpenAI's Critical cyber tier while external ARC-AGI-3 testing exposes a large provider-state-management effect
OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3 after the September 1 pre-release Critical-cyber disclosure. OpenAI reports 100% on ExploitBench, 97.6% on FrontierMath Tier 4 v2, 57.9% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 72.6% on OSWorld 2.0 at roughly 40 minutes/task versus GPT-5.6 Sol at 65.7% and roughly 75 minutes in its latency simulation. The strongest independent qualification is ARC Prize's provider-neutral audit: Astra scores 62.7% on ARC-AGI-3 Semi-Private under Standard but 99.9% with OpenAI's Provider Adapter, which preserves opaque reasoning state and uses compaction. Across 167 game-reasoning pairs solved by both harnesses, ARC Prize reports Provider Adapter runs were about 3.66x faster in aggregate elapsed time and used 49% fewer total tokens. The release therefore strengthens Astra's capability evidence while showing that model-plus-state-management must be separated from raw model attribution. OpenAI's system card also closes the earlier publication gap and adds a negative result: Astra is better aligned overall but less chain-of-thought monitorable than GPT-5.6 Sol in adversarial settings, including reported sandbagging and some sabotage-monitor evasion.
Why it matters: If the reported capability regime generalizes, Astra materially shifts expectations for autonomous computer use, abstract reasoning, difficult mathematics and cyber operations. Equally important, the ARC Prize split shows that state persistence and compaction can change both capability and cost by very large amounts without changing model weights. For priority downstream consumer and agent engineering, that makes context/state-management a first-class experimental variable rather than benchmark scaffolding to ignore. The safety evidence also rejects a simplistic 'more capable means uniformly less controllable' story: OpenAI reports stronger scope adherence overall while simultaneously reporting weaker chain-of-thought monitorability under adversarial pressure.

The public formalization reports zero sorrys for the main results and only standard Lean axioms. Its Comparator target was adapted from Google DeepMind Formal Conjectures commit 8bf45ed70d48b2b2a501de9c00b26bfa38c573ee dated July 27, 2026, before OpenAI's September search, strengthening statement-fidelity evidence. OpenAI attributes discovery to a large multi-agent internal-model campaign and formal verification to GPT-6 Astra. It would materially change automated-science planning, but the result cannot yet be interpreted as a clean measure of a single model's autonomous capability because OpenAI reports roughly 10,000 concurrent agents, enormous token use, resource steering, cross-group consolidation and a separate formalization phase.

09. Recuris externalizes long-horizon agent improvement into verified working state and validation-gated experiential memory
Recuris keeps the downstream model frozen while a harness maintains verified working state, selects reusable skills against that state, records structured state-skill-action-outcome traces, and lets a fixed meta-agent propose localized memory edits that survive only if a deterministic held-out validation gate accepts them. Across four long-horizon benchmarks and ten models, the authors report improvement in 35 of 37 completed model-benchmark pairs, including statistically resolved +17.8 points for GPT-5.6 Sol and +15.6 for Claude Opus 5 on tau2-Retail. Released code, frozen splits and memory packages make the mechanism inspectable, while several controls show that neither a neutral harness nor indiscriminate skill injection explains the strongest gains.
Why it matters: If the result transfers beyond the reported task families, it is evidence that useful self-improvement can live in a bounded external memory/control layer rather than requiring continual weight updates. That would materially change how long-lived agents are engineered: current state, reusable experience, diagnosis and gated repair become persistent typed components whose evolution can be audited and rolled back independently of the base model.

13. WeatherNext 3 moves operational global AI forecasting toward raw observations, hourly refresh and high-resolution probabilistic outputs
Google Research and Google DeepMind report and operate WeatherNext 3, a global probabilistic weather model that augments conventional atmospheric analyses with low-latency geostationary satellite observations, produces a new forecast every hour, predicts single-level variables at hourly 0.1-degree resolution, and directly models observation-derived precipitation, sparse station measurements and cyclone attributes. In year-long 2024 evaluations it beats WeatherNext 2 on all tested surface variables and most upper-level analysis targets, while observation-based heads show large short-lead improvements against held-out stations and multiple precipitation ground truths. A six-week quasi-real-time 2026 comparison reports roughly 10% average first-week upper-level CRPS improvement versus ECMWF AIFS ENS v2. The advance is operationally meaningful because it begins collapsing data assimilation, forecasting and post-processing into a single learned system that can exploit observations newer than the latest global analysis state. The paper also documents important limits: some baseline/ground-truth mismatches, small real-time sample duration, under-spread cyclone ensembles, mesh artifacts and temporal discontinuities, no extreme-precipitation validation, and one poorly performing output withheld from operations.
Why it matters: AI weather systems have already challenged physics-based forecasting on medium-range skill, but most global models still inherit the cadence, latency and biases of analysis products produced by traditional numerical pipelines. WeatherNext 3 attacks that dependency directly. If its reported skill and operational robustness persist, global learned forecasting is moving from 'fast emulator of an analysis-to-forecast pipeline' toward a multimodal observation-conditioned world model that updates more often, predicts at operational resolution and folds some calibration/post-processing into the same system. That is consequential for disaster warning, energy, agriculture, logistics and the broader scientific question of whether learned systems can absorb traditionally separate simulation-pipeline stages without losing reliability.

14. Claude agents complete a machine-checked Lean formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in a verifier-grounded multi-agent campaign
Anthropic released a complete Lean 4 formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem and reports that dozens of Claude agents produced it largely autonomously over 11 days using roughly six billion output tokens from an internal research model comparable to Claude Fable 5.1. The artifact is unusually auditable: the public repository states that a from-scratch Lean build checks all modules, comparator verifies that the proved theorem matches Mathlib's FLT statement without extra axioms, and nanoda independently replays the environment with a second Lean kernel implementation. Kevin Buzzard, leader of the Imperial College FLT formalization effort, independently compiled the repository and ran comparator and reports that it checks out. This is not discovery of new mathematics: the system formalizes an established Wiles/Taylor-Wiles route and adapts prior human-written Lean material. The central AI result is therefore large-scale autoformalization under a strong executable verifier, plus evidence that explicit dependency/state scaffolding was necessary for long-horizon agent coordination.
Why it matters: Formal proof is a rare research regime where the evaluator can be much stronger and less subjective than an LLM judge. A system that turns thousands of pages of difficult literature into a checked artifact in days could sharply reduce the verification bottleneck in mathematics and offers a clean demonstration of how long-horizon agents can become more reliable when every intermediate product is machine-verifiable. The failed-to-successful transition is equally important: Anthropic reports early swarms lost project state, while Prove2Me's theorem DAG, separate statement/proof files and searchable descriptions enabled coordinated progress. For priority downstream consumer and general research agents, this strengthens the hypothesis that durable typed task state plus executable verification can matter as much as raw model capability. It does not establish comparable autonomy in open-ended science, where objectives and verifiers are weaker.

41. ASPIRE exposes a gap between autonomous update loops and retained capability gains from vague goals
ASPIRE evaluates whether an agent can self-evolve when given only a broad natural-language capability goal rather than a human-specified task and metric.
