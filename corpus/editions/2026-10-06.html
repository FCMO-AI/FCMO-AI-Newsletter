Policy & Geopolitics

China's cybersecurity standards committee publishes AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0 with an agent lifecycle risk and control model

Evidence AconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

On 2026-09-14, China’s National Technical Committee 260 on Cybersecurity (TC260), under CAC guidance, published the Chinese-language AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0. Its 136-page document classifies risks and recommends mitigations across AI-agent design, installation/deployment, instruction input, reasoning/planning, tool execution, memory, output and shutdown. It identifies prompt injection, context overflow, tool poisoning and overreach, memory contamination/theft, behavior escape, and residual credentials/processes after shutdown. Recommended controls include pre-deployment risk assessment, staged increases in access and autonomy, unique agent identity and least privilege, revocable credentials, human approval and ability to interrupt high-risk operations, fail-closed approval handling, runtime guardrails, and memory isolation. Comparison with the official 2025 Framework 2.0 shows that 2.0 already discussed autonomous agent planning and privilege/file-access risks, but did not have a dedicated agentic-AI lifecycle appendix. Version 3.0 adds that appendix and separately maps agent identity/permission, planning, tool-execution, memory, embodied and multi-agent risks. This documents a change in framework scope; neither publication establishes binding law, broad adoption or control effectiveness.
