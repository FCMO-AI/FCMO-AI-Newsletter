Evaluation & Science

OpenAI manuscript claims the irrationality exponent of π is exactly 2

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 8/10 · Field-shiftingdeveloping

OpenAI’s 23-page manuscript claims that for every ν>2 there is a threshold Q(ν) such that |π−p/q|≥q^-ν for all integers p and q≥Q(ν), and concludes that π has irrationality exponent 2. If correct, this would sharply improve the prior upper bounds discussed in the paper. The threshold is ineffective; the manuscript does not prove a uniform c/q² lower bound or bounded continued-fraction partial quotients. OpenAI’s public family-017 note describes a Lean statement covering the exponent claim, but no independent build, semantic audit or number-theory review was performed here. Treat the theorem as claimed, not established.

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Evaluation & Science

SpecGuard uses Lean certificates to detect task–test conflicts before coding agents run

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Biyani and Dvijotham introduce SpecGuard, a pre-execution verifier for coding tasks whose natural-language intent may conflict with their tests. Separate agents formalize the task description/codebase and the test requirements, and a Lean kernel checks whether the generated representations can both be satisfied; the result may be conflict, no-conflict or inconclusive. In the authors’ mutated SWE-bench evaluation, GPT-5.6 Sol formally certified 51.1% of conflicts, while its conflict-miss rate was 8.1% versus 39.8% for an LLM-judge baseline. On 22 selected naturally occurring GitHub conflicts, the CLI returned nine conflict verdicts, eight with Lean certificates. These are author-reported preprint results. The kernel proves inconsistency between generated formal representations; semantic fidelity to the original task remains a separate and material limitation.

Evaluation & Science

A 112-language audit reports language-conditioned geopolitical framing differences across three models

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Maxim Chupilkin’s arXiv:2609.20005v1 evaluates 20 curated English statements about the Russia–Ukraine war after translating them into 112 language/script conditions. Three fixed model/API versions produced 67,200 scores. The author reports that the balance between agreement with the paired Russia-leaning and Ukraine-leaning statements varies by language, while responses lean Ukraine-ward in all 112 conditions. Country-level mapped language averages correlate with public Russia favourability (Pearson r=0.569 across 35 countries), UN non-support (r=0.384 across 183) and Ukraine aid allocations relative to GDP (r=-0.707 across 41 tracked donors). The topics were not preregistered or randomly sampled; automatic translation checks do not establish semantic equivalence, provider defaults differ, and data/code are described as forthcoming.

Agents & Memory

Counterfactual re-execution improves reported robot recovery supervision from ambiguous failures

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

MAGMA-GEN uses a privileged coach to suggest localized corrections or recovery actions after an ambiguous robotic failure, then re-executes each candidate from the same simulator state and retains it only when downstream progress improves. In the authors’ simulated manipulation evaluation, four generation runs report a 26.25% mean recovery rate versus 16.66% for the strongest Best-of-N retry baseline and a 16.79% complete-task success rate versus 14.15%. With branching factor B=2, the authors report 15/30 real-robot trials succeeded, compared with 12/30 for expert-rollout distillation and 11/30 for CLEANER-adapted; each method used one checkpoint, so the small physical trial does not establish statistical superiority. The paper reports a bounded mechanism and transfer feasibility, not robust long-horizon robotics. The linked repository is now a v2 beta while the paper describes software v0.1; exact version correspondence and independent reproduction remain open.

Evaluation & Science

Lei and Ren detail a Navier–Stokes blowup profile construction with admissible stress and flat remainder

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Zhen Lei and Xiao Ren present a detailed construction of smooth axisymmetric self-similar profiles related to OpenAI's proposed forced Navier–Stokes blowup proof. In the arXiv record, v1 was submitted on 28 September and v2 revised on 29 September; the authors describe the work as expository and say it will not be submitted to a journal. They report matching five profile moments, constructing an admissible stress and obtaining a remainder that vanishes to infinite order on fixed similarity sectors. The paper is Part I: it identifies cancellation of the remaining residual by oscillatory pulses as a companion Part II step. It therefore supplies a bounded profile-construction result and an inspectable stage of the argument, not a completed finite-time blowup theorem or resolution of the Clay problem.

Parameterized economic-data web skills improve matched transfer, while approximate retrieval erases gains on uncovered tasks

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

In arXiv:2609.19523v1, the authors evaluate 50 reusable procedures for retrieving live economic data from authoritative websites. In a controlled study using gpt-5-mini with BrowserGym, they report that a known matched skill raised success from 41.0% to 49.3% across 100 held-out variants repeated with three seeds. Agents & Memory

A paired-replay study finds weak history signals for predicting when agent context compaction will hurt

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

Pakhomov and Nijkamp use TRACE’s paired-replay corpus to test whether an agent’s pre-compaction history predicts the harm caused by summarizing that context. Across 590 harness-triggered boundaries from 275 trajectories on 147 AppWorld tasks, the prespecified contrast between histories with and without prior writes is a wide null (dU@3 −0.062; 95% interval −0.209 to +0.079); a naive “has written” label largely tracks trajectory phase. The best history-based trigger reaches held-out AUROC 0.66, below a same-boundary replay yardstick of 0.72. A frozen two-feature logistic trigger at 83.7% retention avoids 20.6% of positive-burden boundaries by count, but has no detected advantage on burden mass. The study does not establish a deployable compaction policy: it covers one agent/compressor, one AppWorld cohort and a 4,096-token window, and cannot compare with a token-budget rule at matched retention.

Evaluation & Science

A controlled Gemma 4 study finds learning-rate-sensitive gains in small-model negotiation

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

Tabacof and Joglekar report a controlled GRPO study of four Gemma 4 seller checkpoints (2.3B–31B effective parameters) in bilateral multi-issue bargaining. With one shared 1e-6 learning rate and 104 steps, author-reported gains over each base checkpoint rise from +0.001 (95% interval −0.019 to +0.021; not significant) at 2.3B to +0.023 at 4.5B, +0.063 at 12B and +0.078 at 31B; each arm has one training run. At early checkpoints, a 3× learning rate improves over the shared rate at all sizes, while after 104 shared-rate steps the difference is not detectable at 12B or 31B. The study does not establish a scaling law: size and architecture co-vary, training is not FLOP-matched, and the authors report only two held-out buyers and one underpowered held-out domain. These are paper-reported results in one synthetic negotiation environment, not evidence of commercial transfer.
