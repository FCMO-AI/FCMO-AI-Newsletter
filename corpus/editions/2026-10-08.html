Evaluation & Science

A mathematical-analysis preprint reports mismatches between OpenAI’s Navier-Stokes proof and cited Lean estimates

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen compare OpenAI’s natural-language Navier-Stokes proof with cited Lean declarations. In Lemma 8.6, equation (8.19), they report that the paper states an m+4 derivative estimate while the cited Lean estimate requires m+5 derivatives. For equation (10.19), they report a distinct pressure-flux bound and proof route in Lean. The authors argue that these examples challenge whether the formalization faithfully verifies the announced prose. This is a source-reported analysis, not an independent refutation of the underlying blow-up theorem; it is an October 6 preprint without established peer review or specialist consensus in this check.

Agents & Memory

Mingbird reports improved artifact completion by small local models on a bounded agent benchmark

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

The authors compare a local-first agent harness with three stock harnesses across four small open models and 18 self-built tasks, reporting an average task score of 0.886 for Mingbird versus 0.631, 0.479 and 0.405 for the named baselines. A separate 278-task tau-squared comparison reports 0.856 under a common user simulator. The paper publishes per-cell results and discusses artifact checks, finish gates and anti-loop controls. These are author-reported benchmark results: the primary matrix uses one machine, one counted run per cell and tasks co-developed with some harness mechanisms.

Evaluation & Science

A 66-configuration study reports model–harness ranking reversals across agent tasks

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Li et al. report 66 model–harness configurations across TUA-Bench, ALE-CLI and a 63-task non-H100 subset of Terminal-Bench 4. Rankings reverse by harness: on the paper’s Terminal-Bench subset, Claude leads GPT-6 Astra by 7.94 points under OpenHands but trails by 30.16 points under PI. GPT-6 Astra scores 60.32% at $4.66 per task under PI versus 52.38% at $19.94 under DeepSeek Harness. The authors release adapters, evaluation code and 6,204 scored trajectories. These are author-reported results from one counted run per task; harness defaults and effective reasoning budgets differ, so the comparison does not isolate a causal harness effect or establish ranking stability.

Policy & Geopolitics

China opens 2026 green-compute facility recommendations with energy, carbon, water and utilization criteria

Evidence Astrongly_supportedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A five-agency Chinese notice opens recommendations for 2026 national green-compute facilities. It requires at least one year of stable operation, specified energy-efficiency and green-data-center levels, renewable-electricity share at least the provincial responsibility weight, and measured energy, water and compute-utilization information; ordinary facilities generally need at least 3,000 standard racks and 60% rack occupancy, with stated exceptions. Applications are due 16 November 2026 and the agencies say they will review submissions before publishing a recommended list. These are eligibility and screening rules, not evidence of facility compliance, selection outcomes or improved AI-compute economics.

Evaluation & Science

A paper proposes carbon reporting and a smallest-model selection rule for AI research

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The authors introduce carbonbenchmark, which combines CodeCarbon measurements with fitted training curves, and propose a “Smallest Model that Achieves the Job” (SMAJ) selection approach. They report illustrative fits on Fashion-MNIST, GLUE SST-2 and UCI Adult, and an automated review of 5,285 NeurIPS 2025 papers using LLM classification. Their proprietary-inference emissions examples use assumed active parameter counts and generic hardware/grid factors; these are estimates, not measured model-specific emissions. The paper argues for reporting research emissions and treating environmental cost alongside accuracy.

Evaluation & Science

An occupational audit finds LLM judges rank responses better than they match worker score levels

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The authors evaluate 33 LLM-judge configurations against 4,501 held-out worker ratings spanning 307 occupations in a US English text corpus. They report above-chance tie-aware response ranking for all configurations, while predicted acceptance shares range from 3.0% to 97.9% against the sampled workers’ 61.1%. A prespecified validation contrast within one fine-tuned lineage reports that a bundled few-shot/listwise protocol improves ordering while reducing task- and occupation-level agreement. The study audits agreement with sampled worker ratings; it does not measure objective work quality, AI productivity or employment effects.

Policy & Geopolitics

Six national cybersecurity agencies publish joint guidance for secure adoption of agentic AI

Evidence Astrongly_supportedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

On 2026-05-01, cybersecurity agencies from Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States published joint nonbinding guidance for LLM-based agentic AI systems. The 29-page document recommends distinct cryptographically anchored identities for agents, mutual TLS for agent/API calls, least-privilege permissions, trusted registries, controlled delegation with expiry and recorded grant chains, risk-based human approval, and continuous identity and authorization checks through a central policy decision point for each action. It also calls for threat-modelled evaluations, red-teaming, monitoring and staged deployment. These are recommendations, not a binding rule or evidence that any government has adopted the controls or that they measurably reduce attacks.

Agents & Memory

A paired-replay study finds weak history signals for predicting when agent context compaction will hurt

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

Pakhomov and Nijkamp use TRACE’s paired-replay corpus to test whether an agent’s pre-compaction history predicts the harm caused by summarizing that context. Across 590 harness-triggered boundaries from 275 trajectories on 147 AppWorld tasks, the prespecified contrast between histories with and without prior writes is a wide null (dU@3 −0.062; 95% interval −0.209 to +0.079); a naive “has written” label largely tracks trajectory phase. The best history-based trigger reaches held-out AUROC 0.66, below a same-boundary replay yardstick of 0.72. A frozen two-feature logistic trigger at 83.7% retention avoids 20.6% of positive-burden boundaries by count, but has no detected advantage on burden mass. The study does not establish a deployable compaction policy: it covers one agent/compressor, one AppWorld cohort and a 4,096-token window, and cannot compare with a token-budget rule at matched retention.

Evaluation & Science

A controlled Gemma 4 study finds learning-rate-sensitive gains in small-model negotiation

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

Tabacof and Joglekar report a controlled GRPO study of four Gemma 4 seller checkpoints (2.3B–31B effective parameters) in bilateral multi-issue bargaining. With one shared 1e-6 learning rate and 104 steps, author-reported gains over each base checkpoint rise from +0.001 (95% interval −0.019 to +0.021; not significant) at 2.3B to +0.023 at 4.5B, +0.063 at 12B and +0.078 at 31B; each arm has one training run. At early checkpoints, a 3× learning rate improves over the shared rate at all sizes, while after 104 shared-rate steps the difference is not detectable at 12B or 31B. The study does not establish a scaling law: size and architecture co-vary, training is not FLOP-matched, and the authors report only two held-out buyers and one underpowered held-out domain. These are paper-reported results in one synthetic negotiation environment, not evidence of commercial transfer.
