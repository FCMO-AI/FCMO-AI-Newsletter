Evaluation & Science

A preprint measures correlated errors across LLM judges and shows when consensus overstates evidence

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

The authors of arXiv:2609.22512 report correlated errors across open-weight and frontier LLM judges. In their main ten-judge open-weight bank, average pairwise error correlation is 0.206 and the variance-based effective ensemble size is 3.51 on 1,133 RewardBench preference pairs; this is not a claim about majority-vote accuracy. In resampled 100-pair Focus panels, a pooled-vote significance test disagrees with an item-level test in 28% of panels. Three cross-provider frontier judges show error correlation of 0.56 on RewardBench, based on a small bank and relatively few residual errors. The authors find that different error structures favor different filters, while their tested dependence-aware filters do not outperform plain majority on the screened factuality and code settings. These are preprint results, not independently reproduced findings.

Inference & Systems

H-Spec removes the separate drafter KV cache in parallel speculative decoding

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

A September 21, 2026 arXiv paper introduces H-Spec, a parallel speculative-decoding drafter that combines Mamba state initialized from the target model’s last-position hidden state with attention that reuses the target KV cache in place. This removes a separate drafter-side KV cache. Under the authors’ matched training recipe, H-Spec reports 5.0–13.3% higher mean accepted length and 5.3–12.6% higher batch-size-1 inter-token-latency speedup than the best baseline across three targets and eight tasks, with paired bootstrap 95% intervals above zero. On one NVIDIA A100 80G using vLLM, the paper reports higher throughput and lower KV-cache utilization across concurrency 8–128. These remain author-reported results; the paper notes slower drafting below concurrency 32 and initially on shorter inputs, and requires matching drafter and target K/V dimensions. Independent serving reproduction and transfer beyond the measured setup remain open.

Evaluation & Science

Study reports pairwise judging can recover capability signals from saturated benchmark transcripts

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Hamada, Yeon, Migliarini, Olive and Dubois evaluate whether correctly solved transcripts from saturated benchmarks retain information about model capability on harder items. In a retrospective study covering 16 target models across science, mathematics, coding and cybersecurity, the authors report that pairwise LLM judging predicts held-out capability better than a score-only baseline. Its pooled mean squared prediction error is 0.26 versus 1.37 for baseline assignment (a reported 5.27-fold reduction), but performance varies across domains and time: the method fails to beat baseline in some historical periods and substantially underestimates the first reasoning models. The work is a paper-reported evaluation method, not an independently validated safety assessment.

Evaluation & Science

A conditional reliability model shows how shared failures erase nominal AI-control redundancy

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

An arXiv preprint models one published AI-control deployment route as access control, monitoring plus response, or visibility plus tracing plus containment. Under independent equal rare failures, its visible-component model has six size-three minimal cuts and failure probability asymptotic to 6ε³. Illustrative shared failure domains reduce the minimum underlying cut size to two or one, and a conditional derivation shows why stronger prevention can concentrate later recovery demands in harder failure regimes. The paper recommends explicit dependency mapping and recovery tests that preserve the initiating failure. These results are conditional analysis: the common-cause examples are not claims about Google DeepMind’s implementation, and no operational probabilities are calibrated.

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

RoboFollow is an author-designed benchmark intended to test whether embodied policies use language to select a task when the visual scene supports multiple valid actions. Across four controlled scene families, nine evaluated VLA/WAM policies and L0–L3 visual or semantic perturbations, the paper reports sharp declines in selected policies’ Intent Scores—for example, pi0 falls from 98.2% to 0.0% on a Scene 1 layout change, and pi0.5 falls from 100.0% to 34.2% on a Scene 2 combined perturbation. Evaluation & Science

Agent-security attack success rates lack a shared measurement contract

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

An arXiv preprint analyzes 259 papers reporting agentic-security attack success rates from a query-retrieved English-language arXiv corpus. The authors report that 58.0% of a seeded hand-coded sample of 50 papers (95% Wilson CI 44.2–70.6) reported neither a variance estimate nor repeated runs; revised automated detectors estimate 65.3% across all 259. They identify six choices that can change what an ASR measures, including evaluation unit, success oracle, attacker adaptivity, and defense knowledge. The paper argues these differences can make cross-paper values incomparable. It does not claim any individual paper is wrong, and its ranking-reversal estimates are analytical rather than observed defense comparisons.

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Reasoning & Post-training

ACLArena reports task forgetting in sequential agent training and tests routed LoRA experts

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

An arXiv preprint introduces ACLArena, a controlled study of capability retention across multi-stage agent post-training. On Qwen3-8B-Base, the authors report that sequential math, search, e-commerce and instruction-following stages can overwrite earlier capabilities: after e-commerce training, AIME26 fell from 23.33 to 6.04 and Natural Questions from 45.2 to 14.6. They compare multi-teacher on-policy distillation, replay-based supervised fine-tuning and model merging, then propose Mixture of Low-Rank Experts (MLE), which combines replay-trained shared behavior with separate RL-trained LoRA experts routed by environment context. Reported results approach task-specific experts on several tasks, but the fixed curriculum, single model family and observable-context routing leave transfer and deployment costs unresolved.

Reasoning & Post-training

Fixed-SAE Track traces bounded, format-heavy RL shifts in a small reasoning model

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 14, 2026 arXiv paper introduces Fixed-SAE Track: it trains one shared sparse autoencoder per analyzed layer on activations pooled across a base model and its RL checkpoints, then freezes the feature directions to track representation shifts. In the main Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct math-RL setting, the authors report gradual late-layer drift concentrated in formatting and reasoning scaffolds. Steering amplified features at dose α=10 raises strict accuracy from 0.050 to 0.192, close to the short-RL final model’s 0.175, while lenient reasoning accuracy changes more modestly. The paper also reports that all 29 selected GSM8K problems that shift from greedy-base-wrong to RL-right are solved by base sampling at k=64; however, 2000 additional RL steps raise lenient accuracy from 0.300 to 0.408, and directly injecting a necessary reasoning feature set recovers little. The approximately 80% headline therefore concerns a bounded short-RL extractability/format effect, not a general claim that RL only elicits existing capability. Results are author-reported and not independently reproduced.

Google DeepMind’s 15 October walkthrough describes a researcher-steered Antigravity/Gemini 3.8 Flash workflow that searches UniProt candidates, cross-checks sequence similarity, compares predicted structures and genomic context, and prepares a wet-lab protocol. The example names UniProt A0A0U0D591, a 97-amino-acid protein assigned to the Lactococcin 972 family; the company says the candidate and others are undergoing wet-lab validation. This is a first-party account of a computational candidate-selection workflow, not evidence of antibacterial efficacy or independent discovery performance.
