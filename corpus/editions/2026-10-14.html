Reasoning & Post-training

SkillGym reports agent training gains from executable human-workflow environments

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 7/10 · Very majoractive

A September 2026 preprint introduces SkillGym, a pipeline that turns human-written agent skills into executable tasks with code-based outcome checks and contrastive skill-dependence labels. The authors report 2,756 environments across 12 categories and 8,364 successful trajectories, then fine-tune Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on SkillGym trajectories. They report gains on GDPval-AA v2, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SkillsBench, including performance that persists without inference-time skills. These remain paper-reported results. The paper’s v2 abstract and v2 HTML disagree on the no-skills SkillsBench gain (12.38 versus 14.68 points), and the reported benchmark comparisons depend on specific harnesses; code, dataset and model artifacts were not audited in this review.

Reasoning & Post-training

RLVR rollout batches trade sample efficiency against training stability

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

An arXiv paper reports controlled GRPO experiments on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and related checkpoints. Across 21 unclipped runs spanning a 32-fold optimizer-batch range and nearly sixfold learning-rate range, the authors find that training failure timing is more consistent with consumed response samples than optimizer steps along square-root learning-rate scaling. They also report declining sample efficiency above a learning-rate-dependent critical batch, with clipping delaying failure but not removing the progress gap. The results are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

Architectures & Scaling

Theory paper separates parallelism limits across diffusion language model paradigms

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

A September 2026 theory paper compares parallel sampling complexity for masked, uniform and Gaussian diffusion. It proves that, under stated score-estimation assumptions, uniform and Gaussian samplers can scale forward-pass complexity with the target distribution's dual total correlation. For a specified family of random empirical measures, the authors give Õ(√d) necessary-and-sufficient pass complexity for uniform or Gaussian diffusion, while constructing approximate score oracles for which masked diffusion requires Õ(d) passes. They attribute the separation to narrower critical windows in the masked process. These are conditional theoretical results; the paper does not show a measured speedup in trained diffusion language models or deployed inference.

Evaluation & Science

Terminal-Bench audit shows why an all-fail score does not certify task hardness

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

A September 2026 paper audits the frozen Terminal-Bench 3 / Frontier-Bench 0.1 production record to ask what an all-fail score actually supports. It covers 1,081 pull requests, 639 scored tasks and 28,801 trials. Of 125 tasks with no honest pass, an ordered screen retains 78 as certified-unsolved candidates; the other 47 are broken-oracle, infrastructure-limited, exploit-only-passable or unresolved cases. The paper defines certified-unsolved narrowly: a reference route passed, infrastructure did not dominate, no strict verifier bypass was observed, and all evaluated agents failed in the frozen setup. This is not proof of intrinsic hardness or failure at the intended capability. The task-level audit and token-cost analysis are author-reported and have not been independently reproduced here.

Compute & Hardware

EBRL reports 1.30×–3.47× higher throughput for simulated embodied-RL training

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

The EBRL preprint describes a system built on RLinf that overlaps rollout with model training, pipelines simulation and generation, schedules environment work asynchronously, and reallocates CPU cores and GPU streaming multiprocessors as stage demands change. Across five simulator-policy configurations and several accelerator testbeds, the authors report 1.30×–3.47× end-to-end trajectory throughput over compared embodied-RL systems. Their ablations report that removing resource allocation, group pipelining or asynchronous execution reduces throughput. These are author-reported simulation results; no independent rerun or physical-robot evaluation was performed here.

Reasoning & Post-training

Tev reports long-horizon agent training gains from verified environments and dense progress rewards

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

A 13 October preprint introduces Tev-Gym, a 1,170-environment synthetic training corpus with hidden latent-world specifications, strict task verifiers and staged progress scores. The authors use progress-potential reward shaping to train Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.8-27B, reporting higher scores than their base checkpoints on Agents’ Last Exam’s computing-and-math subset (ALE-CM). Tev-27B’s reported Pass@5 is 0.28 versus 0.17 for its base and equals the paper’s reported GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 values under the listed harness. The authors also report transfer to selected Terminal-Bench computing-and-math subsets and a larger GLM-5.3-Flash model. These are author-reported results; this review did not independently reproduce them, and the project page lists code and data as forthcoming.

Agents & Memory

Graph-guided red-teaming finds more harmful cross-service paths in a simulated personal-agent ecosystem

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

The October 2026 X-AgentRed preprint uses a graph of tool effects and execution preconditions to generate cross-service red-team scenarios, then uses execution feedback to diversify successful paths and repair blocked ones. In one evaluation run per configuration across four target agents in a simulated ecosystem of 30 services and 914 tools, the authors report a 19.2% harmful-path rate, versus 10.5% for STAC and 2.7% for TRACE; average tool coverage is 41.5%, versus 13.9% and 23.7%. These are paper-reported simulator results, not estimates of real-world agent risk.

Evaluation & Science

ProbeGuard reports risk-controlled abstention from medical answer-consensus trajectories

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Wang et al. introduce ProbeGuard, which uses the formation history of a multi-round medical question-answering consensus to decide when to abstain. Its process features track agreement, minority persistence and retrieval; for immediately unanimous answers it also checks rationale agreement and probes with counter-evidence. On MedQA, the authors report a process-score AUROC of 0.696 versus 0.512 for terminal agreement. Their stratified Learn-then-Test rule answered 55.3% of the unanimous layer at 9.0% observed risk against a 15% target across 50 splits, under an assumption that calibration and deployment questions are i.i.d. within each stratum. In specialty analysis, emergency medicine had 19.1% realized risk at the same layer-level threshold, and was not separately certified. These are author-reported multiple-choice benchmark results, not clinical deployment evidence.

Agents & Memory

ServeLearnBench reports that agents struggle to learn changing hidden policies from service feedback

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Zheng et al. introduce ServeLearnBench, a benchmark for learning policies that change during service interactions. Its three domains contain 53 environment windows, 4,508 feedback-bearing serving tasks and 3,210 held-out tasks. Across six models and five learning harnesses, the authors report a large gap between following a disclosed policy and inferring it from outcomes: on Retail L3 the best of 28 tested model–harness pairs scored 59.6% and the median 22.0%. They also report higher costs for hidden-policy inference and cases where adaptation reduced performance on fully specified tasks. These are author-reported results from a designed benchmark, not an independent measurement of deployed services; harnesses retained different native resource budgets.

Agents & Memory

R3Con reports large gains from task-focused context representation on two long-context benchmarks

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

A September 2026 preprint introduces R3Con, a harness that repeatedly extracts task-relevant context from large document collections, builds a flexible structure such as a table or graph, then uses a coding agent to reason over it. On CorpusQA and Loong, the authors report overall accuracy gains of 19.88 and 8.42 percentage points over the strongest evaluated baselines. They also report that 4B and 9B R3Con configurations exceeded all evaluated 35B baselines, and that a Qwen3.5-35B-A3B R3Con run exceeded a Claude Code/Sonnet 5 comparison at about 3.7 times lower reported cost. These are single-paper results on two benchmarks; both use LLM judging, and the cross-system cost comparison does not isolate model and harness effects.

Evaluation & Science

A real-click study finds a no-persona LLM ranker outperforms a synthetic persona panel

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A July 2026 paper tests whether a demographic persona panel improves LLM forecasts of real audience response. It compares a fixed ten-persona panel with a no-persona ranker on held-out Upworthy headline A/B tests. Because most tests had no statistically reliable winner, the primary analysis uses 399 of 1,695 packages. On that subset, the paper reports Kendall tau 0.361 and top-1 accuracy 49.2% for the no-persona baseline, versus 0.084 and 34.6% for the persona panel. The direction holds across three Upworthy splits and tested model tiers; the paper also reports a significant gpt-4.1 cross-family gap. A public repository contains scripts and aggregated artifacts for regenerating reported tables, while raw persona generations are withheld. Results concern aggregate ranking of historical English headlines, not all persona use or current marketing outcomes.

Evaluation & Science

COMED selectively escalates cross-model collaboration to trade rescue gains against collaboration harms

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The Sep. 22, 2026 COMED paper studies when a multi-model system should trust its initial answer, check it with a peer, or invoke fuller collaboration. The controller uses anchor self-consistency, router ambiguity and a lightweight peer probe to direct additional model calls toward uncertain or persistently disagreeing cases. In its reported benchmark suite, COMED improves its fixed or routed anchor in all 16 open-weight settings, averaging 4.6 percentage points while using at most two distinct models per query and about 33% fewer decoded tokens than dense collaboration. A Frontier-3/Humanity's Last Exam stress test reports GPT-5.5 rising from 23.13% to 28.13%, with collaboration on 70% of examples. These are paper-reported results; independent reproduction and implementation evidence remain open.

Reasoning & Post-training

RECAP uses dependency-aware step credit to reduce reasoning tokens in math models

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The arXiv preprint introduces RECAP, which gives GRPO training step-level credit using LLM-annotated semantic dependencies plus a gold-answer likelihood signal. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the authors report 2.0–3.7 percentage-point pass@1 gains and 8–31% fewer reasoning tokens than GRPO across GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 2024 and AIME 2025. Results on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B are mixed, so the paper does not establish a uniform cross-model improvement. These are author-reported experiments, not independently reproduced; the tested regime is limited to two 7B math models and four benchmarks.

GroundedGEO studies whether generative search rankers can elevate relevant-looking product text without evidence support. CC-OPD uses counterfactual teacher scores to improve multi-constraint instruction following

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The CC-OPD preprint proposes assigning token-level training signals to individual instruction constraints by comparing a frozen teacher's scores with and without each constraint. Evaluation & Science

A Lean-formalized search target for the open unforced Navier–Stokes problem

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 2026 preprint defines a positive energy defect for a periodic-cube, unforced Leray–Hopf solution at fixed viscosity and reports a Lean 4 formal reduction: the defect is equivalent to a uniform positive lower bound on time-averaged energy flux through all sufficiently fine dyadic Fourier shells. The paper gives necessary admissibility conditions and argues that finite Galerkin computation cannot witness the required uniform ceiling; its 12 pseudo-spectral runs at 128³ and 256³ meet a temporal flux condition but fail to sustain the floor toward the Kolmogorov scale. This is a bounded mathematical search specification, not an unforced singularity result. The authors state that their Lean library contains no Navier–Stokes object and does not connect the reduction to a separately formalized Leray–Hopf theory; the target remains open.

Evaluation & Science

SEMV reports improved multimedia verification and safer reuse of corrected cases

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The authors introduce SEMV, a multi-agent multimedia verification system that represents evidence as provenance-bearing support and attack arguments, routes human corrections to affected stages, and gates memory reuse through verification and conflict retention. On the 1,700-record COSMOS test set, the Qwen3.6 variant reports 91.88% accuracy versus 89.10% for the strongest directly comparable full-test baseline (unpaired two-proportion z-test p=0.006); other backbones show smaller or nonsignificant differences. In a Qwen memory ablation, unrestricted updates show 5.7% negative transfer, versus 0.2% when verification and conflict retention are added. The authors also report 96.7% correction of initial errors and 52.8% compute savings in a constructed contestation benchmark. These are author-reported results: COSMOS is controlled binary image-caption consistency, MV2026 evaluates only 10 validation and 10 private-test cases, and the 50 CTR episodes come from 10 independent source cases. No independent reproduction was conducted.

Agents & Memory

Unity Insight reports lower agent navigation cost on two Unity projects

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

The Unity Insight preprint describes a read-only index that links C# code with serialized Unity scenes, prefabs, ScriptableObjects and GUID references, exposing that graph to a coding agent through typed query tools. In a paired study of 28 project-specific questions across two Unity games, the authors report 53.4% fewer tokens and 51.6% lower wall time than a general-purpose exploration agent using the same model and harness; one project is internal and each question has one run per arm. The study measures navigation cost, not coding correctness or software quality, and has not been independently reproduced here.
