1. EgoWild2Dex reports transferring in-the-wild human video to bimanual robot manipulation with limited robot data

Desk: multimodality_world_models Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · developing · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If the reported transfer and data-efficiency results generalize, natural human demonstrations could reduce robot-specific data collection for dexterous manipulation. Score 6 reflects potential importance to embodied-model training; the current evidence is one paper on three tasks and does not establish broad transfer or remove robot fine-tuning.

What changed: The authors present EgoWild2Dex, which aligns egocentric human observations with robot views and trains a dexterous manipulation policy through broad human-data pretraining, task-specific human–robot co-training and robot-domain refinement. Their EgoWild corpus contains 538.9 hours, 179,049 episodes and 125,961 task descriptions. On three long-horizon bimanual tasks, the authors report 90%–100% success using less than one hour of robot demonstrations per task; object-level success on three unseen package contents is 30%–40%. GeoFormer image alignment is reported at 7.43 ms mean latency versus 162.58 ms for a projection-and-inpainting baseline, excluding image decoding and model loading. These are author-reported results from a limited task and robot setting; the paper says the data, models and code will be released and still requires robot fine-tuning. Why it matters: The work tests a path from large, naturally collected human manipulation data to dexterous robot behavior with limited task-specific robot demonstrations. If the transfer holds beyond three tasks and the reported dataset becomes accessible, it could change the data and collection-cost requirements for bimanual manipulation. Current results still depend on robot fine-tuning, specific hands and a staged training pipeline, and do not establish broad zero-shot robot transfer.

Demonstrated: The paper specifies an in-the-wild corpus, staged training method and author-run real-robot and alignment evaluations. Measurements and success rates remain paper-reported and were not independently reproduced.

Claimed: Authors report 90%–100% success across three long-horizon bimanual tasks using less than one hour of robot demonstrations per task. Three unseen object categories yield 30%–40% success (33.3% mean). GeoFormer has 7.43 ms mean and 12.97 ms P95 latency versus 162.58 ms and 194.16 ms for projection plus inpainting; estimated processing cost is 2.06 versus 45.16 GPU-hours per million frames.

Mechanism: EgoWild2Dex first trains on unscripted egocentric human manipulation data, then aligns task-specific human views with robot views using GeoFormer, co-trains on aligned human, glove and limited robot demonstrations, and refines on robot execution/recovery data. Human and robot actions are retargeted into a shared robot-native action space.

Strongest baseline: For full-policy task results, direct fine-tuning of π₀, π₀.₅ and GR00T-N1.7 has much lower success under the paper's protocol. For image alignment, GeoFormer is compared with MoGe projection plus LaMa inpainting; task success on Open-Box is 90% versus 80%, with faster reported processing. These are author-run comparisons, not independent matched replications.

Regime: 538.9 hours of egocentric human data (179,049 episodes); three long-horizon bimanual tasks; less than one hour of robot demonstrations per task plus task-specific human/glove demonstrations and robot-domain refinement. 2. WaveletECO reports a specialized local model matching a frontier API on chip-repair scores at lower estimated inference cost

Desk: efficiency Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · developing · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If the benchmark and inference-cost results generalize, specialized local models could alter the cost and provider-dependence trade-off for physical chip repair workflows. Score 6 reflects potential importance to a high-cost engineering domain, not truth or transfer beyond the tested designs.

Demonstrated: The inspected paper documents the workflow, benchmark protocol, score definition, author-reported measurements and cost model. Neither performance nor cost was independently reproduced in this activation.

Claimed: In 594 runs over 22 designs (three repeats for each of nine model variants per design), WaveletECO-Policy INT8 scores 79.65 and BF16 79.63, versus GPT-6 Astra 77.44 under the shared protocol. Desk: evaluation_science Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · active · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If the reported mismatches hold, a machine-checked Lean result cannot be treated as verification of the specific natural-language proof without a semantic bridge. Strongest baseline: OpenAI’s natural-language paper and its cited Lean declarations are compared directly.
