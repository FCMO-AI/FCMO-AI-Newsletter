1. Tencent Hy4 preview combines 770B MoE capacity with sparse attention, IndexCache and native MTP

Desk: architectures_scaling Evidence: A · confirmed · active · importance 8/10 (Field-shifting) What changed: Tencent released Hy4 preview under Apache-2.0 with a 770B-parameter MoE backbone, 49B active parameters per token, 1M context, Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention, cross-layer IndexCache reuse, four-stream identity Hyper-Connections, and a native MTP layer intended for speculative decoding. Tencent lists API prices of CNY 6 per million input tokens, CNY 18 per million output tokens and cache-hit prices from CNY 0.3; its separate 31.8% throughput gain is a vendor claim without enough matched measurement detail for independent comparison. Why it matters: Hy4 is a third major open-weight frontier release in the same week emphasizing sparse long-context retrieval and richer residual routing, while also shipping native multi-token prediction. It strengthens the architectural-convergence signal but does not by itself establish that these mechanisms outperform matched dense or hybrid alternatives. The official Chinese release also supplies concrete API prices and a vendor-reported 31.8% throughput gain, while leaving total cost, matched serving conditions and independent confirmation open.

Demonstrated: Released BF16/FP8 model artifacts and configuration expose a 770B backbone with 49B active parameters per token, 1M context, 256 routed experts, top-8 routing, Gated DSA, four residual streams and native MTP. Apache-2.0 licensing is present in the released artifact.

Claimed: Tencent reports its largest generation-over-generation capability gain and small wins over GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 in an internal blind evaluation of 163 experts across 203 engineering tasks. Tencent’s Chinese release additionally reports an internal blind evaluation of 163 experts over 203 engineering tasks (mean 2.99/4 vs. GLM 5.3 at 2.92/4 and Kimi K3 at 2.94/4), and a 31.8% end-to-end throughput gain over an unspecified baseline after model-led operator optimization; both remain vendor claims.

Mechanism: A 78-layer MoE backbone: one dense FFN layer followed by 77 MoE layers with 256 routed experts plus one shared expert; top-8 routed experts are active per token. Attention uses Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention with IndexCache to reuse sparse indices across layers. The residual path uses four-stream identity Hyper-Connections. A separate native MTP layer adds 10B total/0.7B active parameters for speculative decoding.

Strongest baseline: Tencent's internal blind comparison against GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3; no matched-compute architectural ablation is supplied in the release card.

Regime: Frontier-scale 770B-total/49B-active open-weight model; serving remains hardware-heavy despite sparse activation. Internal productivity evaluation is vendor-run and not an independent reproduction.

Limitations: The strongest quality comparison disclosed in the model card is vendor-run and narrowly separated from GLM-5.3/Kimi K3; it is not independent evidence.; No matched-compute ablation isolates the contribution of sparse attention, IndexCache, iHC or native MTP.; 49B active parameters still implies a heavy local serving regime; sparse activation does not make the 770B stored model a small local model.; Tencent explicitly notes overlong reasoning and over-verification in this preview.; Tencent’s Chinese release reports a 31.8% end-to-end throughput improvement after model-led operator optimization, but the inspected statement does not give enough hardware, request-load or baseline detail to reproduce or generalize it.; Published API token prices do not establish total cost of ownership, local deployment cost, or throughput-adjusted unit economics.

Open evidence gaps: No matched training/inference budget ablation establishes the isolated gains from Gated DSA, IndexCache, iHC or native MTP.; Vendor quality and productivity results lack independent reproduction.

7. Self-Building Labs builds missing laboratory capabilities around a scientific goal

Desk: evaluation_science Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · active · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If the paper's bounded demonstrations generalize, agents that construct missing lab representations, robot skills and workflows could reduce a practical bottleneck in experimental automation and broaden which scientific procedures can be automated; the consequence is meaningful, but generality and productivity gains are not yet measured.

What changed: Bai et al. describe a self-building laboratory system that starts from a scientific goal and an existing lab description, then constructs missing digital-twin components, robot/device skills and experimental workflows. Their arXiv v1 reports one physical cadmium-sulfide quantum-dot workflow: the system prepared a 96-well plate through 236 liquid-handler commands and 19 robotic transfer stages, with human approval and live sensing. The paper also demonstrates coordination across robots and multiple workstations in simulation. This is a source-reported prototype result, not evidence of general-purpose autonomous science: the authors state that successive-cycle performance, reduced engineering effort and reduced human intervention remain to be measured; they also report cases where device failures were not observable without human expertise. Why it matters: Laboratory setup and workflow engineering can constrain the spread of automated experimentation. If agents can safely build and validate task-specific capabilities from a high-level objective, more experiments may become automatable on existing equipment. The paper makes this direction concrete in one physical workflow, while leaving generality, reliability, human labor and economic benefit open.

Claimed: A system that constructs task-relevant lab digital twins, device interfaces, embodied skills and experimental workflows from a high-level goal; one physical quantum-dot synthesis workflow is reported.

Mechanism: The system uses a physics-based digital twin to construct task-relevant representations, develop and test candidate skills, and plan workflows; physical actions use authenticated device APIs, status feedback and force/torque sensing. Successful exploratory sequences are converted into deterministic executable skills with sensor conditions and recovery branches. A human-approved plan, stop interface, speed limits and force-triggered stops constrain execution.

Strongest baseline: The paper frames existing self-driving laboratories as configured for known procedures and requiring specialist engineering to add capabilities; it does not report a matched baseline measuring task success, time, human labor or engineering cost against a conventional automation team.

Regime: One University of Toronto laboratory chemistry setup and a CdS quantum-dot synthesis workflow. The paper also tests multi-robot and multi-workstation arrangements in simulation. No cross-laboratory physical transfer or broad task distribution is established.

Limitations: Evidence is one author-reported physical chemistry workflow on a particular laboratory setup; it does not establish general-purpose autonomous science or transfer across laboratories.; Multi-robot and multi-workstation scale-out is simulated; it is not a multi-station physical demonstration.; Human approval is required for the physical plan, and the authors report that some device failures were not observable through APIs and that interpretation and operational decisions still required human expertise.; Simulation and collision checks cover modeled geometry and states; they do not by themselves establish chemical safety or reliable handling of uncertain physical contact.; The paper does not quantify task success across repeated runs, failure rates, setup time, engineering labor, human intervention, cost, or gains over a matched conventional baseline.; No independent replication was established in this activation.

Open evidence gaps: No independent reproduction of the reported end-to-end quantum-dot workflow was established.; The paper does not measure how task success, engineering labor, setup time or human intervention change across tasks or successive cycles.; Physical multi-robot/multi-station execution and cross-laboratory transfer are not shown; those scale-out demonstrations are simulation-only.

PDE authors prove conditional regularity for an axisymmetric Navier–Stokes blowup profile with analytic forcing

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Peter Constantin, Mihaela Ignatova and Vlad Vicol report a theorem for suitable weak solutions of forced 3D Navier–Stokes: if the force is bounded in C² up to the proposed singular time and spatially real analytic locally before it, while the angular mean obeys specified anisotropic Type II estimates and the full solution is exactly axisymmetric on a shrinking core at every pre-singular time, then the solution is regular at the point. Their arXiv v2 appendix derives two velocity hypotheses from statements in OpenAI’s proposed construction and conditionally concludes that a force in that construction cannot vanish identically near the singular point or be locally spatially analytic under the stated bound. The paper expressly says it does not verify the OpenAI construction. OpenAI’s described compactly supported smooth residual force is nonanalytic, so the theorem constrains a force class and does not refute the proposed construction or resolve the Clay problem.
