1. Anthropic reports Claude leads 26% of measured AI R&D while none is fully autonomous

Desk: agents_memory Evidence: A · strongly_supported · active · importance 8/10 (Field-shifting) Importance rationale: If sustained and independently validated, AI leading a quarter of frontier-lab R&D while collaborating on over 90% materially changes the speed and structure of frontier development and creates a measurable precursor to stronger recursive acceleration; it is field-shifting but not evidence of autonomous RSI.

What changed: Anthropic published a prototype R&D Automation Index based on roughly 15,000 sampled granular R&D tasks organized into 378 leaf categories and rated on Epoch AI's AL0-AL5 automation scale. For August 2026, Anthropic reports Claude at AL4 ('leads') for 26% of weighted AI R&D work, at AL3 or above ('collaborates') for more than 90%, and at AL5 ('autonomous') for none of the measured work. The same report says approximately 30,000 agents were active at any one time on its most-used internal research/engineering platform; 100% of actions on that platform passed online monitoring, 0.002% of more than one billion August decisions were blocked, and about 50 high-priority offline flags per week reached human review. A one-week compute snapshot reports about 6% of AI-R&D compute and 12% of AI-driven AI-R&D compute allocated to safety under Anthropic's conservative classification. Why it matters: This is unusually concrete first-party evidence about the production process of frontier-model R&D rather than a benchmark proxy. If the methodology survives independent audit and becomes comparable across labs, it gives the field a measurable way to track AI-driven acceleration, oversight load and safety-compute allocation before fully autonomous recursive self-improvement. The reported 26% AL4 share is material, but the explicit zero-AL5 result is equally important: it argues for rapid partial automation, not autonomous self-improvement today.

Demonstrated: Within Anthropic's internal measurement system, August 2026 ratings put 26% of weighted AI R&D at AL4, over 90% at AL3+, and 0% at AL5. Model-vs-human exact automation-rating agreement is reported as 59%, human-vs-human as 35%, with model and human within one level 97% of the time. On the measured agent platform, online monitor coverage is reported at 100%, with 0.002% of over one billion August decisions blocked.

Claimed: Anthropic argues these metrics can give the public and governments visibility into the pace of frontier development and could support cross-lab reporting, independent verification and pacing triggers.

Mechanism: A frozen taxonomy of 378 leaf R&D task categories is built from sampled internal work records. Claude research agents gather evidence on how each category is performed, an independent Claude judge assigns Epoch AI AL0-AL5 automation levels, and categories are weighted by sampled person-time. Separate platform telemetry measures agent-monitor coverage, review latency and block/escalation rates; compute accounting classifies R&D workloads by purpose.

Strongest baseline: The most relevant baseline is Anthropic's own February 2026 AL4 share, reported as under 1%; cross-lab comparison is not yet valid because methodology is not standardized.

Regime: Anthropic internal AI R&D and its most-used internal research/engineering agent platform; August 2026 automation/oversight snapshot plus July 13-20 compute snapshot.

Limitations: Automation ratings and much of the evidence extraction use Claude itself, creating correlated-judge risk.; Underlying internal work records and telemetry are unavailable for independent audit.; Person-time weighting is a crude proxy for contribution to model progress and may underweight rare bottleneck tasks.; The 30,000-agent oversight figures cover only Anthropic's most-used internal platform, not all company agent activity.; Cross-lab comparisons are currently invalid without common task definitions, weighting and independent verification.; A high AL4 share does not imply equivalent acceleration in end-to-end model capability because compute and non-automatable bottlenecks can dominate.
