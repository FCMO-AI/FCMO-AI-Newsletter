Evaluation & Science

A cryptographic construction illustrates a limit of elicitation-based model capability audits

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 7/10 · Very majoractive

The paper constructs encrypted computations that can be embedded in model weights and conditionally hidden from polynomial-time elicitation under stated cryptographic assumptions. Its PyTorch proof of concept tests a small chemistry classifier: authors report resistance to supervised fine-tuning and latent adversarial training, while 8-bit gradient-based trigger search exhaustively enumerated 256 candidates and matched random search; the evaluated 16-bit query budget remained near chance. Correct-trigger chemistry accuracy was 73.5%. The authors verified individual compiled GLU units in Gemma 3 270M, but not the complete construction in a pretrained model. They also report raw compiled weights are anomalous, while obfuscation leaves possible within-layer correlation signals. This is a conditional construction and limited experiment, not evidence of hidden capabilities in deployed frontier models.

Evaluation & Science

A mathematical-analysis preprint reports mismatches between OpenAI’s Navier-Stokes proof and cited Lean estimates

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen compare OpenAI’s natural-language Navier–Stokes proof with cited Lean declarations. For Lemma 8.6, equation (8.19), they report that the manuscript states an m+4 derivative estimate while the cited Lean estimate requires m+5. For pressure-flux equation (10.19), they report a different Lean bound and proof route, including an additional localized gradient norm. They note one similar auxiliary bound in the repository does not appear to be directly used in the proposed theorem, leaving the effect on the actual proof dependency chain unresolved. The authors explicitly do not claim the natural-language blow-up proof is incorrect. This remains a source-reported critique in an arXiv preprint, without independent reproduction or established specialist consensus.

Agents & Memory

A paper models how distractor growth can weaken evidence retrieval in long-context models

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The authors formalize “context poisoning” as competition between a decisive evidence score and the maximum score among distractors. Under a softmax retrieval abstraction, they derive a finite-sample bound in which the evidence margin needed to hold accuracy above base rate grows as Ω(√log N), where N is the effective distractor count. Controlled experiments report decreasing retrieval accuracy as context grows with hard negatives; same-format distractors produce the largest drop among the tested constructions, while retrieval gating helps only when evidence recall is preserved.

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A preprint compares 16 one-week autonomous materials-discovery campaigns given the same 12,499-entry metal-organic-framework database, methane-storage objective and pinned protocol. The authors report divergent search strategies but convergence near 200 cm³/cm³, with an independent calculation placing its nine best structures among agent reports. Reasoning & Post-training

Critical-State RL selects trainable calls before optimizing multi-turn tool use

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The preprint proposes diagnosing which occurrence in a multi-turn interaction is trainable before applying a local reinforcement-learning update. Nested sampling holds each candidate action fixed while resampling reward-only continuations to separate action-dependent signal from downstream noise. In a four-cell BFCL v4 multi_turn study using Gemma-4-26B-A4B, authors report that training the diagnostically selected recovery call raised missing-function accuracy from 0.14 to 0.283 ± 0.015 across four seeds on 200 paired examples; training the alternative decision call reduced it to 0.095. In the missing-argument category, the selected decision call rose from 0.435 to 0.473 ± 0.010, while the alternative recovery call reached 0.445. These are paper-reported results, not an independent reproduction.

Architectures & Scaling

Complex KDA extends Kimi Delta Attention state-transition expressivity

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

Complex KDA (CKDA) combines two parameter-range extensions—signed channel gates and a delta coefficient up to 2—to make Kimi Delta Attention express 2D rotations using a single diagonal-plus-rank-one recurrent transition. The paper proves exact representability for orthogonal diagonal-plus-rank-one matrices and reports finite-group state-tracking, synthetic periodic-audio and language-model experiments. The authors report stronger length extrapolation on selected synthetic tasks and language-model results comparable to KDA, not a broad demonstrated language-model advantage. Open code and committed measurements aid inspection; no independent proof audit or training reproduction was conducted.

Multimodality & World Models

THAW-VLA distills world-model features into a compact robot policy

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

THAW-VLA uses a frozen world model to produce cached training-frame features, then adds a feature-alignment loss to a compact vision-language-action policy. The teacher and projector are removed before deployment, so inference uses the undistilled student graph. The authors report a 0.8B policy moving from 95.3% to 97.9% mean success on LIBERO and from 48.2% to 50.5% on RoboCasa-GR1; they also report gains over the same student on single-arm and bimanual real-robot tasks, with 30 trials per cell. A single RTX 5090 profile is reported at 32 ms and 1.86 GB. Results remain author-reported and task-specific; independent reproduction and broader robot/task coverage are open.

Agents & Memory

PhysAI-Bench reports a 52% best score on trace-derived UAV decisions

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The authors present PhysAI-Bench, a multiple-choice benchmark for foundation-model action selection from trace-derived autonomous UAV missions. Its repository describes 10,178 decisions with four candidate actions, a 35-instance human-verified development set and a 500-instance episode-disjoint held-out set. After selecting a model-specific prompt and temperature configuration, the authors report GPT-5.3 Chat at 52.00% held-out decision accuracy, the highest listed score. Evaluation & Science

Available Guardrails quantifies when selective prediction can be certified

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

The September 2026 Available Guardrails preprint studies whether finite calibration data can certify target precision for every reporting unit in a selective prediction system. It uses exact-binomial inversion and dynamic programming to expose the trade-off among certified precision, subgroup granularity and served traffic. In author-run evaluations, split-sample partition selection gains a reported mean 0.060 coverage over support balancing, with a positive direction in 59 of 60 model effects across three intent-routing datasets and two architectures; a naive estimator captures only 0.005 of a reported 0.157 population-known opportunity.

Multimodality & World Models

A compositional benchmark exposes the reuse–forgetting tradeoff in continual robot world models

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 18, 2026 arXiv preprint introduces a benchmark for continual world models in robot manipulation. Six simulated task curricula end with compositions of earlier action and perception primitives, separating reuse on a new task from forgetting on earlier tasks. The authors evaluate DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 with fine-tuning, replay, EWC and PackNet, plus a modular Prismatic World Model. In a diagnostic with an encoder pretrained on demonstrations from all benchmark tasks and then frozen, PWM reports mean forgetting score 3.85 and forward-transfer score 36.18 across six suites, compared with 34.97 and 38.11 for TD-MPC2 using that same encoder; lower forgetting and higher forward transfer are better. Without this privileged encoder, PWM is reported on par with TD-MPC2. The study is limited to simulation, three seeds, selected model settings, and no controlled parameter or compute budgets; it does not establish physical-robot performance.

Multimodality & World Models

A preprint reports that persistent spatial guidance improved object acquisition in a small blind-user study

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 18, 2026 arXiv preprint describes Touvigation, a phone-based assistive object-acquisition system that combines vision-language interpretation with persistent local 3D tracking. Its audio guidance shifts from room-scale orientation and step counts to body-relative reaching cues and tactile verification. In a within-subject study, the authors report 48 successful trials out of 48 with Touvigation, versus 28/48 with ByteDance's Doubao assistant and 41/48 without AI; they also report faster completion and lower workload. The finding is bounded to 12 blind adults, two unfamiliar rooms and short-term use after familiarization. Participants were from one regional community and one severity category; the system depends on a LiDAR-equipped chest-mounted iPhone, initial room scanning, stable tracking and cloud processing. The study has not been independently replicated.

Agents & Memory

A preprint reports that separating environmental facts from task procedures can help evolving agents

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 21, 2026 arXiv preprint proposes TTSE, which evolves environmental facts (FACT) separately from task-conditioned procedures (TIP), using interaction evidence to revise or retire both kinds of rule. The paper frames representation errors and execution errors as distinct contributors to agent failure and evaluates the framework on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, GDPevo, SOPBench and PinchBench. Authors report gains over selected baselines and an end-to-end PinchBench score of 0.7822 versus 0.7439 across three runs. In the SOPBench comparison, the reported dual-track gain over a single-track Bayesian-memory ablation is 2.99 percentage points over 892 tasks, but warehouse inspection contributes 78% of the gain; without that domain the reported gap is 0.67 points, and dangerous-goods tasks slightly favor single-track. The preprint reports no independent reproduction; its GDPevo dual-versus-TIP ablation is not formally significance-tested, and induced rules lack a structured verification gate.

Multimodality & World Models

A preprint reports label-free visual grounding gains at inference, but not from pilot distillation

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 21, 2026 arXiv preprint evaluates a model's sensitivity to question-relevant image crops as a label-free visual-evidence signal. On one 191-example V*Bench evaluation using three Qwen vision-language models, the authors report that selecting among 16 candidate crops raised fine-grained answer accuracy from about 70% to 85% and localized target regions 4.4–5.1 times above chance. Combining the evidence-gap signal with model confidence improved correctness prediction in the reported test. The authors also tested gated self-distillation: on HR-Bench, no gating, trajectory gating and token gating all scored below the base model, with more aggressive gating performing worse. The inference and training findings come from different tests and should remain separate. Results are author-reported, unreproduced and limited to one small benchmark; the crop search also requires 16 forward passes per example.

Across 42 RoboDojo simulation tasks and 2,100 trials, the authors report 22.48% average success and a score of 28.97, above the 40 public policies in their September 10 leaderboard snapshot. Evaluation & Science

A systematic review maps specification, verification, and enforcement gaps in LLM agent safety

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A June 2026 preprint surveys safety methods for LLM agents that take multi-step actions. The authors report a PRISMA 2020 review of 38 studies and organize the field around specification, verification, and enforcement. Their synthesis identifies errors in translating natural-language requirements into formal rules as a major bottleneck and notes that action-level blocking does not necessarily yield safe task completion. The strongest verifier-tax result comes from one study of tau-bench Airline/Retail tasks: in that study, per-proposal interception reached 94.1% in one setting while safe-success rates were below 5% in most tested configurations; the authors also report 2.0–2.8× token inflation for their Triad architectures. The study used two open-weight models, different 15/30-turn caps, and an unseeded stochastic user simulator, and has no independent reproduction identified. The survey itself was unregistered and conducted by a single reviewer without an inter-rater check, so it is useful field orientation rather than a settled comparative assessment.

INSPECT learns robot inspection views from assembly-assistant records

Evidence Bcredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A September 2026 preprint introduces INSPECT, which learns robot view preferences from egocentric assembly-assistant records by separating evidence needed to resolve a claim from observation changes a robot camera can reproduce, then calibrating relative views to robot pose. In offline fixed-lattice replay on gearbox assemblies, the authors report mean human-rated view utility of 1.205 and full verifiability of 41.7% across 132 matched trials, versus 1.045 and 34.8% for the current-view baseline. These are author-reported results in two constrained replay settings, not a live closed-loop deployment or independent reproduction.

A preprint evaluates CoSLR, a multi-agent system for systematic literature reviews with retrieval grounding and mandatory human checkpoints. Reasoning & Post-training

A targeted SFT and RL routing study reports gains on a bounded set of enterprise analytics skills

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

A preprint studies how to allocate supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) for long-horizon analytics agents using production-mirroring beta APIs. It separates retrospectively observed trajectories into imitation, lift and discovery regimes, then uses teacher support and reward-observable headroom to route features to SFT, RL or further environment work. Across 18 subsequent feature-specific experiments, the diagnostic reportedly predicted 15 trajectories. On GPT-OSS 120B, targeted SFT-then-RL produced positive point estimates on seven of eight advertiser skills versus a frontier control; five gains had paired 95% confidence intervals excluding zero and one skill had a supported regression. A matched comparison reports a higher seven-skill mean delta with 43% less incremental RL compute than uniform routing.

Reasoning & Post-training

ProcessLight applies step-level process supervision to traffic-signal control

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

ProcessLight/ STeP-PO trains an LLM traffic-signal controller with process-level credit: it scores intermediate traffic claims against observations, estimates each step’s effect on the final phase by removing it, and merges semantically similar steps into a tree for relative advantage assignment. The authors report Qwen2.5-3B trained on one synthetic flow and evaluated zero-shot in CityFlow on five real traffic-flow datasets from Jinan and Hangzhou; the method leads on most reported metrics. On two datasets, the paper reports lower average travel time than outcome-only RL, and a five-seed Holm-corrected analysis finds 81 of 90 baseline comparisons significant in its favor. The appendix also reports Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B and Llama3-8B variants and an alternate teacher, with results varying by dataset and metric. All findings remain paper-reported CityFlow simulation results for the paper’s intersection setup, not on-road deployment evidence; this review did not independently rerun the code.

Reasoning & Post-training

Listen Then Reason weights audio test-time RL by acoustic grounding

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

Listen Then Reason proposes Perception-Grounded Test-Time RL (PG-TTRL) for audio-language models. It estimates each generated answer’s reliance on audio by comparing token probabilities with full audio access and with audio attention masked; a grounding margin then weights the majority-vote advantage used for unlabeled test-time RL. The authors evaluate Qwen2.5-Omni 3B and 7B on MMAR and MMAU and report higher greedy and Avg@8 scores than standard TTRL in all four model/benchmark pairings, with gains over the base model reaching 4.6 points on MMAR and 4.9 on MMAU. Majority@8 does not improve uniformly. The method adapts LoRA weights on unlabeled prompts from the benchmark test sets; added inference compute and cross-domain transfer are not established, and no independent reproduction was conducted.

Agents & Memory

CICL reports improved first-file retrieval from decision-aware reranking on a small coding benchmark

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

The v4 preprint presents the Counterfactual-Inspired Context Layer (CICL), which ranks candidate repository evidence by estimated effect on an agent’s next action and can compress selected context into typed memory cards. On 50 SWE-bench Verified file-retrieval instances, the authors report that Qwen3.6-Plus reranking of BM25 top-50 candidates raises hit@1 from 0.58 to 0.78 and MRR@10 from 0.634 to 0.790. Top-10 recall is 0.714 versus 0.716, and the result measures file retrieval rather than patch success. The paper’s own terminology is counterfactual-inspired; it does not claim formal causal identification.

LumoTree reports modest replay gains for speculative verification on a hybrid language model

The LumoTree v3 preprint combines path-parallel tree verification with accepted-path state replay for a recurrent-hybrid language model. Agents & Memory

MemCalib measures whether language-model agents give stored facts the right influence

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

An October 8 v3 preprint introduces MemCalib, a benchmark that scores whether each fact in an agent’s stored memory should be ignored, used as bounded support, or allowed to control the answer. The authors report 15,000 examples across health, general assistance and coding. On its 1,500-example test set, the highest listed exact calibration is 28.40% for GPT-5.6-SOL; most evaluated models more often over-use memory than under-use it. The authors report that MemCalib-RL improves Qwen3-8B atom-use accuracy on this benchmark and transfers to a 150-query RPEval subset, while increasing under-use relative to the base model. Results are paper-reported and rely on constructed memory examples and rubric-guided model judging; real-world transfer is unknown.
