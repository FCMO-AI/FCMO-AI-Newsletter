1. Paper reports that fixed activation shifts recover most RL reward gains in diffusion models

Desk: reasoning_posttraining Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · active · importance 5/10 (Notable) Importance rationale: If sustained, the result could change how teams interpret and implement RL post-training for diffusion generators by suggesting that many measured reward gains can be recovered with fewer trainable parameters. Its relevance remains bounded to the tested models and metrics.

What changed: A 19 November preprint reports that small vectors shared across prompts, tokens and sampling seeds, when added to frozen diffusion-transformer block outputs, recover 90.0–96.3% of reported reward gains across six image models and 89.8%/111.9% on two video models. The paper compares a 0.29-million-parameter vector set with an 18.8-million-parameter rank-32 LoRA on SD3.5-M. The authors say they make no claim that the vectors are necessary or that RL updates are low-rank; video evidence covers two models. Why it matters: If reproduced, this mechanism could affect adaptation cost and how reward-tuned visual generators are understood. It does not establish a general account of RL or real-world user benefit.

Demonstrated: The preprint and full HTML are public; the methods, evaluation tables, pairwise study and limitations were inspected. The study reports about 500 judgments over 50 prompts and 25 raters and finds no significant preference difference from the RL-tuned outputs. Claimed: The performance figures are author-reported and were not independently reproduced. The linked project page could not be accessed in this reader. Open evidence: Independent reproduction of reward recovery and inspection of implementation/checkpoint artifacts. The paper's results do not imply necessity, low-rank RL updates, broader generalization or downstream user value. — FCMO-3D8BB7DBA1A7

2. Anthropic publishes a Claude-attributed subexponential #3-SAT algorithm claim with Lean materials

Desk: reasoning_posttraining Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · active · importance 9/10 (Paradigm-level) Importance rationale: If correct, deterministic subexponential exact counting for fixed-width k-CNF would contradict ETH and require reassessment of many conditional lower-bound results. This score represents consequence if true, not confidence; independent review is absent and practical impact is not shown.

What changed: Anthropic contributed a version-one preprint on October 27 describing a deterministic algorithm that counts satisfying assignments for fixed-width CNF formulas in subexponential time, including #3-SAT, and attributes the core algorithm to an internal Claude research model. Anthropic’s official repository includes the paper and a Lean 4 formalization. GitHub reports that the repository’s exact commit passed its verify (eth) CI workflow, which the README describes as a pinned Lean build, Comparator check and nanoda recheck. This repository-hosted result was not independently rerun here. The README explicitly says machine checks do not establish that the formal statement matches the paper; independent mathematical review remains open. The result is asymptotic and does not establish practical SAT performance. An independent external formal-methods audit later reports replaying Anthropic's exact pinned Lean proof with Lean, Comparator and nanoda, a fresh-environment replay, and a Lean bridge from the formal statement to textbook-style ETH in Anthropic's own machine model. The auditor explicitly leaves paper-to-statement and machine-model/input-encoding correspondence to prose and does not certify the paper's mathematical argument. This strengthens the reported reproducibility of the formal artifact, not the truth status of the theorem. A current external audit report adds a port of Anthropic’s pinned Lean proof and a checked bridge to a standard multitape Turing-machine/binary-encoding statement in one newer Lean environment. The auditor reports independent-kernel and fresh-environment checks, but also discloses that the proof port was AI-authored, was not byte-identical (47 of 809 files changed), and was reported by its own author. It still does not establish paper-to-Lean correspondence or mathematical correctness. Why it matters: If the claimed exact-counting algorithm is correct for the stated regime, it contradicts the Exponential Time Hypothesis and would affect research that relies on ETH-based lower bounds. The consequence is substantial, but the paper is a preprint, independent mathematical review is not established, and the source explicitly disclaims practical performance implications.

Claimed: Anthropic claims its internal Claude research model found a deterministic subexponential algorithm for exact #3-SAT counting, which refutes ETH. The paper states a 2^{O(n log log n / sqrt(log n))} time form for fixed-width counting; correctness and its precise correspondence to standard ETH remain subject to independent review.

Mechanism: The paper describes a tensor-network contraction method and derives an asymptotic subexponential bound for exact #k-SAT counting. The repository README gives a condensed tensor-contraction argument, but this activation did not inspect the full paper proof or independently derive the bound.

Strongest baseline: ETH is the standard conjecture that 3-SAT requires 2^{Ω(n)} worst-case time. The paper presents an asymptotic contradiction, not a measured comparison on practical formulas.

Regime: Fixed clause width k, exact satisfying-assignment counting, and asymptotic input size. The README explicitly says the result does not establish practical performance.

Open evidence gaps: No independent complexity theorist has certified the tensor-contraction argument or established that the printed paper theorem is correctly represented by the Lean statement. An external audit reports kernel-checked proof and model bridges, but explicitly leaves the paper-to-statement relation as human reading.; The independent audit now reports a Lean-checked bridge from Anthropic’s formal statement through its own machine model to a standard multitape Turing-machine/binary-encoding statement. It does not machine-check the correspondence between the printed paper theorem and Lean statement; the final identification with textbook ETH remains a reading.; No measured performance on practical SAT instances is reported; the repository characterizes the result as purely asymptotic.

  • An authorization-bounded agent selects and interprets measurements in a physical self-driving laboratory campaign — importance 5/10 · A new cs.AI preprint reports a language-model agent operating at the analysis-to-design interface of a self-driving laboratory. This is one chemistry campaign: the replay used prior data, at most three runs per budget, and no informed non-agent baseline. The paper also documents a researcher changing the approved budget directly in the campaign database, outside the approval workflow. — FCMO-44ED8670B649
  • ROGUE-AI introduces matched-control replication of reported agent security incidents — importance 5/10 · A new preprint describes ROGUE-AI, a framework that turns reported agent incidents into offline tasks with incident and matched-control conditions, checks harm from environment state, and tests models across repeated runs. The authors report 108 replications of 96 incidents in 38 classes, including five current model families. Their paper reports that 89% of replications reproduced the reported harm after a diagnosis-and-repair loop; in the main runs, model harm rates were 18–33% under the incident condition and no more than 1% in controls. These are author-reported simulation results, not independent measurements of deployed-agent risk. — FCMO-9F4A94314537

A 66-configuration study reports model–harness ranking reversals across agent tasks

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

Li et al. report 66 model–harness configurations across TUA-Bench, ALE-CLI and a 63-task non-H100 subset of Terminal-Bench 4. Rankings reverse by harness: on the paper’s Terminal-Bench subset, Claude leads GPT-6 Astra by 7.94 points under OpenHands but trails by 30.16 points under PI. GPT-6 Astra scores 60.32% at $4.66 per task under PI versus 52.38% at $19.94 under DeepSeek Harness. The authors release adapters, evaluation code and a dataset of 6,204 scored runs with reward, OpenRouter cost, token-use and agent-time fields; the card says 6,067 include conversations and 137 are results-only, mostly because environments did not start or native logs were lost. These remain author-reported results from one counted run per task; the package omits verifier files, test outputs and benchmark reference data, harness defaults and effective reasoning budgets differ, so the comparison does not isolate a causal harness effect or establish ranking stability.

Reasoning & Post-training

Fixed-budget reasoning training may trade accuracy near the rollout cap for weaker test-time scaling

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majordeveloping

A 19 November preprint reports that fixed rollout caps during reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards can improve accuracy around the training budget while reducing the benefit of larger inference budgets. Across Qwen3.5-4B, Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B, the authors report shorter responses and high-budget accuracy saturation; the strongest AIME replication uses three seeds, while most other runs use one. The paper reports that the effect can be useful where extra tokens add little, but harmful when longer reasoning helps. These are author-reported results on 4–5B-active-parameter models; independent reproduction, code audit and broader scale/algorithm generality remain open.

OpenAI reports lower protected-reasoning extraction rates with updated GPT-6.2 safeguards

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

In a 20 November 2026 article, OpenAI reports that an internal adaptive black-box red-team evaluation found lower protected-reasoning extraction rates with updated GPT-6.2 safeguards than with the original GPT-6 Astra safeguards. For extraction of reasoning from an earlier request, reported average attack success fell from 54.0% to 0.2%; for current-request extraction it fell from 22.9% to 0.9%. The article also reports that exact reasoning traces can improve a student model’s cyber-benchmark results relative to reasoning summaries in some tested regimes. These are provider-reported internal findings; the attack suite and underlying data were not independently inspected or reproduced.

Paper reports fixed activation shifts recover most RL reward gains in diffusion models

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A 19 Nov preprint reports that small, prompt-independent activation vectors added to frozen diffusion-transformer blocks can recover most of the reward improvement of several RL-tuned image and video generators. The authors report 90.0–96.3% recovery across six image models and 89.8%/111.9% on two video models; a 0.29-million-parameter vector set is compared with an 18.8-million-parameter rank-32 LoRA on SD3.5-M. These are author-reported results. The authors explicitly make no claim that the vectors are necessary or that RL updates are low-rank, and the video finding is limited to two models.

An authorization-bounded agent selects and interprets measurements in a physical self-driving laboratory campaign

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notabledeveloping

A new cs.AI preprint reports a language-model agent operating at the analysis-to-design interface of a self-driving laboratory. In one physical LC–MS campaign, it selected and interpreted measurements for a 28-well p-toluidine oxidation screen under researcher-approved limits; the agent could not directly control the instrument. The paper reports software checks refusing or deferring 12 of 465 tool calls and expert review of the product assignment. A replay of the same measured plate found the best condition around the agent's seventh measurement, versus 14.5 on average under random ordering. This is one chemistry campaign: the replay used prior data, at most three runs per budget, and no informed non-agent baseline. The paper also documents a researcher changing the approved budget directly in the campaign database, outside the approval workflow.

ROGUE-AI introduces matched-control replication of reported agent security incidents

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

A new preprint describes ROGUE-AI, a framework that turns reported agent incidents into offline tasks with incident and matched-control conditions, checks harm from environment state, and tests models across repeated runs. The authors report 108 replications of 96 incidents in 38 classes, including five current model families. Their paper reports that 89% of replications reproduced the reported harm after a diagnosis-and-repair loop; in the main runs, model harm rates were 18–33% under the incident condition and no more than 1% in controls. These are author-reported simulation results, not independent measurements of deployed-agent risk.

Agents & Memory

IBM reports model-dependent gains and deployment failures in agent memory systems

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfuldeveloping

An IBM-authored preprint reports that an agent-memory pipeline mined guidelines from AppWorld trajectories and was deployed in CUGA and watsonx Orchestrate. On AppWorld test_normal, the authors report DeepSeek-V3.2 gained 9.5 percentage points in task completion and 16.1 in scenario completion with all 191 guidelines. For gpt-oss-120b, a selected subset of about 29 reportedly scored 55.4% task completion on average over two runs, versus 51.2% with all 199 and 39.9% baseline. The paper also documents run noise, an incorrect high-support guideline, provenance loss, a scaffold-confounded comparison and silent API/write-path failures. These are single-paper results; configuration selection used test_normal and no independent reproduction or measured product impact is established.

TwinLab-Bench tests whether AI agents can design experiments in twins of real laboratories

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

A 19 November arXiv preprint introduces TwinLab-Bench, a benchmark of 52 tasks based on records from laboratories in physical sciences, life sciences and engineering. An actor agent receives lab notebooks, raw data and interim conclusions, requests experiments of its own design through a task-specific agent environment, and submits a report scored against a hidden result from later lab records. The authors report that Claude Fable 5.2 scores 45.0/100 on average, the highest among 13 evaluated models. This is an author-run benchmark result: the environment uses recorded results and task-specific calculators, not physical experiments during evaluation.

Brazil launches online federal AI ethical-impact self-assessment for SISP bodies

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

Brazil’s Ministry of Management and Innovation in Public Services (MGI) announced on 12 November that the online Ethical Impact Self-Assessment for public-sector AI is available to more than 250 SISP bodies. MGI reports that the World Bank advised its development and that the system was made available as open source. Official guidance describes a two-stage process: a questionnaire classifies project ethical risk as low, medium, high or excessive, then follow-up questions assess the team’s ethics and governance practices. The official platform landing page is login-gated to Brazilian public servants; this review did not sign in or establish agency use, binding effect or outcomes. The Ministry of Communications published a related announcement on 18 November.
