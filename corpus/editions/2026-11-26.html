2. Google DeepMind reports Lean-verified agent discovery of lower-cost quantum algorithms

Desk: reasoning_posttraining Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · active · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If the bounded resource reductions hold, formally verified AI-assisted algorithm search could change how researchers optimize quantum programs; the results remain first-party cost estimates beyond early hardware and are not execution results.

What changed: Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI report a Lean-based multi-agent workflow that discovered formally verified quantum programs for five tasks. The paper reports resource-cost reductions including 2.57× for one parallel Grover-search regime, 290× for a fixed-point arcsin routine, and 11.8×, 8.22× and 2.49× for simulations associated with inertial-confinement fusion, photochemistry and drug metabolism. These are first-party formal resource estimates under specified circuit models and baselines; the circuits were not run on quantum hardware, and the scientific simulations remain far beyond projected early fault-tolerant hardware. Why it matters: Formal search makes algorithm discovery possible in regimes where classical simulation cannot directly validate candidate circuits. If these bounded resource reductions reproduce under their stated cost models, the work shows a path for AI-assisted research to improve quantum algorithms before suitable hardware exists. The strongest practical gap is that the resulting scientific simulations still require many more resources than projected early fault-tolerant machines.

Claimed: The authors report that heterogeneous Gemini 4 agent teams, working against frozen Lean specifications with external proof checking, found lower-cost quantum programs across five tasks: up to 2.57× fewer expected oracle rounds for one parallel Grover find-all regime; a 290× spacetime-cost reduction for fixed-point arcsin under its 25-bit accuracy regime; and 11.8×, 8.22× and 2.49× spacetime-cost reductions for specified fusion, photochemistry and cytochrome P450 simulations.

Mechanism: Researchers specify quantum programs and correctness/resource-cost theorems in a Lean quantum Hoare-logic library. Gemini 4 agent teams search for candidate programs; an external verifier checks proofs against frozen specifications, allowed axioms and cost theorems. Teams exchange verified code through a shared wiki and unverified ideas through scheduled conferences; only externally verified solutions enter the shared record.

Strongest baseline: Published task-specific quantum-algorithm resource baselines under the paper’s stated encodings, cost models and error regimes; these were not independently recomputed.

Regime: A first-party study of five quantum-program tasks. The Grover result concerns parallel queries and expected oracle rounds in stated multi-target regimes; the arcsin result concerns 25-bit fixed-point inputs at stated accuracy; the scientific applications use specified Hamiltonians, encodings, error budgets and logical spacetime-volume cost Q×T. No result is an executed hardware performance measurement.

Open evidence gaps: The proof pipeline and resource estimates have not been independently replayed or recalculated in this activation.; The paper does not execute these circuits on quantum hardware or establish feasibility on early fault-tolerant machines.; Generalization beyond the five selected tasks, encodings and cost models is unknown.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09822 · https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/

3. AISI finds attack-policy choices can sharply change measured AI monitor miss rates

Desk: evaluation_science Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · active · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If attack-policy choice shifts reported monitor false-negative rates in this control-evaluation regime, it changes the evidence used to assess AI-agent safeguards. The reported study is limited to one agent-monitor setup and synthetic attack proxies.

What changed: The UK AI Security Institute reports that attack generation method and optimization effort changed an ex-production OpenAI monitor’s false-negative rate in a fixed control-evaluation setup. The study reports rates from 0.5% to 20.8% across reinforcement-learning checkpoints and 6.4% to 23.0% across the strongest tested methods. Attacks were developed against proxy monitors and transferred to a held-out GPT-5.4 monitor. The authors emphasize that synthetic transcripts are only proxies for deployment behavior; this single agent-monitor case study does not establish deployment miss rates or general performance of monitor systems. Why it matters: Monitor evaluations can appear reassuring when they use weak or lightly optimized attack policies, even when stronger policies produce higher measured miss rates in the same test setup. The work highlights attack-policy selection and proxy validity as separate evidence gaps for claims about deployed monitor reliability.

Demonstrated: The inspected primary paper reports this single case study. No independent reproduction or deployment-performance measurement was conducted.

Claimed: The authors report that measured monitor false-negative rate changed substantially with attack-policy choice: 0.5%–20.8% across reinforcement-learning optimization levels and 6.4%–23.0% across methods at their strongest observed settings. All policies were developed against proxy monitors and tested on a held-out GPT-5.4 monitor.

Mechanism: Attack transcripts were generated by basic prompting, feedback-optimized prompting, reinforcement learning, or transcript editing, at different optimization levels. The generated transcripts were scored by a held-out monitor while evaluation tasks remained fixed.

Strongest baseline: Weak/basic or lightly optimized attack policies in the same evaluation setup. The paper does not establish deployment representativeness of any policy.

Regime: One agent-monitor pair and a fixed set of 25 control tasks in a hypothetical deployment scenario; synthetic attacks and one ex-production OpenAI monitor.

Limitations: The empirical results are first-party author reports; no independent reproduction or raw-data reanalysis was performed here.; The case study uses one ex-production OpenAI monitor (GPT-5.4), one agent model (Qwen 3.8 27B), a hypothetical deployment scenario and a fixed set of 25 control tasks.; The attack transcripts are synthetic proxies. The authors report artifacts that can affect monitor judgments and say that convincing arguments for proxy relevance remain an open problem.; The study tests a limited set of attack-generation methods and optimization levels; it does not establish real-world monitor miss rates or generalize to other deployments.

Open evidence gaps: The reported false-negative rates and transfer results have not been independently reproduced or recalculated from released data in this activation.; The paper does not establish when its synthetic attack-policy transcripts represent dangerous behavior in deployment.; Generality beyond one agent model, monitor, scenario and 25 control tasks remains unknown.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934 · https://www.aisi.gov.uk/research/attack-policy-selection-can-mislead-monitor-evaluators

Reasoning & Post-training

DART expands accepted math-reasoning traces for post-training at matched trace budgets

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

A NeurIPS 2026 workshop preprint introduces Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces (DART), which takes a rejected greedy math trace, selects a non-final step using position-weighted uncertainty, proposes a deliberately different alternative with reference-answer guidance, and greedily completes a replacement trace. On greedy-unsolved problems from DeepMath-103K and OpenMathInstruct-2 using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the authors report more problems recovered than full-trace Monte Carlo sampling at matched completed-trace counts: 37% more in the abstracted DeepMath result, with nearly identical estimated generation cost per recovered problem. After supervised fine-tuning on mined traces, DART has the highest point estimate in five of six MATH-500/AIME evaluations and up to a five-point gain over Monte Carlo sampling. These are paper-reported results; evaluation uses final-answer checking, reference answers are used during mining, and training-seed/checkpoint-selection uncertainty is not included in the reported intervals.

The metric definition, independent comparison and crop-yield effects are not reported. RDA plans a staged introduction to 15 small and medium hydroponic farms from 2027.
