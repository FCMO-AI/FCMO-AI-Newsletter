1. FrontierPhysics benchmarks end-to-end AI-agent research across nine physics domains

Desk: evaluation_science Evidence: A · credible_unconfirmed · developing · importance 6/10 (Major) Importance rationale: If the reported evaluation is sound, a multi-domain benchmark built from active physicists’ own open-ended research could materially change how scientific-agent capability is measured and expose the gap between task correctness and publishable research. The score reflects that consequence, not confidence in the paper’s results.

What changed: A 4 November arXiv preprint introduces FrontierPhysics, an end-to-end benchmark built from physicists’ own research projects. The authors report 56 tasks from 42 physicists across nine domains, with agents required to plan research, run analyses or experiments, and produce verifiable outputs and manuscripts. Across 22 models and 26 configurations, they report pass rates below 30%; Claude Opus 5.5 is highest at 27.4%. The authors also report manually correcting nine false failures among 168 GPT-6 Astra xhigh rollouts. These are paper-reported results, not an independent rerun. Why it matters: The benchmark tests whether agents can carry a multi-stage research project through to a correct, defensible manuscript, rather than solve a fixed problem or reproduce a supplied paper. If its task curation and grading prove robust, it could sharpen capability comparisons and expose where agents still need expert judgment. The current evidence supports reporting the benchmark and its authors’ results, not a general conclusion that today’s agents can or cannot perform scientific research.

Demonstrated: The arXiv submission and the paper’s reported methods and results are verified as published claims. No benchmark outcome was independently reproduced here.

Claimed: Authors report 56 end-to-end tasks from 42 physicists across nine physics domains; evaluation of 22 models in 26 configurations; highest Pass@1 of 27.4% for Claude Opus 5.5; and a corrected 20.2% pass rate (34/168) for GPT-6 Astra xhigh after manual correction of nine false failures.

Mechanism: Each task begins from an open research prompt based on a contributor’s own project. Agents must investigate literature, plan and conduct research, and produce numerical artifacts plus a manuscript. The benchmark combines deterministic verifiers with task-specific expert rubrics; failure analysis includes manual review.

Strongest baseline: The paper compares its benchmark design with earlier physics and scientific-agent benchmarks; results are within its own FrontierPhysics v0.1 tasks and should not be treated as a matched universal capability ranking.

Regime: Physics only: 56 tasks across nine domains; 22 models in 26 configurations; three trials per model-task and a 10-hour timeout cap per rollout. The study uses different agent harnesses and shared reviewer settings as described by the authors.

Limitations: All benchmark performance numbers are author-reported in an arXiv preprint and were not independently reproduced in this activation.; The same reviewer model, GPT-5.6 Sol, graded all rollouts; the paper reports a Claude Opus 5.5 regrade was more lenient on its own model family.; The paper reports nine false failures corrected during manual review of GPT-6 Astra xhigh rollouts, showing that the benchmark’s error rate and pass counts are sensitive to evaluation review.; This physics task sample does not establish performance on other scientific disciplines or real-world research programs.

Open evidence gaps: No independent reproduction or external audit of the task set, rubric judgments or model pass rates was inspected.; Although the paper says workspace and trajectory files are collected for Hugging Face datasets, this activation did not identify or inspect the corresponding public dataset and code release.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910

Inference & Systems

Turn-level action selection improves reported agent success with substantial token overhead

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notabledeveloping

A 4 November arXiv preprint evaluates turn-level best-of-N (Turn-BoN): at each step, a generator proposes 4 or 8 candidate tool actions and a separate language-model selector chooses one before it is executed. On 89 Terminal-Bench v2.1 tasks across four trials, the authors report higher pass rates for Qwen3.6-35B and K2-Horizon-375B generators when paired with Kimi-K3 or DeepSeek V4.1 Flash selectors. Reported gains include K2-Horizon 73.3% to 77.9% at N=4 and Qwen3.6 47.5% to 52.8% at N=4 or 55.2% at N=8. The authors also report 40–50% fewer turns and reduced looping, while output tokens rise to about 3.6–8.2 times baseline. These preliminary paper-reported results use two generator models and stronger selectors; they were not independently reproduced.

Agents & Memory

Evidence-gated authority in language-model review trades fewer corruptions of correct proposals for missed welfare-only repairs

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

In a September 2026 paper, Fuad Ali compares broad reviewer override with a rule that lets a reviewer replace an upstream proposal only after it supplies a mechanically valid certificate of a binding-constraint violation and a compliant repair. The paper reports paired experiments with Qwen3-8B and Mistral-Small-24B on 96 synthetic spatial-policy tasks and a 96-task portfolio-transfer benchmark. The gate prevents changes to correct proposals by construction, but also bars welfare improvements to compliant, suboptimal proposals. A prospective retain/replace/escalate interface protects correct proposals for Qwen, which then declines welfare-only repairs; Mistral still replaces and corrupts all 48 correct proposals in that test. These are author-reported results from quantized local models and structured synthetic tasks, not evidence of deployment prevalence or a model-independent safety guarantee.

A 2026-11-04 arXiv paper introduces BEAKER, an offline benchmark of 1,000 expert-curated question-answer samples across 11 image and video task types for multimodal models considered as embodied systems in self-driving chemical laboratories. A paper proposes auditability as a fourth machine-unlearning criterion and reports a two-stage method with dataset tradeoffs

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

The Nov. 4 arXiv preprint “Widen the Gap, Hide the Gap” treats auditability—whether a provider can distinguish requested-forget examples from retained examples under a chosen statistic—as a fourth criterion for machine unlearning. It proposes GapWiden, which trains an audit-statistic gap into the unlearned model, and GapVeil, an inference-time rejection sampler intended to reduce disclosure of that gap. The authors report evaluations over eleven unlearning methods on TOFU and MUSE. Results are paper-reported: auditability rises toward 0.99 on MUSE, but retain performance on MUSE News falls from 0.569 to 0.380. These benchmark results do not establish deletion from a deployed model or proof of deletion for an individual request.
