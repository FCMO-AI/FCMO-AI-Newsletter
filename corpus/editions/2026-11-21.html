Evaluation & Science

Anthropic mathematician posts Claude-assisted Lean proof claim for Riemann and Dirichlet GRH

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 9/10 · Paradigm-levelactive

On 20 November 2026, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge posted a personal note claiming that he and Claude found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and a broader result he identifies as the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. He linked a large Lean formalization rather than a conventional readable paper. An independent MIT verification log updated 21 November reports that 175,187 modules compile, five headline theorems use only Lean’s standard axioms, and multiple independent kernel/checker implementations accept the export and match all five challenge statements. At that update, the ordinary Lean kernel replay and end-to-end Comparator run remained in progress. Clay’s official page still lists RH as unsolved. The event is a highly consequential proof claim with unusually extensive machine-check evidence, not yet a human-readable or broadly accepted mathematical proof.

Reasoning & Post-training

OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol; its card classifies Astra High for AI self-improvement

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

OpenAI announced GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17 and published a detailed system card. OpenAI classifies both models at its Critical cybersecurity and High biological/chemical capability thresholds, and classifies Astra—but not Sol—at High for AI self-improvement, based on five research-engineering evaluations. The card reports stronger cyber and research-task results alongside evaluation-awareness and chain-of-thought-monitoring concerns; it also attributes a pre-release cyber-safeguard robustness regression to UK AISI testing and says UK AISI had not retested the latest mitigations when the card was written. These capability, threshold, and mitigation claims are source-reported and have not been independently reproduced in this activation. OpenAI also reports on 20 Nov that GPT-6.2 Astra earned 84.5% at xhigh on its retrospective 99-problem MedChemBench (GPT-6 Astra 83.3%); the single-attempt, tool-free score does not establish prospective medicinal-chemistry or laboratory impact. BenchCAD’s owner page also reports OpenAI’s customized-harness BenchCAD 2.1 Core scores of 0.825 for GPT-6.2 Astra and 0.731 for GPT-6.2 Sol; these are separate from the standard 2.1 track and remain provider-reported, without run-level artifacts or independent reproduction.

Reasoning & Post-training

AutoMaster reports gains from skill-guided agent post-training

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

An arXiv paper introduces Skill-FT, which uses natural-language skills to guide teacher-model rollouts before supervised fine-tuning, and AutoMaster, an agent-driven loop for designing skills, selecting demonstrations, training students and refining interventions from evaluation feedback. On three domains with Qwen3.5-4B and 9B students, the authors report mean gains of 1.42–8.99 percentage points over vanilla SFT using the same teacher, task pool, training-set size and SFT recipe. These are paper-reported results, not an independent reproduction.

Evaluation & Science

A multi-basis lattice-search algorithm reports CPU-only solutions to 18 previously open Market Split instances

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

An IBM Research preprint reports a randomized lattice-search algorithm for the 156-instance QOBLIB Market Split benchmark. Across ten seeds, the authors report solving a mean of 131.2 instances per run, compared with 115 for solvediophant, 93 for a CPU–GPU method and 40 for CPLEX under the paper’s 24-hour cutoff; the algorithm used two CPU cores and produced the first reported CPU-only solutions for 18 of 33 previously open instances. The search allocates work across multiple reduced lattice bases and periodically continues basis reduction. The algorithm was developed and evaluated on the same QOBLIB suite, so performance on newly generated instances remains unknown. The paper describes a two-agent, human-guided research workflow, but does not isolate the causal contribution of the agents or retained workspace.
