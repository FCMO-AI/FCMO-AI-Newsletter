NVIDIA underwrites up to $105B of OpenAI-linked 4.25 GW data-center leases as it expands into AI infrastructure finance

Evidence Astrongly_supportedImpact 7/10 · Very majoractive

industry_compute_infrastructure · development FCMO-955A495823F7 · verified 2026-09-06T21:17:00Z

Importance: 7/10 — Very major. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
If the disclosed agreements are realized in the stated regime, this is very major for the structure of frontier AI compute: a dominant accelerator vendor is using guarantees and ecosystem financing to unlock multi-gigawatt capacity for a frontier lab, with OpenAI contracting for approximately 8 GW at one campus while NVIDIA separately pursues hundreds of billions of third-party infrastructure capital. That can materially change compute availability, financing power and vertical dependence across the AI industry. It is not 8-10 because most capacity is years from service, NVIDIA's signed initial credit support covers 4.25 GW while extension support is optional, the $500B financing figure comes from preliminary MOUs, and construction, permitting, power, financing and tenant risk remain substantial.

NVIDIA, OpenAI and SB Energy announced on August 17, 2026 a PORTS-Pike infrastructure arrangement under which OpenAI has agreed to secure approximately 8 GW of IT capacity in Pike County, Ohio. NVIDIA is the exclusive AI-compute infrastructure provider, will invest $1.5B in SB Energy, and provides land/power/shell credit support for the initial 4.25 GW; NVIDIA's later fiscal Q2 2027 Form 10-Q discloses guarantees capped at $105B for those initial obligations and an option for additional support covering roughly 3.8 GW as the site scales. The nine-phase site uses 20-year OpenAI leases; OpenAI says the first 800 MW is expected in 2028, while NVIDIA's filing describes service beginning in fiscal 2029. The same 10-Q shows a broader strategic shift: $279B of supply/capacity commitments, $36B of AI-cloud agreements and August MOUs intended to mobilize more than $500B of third-party AI-infrastructure capital, although those MOUs are explicitly preliminary.

Why it matters: The AI compute bottleneck is increasingly land, power, construction and financing rather than accelerator supply alone. A GPU vendor using its balance sheet and ecosystem contracts to de-risk multi-gigawatt customer infrastructure can materially change who can finance frontier-scale training/inference and can tighten vertical coupling among chip vendor, cloud/intermediary, capital provider and model lab. OpenAI's primary announcement says it has agreed to secure approximately 8 GW at the campus, but the full capacity is phased and conditional; NVIDIA's signed credit support is for the initial 4.25 GW, with the remaining roughly 3.75-3.8 GW covered by an option rather than equivalent current guarantee exposure.

Technical record

Mechanism
NVIDIA provides capped credit guarantees on defined portions of land, power and shell lease/payment obligations for SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus on behalf of an OpenAI affiliate. Guarantees become effective as nine construction phases enter service, decline as OpenAI performs lease payments, and can terminate on specified conditions including satisfactory OpenAI credit status. SB Energy builds, owns and operates; OpenAI leases capacity and says it starts paying as completed capacity becomes available; the campus is tied to NVIDIA AI infrastructure. In parallel NVIDIA uses supply commitments, AI-cloud service agreements, equity investments and financing-platform MOUs to remove capital/infrastructure bottlenecks around deployment of its compute.
Demonstrated result
The August 17 counterpart announcements establish the commercial arrangement and OpenAI's approximately 8-GW capacity agreement. NVIDIA's later SEC-filed 10-Q discloses up to $105B aggregate guarantee exposure for the initial approximately 4.25 GW, with nine phases, 20-year leases and an option for support of roughly 3.8 GW more. The filing also reports $279B of supply/capacity commitments as of July 26, 2026 and $36B of AI-cloud agreements.
Publisher claim
NVIDIA and SB Energy say the arrangement addresses land, power and shell constraints; OpenAI says it is ordering the capacity against projected frontier-training and product demand and that the first 800 MW is expected in 2028. NVIDIA's filing also says August MOUs with large capital providers are designed to mobilize more than $500B of third-party capital over time for AI infrastructure.
Strongest baseline
Traditional hyperscaler/model-lab procurement in which customers independently secure land, power, shell, financing and accelerator purchases without material vendor-backed credit support.
Regime
Long-horizon U.S. AI data-center infrastructure; PORTS-Pike campus in Ohio; OpenAI agreement for approximately 8 GW IT capacity, NVIDIA signed support for initial 4.25 GW plus an option for roughly 3.75-3.8 GW extension; first 800 MW expected by OpenAI in 2028; 20-year leases.
Implementation
Commercial agreements and initial NVIDIA credit support are signed/disclosed, but construction/service remains future and conditional. OpenAI says the first 800 MW is expected in 2028; NVIDIA's filing describes nine phases beginning service in fiscal 2029, which is compatible with calendar 2028 under NVIDIA's fiscal calendar. The additional roughly 3.8 GW support is an NVIDIA option. The >$500B capital-platform arrangements are memorandums of understanding and explicitly may not lead to definitive agreements.
Compute / cost
NVIDIA guarantee exposure is capped at $105B for the SB Energy/OpenAI-linked initial arrangement. NVIDIA reports total maximum gross guarantee exposure of $108.5B including $3.5B for other AI-cloud land/power/shell guarantees. NVIDIA also announced a $1.5B equity investment in SB Energy. Separate commitments include $279B supply/capacity, $36B AI-cloud agreements, and preliminary financing platforms targeting >$500B third-party capital.
Novelty
The material shift is vertical expansion from accelerator/system supplier toward infrastructure underwriter and capital-market coordinator, using NVIDIA's balance sheet/ecosystem position to unlock customer compute deployment.
Reproducibility
Contractual/financial disclosure is independently auditable across NVIDIA's SEC-filed 10-Q plus matching OpenAI and SB Energy primary announcements, but future physical capacity, utilization and economics cannot yet be reproduced or verified because the data centers are not in service.

Evidence claims

  • DEMONSTRATED — NVIDIA's 10-Q discloses August 2026 guarantees capped at $105B supporting approximately 4.25 GW of OpenAI-linked IT load at SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus.
  • DEMONSTRATED — OpenAI announced an agreement to secure approximately 8 GW of IT capacity at PORTS-Pike under a 20-year lease structure, while SB Energy builds, owns and operates the data center and NVIDIA supplies the site's AI-compute infrastructure.
  • DEMONSTRATED — NVIDIA's signed initial support covers 4.25 GW and NVIDIA has an option for additional support covering roughly 3.75-3.8 GW as the site scales.
  • CLAIMED — NVIDIA's August financing-platform MOUs are designed to mobilize more than $500B of third-party AI-infrastructure capital over time.
  • INFERRED — NVIDIA is becoming a material infrastructure-finance coordinator in addition to an accelerator supplier, which can alter frontier-lab compute access and industry vertical dependence.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • OpenAI's approximately 8 GW is contracted future IT capacity, not currently operational compute; capacity is delivered in phases and remains dependent on construction readiness, infrastructure, permits, environmental review and financing.
  • NVIDIA's $105B cap is a guarantee exposure, not a statement that NVIDIA will spend or pay $105B; exposure declines with tenant performance and covers defined portions rather than full site cost.
  • NVIDIA's signed credit support is for the initial 4.25 GW. Support for the remaining roughly 3.75-3.8 GW is an option rather than equivalent current guarantee exposure, even though OpenAI says it has agreed to secure approximately 8 GW of site capacity overall.
  • The >$500B figure concerns preliminary MOUs intended to mobilize third-party capital and may never become definitive agreements.
  • Power contracted in GW does not translate directly into delivered accelerator FLOPs; hardware generation, utilization, PUE, networking and tenant workload mix are unknown.
  • OpenAI's first-800-MW 2028 date is an expectation, not delivered capacity.
