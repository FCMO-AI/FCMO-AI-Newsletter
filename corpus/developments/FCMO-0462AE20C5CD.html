Claude agents complete a machine-checked Lean formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in a verifier-grounded multi-agent campaign

Evidence Astrongly_supportedImpact 7/10 · Very majoractive

repository_release · development FCMO-0462AE20C5CD · verified 2026-09-06T22:23:00Z

Importance: 7/10 — Very major. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
Very major if the central capability claim generalizes: frontier agents can now convert thousands of pages of difficult modern mathematics into a fully machine-checkable proof artifact on an approximately two-week timescale when supplied a formal verifier and dependency-aware collaboration scaffold. That could transform formal mathematics, theorem review and other verifier-rich technical workflows. It is not scored 8+ because the feat formalizes known mathematics rather than discovering a new field-level result, uses an unusually strong verifier, depends on substantial prior formal libraries and human source material, and the exact 11-day autonomy/cost process remains first-party rather than independently instrumented. Importance is consequence if true, separate from confidence.

Anthropic released a complete Lean 4 formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem and reports that dozens of Claude agents produced it largely autonomously over 11 days using roughly six billion output tokens from an internal research model comparable to Claude Fable 5.1. The artifact is unusually auditable: the public repository states that a from-scratch Lean build checks all modules, comparator verifies that the proved theorem matches Mathlib's FLT statement without extra axioms, and nanoda independently replays the environment with a second Lean kernel implementation. Kevin Buzzard, leader of the Imperial College FLT formalization effort, independently compiled the repository and ran comparator and reports that it checks out. This is not discovery of new mathematics: the system formalizes an established Wiles/Taylor-Wiles route and adapts prior human-written Lean material. The central AI result is therefore large-scale autoformalization under a strong executable verifier, plus evidence that explicit dependency/state scaffolding was necessary for long-horizon agent coordination.

Technical record

Mechanism
Dozens of Claude agents operated through a Claude Code-based harness and Prove2Me. Prove2Me maintained a directed acyclic graph of theorem statements, separated theorem statements from proofs to reduce compilation/resource coupling, and attached searchable natural-language descriptions to support reuse. Lean served as the executable verifier for intermediate and final work. Anthropic reports that earlier attempts without this coordination structure lost track of project state and stopped collaborating effectively.
Demonstrated result
A public Lean repository contains an end-to-end formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem. The repository reports a from-scratch build under Lean 4.33.1, comparator verification against a Mathlib-only statement of FLT with no extra axioms, and replay through nanoda, an independent Lean kernel implementation. Kevin Buzzard independently states that he compiled the code base and ran comparator and that it checks out.
Publisher claim
Anthropic reports that dozens of Claude agents, using a general-purpose internal model roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1, completed the formalization largely autonomously in 11 days, produced about 13 million lines of Lean, proved about 30,300 theorems with roughly 29,500 used in the final proof, and consumed about six billion output tokens. Human mathematical guidance is described as occasional high-level priority instructions.
Strongest baseline
The strongest qualitative baseline is the ongoing multi-year Imperial College London human formalization effort led by Kevin Buzzard, but it follows a different modern proof path and has different goals such as Mathlib-quality reusable infrastructure and human-readable exposition, so direct time or cost ratios are not matched. The most informative internal ablation is Anthropic's own reported failure of earlier swarms that lacked Prove2Me-style shared theorem-state coordination.
Regime
Known theorem with a mature human proof route, large existing formal libraries, an exact proof checker and highly decomposable intermediate obligations. This is a far more verifier-rich regime than open-ended empirical science, agent strategy or AI R&D.
Implementation
The full proof artifact is public under Apache-2.0. Anthropic describes it as a research artifact rather than a maintained Mathlib-quality library. Prove2Me is separately public as an open collaboration platform.
Compute / cost
Anthropic reports roughly six billion output tokens. A complete first-party inference/GPU dollar cost, failed-branch cost and human-attention ledger was not published in the recovered primary materials, so the research desk does not convert token count into a definitive project cost.
Novelty
The mathematical theorem and proof route are not novel. The novelty is the scale and speed of end-to-end AI autoformalization, the public verifiable artifact, and the coordination pattern showing that explicit dependency state plus executable checking can sustain a large multi-agent technical campaign.
Reproducibility
Artifact correctness is unusually reproducible because the proof repository and verifier stack are public. The production process is less reproducible: the exact internal model, full agent traces, scheduler, compute allocation and complete failed-run history are not public, so an external group cannot yet reproduce the claimed 11-day generation regime exactly.

Evidence claims

  • DEMONSTRATED — The released repository contains a complete Lean formalization whose final statement matches Mathlib's Fermat's Last Theorem statement and is accepted without extra axioms beyond Lean's standard three.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Kevin Buzzard independently compiled the repository and ran comparator and reports that it checks out.
  • CLAIMED — Anthropic reports that dozens of Claude agents completed the formalization largely autonomously in 11 days using about six billion output tokens from an internal model roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1.
  • CLAIMED — Anthropic reports that early attempts failed because agents lost project state and collaboration degraded, while the successful campaign used Prove2Me's theorem DAG and searchable shared structure.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The result demonstrates large-scale autoformalization of known mathematics, not discovery of a new proof or new mathematical theorem.
  • INFERRED — The failed-to-successful coordination change supports the hypothesis that durable typed dependency state and executable verification are first-class causal variables for long-horizon agent reliability.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • The system formalized known mathematics along an established proof route; it did not discover Fermat's Last Theorem or a novel proof.
  • The final repository adapts substantial human-written Mathlib, Imperial FLT and flt-regular material and should not be described as 13 million lines generated from a blank mathematical substrate.
  • The strongest autonomy, duration and token-use figures are Anthropic process claims; external verification confirms the artifact far more strongly than it confirms the exact production process.
  • Lean provides an unusually exact verifier. Transfer to open-ended science or AI R&D, where correctness cannot be checked locally and cheaply, is uncertain.
  • The artifact is extremely large and machine-oriented; verifier acceptance does not imply readable, maintainable or Mathlib-quality mathematical exposition.
  • A matched human-versus-agent cost comparison is unavailable because the Imperial project follows a different modern proof path and optimizes for reusable library contributions and exposition in addition to theorem completion.
  • Buzzard stresses that the work tells mathematics essentially nothing new; this sharply limits any claim that the event is a scientific-discovery breakthrough even while supporting the autoformalization capability claim.
  • Early agent attempts reportedly lost track of state and failed to collaborate effectively, contradicting a model-only explanation of the final success and making orchestration/state management load-bearing.
  • Human-review integration remains a bottleneck: a giant checked artifact is not equivalent to concise reusable library code or a comprehensible exposition.

Open evidence gaps

  • open · reproduction_missing — No independent group has reproduced the reported 11-day generation process with a comparable model/harness under matched budget. — next: Replay a medium-scale formalization campaign with public models and fixed token/parallelism budgets, comparing free-form collaboration against theorem-DAG coordination.
  • open · regime_unclear — It is unclear how much of the result transfers from formal mathematics with a near-perfect verifier to open-ended empirical research where evaluation is noisy and delayed. — next: Construct matched verifier-rich and verifier-poor research tasks with similar decomposition depth and compare intervention, false-discovery and recovery rates.
  • open · other — Complete cost accounting for inference, orchestration, failed branches, compilation and human guidance is unavailable. — next: Obtain or estimate a process ledger that separates successful proof tokens, abandoned branches, verifier compute, wall-clock, and active human minutes.
