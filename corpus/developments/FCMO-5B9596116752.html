Google announces Gemini 4 Carbon with vendor-reported cybersecurity and long-horizon results

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 7/10 · Very majoractive

model_or_system_release · development FCMO-5B9596116752 · verified 2026-10-29T01:14:03.805361Z

Importance: 7/10 — Very major. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
If Google’s reported cybersecurity and sustained coding capabilities hold under independent, matched evaluation, they could materially change frontier model selection, cyber-defense workflows and agent deployment risk. This score reflects consequence if true; results remain vendor-reported and initial access is restricted.

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Oct 28, 2026, with initial access for a subset of cyber defenders and broader access described as forthcoming. Google reports strong cybersecurity and agentic benchmark results and internal improvements in secure-code generation and alignment-related measures. At review time, Artificial Analysis lists Carbon at Intelligence Index 59 and the CWE-bench owner lists it at 71% deterministic pass@1 and 81% pass@4 over 120 private held-out tasks. Those leaderboard entries provide third-party benchmark-owner reporting, but this review did not inspect raw outputs or rerun either evaluation; SEC-bench Pro did not list Carbon and the linked ExploitGym repository was inaccessible. The announcement lists introductory API prices, but current public pricing docs did not show a Carbon SKU and no API access or billing was verified. The Carbon post links a Safety and Security PDF, but the linked document is the report identified in Google’s Gemini 4 Argon model card and Argon safety post; this check found no Carbon-specific model card or safety report in those official links. The PDF was too large for this review to inspect, so Carbon-specific safety-evaluation coverage remains unverified.

Why it matters: A model that combines long-running coding capability with strong vulnerability discovery, validation and repair could affect cyber-defense workflows and frontier-model evaluation. The announcement makes the result worth tracking, while its vendor-reported metrics, restricted initial access and lack of independent reproduction limit what can be concluded about actual capability or deployment impact.

Evidence claims

  • DEMONSTRATED — Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Oct 28, 2026, saying initial access would go to a subset of cyber defenders and broader developer, business and consumer access would follow later. The announcement lists introductory API prices of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with $4 and $20 prices after the introductory period. The current Gemini API pricing page lists those dated prices for Argon but did not show a Carbon SKU during this review; Carbon access and billing were not verified.
  • CLAIMED — Google reports that Gemini 4 Carbon scored 59 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8% on ExploitGym and 71.0% on CWE-bench. At this review, Artificial Analysis independently lists Carbon (high) at Index score 59, and CWE-bench lists 71% deterministic pass@1 and 81% pass@4 (71%/83% on its three-judge panel). SEC-bench Pro’s visible leaderboard does not list Carbon; ExploitGym’s linked repository was inaccessible in this review. No raw Carbon outputs were audited or benchmarks rerun.
  • CLAIMED — Google reports internal improvements over Argon in secure-code generation and alignment-related measures, including more than 2x uplift on an internal secure-code benchmark, nearly 80% fewer high-severity monitoring alerts in internal live coding, about 2x fewer unsupported claims, and 26% lower overreach with 29% lower under-service. These remain Google-reported internal measurements, not independently validated here.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The reviewed sources establish public leaderboard entries for Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and CWE-bench, but do not establish raw outputs, full evaluation reproducibility, matched conditions across all claimed benchmarks, broader customer outcomes, deployment safety or general reliability. SEC-bench Pro does not currently list Carbon; ExploitGym protocol access failed.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • All model performance and safety metrics in the announcement are company-reported; they were not independently reproduced in this review.
  • Benchmark-specific protocols, inference settings, tool access, guardrails and scoring conditions were not established across the cited results.
  • Initial access is restricted to selected cyber defenders; broad developer, business and consumer availability is not yet established by this announcement.
  • Posted introductory prices do not establish realized cost, latency, reliability or comparative economic value.
  • Artificial Analysis reports an independent index score, but per-task Carbon outputs and the complete evaluation trace were not inspected in this review.
  • The SEC-bench Pro leaderboard page did not list Carbon during this check, so it does not corroborate Google’s reported 88.2% result.
  • ExploitGym repository retrieval failed; its protocol and Google-reported 54.8% result remain unassessed here.
  • Google’s current API pricing documentation lists the dated Argon prices but did not show a Carbon SKU in this review; announced Carbon rates and restricted rollout were not verified through API use.
  • The Carbon announcement’s linked Safety and Security PDF is identified by Google’s Argon model card and safety post as the Argon report. The PDF exceeded the browser tool’s retrieval limit in this review; no Carbon-specific report/card was found in the checked official links. This bounded result does not establish that no such document exists elsewhere or that Carbon was not evaluated.
