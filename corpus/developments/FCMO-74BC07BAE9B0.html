Cisco reports cross-vendor H200/MI350X inference above standalone throughput sum

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notableactive

dataset_or_benchmark · development FCMO-74BC07BAE9B0 · verified 2026-10-12T00:20:32Z

Importance: 5/10 — Notable. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
If validated and reproducible across workloads, combining different accelerator vendors for prefill and decode could make installed compute more flexible and reduce the cost of phase-specific capacity planning. Current evidence is one submitter's benchmark result with an inaccessible raw workbook and one configuration, so it is notable rather than proof of broadly improved economics or interoperability.

Cisco's September 21, 2026 report describes an MLPerf Inference v6.1 GPT-OSS 120B Server submission that split prefill across eight NVIDIA H200 GPUs and decode across eight AMD Instinct MI350X GPUs over a Cisco Ethernet fabric. Cisco reports 70,085.48 tokens/s as the arithmetic sum of separately measured system throughputs and 73,357.62 tokens/s when combined (104.7% of that sum), while meeting applicable p99 TTFT and TPOT limits. MLCommons separately identifies Cisco as the first cross-vendor heterogeneous accelerator submission. The accessible MLCommons summary does not repeat Cisco's exact throughput figures, and the official raw workbook was not retrievable in this review; the numeric performance comparison therefore remains attributed to Cisco.

Why it matters: AI inference clusters increasingly combine different hardware generations and suppliers. Cisco's MLPerf v6.1 submission reports that phase-specific prefill and decode across NVIDIA and AMD accelerators exceeded the arithmetic sum of standalone throughput in one GPT-OSS 120B Server configuration. The mixed-vendor submission is corroborated by MLCommons, while the exact throughput claim still needs raw-row inspection and independent reproduction.

Prefill runs on H200 accelerators and decode on MI350X accelerators. NIXL/UCCL move request-specific KV state over a Cisco Ethernet fabric, using a compatible FP8 cache format; Cisco reports that this phase-specific assignment offsets network transfer cost in this submitted scenario.

Evidence claims

  • CLAIMED — Cisco reports that its GPT-OSS 120B Server PD-disaggregated configuration—eight H200 GPUs for prefill and eight AMD Instinct MI350X GPUs for decode—produced 73,357.62 tokens/s, or 104.7% of the 70,085.48 tokens/s arithmetic sum of separate measurements, while meeting the applicable p99 TTFT and TPOT limits. The exact figures were not checked against the inaccessible raw result workbook in this review.
  • DEMONSTRATED — MLCommons identifies Cisco's MLPerf Inference v6.1 submission as the benchmark's first cross-vendor heterogeneous accelerator deployment using eight NVIDIA H200 and eight AMD Instinct MI350X GPUs. This establishes the submission and configuration description, not a general multi-vendor performance advantage.
  • CLAIMED — Cisco says the combined inference service met the benchmark's applicable p99 time-to-first-token and time-per-output-token limits. The raw result detail was not inspected here.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • The 104.7% throughput comparison and exact GPT-OSS 120B Server rates are Cisco-reported; the raw result workbook was inaccessible in this review.
  • The reported configuration uses eight H200 GPUs for prefill and eight MI350X GPUs for decode, Cisco G200 Ethernet, vLLM, compatible FP8 KV-cache format, NIXL and UCCL. The report's outcome is specific to this model, serving stack, system and benchmark scenario.
  • The comparison is to the arithmetic sum of standalone system measurements, not a matched single-vendor deployment or cost/performance baseline. No hardware acquisition, power, network, or operating costs are compared.
  • MLCommons independently documents the first cross-vendor heterogeneous submission/configuration, but its accessible summary and supplement do not repeat the 104.7% GPT-OSS 120B figure.
  • No general claim of architecture interoperability, absence of cross-architecture overhead on other workloads, production reliability or unit-economics improvement is established.
