Brazil ties data-center tax relief to clean power, local capacity and R&D commitments

Evidence AconfirmedImpact 6/10 · Majoractive

policy_or_regulation · development FCMO-9E06CC5FA8A5 · verified 2026-10-06T00:09:54Z

Importance: 6/10 — Major. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
Conditional tax relief could materially shift data-center equipment costs and investment toward Brazil while attaching substantial domestic-capacity, energy, water-efficiency and R&D commitments.

Brazil's Lei 15.504/2026 creates Redata, a five-year tax-incentive regime for qualifying data-center installation, modernization and expansion, including AI training and inference. Applicants must make at least 10% of the effective processing, storage and data-treatment supply installed with Redata benefits available to Brazil's domestic market; the law calculates that commitment using domestic gross revenue as a share of total gross revenue from the benefited services, with implementation details left to regulation. Applicants must also meet renewable or low-emission electricity and sustainability requirements, keep cooling WUE at or below 0.05 L/kWh, and invest 2% of incentivized ICT-product purchases in local digital-economy R&D. The law suspends specified taxes on eligible ICT equipment; PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions currently run through 2026-12-31 under the tax-transition clause. A separate complementary law, LC 237/2026, also places Redata within specified 2026 exceptions to tax-benefit restrictions, subject to primary-balance and annual-budget conditions, and requires MDIC and the Ministry of Finance to monitor and evaluate the benefits against their objectives.

Why it matters: The regime trades conditional import and equipment-tax relief for domestic compute availability, clean-energy sourcing, water efficiency and local innovation spending. These terms can affect data-center location and project economics, but actual tax expenditure, approvals, construction and compliance outcomes remain unmeasured. LC 237/2026 defines Redata’s treatment under specified 2026 tax-benefit controls and creates a statutory monitoring/evaluation duty, while preserving primary-balance and budget conditions; neither provision establishes actual tax expenditure or impact.

Technical record

Mechanism
Federal Revenue habilitation links tax suspension on qualifying ICT fixed assets to domestic service availability, eligible energy sourcing, water-efficiency, sustainability and R&D commitments; the domestic-service commitment is measured by domestic gross revenue share, and failure can trigger tax recovery, penalties or loss of authorization.
Demonstrated result
Enacted eligibility, tax terms and conditional commitments for Brazilian data-center projects, including AI training and inference.
Publisher claim
The government describes Redata as a way to attract data-center investment, strengthen digital sovereignty and support low-carbon development.
Strongest baseline
The new statute formalizes a dedicated Redata regime rather than generic data-center incentives; its commitments and qualifying equipment lists depend on regulation.
Regime
Brazilian data-center installation, modernization or expansion; statute covers cloud, HPC and AI training/inference. Five-year duration subject to tax-transition clauses and implementing regulations.
Implementation
Lei 15.504/2026 entered into force on publication on 2026-09-15; eligibility and co-eligibility procedures, qualifying goods and several sustainability/accounting details require executive regulation. Lei Complementar 237/2026 (2026-09-15) adds Redata to specified exceptions from 2026 tax-benefit restrictions, conditional on primary-balance compatibility and annual-budget inclusion, and requires MDIC and the Ministry of Finance to monitor and evaluate the benefits against stated objectives.
Compute / cost
The law creates specified tax suspensions; actual savings, tax expenditure, equipment uptake and facility costs have not been measured.
Novelty
An investment tax regime for data centers directly conditions equipment-tax relief on local compute availability and quantified energy, water and R&D obligations.
Reproducibility
Binding statute is publicly available; implementation and realized outcomes remain open.

Evidence claims

  • DEMONSTRATED — Lei 15.504/2026 establishes Redata for projects installing, modernizing or expanding data-center services in Brazil; the statutory definition expressly includes cloud, high-performance computing and AI model training and inference.
  • DEMONSTRATED — To qualify, applicants commit to make at least 10% of the effective processing, storage and data-treatment supply installed with Redata benefits available to the domestic market. The statute measures domestic allocation using annual gross revenue from domestic-market services divided by total gross revenue from the benefited services; free transfers to eligible ICTs or public bodies may count using a regulatory multiplier, and the 10% availability commitment may instead be replaced by an additional 10% investment in eligible R&D. Applicants must also meet sustainability rules; supply all contracted electricity from renewable or low-emission generation via contracts or self-production; report annual cooling WUE no higher than 0.05 L/kWh; and invest 2% of benefited ICT-product purchases in eligible Brazilian digital-economy R&D. Certain local-service and R&D commitments are reduced by 20% for facilities in the North, Northeast and Center-West.
  • DEMONSTRATED — The law suspends PIS/Pasep, Cofins, IPI and import duty on specified qualifying ICT equipment for data-center fixed assets. Import duty relief is limited to equipment without an equivalent domestic product; the regime lasts five years, while PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions are stated to produce effects through 2026-12-31 under the tax-transition clause.
  • DEMONSTRATED — For installations in the North, Northeast and Center-West, the statutory domestic-service and baseline R&D commitments are reduced by 20%; at least 40% of the specified R&D-program resources must be applied to those regions.
  • CLAIMED — The Ministry of Finance says roughly 60% of Brazilian data are stored outside Brazil and frames Redata as a response to digital-service dependence; this is a ministry-reported rationale, not an independently validated estimate in this record.
  • DEMONSTRATED — Lei Complementar 237/2026 includes Redata among exceptions to specified 2026 tax-benefit restrictions, provided that the measure is compatible with the applicable primary-balance target and included in the annual budget; it also requires MDIC and the Ministry of Finance to monitor and evaluate the benefits against their objectives.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • The government’s 60% foreign-hosted-data figure is retained only as a first-party rationale, not independently verified prevalence evidence.
  • Statutory eligibility does not show which companies will receive authorization or whether projects will be built.
  • Several commitments, equipment lists and measuring rules await executive regulation; do not treat announced benefits as realized savings.
  • The five-year regime is subject to the transition clause: PIS/Pasep, Cofins and IPI provisions currently run through 2026-12-31.
  • The statutory inclusion among exceptions to specified 2026 tax-benefit restrictions remains conditioned on primary-balance compatibility and annual-budget inclusion; the complementary law does not quantify realized tax expenditure or demonstrate project uptake.

Open evidence gaps

  • open · other — Executive regulations, equipment lists, authorized projects, actual tax expenditure, investment, domestic capacity delivered, energy and water compliance, regional distribution, and any published evaluation remain unverified. LC 237/2026 requires MDIC and the Ministry of Finance to monitor/evaluate objectives but is not itself an outcome report. — next: Track implementing decrees and Federal Revenue habilitation guidance, then inspect approved-beneficiary and annual sustainability disclosures when published.
