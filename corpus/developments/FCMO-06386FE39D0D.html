CogGym reports a large matched human–model evaluation suite with persistent behavioral gaps

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 5/10 · Notabledeveloping

paper · development FCMO-06386FE39D0D · verified 2026-10-15T19:15:02Z

Importance: 5/10 — Notable. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
If the framework and measurements hold up, a shared pipeline spanning 258 cognitive experiments and three modalities could improve evaluation coverage and expose behavioral limits missed by formal benchmarks. Score 5 reflects useful field-wide infrastructure, not truth confidence; the dataset is scoped to commonsense experiments and the reported comparisons remain author-run.

A September 18 preprint introduces CogGym, a human-in-the-loop pipeline that standardizes cognitive-science experiments into a common format for matched human and language-model evaluation. The initial release reports 258 experiments from 100 papers across text, image and video, with 50 language models evaluated. On the paper’s reported human–model fit measure, the best models reach R² of 0.59 for text, 0.58 for image and 0.43 for video, below estimated human split-half references of 0.93, 0.95 and 0.92. A 15-experiment replication with 521 participants reports closer agreement between new and original human responses than between Gemini 3.1 Pro and the original responses. These are author-reported results; the 15-study check is a subset, not an independent reproduction of the full suite.

Why it matters: A reusable, multimodal translation layer for cognitive-science experiments could make human–model behavioral comparisons easier to update as models change. The reported results also caution against treating strong math or coding performance as evidence of human-like judgments. The current release focuses on commonsense tasks, has missing or unrecoverable archived data in some analyses, and does not establish broad cognitive equivalence or a causal effect of scale.

Technical record

Mechanism
A semi-automated pipeline with researcher review converts heterogeneous cognitive-science experiments into an Experiment Markup Language (EML) representation of instructions, stimuli, trial structure and response format. The standardized tasks can then be rendered for humans and models and their response patterns compared.
Demonstrated result
The preprint documents the framework, experiment corpus, evaluation measures and author-reported analyses. the research desk did not download or run the code/data, reproduce any study, or independently validate model or human scores.
Publisher claim
The authors report 258 standardized experiments from 100 papers and 50 evaluated models. Best reported model–human R² is 0.59 (text), 0.58 (image) and 0.43 (video), compared with human split-half references 0.93, 0.95 and 0.92. In a 15-experiment, 521-participant replication check, new human responses have mean R² 0.84 against original responses versus 0.62 for Gemini 3.1 Pro.
Strongest baseline
Human split-half reliability, estimated from random participant splits and corrected with Spearman–Brown, is the paper’s human agreement reference: R² 0.93 for text, 0.95 for image and 0.92 for video. In the 15-experiment replication subset, newly collected human responses (mean R² 0.84) are compared with Gemini 3.1 Pro (0.62) against original human data.
Regime
Initial suite: 258 cognitive experiments from 100 papers, focused on commonsense reasoning, with text, image and video stimuli; 50 frontier/open-weight models. Human split-half estimates use response items with at least 10 observations and 172 recoverable archived exports (80 text, 33 image, 59 video); the reliability-eligible model subset has 179 experiments. A separate random 15-experiment replication recruited 521 Prolific participants and evaluated Gemini 3.1 Pro against original data.

Compute / cost
The paper does not report a comparable inference-cost or human-study cost analysis for the full 50-model suite. The 521-participant replication is limited to 15 sampled experiments.

Reproducibility
The paper describes an EML format and human-in-the-loop conversion workflow, but the research desk did not inspect or execute the released code/data. The authors’ 15-experiment human replication is an internal validity check, not an independent replication of the full model comparison.

Evidence claims

  • CLAIMED — The authors report standardizing 258 cognitive experiments from 100 papers and evaluating 50 language models on matched human-response tasks spanning text, image and video.
  • CLAIMED — The paper reports best model–human fit R² values of 0.59 for text, 0.58 for image and 0.43 for video, below human split-half references of 0.93, 0.95 and 0.92; these estimates use reliability-eligible/recoverable subsets rather than all 258 experiments.
  • CLAIMED — For a 15-experiment replication subset with 521 participants, the authors report that new human responses agreed more closely with original human responses (mean R² 0.84) than Gemini 3.1 Pro responses did (mean R² 0.62). This subset check does not validate the full suite.
  • NOT_ESTABLISHED — The preprint does not establish that model–human fit is equivalent to general intelligence, transfers beyond its selected commonsense experiments or model set, or causally improves with model scale.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • The initial suite focuses on human commonsense reasoning; it does not measure general intelligence or establish that models think like humans.
  • The 258-experiment inventory is not the analysis denominator for every metric: human reliability references use 172 recoverable archived exports, while the model reliability subset has 179 experiments.
  • A 15-experiment replication with 521 participants is a bounded validity check and does not independently reproduce the full 258-experiment model evaluation.
  • Associations with model size and release date do not establish causal effects of scaling; model lineups and task coverage are heterogeneous.

Open evidence gaps

  • open · other — The main release focuses on commonsense tasks, and human split-half references rely on 172 recoverable exports; some analyses cover smaller eligibility subsets. Coverage outside these tasks and modalities is unknown. — next: Compare the 258-study inventory with its source papers and report conversion completeness, missing records and results by topic/modality.
  • open · other — Cross-sectional associations between model size/release date and human-response fit do not establish causal scaling effects or broad behavioral equivalence. — next: Run matched model-family comparisons with controlled task, prompt, sampling and release conditions, reporting uncertainty and preregistered causal limits.
