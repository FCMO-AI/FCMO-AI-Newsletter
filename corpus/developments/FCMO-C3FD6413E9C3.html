EnterpriseWorldShift tests whether agents can keep business-rule world models current as systems change

Evidence Acredible_unconfirmedImpact 4/10 · Meaningfulactive

dataset_or_benchmark · development FCMO-C3FD6413E9C3 · verified 2026-10-08T13:09:40Z

Importance: 4/10 — Meaningful. Importance measures consequence if true, not evidence quality.
If replicated beyond this constructed setting, the benchmark exposes a meaningful evaluation gap for enterprise agents: carrying forward learned rules can improve prediction after rule edits/additions yet preserve stale rules after silent removals. The current evidence is one author-reported platform-specific benchmark, so consequence is bounded to evaluation and design choices.

Mishra et al. introduce EnterpriseWorldShift, a ServiceNow-based benchmark with nine tables, 25 baseline hidden business rules and four successive versions that modify, add or remove one rule at a time. In author-reported tests across three model backbones, a Continual Discovery Agent (CDA) that carries and revises an explicit rule model beat a rule-lookup baseline by 6.99–8.98 intersection-over-union points across 600 held-out actions per backbone. Yet none of the three backbones retired a removed rule, despite observing its effects disappear and reading a rule list without it. The result is bounded to one constructed environment with readable rules; it is not an independent reproduction or evidence of production enterprise performance.

Why it matters: Enterprise agents depend on organization-specific rules that can change. This benchmark makes silent rule retirement a distinct failure mode: memory helps with some changes but can preserve false expectations when effects stop occurring. It motivates explicit tests for absent expected effects and rule retirement, while the single-platform, readable-rule setup limits transfer.

Technical record

Mechanism
CDA discovers rules by editing records and observing resulting effects, stores each rule as a trigger/effect pair, and carries that model across worlds. Revision and addition are driven by unpredicted observed effects; the paper identifies a missing comparison between expected and absent effects as the retirement failure.
Demonstrated result
The primary paper reports a 6.99–8.98 point IoU advantage over rule lookup across three backbones on its held-out benchmark, alongside universal failure to retire one removed rule. These are reported observations in the paper-defined ServiceNow regime, not independently demonstrated by the research desk.
Publisher claim
In the authors' controlled benchmark, maintaining an explicit discovered business-rule model improves held-out effect prediction over retrieving rules for each question, while stale-rule retirement remains a failure across the three tested backbones.
Strongest baseline
The Discovery Agent reads relevant rules from the live system for every question and retains no model between questions.
Regime
One live ServiceNow instance configured as a benchmark: nine tables, 50 fields, 252 seeded records, 25 baseline rules and 600 held-out evaluation actions across four versions; three backbones. Rules are readable to the relevant conditions; changes are isolated one at a time.

Compute / cost
The paper reports 91–480 discovery actions across worlds by backbone and estimates the fixed discovery cost is repaid after 45–86 questions under its measured query-cost assumptions; no independent system-cost audit was performed.

Evidence claims

  • CLAIMED — The authors report that EnterpriseWorldShift models one ServiceNow enterprise setting with nine tables, 25 baseline rules and four worlds; rule changes are isolated as one modification, one addition and one removal.
  • CLAIMED — Across 600 held-out actions per backbone, the authors report that CDA exceeds a rule-lookup baseline by 6.99–8.98 IoU points for Gemma-4-31B, Qwen-3.6-27B and DeepSeek-V4-Pro.
  • CLAIMED — All three tested backbones revised a changed rule and added a new rule, but none retired the removed rule; logs showed the expected effect was absent and the active rule list no longer contained it.
  • CLAIMED — The authors report that the benchmark's controlled four-world lineage uses readable rules and leaves simultaneous or correlated rule changes for future study.

Limitations / contrary evidence

  • Results are author-reported; no independent reproduction or code execution was performed.
  • The benchmark is one constructed ServiceNow environment with readable rules and controlled, one-rule-at-a-time changes.
  • The rule representation cannot express some record-creation and cross-record effects; five rules are excluded from its rule-recovery denominator.
  • All three backbones failed to retire the removed rule, and continual adaptation underperformed reset in the removal world.
  • The paper's setup does not establish production performance or transfer to opaque, simultaneous or correlated rule changes.

Open evidence gaps

  • open · missing_independent — No independent reproduction of the benchmark or CDA/DA result was performed. — next: Inspect a pinned public implementation if released and independently reproduce the three-backbone comparison and stale-rule retirement behavior.
  • open · other — The benchmark uses one constructed ServiceNow environment with readable rules and one-rule-at-a-time changes; production systems, opaque rules and simultaneous changes are untested. — next: Evaluate independently authored enterprise environments with opaque rules and simultaneous/correlated changes, reporting rule-retirement and false-positive rates.
